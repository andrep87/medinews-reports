ADC Revolution Reshaping Metastatic Breast Cancer Treatment

"Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) [drugs that link chemotherapy to cancer-targeting antibodies for precise delivery] are moving into earlier treatment positions across breast cancer subtypes. Two first-line approvals (Enhertu+pertuzumab for HER2-positive, Datroway for triple-negative) show significantly longer disease control than previous standards. A third ADC (trastuzumab botidotin) shows strong activity in previously treated HER2-positive disease. Choosing and sequencing ADCs after progression remains a major unresolved question."

Two major Phase III trials showed ADC-based regimens significantly improve progression-free survival as first-line treatment: DESTINY-Breast09 (Enhertu [trastuzumab deruxtecan] + pertuzumab) for HER2-positive disease (median PFS 40.7 vs. 26.9 months, HR 0.56) and TROPION-Breast02 (Datroway [datopotamab deruxtecan]) for triple-negative breast cancer without immunotherapy option (10.8 vs. 5.6 months PFS, plus an overall survival benefit of 23.7 vs. 18.7 months).
Separately, the KL166-III-06 Phase III trial showed a new Chinese HER2 ADC (trastuzumab botidotin) reduced the risk of progression or death by 61% vs. T-DM1 (median PFS 11.1 vs. 4.4 months, HR 0.39) in previously treated HER2-positive disease — a second-line setting, not first-line.
Regulatory agencies acted: Japan approved both Enhertu+pertuzumab and Datroway for first-line use; the UK's NICE reversed its prior rejection and now recommends Enhertu for HER2-low metastatic breast cancer after pricing and methodology changes.
The ABC8 international guidelines now incorporate these ADCs earlier in treatment algorithms across subtypes, while explicitly flagging ADC sequencing after progression as a major unresolved question.
Trastuzumab botidotin uses a different chemotherapy payload (MMAF) than Enhertu, with a notably different side-effect profile — mainly reversible eye-related events rather than the lung inflammation (ILD) seen with Enhertu.
For patients, moving effective ADCs to first-line treatment means starting with highly potent targeted therapy when tumor burden is lowest, potentially delaying disease progression by many months compared to older chemotherapy/antibody combinations.
In HER2-positive disease, Enhertu+pertuzumab extends median PFS by about 14 months (roughly 50% improvement) compared to the decade-old standard, setting a new benchmark where median disease control exceeds 3 years.
In triple-negative breast cancer, Datroway provides the first TROP2-directed ADC [a drug targeting the TROP2 protein found on many cancer cells] with a proven overall survival benefit in the first-line setting for patients who cannot receive immunotherapy — a group historically limited to chemotherapy alone.
Trastuzumab botidotin introduces a new ADC class with potentially less lung inflammation risk (ILD 1.1% vs. Enhertu's 12.1% globally), expanding options if Enhertu-related lung toxicity occurs.
HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer: Adults with unresectable or recurrent disease starting first-line systemic therapy (the DESTINY-Breast09 population).
HER2-low metastatic breast cancer (IHC 1+ or 2+/ISH-) [cancers with low but detectable HER2 protein]: Patients whose disease progressed after endocrine-based therapy, now eligible for Enhertu per NICE and FDA approvals.
Triple-negative metastatic breast cancer without immunotherapy option: Patients with PD-L1-negative tumors, prior immunotherapy exposure, or access barriers — the TROPION-Breast02 population.
Previously treated HER2-positive disease: Patients who received prior trastuzumab + taxane, now with trastuzumab botidotin as a new second-line ADC option (approved in China; not yet available globally).
Ask your oncologist: 'Is my cancer HER2-positive, HER2-low, or HER2-zero? Has HER2 status been retested on a recent biopsy or metastatic site?' HER2 expression can change over time and affects ADC eligibility.
If you have HR+/HER2-low metastatic disease progressing on endocrine therapy + CDK4/6 inhibitor, ask: 'Am I a candidate for Enhertu as my next treatment?'
If you have TNBC and were told immunotherapy isn't an option, ask: 'Should I receive Datroway as first-line therapy based on TROPION-Breast02?'
Discuss lung monitoring before starting any ADC: request a baseline chest CT, understand symptoms to report immediately (new cough, fever, shortness of breath), and confirm your team has a rapid steroid-access protocol for suspected ILD [interstitial lung disease, a potentially serious inflammation of the lungs].
If you develop ILD on one ADC, ask about switching to a different ADC class (such as a TROP2-directed drug vs. a HER2-directed one) — current data suggests switching targets or payloads may reduce cross-resistance risk (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: First-line ADC use can deliver years of disease control (40+ months PFS in HER2+), convert TNBC from chemotherapy-only to targeted therapy with a survival benefit, and offer new options after prior ADC failure via different targets/payloads.
  • Reality — Access and Cost: NICE took 2 years to approve Enhertu after its initial rejection. Many countries still lack access. ADC sequencing requires multiple expensive drugs over a treatment lifetime (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality — Toxicity management: Enhertu requires ongoing lung monitoring (ILD can occur at any time during treatment). Datroway needs proactive mouth care and eye care. Trastuzumab botidotin requires slit-lamp eye exams initially, which may not be feasible in all settings.
  • Reality — Not a cure: Metastatic breast cancer remains incurable. ADCs extend disease control but resistance emerges. Overall survival benefit is not yet proven for all regimens (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Enhertu (trastuzumab deruxtecan): HER2-directed ADC. Approved for first-line HER2+ metastatic (with pertuzumab), HER2-low metastatic, and other settings. First-line approval with pertuzumab in Japan, US, EU, and 40+ countries.

Datroway (datopotamab deruxtecan): TROP2-directed ADC. Approved for first-line TNBC without immunotherapy option (Japan, US) and HR+/HER2- post-endocrine (US). TROPION-Breast02 showed an overall survival benefit in TNBC.

Trastuzumab botidotin: HER2-directed ADC with MMAF-derivative payload. Approved in China for second-line+ HER2+ metastatic breast cancer. Notable for low ILD (1.1%) and low blood/liver toxicity; main toxicity is reversible eye-related events (93% any grade, 38% grade 3+).

ADC sequencing: ABC8 guidelines state that evidence for routine sequential ADC use remains insufficient. Ongoing studies are evaluating different sequencing strategies and 'chemo sandwich' approaches between ADCs.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strongest evidence: DESTINY-Breast09 (n=1,157, HR 0.56 for PFS, 40.7 vs. 26.9 months); TROPION-Breast02 (n=644, HR 0.57 PFS, HR 0.79 OS with 23.7 vs. 18.7 months); KL166-III-06 (n=365, HR 0.39 PFS, 11.1 vs. 4.4 months). All randomized and controlled.

Key uncertainties: DESTINY-Breast09 overall survival is immature. TROPION-Breast02 OS benefit is modest (5 months). KL166-III-06 overall survival immature (17.5% maturity).

ADC sequencing unknown: No pivotal trial has enrolled patients with prior ADC exposure. Cross-resistance mechanisms (payload vs. target vs. linker) are not defined. Real-world sequencing is currently based on physician judgment.

Toxicity trade-offs: Enhertu ILD 12.1% globally (33% in a small Japanese subgroup of 39 patients); Datroway mouth sores/eye/ILD approximately 3%; trastuzumab botidotin eye events 93% (grade 3+ 38%), ILD 1.1%. Cardiovascular risks of ADCs are under study (per JACC CardioOncology primer).

Population gaps: Trastuzumab botidotin tested only in Chinese patients; generalizability unknown. DESTINY-Breast09 Japanese ILD rate (33% in n=39) may not reflect global populations.

What To Watch

DESTINY-Breast09 final overall survival analysis and unblinding of the trastuzumab deruxtecan monotherapy arm — will monotherapy match the combination's efficacy with less toxicity?

TROPION-Breast01 (HR+/HER2- Datroway) and DESTINY-Breast06 (HER2-low Enhertu) mature overall survival data.

ADC sequencing trials: Studies testing chemotherapy between ADCs and biomarker-guided sequencing (HER2 expression retention, payload resistance markers).

Cardiovascular safety: Prospective ADC cardiotoxicity registries (per JACC primer) — patients previously treated with anthracyclines may be at higher risk for heart function decline.

Global access: NICE implementation expected in Wales and Northern Ireland; trastuzumab botidotin global development timeline.
