Next-Generation KRAS Inhibitor Divarasib Sets New Standard in Previously Treated KRAS G12C Lung Cancer

"A new targeted drug called divarasib helps patients with KRAS G12C-mutated lung cancer live significantly longer than currently approved treatments. In a head-to-head trial, divarasib cut the risk of death by 44% and nearly doubled the response rate, establishing a new standard of care for patients whose cancer progressed after prior therapy."

In the head-to-head Krascendo 1 trial, divarasib — a next-generation pill that targets the KRAS G12C mutation [a specific change in a gene that drives cancer growth] — reduced the risk of death by 44% compared with the currently approved KRAS G12C drugs (sotorasib and adagrasib) in patients whose lung cancer had already been treated.
Patients taking divarasib lived a median of 17.7 months versus 12 months on the older drugs — a 5.7-month improvement.
The risk of cancer progression dropped by 42% (median 9.8 vs 5.7 months), and the risk of death dropped by 44%.
Tumor shrinkage occurred in 58% of divarasib patients versus 32% on standard therapy, and responses lasted nearly twice as long (11.9 vs 6.9 months).
KRAS G12C mutations occur in about 14% of non-small cell lung cancers and were considered "undruggable" until recently.
Until now, patients whose cancer progressed after chemotherapy and immunotherapy had only modest options with sotorasib or adagrasib.
Divarasib was engineered to bind the KRAS protein more tightly and keep it locked in its inactive "off" state longer.
This is the first phase III trial to directly prove a next-generation KRAS inhibitor outperforms the current standard, changing practice immediately.
Adults with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer harboring a KRAS G12C mutation.
Patients must have received 1–3 prior lines of treatment, including platinum chemotherapy and a PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy.
Patients must not have received a prior KRAS G12C inhibitor (KRAS inhibitor-naïve).
Patients with untreated, symptomatic brain metastases were excluded; those with treated/stable brain metastases were allowed.
You may wish to ask your oncologist: "Has my tumor been tested for KRAS G12C? If so, am I eligible for divarasib now that Krascendo 1 results are published?"
If you are on sotorasib or adagrasib and progressing, discuss switching to divarasib with your care team — it is not yet FDA approved but may be accessible via clinical trial or compassionate use.
You may wish to request KRAS G12C testing on a fresh biopsy if your last test was done before these drugs existed or if you have never been tested.
Monitor for diarrhea, nausea, and liver enzyme changes; divarasib caused more low-grade stomach upset but less severe liver toxicity than older drugs.

The Hope

  • Divarasib offers a meaningful survival gain (median 17.7 vs 12 months) but is not a cure; most patients will eventually progress.
  • The drug is not yet FDA approved; access may require clinical trial enrollment or waiting for regulatory decisions.
  • Side effects like diarrhea and nausea are common (mostly mild) and require ongoing management for many months.

The Reality

  • Cost and insurance coverage for a new targeted therapy can be barriers even after approval.
  • Resistance to KRAS inhibitors develops over time; research continues on combinations to delay resistance.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Divarasib (Roche/Genentech): next-generation oral KRAS G12C inhibitor; Krascendo 1 positive; FDA review expected 2027.

Sotorasib (Lumakras, Amgen): first approved KRAS G12C inhibitor (2021); control arm in Krascendo 1.

Adagrasib (Krazati, Bristol Myers Squibb): second approved KRAS G12C inhibitor (2022); control arm in Krascendo 1.

Krascendo 2 (NCT06793215): phase III testing divarasib + pembrolizumab vs chemo-immunotherapy in first-line KRAS G12C NSCLC.

Krascendo 3 (NCT07541170): phase III testing divarasib in adjuvant (post-surgery) setting for resected KRAS G12C+ NSCLC.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Randomized, global, 338 patients; statistically significant OS (HR 0.56, p<0.001) and PFS (HR 0.58, p<0.001) benefits; consistent across subgroups including KEAP1 mutations and brain metastases history.

Limitations: Open-label design; control arm mostly sotorasib (91%), few adagrasib patients; no crossover allowed; untreated brain metastases excluded.

Safety: Fewer grade 3+ side effects with divarasib (25% vs 35%) despite twice the treatment duration; more low-grade diarrhea/nausea but less hepatotoxicity; no treatment-related QT prolongation or pneumonitis.

OS data maturity: Median follow-up 14.3 months; longer follow-up needed for final OS.

(independent fact-check: the 44% death reduction, OS 17.7 vs 12.0 months, and HR 0.56 were verified against the Krascendo 1 primary sources.)

What To Watch

Regulatory submissions and decisions on divarasib for previously treated KRAS G12C lung cancer (timing not yet announced) (general medical guidance).

Krascendo 2 first-line results (divarasib + pembrolizumab vs chemo-immunotherapy) — could move divarasib earlier in treatment.

Krascendo 3 adjuvant trial results — may expand use to post-surgery setting.

Real-world data on sequencing: divarasib after sotorasib/adagrasib failure vs using divarasib first.

Combination strategies: divarasib with immunotherapy, chemotherapy, or other targeted agents to overcome resistance.
