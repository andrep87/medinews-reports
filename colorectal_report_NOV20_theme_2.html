Molecular Residual Disease (ctDNA) Testing: Transforming Adjuvant Decisions and Surveillance

"Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) testing after curative surgery is changing how doctors decide who needs chemotherapy and how intensely to monitor for recurrence. Landmark studies show postoperative ctDNA positivity confers high recurrence risk, while ctDNA negativity allows safe adjuvant therapy de-escalation in many patients—ushering in molecularly guided, personalized follow-up."

Several trials are testing whether ctDNA results can guide adjuvant treatment decisions (general medical guidance).
CIRCULATE-Japan GALAXY: Postoperative ctDNA positivity strongly prognostic for recurrence; data informing adjuvant escalation (e.g., adding oxaliplatin) for ctDNA+ patients. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: ctDNA-negative patients can avoid 6 months of oxaliplatin (neuropathy risk 30-50% chronic) and fluoropyrimidine (fatigue, hand-foot syndrome)—major quality-of-life win. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: ~10-15% of ctDNA-negative patients still recur (false negatives); no biomarker is perfect; insurance may deny chemo omission based on ctDNA alone until guidelines update. (general medical guidance)
  • Hope: ctDNA-positive patients get earlier intervention—potentially curative metastasectomy or targeted therapy for oligoprogression. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Reality: Lead time not yet proven to improve OS; intensified surveillance increases cost, anxiety, incidental findings; no standard ctDNA+ management algorithm. (general medical guidance)
  • Hope: Watch-and-wait rectal patients gain molecular safety net—ctDNA+ triggers surgery before clinical regrowth. (general medical guidance)
  • Hope: Post-EMR patients have very low cancer risk—endoscopic cure is durable for most.

Evidence Uncertainty

Several trials are testing whether ctDNA results can guide adjuvant treatment decisions (general medical guidance).

CIRCULATE-Japan GALAXY: Large prospective observational; strong prognostic HR for ctDNA+ (recurrence); but adjuvant escalation benefit not yet tested in RCT. (general medical guidance)

MD Anderson real-world: Retrospective; management changes with ctDNA+ but no control group; lead-time bias possible. (general medical guidance)

Post-EMR: TriNetX study (n=8,164 EMR, 22 CRC events over 4 years); low incidence reassuring but underpowered for subgroup analysis; real-world data missing private follow-up.

Anastomotic leak: Korean multicenter (n=4,338); early leak HR 1.64 DFS, 1.85 OS; late leak HR 1.99 OS; but retrospective, single-country, leak definition variable.

A negative ctDNA test does not rule out all residual disease, and the best way to act on results - including whether to reduce or intensify treatment - is still being tested in trials rather than settled (general medical guidance).
