Critical Gaps in Diabetes Care Delivery—Medication Discontinuation After Hospitalization, Racial Adherence Disparities, and Nursing Knowledge Deficits

"Three studies reveal systemic failures in diabetes care: 1) Over 1/3 of patients on SGLT2 inhibitors stop them after hospitalization (83% higher discontinuation risk), 2) Black/Hispanic Medicare patients have significantly lower adherence to all diabetes drugs including low-cost metformin, and 3) 59% of primary care nurses in Saudi Arabia have poor diabetes knowledge, with 13% dangerously overconfident. These gaps directly harm patients and are addressable."

Australian study (NSW, n>100,000): Over 35% of SGLT2 inhibitor [a class of drugs that lower blood sugar by blocking sugar reabsorption in the kidneys] users had at least one hospitalization. Hospitalization increased the risk of stopping the drug by 83% compared to non-hospitalized periods. SGLT2 drugs are often paused during acute illness due to a rare but serious side effect risk, but frequently not restarted afterward.
UCLA study (Medicare Advantage, n=587,000, 2019): Overall diabetes medication adherence <60%. Black patients 46% vs White 56.5% adherent to metformin (p<0.001); similar gaps for insulin and SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA. Medicaid/Part D Low-Income Subsidy reduced gaps.
Saudi nurse study (n=100): Mean diabetes knowledge 53.7%; 59% scored "poor" (<60%). Self-efficacy moderate-high (3.66/5) but weakly correlated with knowledge (rho=0.38). 13% "overconfident" (poor knowledge, high confidence)—dangerous for insulin/glucagon administration.
UAE study (n=33,524): Only 35% of high-risk type 2 diabetes patients were on guideline-recommended RAS inhibitors [blood pressure drugs that protect the kidneys, such as ACE inhibitors or ARBs]. Among patients with albuminuria [excess protein in the urine, a sign of kidney damage], 62% were on treatment — meaning 38% were untreated despite clear guidelines recommending these medications.
SGLT2i discontinuation: Even 30-day gaps increase heart failure hospitalization and death in patients with cardiovascular disease—these drugs are cardiorenal protective, not just glucose-lowering.
Racial adherence gaps: Metformin is cheap and generic—disparity points to non-financial barriers (pharmacy deserts, health literacy, trust, dosing complexity, side effect fears).
Nurse knowledge gap: Nurses deliver most diabetes education; overconfident nurses may give incorrect insulin/glucagon/hypoglycemia guidance without recognizing their deficits.
RAS inhibitor underuse: These are cornerstone medications for diabetic kidney disease [kidney damage caused by diabetes]. While 62% of patients with albuminuria were treated, 38% were not — a significant gap given the strong evidence supporting their use (general medical guidance).
Any patient on SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin) facing planned or unplanned hospitalization.
Black and Hispanic patients with T2D—especially those on Medicare Advantage—who face structural barriers to consistent medication access.
Patients receiving diabetes education from primary care nurses—knowledge gaps may translate to incorrect self-management advice.
T2D patients with hypertension, albuminuria (UACR ≥30), or CKD stages G1-G4 who should be on ACEi/ARB but aren't.
Before or after hospitalization: Ask the discharge team: 'Which of my diabetes, heart, and kidney medicines were paused, and when exactly should I restart each one? Can you write a clear medication list for me to take home?'
If you are Black or Hispanic and struggling with medication costs: Ask your pharmacist or clinic social worker about Medicaid, Medicare Part D Low-Income Subsidy, or manufacturer assistance programs — the UCLA study found these subsidies help close adherence gaps.
If prescribed metformin but not taking it regularly: Tell your provider honestly what's getting in the way (cost, side effects, schedule, forgetting) — they can help problem-solve or simplify your regimen.
If you have type 2 diabetes with high blood pressure or protein in your urine: Ask 'Am I on an ACE inhibitor or ARB [blood pressure drugs that protect the kidneys] at the highest dose I can tolerate? If not, why not?'
When receiving diabetes education: It's reasonable to ask your educator about their training — and to seek a certified diabetes care and education specialist (CDCES) if available (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Fixable system failures—discharge reconciliation, pharmacist involvement, electronic alerts, nurse competency verification, subsidy enrollment—can close gaps quickly.
  • Reality: Fragmented care transitions, pharmacy deserts, prior authorization burdens, inadequate nursing curricula, and clinician inertia are deeply embedded.

The Reality

  • Cost barrier: Even with Part D LIS, some patients face high out-of-pocket costs; insulin pricing lawsuits (Florida AG vs Lilly/Novo/Sanofi + PBMs) highlight systemic profiteering.
  • Behavior change: "Knowing what to do doesn't guarantee doing it"—sustained adherence requires addressing context (routines, cues, resources, friction), not just education.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin): Cardiorenal protection in T2D, HF, CKD; hospitalization discontinuation crisis identified.

GLP-1 RAs (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide): Adherence gaps for high-cost agents; Part D LIS helps but not universal.

RAS inhibitors (ACEi/ARB): Lisinopril, losartan, valsartan—generic, cheap, guideline-first-line for DKD; 65% untreated in high-risk UAE cohort.

Finerenone: Non-steroidal MRA approved for CKD in T2D (FIDELIO/FIGARO) and now T1D (FINE-ONE); requires UACR monitoring, potassium checks.

Health system interventions: Pharmacist-led discharge reconciliation, electronic alerts for omitted SGLT2i, nurse competency verification programs.

Evidence Uncertainty

SGLT2i study: Retrospective claims data; cannot confirm clinical reasoning for discontinuation; NSW population may not generalize.

Adherence study: 2019 data (pre-IRA insulin caps); Medicare Advantage only; "adherent" = 80% days covered (PDC) may overestimate real-world use.

Nurse study: Single network, convenience sample, self-reported confidence not observed performance; Saudi context may differ from other systems.

RAS inhibitor study: Single-center UAE, cross-sectional; contraindications/intolerance not captured; may overestimate true treatment gap.

What To Watch

Hospital policy changes: Mandatory SGLT2i restart protocols at discharge; pharmacist-led medication reconciliation with patient teach-back.

CMS/Part D reforms: IRA insulin $35 cap expansion; LIS auto-enrollment; adherence metrics in STAR ratings.

Nursing education: Competency-based diabetes curricula with simulation (glucagon injection, hypoglycemia management, Ramadan counseling).

Guideline implementation: ADA/EASD 2026 consensus recommending GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i as first-line; monitoring real-world uptake vs RASi.

Patient navigation programs: Community health workers, digital adherence tools, culturally tailored support for high-disparity populations.
