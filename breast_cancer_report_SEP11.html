Camizestrant (Etcamah) Plus CDK4/6 Inhibitor: First ctDNA-Guided Treatment Switch for ESR1-Mutated Advanced Breast Cancer

"The FDA approved camizestrant (Etcamah) combined with a CDK4/6 inhibitor for hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer. This approval applies when a blood test detects an ESR1 resistance mutation during first-line hormonal therapy. This is the first cancer treatment approved based on a liquid biopsy finding before tumors grow on scans. It offers patients a proactive switch that delays progression by about 7 months compared to continuing standard therapy."

The FDA gave accelerated approval to camizestrant (Etcamah), an oral drug that breaks down estrogen receptors. It is used with any of the three CDK4/6 inhibitors (abemaciclib, palbociclib, or ribociclib) for adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer.
This approval applies when a blood test (Guardant360 CDx) detects an ESR1 mutation [a genetic change that causes resistance to standard hormonal therapy] during first-line treatment with an aromatase inhibitor [a drug that lowers estrogen] plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor, before tumors grow on scans.
The decision came from the SERENA-6 phase III trial. In this trial, 315 patients with an emerging ESR1 mutation but no tumor growth on scans were randomly assigned to switch to camizestrant plus their current CDK4/6 inhibitor or stay on the aromatase inhibitor plus CDK4/6 inhibitor.
Patients who switched to camizestrant had a median progression-free survival [time until cancer worsens] of 16.0 months vs 9.2 months for those who stayed on standard therapy. This is a 56% reduction in the risk of progression or death (hazard ratio 0.44, p<0.00001).
This approval marks a major shift: treatment changes are now triggered by a blood test that detects molecular resistance months before tumors appear on CT or PET scans. This allows earlier intervention while the cancer is still controlled.
About 30% of patients on first-line hormonal therapy plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor develop ESR1 mutations before their cancer progresses on scans. This strategy gives them a targeted next step without waiting for symptoms or scan changes.
Continuing the same CDK4/6 inhibitor while only changing the hormonal drug preserves the cell-cycle blockade that may still be working. This reflects a more precise understanding of how resistance evolves. (general medical guidance)
Patient-reported outcomes showed delayed worsening of quality of life, pain, and physical functioning with camizestrant, meaning patients feel better longer.
Adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer who are on first-line treatment with an aromatase inhibitor (letrozole or anastrozole) plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor (palbociclib, ribociclib, or abemaciclib).
Patients whose blood test (Guardant360 CDx) shows an ESR1 mutation during first-line therapy, but whose scans do not yet show cancer progression.
This does not apply to patients with early-stage breast cancer, HER2-positive disease, triple-negative breast cancer, or those who have already progressed on first-line therapy.
Patients must have adequate heart function (resting heart rate at least 55 beats per minute) and no uncontrolled electrolyte abnormalities due to cardiac safety warnings.

Triple-Negative Breast Cancer: Novel Metabolic-Immune Targets and Diagnostic Advances

"New research reveals how triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) hijacks metabolism to suppress the immune system and spread, identifying several druggable targets including methylglyoxal stress, the KYNU enzyme, and the MINK1-ALDOA axis. Simultaneously, advances in single-cell diagnostics and early PET imaging are moving toward personalized treatment selection for this aggressive subtype."

Researchers at the University of Liège found that methylglyoxal (MG) stress — a toxic byproduct of rapid glucose metabolism — causes a buildup of immunosuppressive cells called g-MDSCs (granulocyte-derived myeloid suppressor cells) in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). This promotes lung metastasis and resistance to PD-1 immunotherapy [a type of immune checkpoint inhibitor]. In resistant models, neutralizing MG with carnosine plus anti-PD-1 reduced metastasis.
Another study found that the bacterium Fusobacterium nucleatum is more abundant in TNBC tumors. It activates a pathway (IL20-JAK-STAT4-IL4I1) that changes tryptophan metabolism, suppresses CD8+ T cells [immune cells that kill cancer], and drives metastasis. Blocking IL4I1 or the JAK-STAT4 pathway reversed these effects.
Scientists at King Khalid University developed KS79356, a potent inhibitor of kynureninase (KYNU) [an enzyme in tryptophan metabolism]. It blocks the TNF-α/NFκB/CD44/Akt inflammatory pathway, reducing TNBC cell growth, invasion, trans-endothelial migration [cancer cells crossing blood vessel walls], and triggering cell death (apoptosis).
A Beijing-led team identified MINK1 kinase as the enzyme that modifies aldolase A (ALDOA) at a specific site (serine 39), stabilizing this sugar-metabolism enzyme and speeding up glucose metabolism. The inhibitor KY-05009 blocked this process, suppressing tumor growth and metastasis in several preclinical models.
One Biosciences and Institut Curie launched the CAST project with €3.3 million funding. They are developing OneMap™, a single-cell diagnostic platform that uses AI and gene expression analysis on standard biopsies to predict response to immunotherapy or chemotherapy in TNBC within two weeks.
The TNPET01 phase II trial showed that FDG-PET scans [glucose metabolism imaging] after just one chemotherapy cycle strongly predicted how much cancer remained at surgery. It outperformed MRI, suggesting early metabolic imaging could help adapt treatment.
TNBC lacks estrogen, progesterone, and HER2 targets, so chemotherapy and immunotherapy are the main options, but they often stop working. These discoveries reveal a new frontier: the crosstalk between metabolism and the immune system.
Methylglyoxal stress increases immunosuppressive cells called g-MDSCs. Separately, the bacterium F. nucleatum suppresses CD8+ T cells [cancer-killing immune cells] through a different pathway. Targeting tumor metabolism or the microbiome could help immunotherapy work better.
KYNU and MINK1-ALDOA are enzymes at key metabolic junctions. Blocking both could hit energy production, inflammatory signals, stem-like traits (CD44), and the ability to spread (trans-endothelial migration). This is a hypothetical combination strategy. (general medical guidance)
Single-cell diagnostics like OneMap™ aim to replace bulk tissue analysis with cell-by-cell mapping of resistance-driving cell groups. This could enable precise matching of patients to therapies.
Early PET scans could identify non-responders sooner, sparing them ineffective neoadjuvant treatment and allowing faster entry into clinical trials or other strategies.

Especially relevant for metastatic TNBC resistant to current immunotherapy, or early-stage patients considering neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy where response prediction is uncertain. (general medical guidance)

HER2-Positive Early Breast Cancer: Neoadjuvant Regimen Innovations

"A phase III trial in China showed that replacing standard dual HER2 blockade (trastuzumab + pertuzumab) plus solvent-based docetaxel with a single biparatopic antibody (anbenitamab) plus albumin-bound docetaxel (HB1801) significantly increased pathologic complete response (tpCR) from 51% to 62% without adding severe toxicity. Meanwhile, chemo-free dual HER2 blockade approaches and pertuzumab biosimilars are expanding de-escalation options."

The BCTOP-HN01 phase III trial (also called Neo-Healer) enrolled 521 patients at 61 Chinese hospitals. It compared six cycles of anbenitamab (KN026, a biparatopic HER2 antibody [binds two HER2 sites]) plus HB1801 (albumin-bound docetaxel [chemo without harsh solvents]) versus standard trastuzumab + pertuzumab + docetaxel (THP) for stage II-III HER2-positive breast cancer.
Total pathologic complete response (tpCR) [no invasive cancer in breast or lymph nodes at surgery] was 62.4% with anbenitamab+HB1801 vs 51.2% with THP (absolute improvement 11.4%, 95% CI 3.2-19.6, p=0.004). The benefit was consistent across hormone receptor status, cancer stage, and whether carboplatin was used.
Grade 3-4 treatment-related side effects were similar (29.3% vs 28.3%). Nearly all patients (98%) finished all cycles. No symptomatic heart failure occurred in either group.
In the PHERGain-2 study, node-negative patients with tumors under 3 cm received neoadjuvant trastuzumab + pertuzumab alone (no chemotherapy). They achieved about 60% pathologic complete response (pCR). Those who achieved pCR continued targeted therapy alone, avoiding chemotherapy entirely.
Pertuzumab biosimilar BCD-178 demonstrated equivalent neoadjuvant pCR rates to reference pertuzumab, potentially lowering cost and expanding access globally (general medical guidance).
Anbenitamab's biparatopic design [binds two different HER2 sites] aims to cause more potent receptor clustering, internalization, and degradation than two separate antibodies. This may overcome resistance mechanisms that limit standard dual blockade (general medical guidance).
HB1801's albumin-bound formulation removes polysorbate 80 and ethanol solvents. This eliminates the need for high-dose steroid premedication and allows rapid infusion, improving convenience and reducing steroid-related side effects.
The regimen improved tpCR without increasing severe toxicity. This balance is critical in curative treatment, where overtreatment can cause long-term morbidity such as neuropathy, cardiac dysfunction, or secondary cancers (general medical guidance).
PHERGain-2 shows chemotherapy can be omitted in a selected favorable group, using pCR as a biomarker to guide whether to escalate or de-escalate treatment — a model for personalized neoadjuvant therapy.

For anbenitamab+HB1801: Benefit was seen in both hormone receptor-positive and hormone receptor-negative tumors, and regardless of carboplatin use. However, the trial only included Chinese patients, so generalizability is pending.
For chemo-free dual blockade (PHERGain-2): Node-negative patients with tumors under 3 cm, HER2-positive (IHC 3+ or 2+/ISH+), who are willing to switch to T-DM1 if pCR is not achieved.
Pertuzumab biosimilar: For all patients indicated for pertuzumab-based regimens, especially in cost-constrained settings (general medical guidance).

Precision Risk Prediction and Early Detection: AI, Liquid Biopsy, and Screening Evolution

"Artificial intelligence models analyzing longitudinal 3D mammograms and breast MRI now predict 5-year breast cancer risk and preoperative lymphovascular invasion more accurately than traditional tools. Simultaneously, serial ctDNA monitoring after surgery detects recurrence nearly a year before scans, and updated guidelines recommend earlier and more frequent screening, enabling truly personalized prevention and surveillance."

NYU researchers built NYU-DRP, an AI model that uses longitudinal 3D mammograms (digital breast tomosynthesis, or DBT) from 161,000 women over several years to predict 5-year breast cancer risk. It scored AUC 0.721, beating single-timepoint DBT (0.707), 2D mammography AI Mirai (0.687), and the clinical Tyrer-Cuzick model (0.563). The Tyrer-Cuzick comparison came from a separate matched case-control group of 432 women, not the main test set.
The model reclassified risk beyond breast density: about 40% of women with extremely dense breasts were actually average risk (0.8% cancer incidence), while about 15% with fatty breasts were high risk (2.6% incidence). This shows density alone is a crude proxy.
In the TRACER study, an ultrasensitive ctDNA test (structural variant digital PCR) found minimal residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy and surgery. Detectable ctDNA after surgery had 100% positive predictive value for metastatic recurrence (22 out of 22 patients), with a median lead time of 346 days before clinical recurrence.
Singapore's Screening Test Review Committee now recommends annual mammograms for women 40-49 and every two years for 50-74 (previously 50-69). It also formalized osteoporosis screening from age 50 using a weight-age risk score (age minus weight in kg).
A Chinese team at Sun Yat-sen University built a transformer-based AI model that uses contrast-enhanced breast MRI to predict lymphovascular invasion (LVI) [tumor cells in lymph or blood vessels, indicating spread risk] before surgery. It achieved AUC 0.873 internally and 0.845 externally, using automated tumor segmentation, radiomics, and BI-RADS features.
Current risk models (Gail, Tyrer-Cuzick) use broad factors like age, family history, and density, missing individual imaging patterns. Longitudinal AI captures dynamic tissue changes invisible to radiologists, enabling truly personalized screening intensity.
Post-surgical ctDNA surveillance changes follow-up: molecular detection gives a median 11-month lead time before clinical recurrence. But lead time is not proven survival benefit; prospective trials are needed to show if acting on ctDNA improves outcomes.
Earlier and more frequent screening starting at 40 aligns with guidelines; formal osteoporosis screening addresses survivorship bone health. (general medical guidance)
