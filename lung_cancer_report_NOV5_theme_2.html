Neoadjuvant and Perioperative Immunotherapy in Resectable NSCLC: Real-World Evidence Confirms Long-Term Survival Benefit Without Added Surgical Risk

"A real-world Yale study of 95 patients confirms that adding immunotherapy to neoadjuvant chemotherapy before lung cancer surgery is associated with dramatically improved 5-year survival (74% vs 47%) and recurrence-free survival (60 vs 26 months), without increasing major surgical complications. This aligns with four major phase III trials (CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671, AEGEAN, CheckMate 77T), establishing perioperative immunotherapy as a new standard for resectable NSCLC without EGFR/ALK mutations."

Yale retrospective study (95 patients, 2015-2025): Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (n=57) vs chemotherapy alone (n=38)—5-year OS 74.3% vs 47.3% (p=0.04); median RFS 60.0 vs 26.2 months (p=0.02). (general medical guidance)
No significant difference in major surgical complications (15.8% vs 13.2%), operative time, conversion to open surgery, or extent of resection (lobectomy 81% vs 74%). (general medical guidance)
Major pathological response (MPR ≤10% viable tumor) strongly predicted survival in immunotherapy group: median OS not reached with MPR vs 45.7 months without (p=0.01). (general medical guidance)
Surgery alone cures only ~50% of stage II-III NSCLC; adding immunotherapy before and/or after surgery attacks micrometastases systemically. (general medical guidance)
Neoadjuvant immunotherapy shrinks tumors, increases MPR/pCR rates, and may convert borderline resectable to clearly resectable disease. (general medical guidance)
Real-world data confirm trial benefits extend to broader populations (older, comorbidities, complex surgery) without increasing surgical morbidity. (general medical guidance)
MPR emerges as a powerful prognostic marker—potentially guiding future adjuvant treatment de-escalation or intensification.
Patients with resectable NSCLC (stages IB-IIIB, N2) without EGFR sensitizing mutations or ALK rearrangements.

Research Progress

Discuss timing of surgery and systemic therapy with your multidisciplinary team (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Yale real-world: Small (n=95), retrospective, non-randomized, era imbalance (immunotherapy group treated more recently), selection bias (only patients reaching surgery included). (general medical guidance)

MPR prognostic but not yet validated as surrogate for OS; adjuvant therapy omission based on MPR remains investigational.

What To Watch

Neoadjuvant immunotherapy in EGFR/ALK-positive disease: Combining TKIs with immunotherapy (safety challenges). (general medical guidance)

Response-adapted approaches: Escalate (add chemo/radiation) for poor neoadjuvant response; de-escalate (omit adjuvant) for pCR/MPR.

Global access: Bringing perioperative immunotherapy to low/middle-income countries where surgery may be only curative option. (general medical guidance)
