First-in-Class Tumor-Activated Immunotherapy Enters Clinical Testing for Advanced Prostate Cancer

"A novel dual-immunotherapy approach (JANX013 + JANX007) has begun Phase 1 testing in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer [cancer that grows despite hormone therapy]. It combines a PSMA-targeted T-cell engager [a drug that redirects immune cells to attack cancer] with a tumor-restricted CD28 co-stimulator [a drug that sustains immune cell energy and memory at the tumor site] — designed to activate anti-tumor immune responses while sparing healthy tissue. This is a first-in-human safety study; efficacy data are years away."

Janux Therapeutics dosed the first patient in a Phase 1 trial (NCT07813637) evaluating JANX013 (a PSMA-targeted CD28 co-stimulatory bispecific antibody) combined with JANX007 (a PSMA-targeted T-cell engager) in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC).
JANX007 is a 'TRACTr' (Tumor-Activated T-Cell Engager) that binds PSMA on tumor cells and CD3 on T-cells [an immune cell activation switch]. Its tumor-activated design aims to restrict T-cell activation to the tumor site, reducing off-tumor toxicity.
JANX013 is a 'TRACIr' (Tumor-Activated Immunomodulator) that delivers CD28 co-stimulation [a 'second signal' that sustains T-cell energy and memory] selectively to tumor-infiltrating T-cells, minimizing systemic immune activation.
The trial plans to enroll approximately 110 patients. The primary endpoint is safety and tolerability. Secondary endpoints include drug levels in the blood, immune cell responses, and preliminary anti-tumor activity.
Prostate cancer has been largely resistant to immunotherapy approaches that work in other cancers. The tumor microenvironment tends to suppress immune activity, and T-cells that enter the tumor often become exhausted (general medical guidance).
CD28 co-stimulation is critical for T-cell survival and long-term immune memory. However, stimulating CD28 throughout the body — rather than just at the tumor — caused dangerous immune overreactions in a past clinical trial (TGN1412, 2006), making tumor-restricted delivery essential (general medical guidance).
Tumor-restricted delivery (active only where PSMA is expressed) aims to unlock CD28 benefits safely.
Combining two PSMA-targeted agents (a T-cell engager plus a co-stimulator) may overcome resistance to single-agent T-cell engagers by keeping T-cells active and functional longer at the tumor site (general medical guidance).
Men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) who have progressed on at least one ARPI.
For dose escalation: must have received taxane chemotherapy or be medically unsuitable for or have declined taxane.
Must have PSMA-positive disease confirmed by PSMA PET scan at screening.
Exclusions include: prior T-cell engager or co-stimulatory agent therapy, active brain metastases, significant cardiovascular disease, or poor performance status (ECOG >1).
If you have mCRPC progressing on standard therapies: Ask your oncologist 'Am I eligible for any T-cell engager or bispecific antibody trials?' Mention trial NCT07813637 specifically.
Ensure you have a recent PSMA PET/CT (within 8 weeks) showing PSMA-expressing disease — this is required for trial screening.
If not eligible for this trial: Ask about other PSMA-targeted immunotherapy trials, which are being tested by several companies (general medical guidance).
Understand that Phase 1 means this is a safety study — the primary goal is determining whether the combination is safe, not proving it works. Efficacy signals are not expected before 2027–2028 at the earliest (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: The first prostate cancer immunotherapy specifically designed to work in tumors that suppress immune activity; tumor-activated platform could minimize the dangerous systemic effects that have plagued CD28 agonists.
  • Reality: Phase 1 means efficacy signals are unlikely before 2027–2028. Most patients will not be able to access this drug until Phase 2/3 or approval, which is 5 or more years away if development succeeds (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: Requires frequent clinic visits (IV infusions with monitoring for immune reactions). Available only at specialized trial sites, not community oncology practices.
  • Reality: Prostate cancer is biologically heterogeneous — some tumors may escape by losing PSMA expression. Combining with other treatments (radiation, ADT) is untested (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

JANX007 (TRACTr): PSMA x CD3 bispecific T-cell engager; Phase 1 ongoing in mCRPC. Its 'tumor-activated' design masks the CD3-binding arm until PSMA is engaged, aiming to reduce off-tumor toxicity.

JANX013 (TRACIr): PSMA x CD28 bispecific co-stimulator; first-in-class tumor-restricted CD28 agonist designed to enhance JANX007's durability.

JANX014: A double-masked PSMA TRACTr (additional safety layer); also in Phase 1.

Broader Janux pipeline: The TRACTr and TRACIr platforms are being applied to other targets (CD19 for autoimmune disease, others undisclosed).

Evidence Uncertainty

What we know: The TRACTr and TRACIr platforms have shown selective T-cell activation in preclinical (animal and lab) models. JANX007 monotherapy Phase 1 is ongoing but data have not been published.

Major uncertainty: Will the dual bispecific combination cause additive cytokine release syndrome [an immune overreaction causing fever, low blood pressure, and breathing difficulty] or neurotoxicity, despite the tumor-restricted design?

Unknown: Optimal dosing schedule, how to sequence JANX013 and JANX007, durability of any response, and how tumors may escape (for example, by losing PSMA expression) (general medical guidance).

Important context: This is Phase 1 — a safety study in a small number of patients. Most Phase 1 oncology drugs do not ultimately reach approval (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

JANX007 monotherapy Phase 1 data: Safety profile, recommended Phase 2 dose, and any preliminary PSA or imaging responses (expected at a major oncology conference in 2027).

JANX013 + JANX007 combination dose escalation: Immune reaction rates, maximum tolerated dose, and T-cell expansion data.

Whether other companies' PSMA-targeted bispecific antibodies report clinical data that help contextualize the Janux approach (general medical guidance).
