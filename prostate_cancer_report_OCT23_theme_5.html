PSMA Radioligand Therapy Moves Earlier: Oligometastatic Disease and Precision Patient Selection

"PSMA-targeted radioligand therapy (like Lu-PSMA-617) is advancing from late-line mCRPC into earlier disease states, with phase II trials (LUNAR, BULLSEYE) showing significant progression-free survival benefits in oligometastatic prostate cancer. Meanwhile, rigorous PSMA PET-based patient selection criteria are being defined to ensure only those with PSMA-avid disease receive this expensive, specialized treatment."

The LUNAR trial (92 patients, 1–5 metastases) randomized men to standard metastasis-directed therapy (MDT) with SBRT (stereotactic body radiation) alone vs MDT plus two cycles of Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy. PFS improved from 7.4 to 17.6 months (HR 0.37); 98% of progressions were out-of-field (occult disease), confirming the drug hits microscopic deposits.
The BULLSEYE trial (58 patients, up to 5 metastases, higher disease burden) compared Lu-PSMA (2-4 cycles) with observation. Median PFS improved from about 5 to 25 months; however, most progressions in treated patients were local (in-field), suggesting combining metastasis-directed therapy with radioligand therapy may improve durability (general medical guidance).
CCTG PR21 (PLUDO) compared Lu-PSMA-617 vs docetaxel in chemotherapy-naïve mCRPC; at ESMO 2026, patient-reported quality-of-life results will be presented (primary PFS/OS already published: similar PFS, OS favored docetaxel but confounded by crossover).
Urology Times Virtual Tumor Board emphasized: PSMA PET avidity across ALL metastatic sites is mandatory before Lu-PSMA; non-avid lesions (including neuroendocrine transformation) will not respond. Disease volume matters — single lesions may be better treated with SBRT to preserve future options.
PSMA PET is now essential before definitive therapy (surgery/radiation) for high-risk localized disease to rule out occult metastases and guide nerve-sparing or radiation margins.
Radioligand therapy delivers radiation systemically to PSMA-expressing cells, potentially eradicating micrometastases that imaging cannot see — addressing the "occult disease" that drives recurrence after local therapy alone.
Moving Lu-PSMA earlier (oligometastatic, hormone-sensitive settings) could delay or prevent castration resistance, preserving quality of life by postponing chemotherapy and intensive hormonal therapy.
Rigorous patient selection (PSMA PET avidity, disease volume, histologic type) prevents futile treatment, reduces toxicity exposure, and preserves the drug for those most likely to benefit.
Understanding PSMA PET agent differences (gallium-68 vs fluorine-18, urinary excretion effects) improves scan interpretation and reduces false positives/negatives.
Men with oligometastatic prostate cancer (1–5 metastases on PSMA PET/CT) who have not yet received systemic therapy or are hormone-sensitive — candidates for LUNAR-type intensification (MDT + Lu-PSMA).
Men with mCRPC progressing after ARPI, being evaluated for Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto) — must have PSMA-PET positive disease at all metastatic sites, adequate PSMA expression, and no dominant non-avid lesions.
Men with high-risk localized prostate cancer undergoing staging — PSMA PET before surgery/radiation detects occult nodal/bone metastases in ~10–20% of conventionally staged patients, altering management.
Men with biochemical recurrence after definitive therapy — PSMA PET at low PSA (0.2–0.5 ng/mL) guides salvage therapy decisions (local vs systemic).
If you have recurrent or metastatic prostate cancer, you may wish to ask your doctor about a PSMA PET/CT scan before starting any new systemic therapy, as it is widely used for staging and treatment selection (general medical guidance).
If considering Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto), you may wish to confirm that all metastatic sites are PSMA-avid on the PET scan, that no lesions are PSMA-negative, and that your kidney function and blood counts are adequate for radioligand therapy (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask about clinical trials adding Lu-PSMA to metastasis-directed therapy (SBRT) for oligometastatic disease (general medical guidance).
If you experience dry mouth on Lu-PSMA, you may wish to ask your care team about salivary-gland protection measures such as hydration and sugar-free lozenges (general medical guidance).
For high-risk localized disease, you may wish to ask whether PSMA PET staging before surgery or radiation is appropriate, since it can change management in some patients (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Radioligand therapy could convert oligometastatic disease from a chronic, repeatedly treated condition into a potentially curable one by eradicating occult micrometastases.
  • Reality: current data are phase II; no phase III trial has yet proven an overall-survival benefit in the oligometastatic setting, and LUNAR compared SBRT plus Lu-PSMA against SBRT alone rather than against standard systemic therapy (independent fact-check: the draft described LUNAR as 'single-arm'; it was a randomized two-arm trial).
  • Hope: for appropriate patients, Lu-PSMA is given as outpatient infusions and may have a different side-effect profile than chemotherapy (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: PSMA radioligand therapy is expensive and requires specialized nuclear-medicine facilities; dry mouth (salivary gland toxicity) can affect quality of life long-term, and not all centers offer it (general medical guidance).
  • Hope: Alpha emitters (Ac-225) may be more effective for small-volume disease.
  • Reality: Alpha therapy has higher hematologic toxicity; manufacturing supply chain for Ac-225 is limited; dosimetry not standardized.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto): FDA-approved for mCRPC after an ARPI and chemotherapy; being studied in earlier settings, including pre-chemotherapy mCRPC, hormone-sensitive disease, and oligometastatic disease (general medical guidance).

Lu-PSMA-I&T: a related radioligand used in the BULLSEYE trial, with a different linker/chelator (general medical guidance).

Ac-225-PSMA (alpha emitter): Higher linear energy transfer; being tested in ANDROMEDA trial (vs Lu-PSMA) for micrometastatic disease; potential for deeper responses but higher toxicity risk.

PSMA PET agents: Ga-68-PSMA-11 (Locametz), F-18-DCFPyL (Pylarify), F-18-PSMA-1007 — differ in urinary excretion (kidney/bladder interference), tumor-to-background ratio, and availability.

Evidence Uncertainty

LUNAR: Phase II, single institution (UCLA), 92 patients; primary endpoint PFS (composite: PSMA PET progression, salvage ADT, death); 98% out-of-field progression supports mechanism; small sample, short follow-up, no OS data yet.

BULLSEYE: Phase II, 58 patients, higher burden; PFS benefit but high local failure rate (75%) without concurrent MDT; suggests MDT + radioligand needed for cure.

PR21: Phase II, 199 patients; Lu-PSMA non-inferior to docetaxel for rPFS (HR 1.02); OS favored docetaxel (HR 1.64) but crossover imbalance confounds; QoL data at ESMO 2026.

Major uncertainty: Is PFS benefit on PSMA PET a valid surrogate for OS? Polymetastatic progression (>5 new lesions) correlates with mortality (HR 3.2), but PET-driven endpoints not yet validated.

What To Watch

PSMA-DC trial (Novartis-sponsored phase III): 4 cycles adjuvant Lu-PSMA-617 + MDT vs MDT alone in oligometastatic hormone-sensitive disease — registrational potential.

ANDROMEDA trial (Novartis IIT): Ac-225-PSMA (1 cycle) vs Lu-PSMA (2 cycles) in oligometastatic disease — tests alpha vs beta emitters.

CCTG PR21 QoL results at ESMO 2026: patient-reported outcomes for Lu-PSMA vs docetaxel in mCRPC.

PSMAddition trial (Lu-PSMA + ARPI + ADT in mHSPC) — phase III, fully enrolled, results expected 2027.

Development of standardized PSMA PET-based response criteria for trials and practice (general medical guidance).
