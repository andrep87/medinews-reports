Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer — Rates Rising in Adults Under 50, Major Research Investment Launched, and Genomic Clues Emerge

"Colorectal cancer in adults under 50 is rising rapidly and is now among the leading causes of cancer death in this age group. A new genomic study of nearly 1,900 metastatic patients shows early-onset and average-onset tumors share the same core mutations (TP53, APC, KRAS), but MYC alterations are more common in younger patients, and DNA repair genes (BRCA2, BARD1) show age-dependent enrichment. Australia just launched a $3 million Center of Research Excellence (CRE ARTEMIS) to investigate causes — from microbiome to immune susceptibility — and train the next generation of researchers."

A Samsung Medical Center study (1,892 metastatic CRC patients, 376 early-onset <50, 1,516 average-onset ≥50) used two next-gen sequencing panels (TruSight Oncology 500, Oncomine Comprehensive) to compare genomic landscapes. Core drivers (TP53 78% vs 77%, APC 70% vs 71%, KRAS 47% vs 49%) were nearly identical.
Key differences: MYC alterations were significantly enriched in early-onset (15.7% vs lower in average-onset, P=0.012). In age-stratified analysis within early-onset, younger age correlated with higher rates of BRCA2, BARD1, FAT1, HIST1H1C, and LRP1B alterations — all involved in DNA damage response.
Bowel Cancer Australia committed $3 million (plus $2.8M institutional matching) to establish CRE ARTEMIS, a 22-member, multi-university Center of Research Excellence led by Prof Daniel Buchanan, investigating etiology, risk factors, tumorigenesis, microbiome, and immune susceptibility in early-onset CRC over 5 years.
The FDA cleared an IND for a first-in-human dual-target CAR T-cell therapy (B7-H3 + IL-8) developed at CU Anschutz for adults with advanced CRC and pediatric solid tumors — B7-H3 is expressed on most colorectal tumors, potentially broadening eligibility beyond biomarker-selected subgroups.
Early-onset CRC is not a genomically *distinct* disease — it shares the same "canonical backbone" — but it shows *age-associated vulnerabilities* (MYC, DNA repair genes) that may explain why it arises earlier and could guide targeted prevention or therapy.
The MYC enrichment and DNA repair gene patterns suggest younger patients' tumors may have distinct biology that could eventually be exploited therapeutically — for example, PARP inhibitors for BRCA2-altered tumors are approved in other cancer types and being studied in CRC (general medical guidance).
CRE ARTEMIS represents a coordinated, well-funded effort to move beyond associations to *causal mechanisms* — including environmental exposures, microbiome, and immune interactions — which have been fragmented until now.
The dual-target CAR T trial addresses a critical gap: most immunotherapies only work in MSI-H/dMMR tumors (~5% of CRC). B7-H3 is broadly expressed, potentially offering a cellular therapy for the majority (MSS) who currently have no immunotherapy options.

The Hope

  • Hope: We are finally investing seriously in why young people get CRC — not just treating it. Genomic clues (DNA repair, MYC) point to biology that may eventually be targetable.
  • Reality: Causes are likely multifactorial — no single explanation will emerge quickly. Prevention strategies are years away (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • The CAR T trial is a safety study first. Solid tumor responses to CAR T therapy have historically been rare and often short-lived (general medical guidance).
  • Genomic profiling may find potentially targetable alterations, but matched drugs may not be approved or accessible for CRC specifically (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Dual-target CAR T (B7-H3 + IL-8) [immune cells engineered to target two molecules on cancer cells]: Autologous T cells manufactured at CU Anschutz Gates Facility; IND [investigational new drug application] cleared by FDA; trial expected to begin enrollment (date not confirmed) for advanced CRC and pediatric solid tumors.

Genomic study findings: MYC alterations enriched in early-onset (15.7%, P=0.012); DNA repair genes (BRCA2, BARD1) show age-dependent enrichment within the early-onset group. Core drivers (TP53, APC, KRAS) are shared across age groups.

CRE ARTEMIS (Australia): 22-member, multi-university research center investigating etiology, risk factors, tumorigenesis, microbiome, and immune susceptibility in early-onset CRC over 5 years.

PARP inhibitors: Currently approved for BRCA-mutated prostate, ovarian, breast, and pancreatic cancers. Rational to explore in early-onset CRC tumors with BRCA2/BARD1 alterations — CRC trials are ongoing (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Genomic study strengths: Large (n=1,892), real-world clinical sequencing, two validated panels, age-stratified analysis revealing gradients missed by binary early/average-onset split.

Limitations: Single-institution (Samsung Medical Center, Korea); Asian population may not generalize to other ethnic groups; metastatic cohort only (not early-stage); MYC alteration significance (amplification vs. mutation) not detailed; no functional validation.

CRE ARTEMIS: Newly launched — no results yet. This is a research infrastructure investment, not a clinical trial.

CAR T trial: First-in-human — safety and efficacy unknown. Solid tumor CAR T therapy has historically faced challenges with trafficking to tumors and the immunosuppressive tumor environment (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

CRE ARTEMIS first publications — expect microbiome, exposure, and immune profiling analyses as the program matures.

CU Anschutz CAR T trial enrollment opening and early safety data.

Prospective early-onset CRC cohorts reporting on environmental and lifestyle risk factors.

NCCN/ASCO guideline updates on germline testing thresholds for early-onset CRC — currently recommended for all patients diagnosed under 50, but implementation gaps exist (general medical guidance).
