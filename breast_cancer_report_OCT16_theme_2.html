Biomarker Testing (ESR1, PIK3CA, HER2DX) Is Changing How Doctors Select and Sequence Treatments

"Testing tumors for specific genetic changes — especially ESR1 mutations in the blood and PIK3CA mutations in tissue — helps guide therapy choices in metastatic hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Molecular tests like HER2DX are also being studied to predict which early HER2-positive cancers might need less chemotherapy."

Testing for ESR1 mutations using a circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) blood test [a blood test that detects fragments of tumor DNA] at progression on endocrine therapy helps identify HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer that may respond to an oral SERD. The SERENA-6 trial studied switching to camizestrant when an ESR1 mutation was detected, before scans showed worsening.
PIK3CA mutation testing (tissue or ctDNA) at metastatic diagnosis identifies candidates for inavolisib (with palbociclib and fulvestrant) or alpelisib (with fulvestrant). Some patients acquire PIK3CA mutations after CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy.
The CompassHER2-pCR trial (a prospective, single-arm trial of 2,175 patients) evaluated HER2DX, a 27-gene test, in a subset of patients to predict pathologic complete response (pCR) [no remaining invasive cancer at surgery] with a less intensive chemotherapy approach (a single taxane plus dual HER2 blockade). A high HER2DX score predicted a pCR 68% of the time versus 19% for a low score.
Earlier-stage research is exploring metabolic biomarkers (such as KARS1 and ACAT2) and a histone (DNA-packaging protein) mutation that may promote breast cancer spread.
ESR1 mutations are a common cause of resistance to aromatase inhibitors (arising in up to about 50% of patients over time). Detecting them via a blood test can allow a switch to an oral SERD (such as elacestrant or camizestrant) while the cancer is still controlled.
Co-occurring ESR1 and PIK3CA mutations add complexity. Data from the EMERALD trial suggest elacestrant retains activity even with a PIK3CA co-mutation, which may offer a simpler, better-tolerated option than adding a PI3K inhibitor in some cases (general medical guidance).
HER2DX could, if confirmed in further study, help spare selected early-stage HER2-positive patients from more intensive chemotherapy, reducing long-term side effects while maintaining high cure rates (general medical guidance).
Understanding resistance at the molecular level (histone mutations, metabolic changes) could open the door to new drug classes, though these remain research ideas for now.
Patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer at progression on endocrine therapy, for whom ESR1 ctDNA testing can guide next steps. Testing decisions are individualized with your care team (general medical guidance).
Patients with newly diagnosed metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative disease, for whom PIK3CA testing (tissue preferred, ctDNA acceptable) can determine eligibility for inavolisib or alpelisib-based regimens (general medical guidance).
Patients with early-stage (stage 2-3A) HER2-positive breast cancer being considered for pre-surgery therapy, for whom HER2DX is being studied to help identify who might respond to less chemotherapy.
Patients with triple-negative breast cancer, for whom metabolic-biomarker approaches remain in early research and are not yet clinical tests (general medical guidance).

At diagnosis of metastatic disease, you may wish to ask: "Should my tumor be tested for PIK3CA mutations to see if I qualify for inavolisib or alpelisib?" (general medical guidance).
If you have early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, you may wish to ask: "Is HER2DX testing appropriate or available to help decide about chemotherapy intensity before surgery?" (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Biomarker-guided therapy is powerful but imperfect: ESR1 detection can lead to effective SERD therapy, but benefit is measured in months, and PIK3CA-targeted drugs add side effects (high blood sugar, rash, diarrhea) (general medical guidance).
  • Testing access varies: FDA-approved companion diagnostics exist but are not universally covered, and HER2DX is not yet widely used in routine US practice (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Tumor heterogeneity means a single biopsy or blood draw can miss resistant clones. Repeated monitoring helps but adds cost and anxiety (general medical guidance).
  • De-escalation based on HER2DX carries risk: if a pathologic complete response is not achieved, outcomes may be worse, and long-term survival data from de-escalation are not yet mature (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Oral SERDs (elacestrant/Orserdu, camizestrant/Etcamah, imlunestrant, giredestrant): degrade the estrogen receptor and are active against ESR1 mutations; elacestrant was approved in 2023, camizestrant in 2026, with others in development (general medical guidance).

PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibitors: alpelisib plus fulvestrant (approved 2019 for PIK3CA-mutated), inavolisib plus palbociclib plus fulvestrant (2024), capivasertib plus fulvestrant (2023), and gedatolisib (2026, PIK3CA wild-type) (general medical guidance).

HER2DX (Reveal Genomics): a 27-gene RNA signature predicting pathologic response and recurrence risk in early HER2-positive breast cancer; evaluated in a subset of the CompassHER2-pCR trial (general medical guidance).

Metabolic- and chromatin-targeted approaches for triple-negative disease remain in preclinical development based on emerging biomarkers (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

ctDNA for ESR1: generally concordant with tissue and can detect mutations early. In SERENA-6, about half of the ESR1 mutations were found at first testing. The best testing frequency and cost-effectiveness are still being defined (general medical guidance).

PIK3CA testing: tissue is the reference standard, but ctDNA may miss mutations in low-shedding tumors. Mutations acquired after CDK4/6 inhibitors complicate the assumption that they are always present from the start (general medical guidance).

HER2DX: evaluated in a subset (about 569 patients) of the prospective CompassHER2-pCR trial; its usefulness for actually guiding de-escalation is not yet proven, and long-term recurrence results have not been reported. Low-score patients (19% pCR) still generally need standard chemotherapy (independent fact-check: the draft described this as 'retrospectively validated in a 2,175-patient trial'; CompassHER2-pCR is prospective and HER2DX was analyzed in a 569-patient subset).

KARS1/ACAT2 and the histone mutation: preclinical/mechanistic data only; no clinical tests or targeted therapies are approved (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

SERENA-6 overall survival data and real-world uptake of serial ctDNA monitoring (general medical guidance).

Trials of oral SERD plus PI3K-inhibitor combinations for co-mutated ESR1/PIK3CA disease (general medical guidance).

Prospective studies testing whether HER2DX can actually guide safe chemotherapy de-escalation (general medical guidance).

Development of clinical tests for metabolic and histone-mutation biomarkers in triple-negative disease (general medical guidance).

Integration of multi-omic profiling into routine decision-making at major cancer centers (general medical guidance).
