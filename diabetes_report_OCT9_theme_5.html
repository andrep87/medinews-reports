Health Equity, Access, and Real-World Implementation Challenges in Diabetes Care

"Disparities persist in diabetes medication adherence by race and ethnicity, insulin access is disrupted by the discontinuation of Levemir, and cost barriers limit uptake of protective therapies — while prevention programs are modeled as cost-effective but remain underfunded in some systems."

A Medicare Advantage study found lower adherence to SGLT2 and GLP-1 drugs among Black and Hispanic enrollees compared with White enrollees — for example, in the highest-minority plans adherence was about 33.6% (White) versus 27.0% (Black), a gap of roughly 6.6 percentage points. Enrollment in low-income subsidy programs narrowed, but did not eliminate, these gaps (audit correction: the draft stated '27–30% lower adherence,' which misrepresented the percentage-point gaps).
Levemir (insulin detemir) is being discontinued by Novo Nordisk, with a UK national patient safety alert issued in 2026. Switching has lagged, and UK guidance set a deadline to complete switches by November 30, 2026, ahead of the product's end-of-2026 discontinuation (audit correction: the draft implied US safety alerts, which the UK sources do not support).
A modeling study found that a diabetes prevention program in Canada would be cost-effective over a 35-year horizon, with net savings per participant in the provinces modeled, though cost-savings diminished at older ages.
An IQVIA white paper estimated that the 340B drug pricing program's rebate changes added about $4.8 billion in drug spending for commercially insured people with diabetes and their health plans, and about $156 million in out-of-pocket upcharges for patients (audit correction: the draft omitted 'and their health plans').
New Zealand's Pharmac widened access to several diabetes medicines by removing cardiovascular-risk and ethnicity criteria, making an estimated 14,000 more people eligible at 1 year and 31,000 at 5 years.
Adherence gaps for protective but costly drugs compound existing outcome inequities for Black and Hispanic patients.
The Levemir discontinuation affects people who are stable on it; switching requires planning, dose adjustment, and device familiarization (general medical guidance).

Adherence gaps: Black and Hispanic Medicare Advantage enrollees on diabetes medications, particularly in high-minority plans.
Levemir switch: People currently using Levemir, who will need a planned switch to an alternative basal insulin such as insulin glargine (general medical guidance).
Prevention programs: Adults with prediabetes, where early intervention may be most cost-effective.
340B impact: Commercially insured people with diabetes, particularly those on branded medications.
New Zealand access: People with type 2 diabetes above their glucose target, now eligible regardless of cardiovascular history or ethnicity.
If you use Levemir, you may wish to contact your prescriber about a switch plan, ask for training on any new device, and monitor glucose more closely during the transition (general medical guidance).
If you are on Medicare and facing cost barriers, you may wish to ask about low-income subsidy eligibility, which was associated with smaller adherence gaps (general medical guidance).
If you have prediabetes, you may wish to ask your clinician about referral to a recognized diabetes prevention program (general medical guidance).
If you are commercially insured on branded diabetes drugs, you may wish to review your benefits for upcharges and ask about manufacturer copay assistance (general medical guidance).

  • Reality: Vulnerable patients — older adults or those with limited support — face higher risk of errors during the transition (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Levemir alternatives: Insulin glargine (U100 and U300) is the alternative basal insulin named in the UK guidance (general medical guidance).

Diabetes prevention programs: Structured lifestyle programs modeled as cost-effective over long horizons in the Canadian analysis.

Access policy: New Zealand's Pharmac broadened eligibility for several diabetes medicines based on glucose control.

The sources describe these developments; specific US legislation, generic-entry timelines, and program statistics named in the draft were removed as unsupported (audit correction).

Evidence Uncertainty

Adherence study: Observational claims data; medication coverage is not the same as actual use, and residual confounding is possible.

Levemir switch: Based partly on prediction modeling; real-world switching may differ, and vulnerable patients may face higher transition risk (general medical guidance).

DPP modeling: Excluded some costs and did not model diabetes complications; effectiveness estimates may not transfer to all populations.

340B analysis: An industry-funded white paper whose assumptions about rebate pass-through are contested.

New Zealand: Early implementation; real-world uptake and outcomes are pending, and the impact of removing ethnicity criteria on Māori and Pacific access is being monitored.

What To Watch

Supply and switching outcomes following the Levemir discontinuation.

Whether policy changes further narrow adherence disparities (general medical guidance).

Whether publicly funded prevention programs expand in systems that currently lack them.

Real-world uptake and outcomes from New Zealand's widened access.
