One Drug, Both Ends of the Disease: Ivonescimab Shows Activity Before Surgery and After Targeted Therapy Fails

"Ivonescimab is a bispecific antibody [a single molecule engineered to hit two targets at once] that blocks PD-1 and VEGF together. Unusually, it is showing activity at opposite ends of lung cancer: in early-stage disease given before surgery, where a Phase II trial reported a 70.1% major pathological response and 50.7% pathologic complete response; and in advanced EGFR-mutated disease after targeted therapy stops working, where the Phase III HARMONi trial's survival results have strengthened with longer follow-up. Note it is NOT an antibody-drug conjugate — it carries no chemotherapy payload (independent fact-check: the draft described ivonescimab as an ADC; it is a PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody)."

In resectable (operable) NSCLC, a Phase II trial gave ivonescimab plus chemotherapy before surgery and reported a major pathological response [most of the tumour destroyed when examined after removal] in 70.1% of patients, and a pathologic complete response [no viable cancer remaining] in 50.7%. The results were published in Nature Medicine.
In advanced EGFR-mutated non-squamous NSCLC that had progressed after a third-generation targeted drug such as osimertinib, the global Phase III HARMONi trial (438 patients) tested ivonescimab plus platinum chemotherapy against chemotherapy alone.
HARMONi's overall-survival results strengthened as follow-up matured: the April 2025 primary analysis gave a hazard ratio of 0.79 but did NOT reach statistical significance (p=0.057); by June 2026 the hazard ratio was 0.76 in both the overall population and the Western subgroup (independent fact-check: verified against the trial sponsor's updated analyses; the later figures use nominal p-values, so the trial's primary endpoint remains unmet).
The Western subgroup result moved from 0.98 in the first analysis to 0.84, then 0.76, converging on what had been seen earlier in Asian patients who had longer follow-up at the time.
Most drugs are developed for one setting. A single agent showing activity both before surgery in curable disease and after targeted therapy fails in advanced disease is unusual, and suggests the PD-1/VEGF mechanism may work across different disease states (general medical guidance).
After osimertinib stops working, platinum chemotherapy has been the standard next step and typically controls disease for only a matter of months — so any improvement in that setting matters (general medical guidance).
The geographic convergence in HARMONi is important: an early result that looks weaker in Western patients can reflect shorter follow-up rather than a real difference in how the drug works.
Before surgery, the goal is different — clearing the tumour entirely improves the chance of cure, which is why pathologic complete response is the endpoint used there (general medical guidance).
People with resectable NSCLC being considered for treatment before surgery — the perioperative trial population (general medical guidance).
People with advanced EGFR-mutated non-squamous NSCLC whose disease has progressed after a third-generation EGFR targeted drug — the HARMONi population.
Not currently available outside trials in most settings: ivonescimab is investigational, with a licence application under review for the second-line-plus setting (general medical guidance).
Not applicable to people who have not had EGFR testing — that result determines whether the advanced-disease setting applies at all (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Ivonescimab: a PD-1/VEGF bispecific antibody. It blocks an immune checkpoint and a blood-vessel growth signal with one molecule; it does not deliver a chemotherapy payload (general medical guidance).

HARMONi: Phase III, 438 patients, ivonescimab plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy after third-generation EGFR TKI progression. Progression-free survival met significance at the primary analysis; overall survival did not.

A biologics license application for the second-line-plus EGFR-mutated setting was submitted in January 2026 (general medical guidance).

Perioperative Phase II: ivonescimab with chemotherapy before surgery in resectable NSCLC, published in Nature Medicine.

Evidence Uncertainty

The most important caveat: HARMONi's primary overall-survival analysis did NOT meet its prespecified significance threshold (hazard ratio 0.79, p=0.057). The improving later figures carry nominal p-values from longer follow-up, which is supportive but is not the same as meeting the primary endpoint.

The perioperative data are Phase II, single-arm in design terms for the response endpoints, and pathologic complete response is an early surrogate — it has not yet been shown to translate into longer survival in this trial (general medical guidance).

Early regional differences in HARMONi were substantial, and the explanation offered is maturity of follow-up rather than a demonstrated biological difference.

Ivonescimab is investigational in most regions; availability depends on regulatory decisions that have not been made (general medical guidance).
