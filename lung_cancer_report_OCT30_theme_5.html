PERSEE Trial Clarifies That Adding Chemotherapy to Pembrolizumab Improves Disease Control But Not Yet Proven Survival in PD-L1-High Lung Cancer

"The PERSEE trial directly compared pembrolizumab alone versus pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in untreated PD-L1-high (≥50%) lung cancer. Adding chemotherapy more than doubled progression-free survival (16.1 vs 6.9 months) and cut early progression from 26% to 6%, but overall survival benefit was not statistically proven. Patients and doctors now have high-quality evidence to weigh better disease control against significantly higher toxicity."

The PERSEE trial asked a long-debated question: for advanced lung cancer with very high PD-L1 [a protein that predicts response to immunotherapy; 'high' means at least 50% of tumor cells carry it], is pembrolizumab (immunotherapy) alone enough, or should chemotherapy be added? Adding chemotherapy more than doubled the time before the cancer worsened (median 16.1 vs 6.9 months) and sharply reduced early progression (5.8% vs 26.5%).
Median PFS: 16.1 months with chemo vs 6.9 months alone (44% lower progression risk, HR 0.56, p<0.001).
Benefit consistent across PD-L1 subgroups: even at 90–100% PD-L1, PFS 25.4 vs 7.1 months (HR 0.60).
Tumor shrinkage: 78% vs 50%; early progression (within 3 months): 6% vs 27%.
Median OS: 50.2 vs 29.9 months (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.56–1.05) — not statistically significant (trial powered for PFS).
(independent fact-check: PERSEE was published in Annals of Oncology; figures verified against the primary report.)
Since KEYNOTE-024 (2016), pembrolizumab alone has been standard for PD-L1 ≥50% — avoiding chemo toxicity.
Many clinicians added chemo anyway for high-tumor-burden patients, but without prospective proof.
PERSEE provides the first dedicated phase III evidence: chemo adds substantial disease control.
The 44% PFS reduction and dramatic drop in early progression (26% → 6%) are clinically meaningful for symptomatic patients.
Untreated advanced NSCLC (stage IV or unresectable III) with PD-L1 TPS ≥50%.
No EGFR, ALK, ROS1 alterations; ECOG 0–1.
Both squamous (paclitaxel/carboplatin) and nonsquamous (pemetrexed/platinum) histologies included.
~23% had brain metastases; ~32% women; median age 64.
Results apply to fit patients — frailer populations (ECOG 2+) not studied.
If you have PD-L1 ≥50% NSCLC and are fit for chemo, discuss with your oncologist: "Given PERSEE, should I add platinum chemo to pembrolizumab for better disease control?"
You may wish to ask specifically about your tumor burden, symptoms, and metastatic sites — high burden/symptomatic patients may benefit more from chemo addition.
If you choose pembrolizumab alone, schedule close imaging (every 6–8 weeks) to catch early progression (26% risk).
If you choose combination, plan for: 4 cycles of IV chemo (every 3 weeks), then pembrolizumab + pemetrexed maintenance (if nonsquamous).
You may wish to request growth factor support (G-CSF) and anti-nausea prophylaxis to mitigate chemo toxicity.

The Hope

  • PFS 16 vs 7 months is a major delay in progression but not a cure; most patients eventually progress.
  • Chemo adds hospital visits, infusion time, blood count monitoring, infection risk, fatigue, neuropathy, hair loss.
  • Pembrolizumab alone still yields 5-year OS ~30% — durable benefit for a subset without chemo misery.

The Reality

  • Decision is personalized: no biomarker (PD-L1 level, TMB, ctDNA) yet identifies who must have chemo.
  • Cost of adding 4 chemo cycles + maintenance pemetrexed is substantial for healthcare systems/patients.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda, Merck): PD-1 inhibitor; standard 1L monotherapy for PD-L1 ≥50%.

Platinum doublet chemotherapy: carboplatin/cisplatin + pemetrexed (nonsquamous) or paclitaxel (squamous).

KEYNOTE-024: established pembrolizumab monotherapy (median OS 26.3 months, 5-year OS 32%).

KEYNOTE-189/598: chemo+immuno vs immuno alone in all-comers and PD-L1-high — mixed results.

IMpower150/CheckMate-9LA: other chemo-immuno regimens not directly compared to PERSEE.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Dedicated phase III in PD-L1-high; long follow-up (38.7 months); blinded central PFS review; consistent subgroup benefit; published in Annals of Oncology.

Limitations: Not powered for OS (CI crosses 1); French population (generalizability); open-label; no biomarker to select who needs chemo.

Safety: Grade 3+ treatment-related AEs 54% with combo vs 19% alone (hematologic, GI, renal); 3 fatal TRAEs each arm (1.9%).

PFS benefit did not translate to statistically significant OS — may reflect effective subsequent therapies or crossover.

What To Watch

Final OS analysis of PERSEE with longer follow-up — may cross significance threshold.

Biomarker analyses (ctDNA, TMB, immune gene signatures) to predict chemo benefit.

Real-world data on PERSEE adoption: are clinicians adding chemo for high-burden PD-L1-high patients?

Comparison with other intensification strategies (e.g., pembrolizumab + chemo + bevacizumab, or dual immunotherapy).

Guidelines updates (NCCN, ESMO, ASCO) incorporating PERSEE for PD-L1 ≥50% first-line recommendations.
