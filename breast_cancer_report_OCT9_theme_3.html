Major Trial Launches to Test If Liquid Biopsy Can Spare Early Breast Cancer Patients From Upfront CDK4/6 Inhibitors

"The global AFT-70 NAVIGATE trial will test whether a highly sensitive blood test (Signatera ctDNA) can identify which early ER+/HER2- breast cancer patients truly need CDK4/6 inhibitors after surgery, and who can safely avoid them. Over 2,000 patients will be monitored with serial liquid biopsies, adding CDK4/6 inhibitors only if molecular residual disease appears."

Alliance Foundation Trials launched AFT-70 NAVIGATE, a randomized phase III trial enrolling >2,000 patients with stage II–III ER+/HER2- early breast cancer at ~200 sites globally.
After standard treatment (surgery, radiation, chemo if indicated), patients with no detectable ctDNA by Signatera Genome MRD test are randomized to: (1) standard endocrine therapy + upfront CDK4/6 inhibitor, or (2) giredestrant monotherapy with serial ctDNA monitoring, adding CDK4/6 inhibitor only if MRD becomes positive.
Primary endpoint: non-inferiority in 4-year distant recurrence-free survival.
Signatera Genome uses phased/structural variants with analytical sensitivity <1 part per million; selected based on PALLAS/MonarchE data.
Current adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitor use is based on clinicopathologic risk at diagnosis, but many patients are cured by surgery/chemo/endocrine therapy alone and may not need years of additional targeted therapy.
CDK4/6 inhibitors cause prolonged side effects (neutropenia, fatigue, GI, QTc prolongation), monitoring burden, and cost; avoiding them in low-risk patients improves quality of life.
This trial shifts from "static risk at diagnosis" to "dynamic molecular risk surveillance"—treating based on actual evidence of residual disease rather than probability.
If positive, could change standard of care from universal escalation to biomarker-triggered escalation, a major de-escalation milestone.
Patients with stage II or III ER+/HER2- early breast cancer eligible for adjuvant endocrine therapy.
Must complete standard local therapy (surgery ± radiation) and chemotherapy if indicated.
Must have no detectable ctDNA (MRD-negative) by Signatera after completing standard treatment.
Excludes patients with ctDNA positivity at study entry (different clinical scenario).
If you have early ER+/HER2- breast cancer finishing adjuvant treatment, ask your oncologist: "Is the AFT-70 NAVIGATE trial open at my center, and am I eligible?"
You may wish to ask your oncologist whether molecular residual disease testing is appropriate in your situation, and what the result would and would not tell you.
If not trial-eligible, ask about current guidelines: "Based on my pathology (tumor size, nodes, grade, Ki-67, genomic assay), do I need a CDK4/6 inhibitor, or is endocrine therapy alone sufficient?"
Understand that MRD negativity does not guarantee cure—late recurrences can occur years later in ER+ disease; surveillance continues regardless.

The Hope

  • Hope: Could spare thousands of patients years of unnecessary CDK4/6 inhibitor toxicity and burden while maintaining excellent cure rates.
  • Reality: Trial will take years to read out; current standard of care should not change based on this announcement alone.

The Reality

  • ctDNA assays have detection limits; very small residual deposits may be missed; false reassurance possible.
  • Cost and logistics of serial liquid biopsies (every 3–6 months for years) not yet established for routine care.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Giredestrant: An investigational oral selective estrogen receptor degrader serving as the endocrine backbone in the experimental arm.

CDK4/6 inhibitors (abemaciclib, ribociclib, palbociclib): Standard in the control arm; in the experimental arm, added only if molecular residual disease is detected.

Signatera Genome MRD assay (Natera): A tumor-informed blood test that tracks structural variants to detect residual cancer DNA.

Other trials are testing similar blood-test-guided strategies in the after-surgery setting (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strength: Randomized trial design with a non-inferiority framework, which is the appropriate structure for testing whether treatment can safely be reduced.

Uncertainty: No efficacy results yet—trial just launching; primary endpoint 4-year DRFS requires long follow-up.

Key unknowns: Persistent MRD negativity reliability in ER+ disease (late recurrences); timing of CDK4/6 inhibitor addition upon MRD conversion; optimal testing frequency.

Experimental arm differs in two ways (endocrine backbone + CDK4/6 timing)—cannot isolate ctDNA effect from giredestrant effect.

What To Watch

Trial enrollment progress and site activation.

Interim feasibility data, including how often molecular residual disease converts from negative to positive and whether patients keep up with serial testing.

Regulatory decisions on giredestrant, which would affect the trial's endocrine backbone.

Results from other trials testing blood-test-guided treatment strategies (general medical guidance).

Cost-effectiveness of serial blood-test monitoring compared with giving CDK4/6 inhibitors to everyone (general medical guidance).
