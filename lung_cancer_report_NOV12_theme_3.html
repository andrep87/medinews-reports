KRAS Inhibitors Move Toward First-Line Use: Olomorasib Combination Earns Breakthrough Designation

"A new KRAS G12C inhibitor, divarasib, significantly outperformed existing drugs (sotorasib, adagrasib) in a head-to-head phase III trial, nearly doubling progression-free survival and extending overall survival. Meanwhile, olomorasib combined with chemoimmunotherapy showed promising first-line activity, moving KRAS-targeted treatment earlier in the disease course."

In Krascendo-1 (338 patients previously treated for KRAS G12C-mutated NSCLC), divarasib 400 mg daily improved median PFS to 9.8 months vs 5.7 months with sotorasib/adagrasib (HR 0.58, P<.0001) and median OS to 17.7 vs 12.0 months (HR 0.56, P=.0005) — a 42–44% reduction in progression/death risk. (general medical guidance)
Divarasib had fewer grade 3–4 side effects (24.6% vs 34.5%), fewer discontinuations (4.8% vs 11.7%), and no pneumonitis or QT prolongation vs the control arm.
In a separate first-line study, olomorasib + pembrolizumab + chemotherapy achieved a 62% response rate, 90% disease control, median PFS 11.6 months, and durable responses across PD-L1 levels; liver toxicity was manageable and reversible. (general medical guidance)
Sotorasib and adagrasib (approved 2021–2022) were major breakthroughs but offered only ~5–6 months median PFS in previously treated patients; divarasib nearly doubles that with better tolerability. (general medical guidance)
Standard first-line care for these patients is chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy, so the question olomorasib raises is whether adding a targeted drug improves on that (general medical guidance).
Divarasib's activity in historically poor-prognosis subgroups (KEAP1 mutations, liver mets, brain mets) suggests it overcomes some resistance mechanisms that limit older drugs.
For divarasib: those who have received at least one prior systemic therapy (chemo, immunotherapy, or both). (general medical guidance)
For olomorasib first-line: treatment-naïve patients regardless of PD-L1 level (phase III testing all comers); breakthrough designation specifically for PD-L1 ≥50%. (general medical guidance)
Patients with treated, stable brain metastases were included in both programs.

The Hope

  • Divarasib offers a clear, meaningful improvement over current KRAS drugs — more time before progression, longer survival, fewer severe side effects. (general medical guidance)
  • But median PFS of ~10 months and OS of ~18 months still mean this is not a cure; resistance develops.
  • Olomorasib first-line data are promising but not yet proven superior to standard chemoimmunotherapy in a randomized trial. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Cost and access: Newer agents will be expensive; insurance may require step therapy through older KRAS inhibitors first. (general medical guidance)
  • Quality of life: Daily oral dosing is convenient, but chronic GI side effects (diarrhea, nausea) require active management to maintain daily function.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Adagrasib + cetuximab (EGFR inhibitor) shows activity in KRAS G12C CRC; similar combos explored in NSCLC. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Divarasib: Randomized, controlled, blinded central review; 338 patients; consistent benefit across subgroups (including KEAP1-mut, liver mets, brain mets); OS benefit at interim analysis (immature but crossed boundary).

Olomorasib: Single-arm phase I/II data (77 patients); no control arm; median follow-up 18.7 months; liver toxicity signal requires careful management; phase III needed for definitive first-line claim. (general medical guidance)

Unknowns: Optimal sequencing after divarasib progression; whether divarasib + chemoimmunotherapy improves on divarasib alone; long-term safety of chronic daily dosing; activity in G12C with co-mutations (STK11, KEAP1) beyond subgroup analyses. (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

Krascendo-1 final OS analysis and quality-of-life data.

Trials combining divarasib or olomorasib with chemoimmunotherapy, SHP2 inhibitors, or EGFR inhibitors to delay resistance. (general medical guidance)
