Colorectal Cancer Screening Is Evolving With New Options and Follow-Up Challenges

"Colorectal cancer screening now includes blood-based tests and at-home stool options in addition to colonoscopy. However, many people with abnormal stool tests do not complete the needed follow-up colonoscopy. Patient navigation and reminders can help close this gap, and Medicare now covers CT colonography as another screening choice."

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Blood-based tests such as Shield detect circulating tumor DNA from a blood draw; the ACS lists blood testing as a non-preferred option because it is less sensitive for precancerous lesions and stage I cancer.

Stool-based tests include FIT, high-sensitivity gFOBT, multitarget stool DNA, and a newer multitarget stool RNA test.

CT colonography uses imaging to find polyps without inserting a scope (general medical guidance).

Colonoscopy remains the reference standard because it can detect and remove polyps in one procedure (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

In the meta-analysis, follow-up colonoscopy completion after a positive stool test was 69% with navigation/reminder interventions versus 48% without, still below the 80% goal (independent fact-check: verify exact figures against the meta-analysis).

Blood tests are less sensitive than colonoscopy and stool tests for detecting precancerous polyps and early cancer, per the ACS.

CT colonography detects cancer well but has historically been used less due to past insurance barriers (general medical guidance).

Screening reduces colorectal cancer incidence and death, though the exact size of the mortality benefit is debated (general medical guidance).
