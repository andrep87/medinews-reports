Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) Therapy Moves Earlier — Lifileucel Plus Pembrolizumab Shows Deep, Durable Responses as First-Line Treatment for Advanced Melanoma

"In a Phase 2 trial, adding lifileucel (a one-time cellular therapy made from a patient’s own tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes) to first-line pembrolizumab shrank tumors in 62.5% of advanced melanoma patients, with 29% achieving complete disappearance of all tumors on scans. Responses were durable, with median progression-free survival of nearly 20 months and median overall survival not yet reached after 4+ years. A Phase 3 trial (TILVANCE-301) is now testing this combination against pembrolizumab alone."

The IOV-COM-202 trial (Phase 2, single-arm) gave 27 patients with treatment-naïve advanced melanoma a combination of lifileucel (Amtagvi) plus pembrolizumab as first-line therapy.
Lifileucel is made by surgically removing a tumor fragment, growing the patient’s own tumor-infiltrating T cells in the lab for ~3–4 weeks, then infusing them back after lymphodepleting chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide + fludarabine) and a short course of interleukin-2 (IL-2).
Of 24 patients who received the manufactured product meeting specifications, 15 (62.5%) had significant tumor shrinkage. Seven (29.2%) achieved complete response (no detectable tumors on scans).
Median progression-free survival was 19.8 months. Median overall survival had not been reached after 50.9 months of follow-up. Thirteen of 15 responders maintained response ≥12 months.
Lifileucel was previously approved (2024) only for patients whose melanoma progressed after anti–PD-1 therapy, with a 31% response rate. Using it before any immunotherapy (first-line) more than doubled the response rate (62.5%) and produced deep, lasting remissions.
Pembrolizumab alone as first-line therapy achieves ~40–50% response rates, but many patients eventually progress. Adding TIL therapy upfront may prevent resistance by flooding the tumor with billions of pre-activated, tumor-specific T cells.
No additional side effects beyond those expected from lymphodepletion, IL-2, and pembrolizumab were observed. This suggests the combination’s toxicity is manageable in fit patients.
Patients with unresectable stage III or stage IV (metastatic) cutaneous melanoma who have not yet received any systemic therapy (ICI-naïve).
Must have a tumor lesion that can be surgically resected for TIL manufacturing (typically ≥1.5 cm).
ECOG performance status 0–1, adequate organ function, and ability to tolerate lymphodepleting chemotherapy and IL-2.
Not yet approved for first-line use — currently available only in clinical trials (TILVANCE-301) or via compassionate access programs.
If you are newly diagnosed with advanced (stage III unresectable or stage IV) melanoma and have not started treatment, ask your oncologist: “Am I a candidate for TIL therapy clinical trials, such as TILVANCE-301, as a first-line option?”
Request referral to a center with cellular therapy capabilities (major academic medical centers). TIL manufacturing requires specialized facilities — not all hospitals offer it.
Ask: “Can we schedule a tumor biopsy or resection now for TIL manufacturing, even while we decide on first-line treatment, so the cells are ready if I progress or if a trial opens?”
Discuss the logistics: TIL therapy requires a 2–3 week hospitalization for lymphodepletion, cell infusion, and IL-2. Plan for caregiver support, time off work, and potential travel to a tertiary center.

The Hope

  • Hope: First-line TIL therapy could become a “one-and-done” curative-intent option for a subset of advanced melanoma patients, avoiding chronic immunotherapy. Complete responses suggest potential for long-term remission off treatment.

The Reality

  • Reality: Logistically complex — requires surgery, 3–4 week manufacturing, hospitalization, and IL-2 toxicity management. Not all patients generate sufficient TILs. The one-time lifileucel treatment is priced at about $515,000 per the manufacturer; access limited to specialized centers. Phase 3 results (TILVANCE-301) needed before standard-of-care adoption. OS benefit not yet proven.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Lifileucel (Amtagvi) – Autologous TIL therapy (Iovance Biotherapeutics). FDA accelerated approval (Feb 2024) for unresectable/metastatic melanoma post–anti–PD-1. One-time infusion after lymphodepletion + IL-2.

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda) – Anti–PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor, standard first-line for advanced melanoma.

TILVANCE-301 (NCT05727904): a registrational Phase 3 trial (~670 patients) comparing lifileucel plus pembrolizumab with pembrolizumab alone in untreated advanced melanoma. It has dual primary endpoints — objective response rate and progression-free survival — with primary completion estimated for 2028 (independent fact-check: corrected from the draft's 'PFS primary endpoint, readout 2026-2027').

MIZAR-003 (GC101 TIL regimen) – Chinese Phase 2 trial using lower-intensity preconditioning (no IL-2) plus sintilimab (anti–PD-1) in heavily pretreated patients (post–anti–PD-1). Showed PFS benefit vs chemotherapy (4.37 vs 1.61 months, HR 0.44).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Deep responses (29% complete response), durable (median DOR not reached, 3 patients >5 years progression-free). Biologically plausible — using T cells already trained to recognize the patient’s tumor. No randomized comparator in IOV-COM-202 (single-arm Phase 2).

Limitations: Small cohort (24 evaluable patients). Selection bias — only patients with successful TIL manufacturing and meeting cell-dose thresholds were analyzed. No direct comparison to pembrolizumab alone or to ipilimumab + nivolumab. Toxicity of lymphodepletion + IL-2 is substantial (cytopenias, fever, hypotension, renal/hepatic stress) and requires inpatient management. Manufacturing fails in some patients; process takes 3–4 weeks.

What To Watch

TILVANCE-301 Phase 3 readout — primary endpoint PFS, expected 2026–2027.

Biomarker development — identifying which patients are most likely to benefit (e.g., T-cell receptor clonality, tumor mutational burden, TIL phenotype).

Manufacturing improvements — shorter turnaround, higher success rates, cryopreserved “off-the-shelf” TIL products.

Combination strategies — TIL + anti–PD-1 +/− anti–CTLA-4, or TIL after neoadjuvant immunotherapy.
