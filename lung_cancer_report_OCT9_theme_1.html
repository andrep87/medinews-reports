Adjuvant and Perioperative Immunotherapy Reshaping Early-Stage NSCLC Care

"Real-world data confirm durvalumab after chemoradiation extends 5-year survival to nearly 50% for unresectable stage III NSCLC, while a new analysis shows adjuvant immunotherapy adds only 1–2 months of average disease-free time after surgery—raising questions about which patients truly benefit."

The PACIFIC-R registry followed 1,153 patients across 10 countries for 5 years after durvalumab consolidation for unresectable stage III NSCLC; 49% were alive at 5 years and 35% remained progression-free.
A systematic review of four adjuvant immunotherapy trials (4,336 patients) found absolute disease-free survival gains of only 1.3–2.0 months at 3 years, with several confidence intervals crossing zero.
A leading radiation oncologist highlighted that only ~50% of stage III patients actually reach surgery after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, far below the 80% assumed in trials.
A phase 3 trial (INTerpath-014) has opened testing a personalized mRNA vaccine (intismeran/V940) with or without pembrolizumab for high-risk stage I NSCLC after surgery.
Durvalumab after chemoradiation is now validated in routine practice, including patients who started treatment late or had low PD-L1, making the survival plateau a real-world expectation.
The modest absolute benefit of adjuvant immunotherapy means many patients endure months of treatment and immune-related side effects for a small average gain—shared decision-making is essential.
If half of stage III patients never reach surgery after neoadjuvant therapy, current enthusiasm may expose some to unnecessary toxicity without curative intent.
Personalized vaccines represent a potential low-toxicity strategy to eliminate microscopic residue after surgery, possibly reducing need for chemotherapy.
Patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC who complete chemoradiation without progression—regardless of PD-L1 level or minor delays starting durvalumab.
Patients with completely resected stage IB–IIIA NSCLC considering adjuvant pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, or durvalumab—especially those with higher recurrence risk (stage III, nodal involvement, large tumors).
Patients with high-risk stage I NSCLC (tumors >2 cm, visceral pleural invasion, STAS, positive margins, or ctDNA positivity) who may qualify for vaccine trials.
Multidisciplinary teams selecting stage III patients for neoadjuvant therapy must verify realistic surgical candidacy upfront.
You may wish to ask your oncology team what your absolute recurrence risk is without adjuvant immunotherapy, and how much disease-free time the data show for patients in your situation.
If you have unresectable stage III disease, you may wish to confirm whether consolidation durvalumab is planned — the real-world data included patients who started later or had low PD-L1 (general medical guidance).
Before neoadjuvant therapy, you may wish to ask about the realistic likelihood of completing the full plan including surgery, and what the alternative is if surgery is not reached.
If you have high-risk stage I disease after surgery, you may wish to ask about clinical trial access for personalized vaccines or ctDNA-guided monitoring (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Nearly half of stage III patients alive at 5 years with durvalumab—a historic shift from <20% pre-immunotherapy.
  • Reality: Adjuvant immunotherapy helps only a minority avoid recurrence; most patients either wouldn't have recurred anyway or recur despite treatment.

The Reality

  • Hope: Vaccines could offer recurrence prevention with minimal side effects (mostly injection-site reactions, low-grade fever).
  • Reality: Vaccine manufacturing takes weeks; trial includes placebo arm; benefit unproven; not yet standard of care.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Durvalumab (Imfinzi, AstraZeneca): Approved for unresectable stage III NSCLC after chemoradiation; PACIFIC-R reported 5-year overall survival of 49% in real-world practice.

Pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, and durvalumab: Approved adjuvant options after surgery; the systematic review found an absolute disease-free survival benefit of roughly 1–2 months at 3 years.

Intismeran (V940) plus pembrolizumab: A personalized mRNA vaccine being tested in the phase 3 INTerpath-014 trial in high-risk stage I NSCLC after surgery.

Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy is moving toward perioperative use, but real-world surgical completion rates warrant scrutiny (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

PACIFIC-R: Observational, no control group; 5-yr OS 49% vs PACIFIC trial 43%—consistency supports durability but not superiority. Median follow-up 67.5 months.

Adjuvant meta-analysis: Reconstructed from published curves (not individual patient data); RMST gain 1.34–1.97 months; DFS probability difference 2.3–6.1 per 100; several CIs include zero. Mature OS not established.

Neoadjuvant surgical rates: Real-world data (ASCO 2025) show ~50% reach surgery vs 80% in trials; selection bias likely. No randomized comparison of adjuvant vs perioperative strategies yet.

Vaccine trial: Phase 3, 3-arm (vaccine+pembro, vaccine±placebo, placebo); 1:1:1 randomization; safety established in earlier phases; efficacy unknown.

What To Watch

Longer-term overall survival from PACIFIC-R beyond 5 years.

Maturing overall survival data from the adjuvant immunotherapy trials and ctDNA-guided de-escalation studies (general medical guidance).

The INTerpath-014 primary endpoint (disease-free survival) readout.

Head-to-head comparisons of adjuvant-only versus perioperative immunotherapy strategies (general medical guidance).
