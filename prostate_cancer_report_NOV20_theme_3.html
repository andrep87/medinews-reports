Liquid Biopsy and Genomic Testing Reshape Prostate Cancer Monitoring and Treatment Selection

"Exploratory data from the TALAPRO-2 trial show that ctDNA [circulating tumor DNA, tiny tumor DNA pieces in the bloodstream] and CTC [circulating tumor cells, intact cancer cells circulating in blood] measurements link to outcomes in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, while tissue genomic testing with Decipher predicts risk in high-risk localized disease—though further validation is needed before routine treatment choices[cite: 3, 4]. Together with newer PSMA [prostate-specific membrane antigen] imaging advances, these tests are shaping how doctors track and personalize care[cite: 3, 4]."

In an exploratory analysis of TALAPRO-2 (talazoparib [a PARP inhibitor, a targeted cancer drug that blocks DNA repair] plus enzalutamide versus placebo plus enzalutamide in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer), detectable ctDNA at baseline was linked to shorter radiographic progression-free survival [time without scan-visible cancer growth] in both groups (hazard ratios 2.46 and 4.03)[cite: 3, 4].
Clearing ctDNA to undetectable levels by week 9 was tied to longer median radiographic progression-free survival: 25.9 versus 10.8 months with talazoparib plus enzalutamide, and 16.5 versus 5.5 months with placebo plus enzalutamide[cite: 3, 4].
A drop in CTC counts by week 9 (from at least 5 to 4 or fewer per 7.5 mL [milliliters] of blood) was also strongly linked to longer radiographic progression-free survival (hazard ratios 0.13 and 0.16)[cite: 3, 4].
Overall agreement between blood ctDNA and tumor tissue testing for HRR gene changes was 84% (and 89% when ctDNA fraction was at least 1%), while AR [androgen receptor, a hormone-binding protein on cells] changes were detected in 33.8% of ctDNA samples versus 4.3% of tissue samples[cite: 3, 4].
In a separate study of the tissue-based Decipher genomic test across STAMPEDE (401 patients) and a real-world group (27,607 patients), higher scores were linked to higher risks of metastasis and death, and lower scores suggested some men might potentially be spared intensive abiraterone treatment, pending further validation[cite: 3, 4].
Blood tests for ctDNA and CTCs offer a less invasive way to track tumor biology and treatment response over time than repeating tissue biopsies[cite: 3, 4].
Because ctDNA can capture genetic changes across multiple tumor sites (including AR alterations), liquid biopsy [a blood test looking for tumor DNA or cells] may play a helpful role alongside tissue testing in molecular profiling[cite: 3, 4].
Genomic classifiers like Decipher are being studied to help distinguish who truly needs added therapy such as abiraterone from who might avoid extra treatment side effects, though routine decision-making still needs more validation[cite: 3, 4].
Men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer receiving or starting treatment such as talazoparib plus enzalutamide, where blood biomarker changes were studied as prognostic [outcome-related] markers[cite: 3, 4].
Men with high-risk localized prostate cancer considering added therapy such as abiraterone, where Decipher is under study to help refine risk assessment rather than serving as an established routine treatment decider today[cite: 3, 4].
Men whose PSA rises after surgery or radiation (biochemical recurrence), where newer PSMA PET imaging such as 64Cu-SAR-bisPSMA is being developed to detect lesions at low PSA levels[cite: 3, 4].
Patients for whom a repeat tissue biopsy is hard to obtain or not feasible, where liquid biopsy may provide a complementary way to check tumor genetics[cite: 3, 4].

If you have high-risk localized prostate cancer and are discussing adding abiraterone, you may wish to ask: "Is genomic testing like Decipher appropriate to discuss here, and how much weight should it carry given that further validation is still needed?"[cite: 3, 4]

The Hope

  • Hope: Blood tests and genomic tools could help doctors tailor treatments more closely and avoid unnecessary side effects for men who do not need extra therapy[cite: 3, 4].
  • Reality: None of these tools is a silver bullet yet—exploratory markers still need prospective validation, and tests or advanced scans may face practical or cost barriers[cite: 3, 4] (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Hope: More sensitive PSMA scans may spot recurrence earlier when PSA levels are low[cite: 3, 4].
  • Reality: Even more sensitive scans or favorable blood-marker readings cannot guarantee long-term disease control on their own[cite: 3, 4].

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

In TALAPRO-2 (805 patients unselected for HRR mutations), researchers evaluated baseline and week-9 ctDNA and CTC changes alongside talazoparib plus enzalutamide versus placebo plus enzalutamide[cite: 3, 4] (independent fact-check: talazoparib plus enzalutamide is FDA-approved for HRR gene-mutated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, and has also been studied in metastatic hormone-sensitive disease in TALAPRO-3)[cite: 3, 4].

64Cu-SAR-bisPSMA is a copper-64–labeled PSMA PET imaging agent that received FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation [an expedited FDA program for promising therapies in serious conditions] after the phase II Co-PSMA study (50 patients, median PSA 0.43 ng/mL) showed 1.26 versus 0.48 lesions detected per patient compared with gallium-68 PSMA-11[cite: 3, 4].

The Decipher genomic test was evaluated across STAMPEDE and large real-world cohorts to link tumor gene-expression risk scores with metastasis and survival outcomes[cite: 3, 4].

In Sweden, researchers at the University of Gothenburg are studying an organized PSA-to-MRI screening pathway (including 37,000 men) and exploring artificial intelligence for diagnostic accuracy, alongside separate biomarker work[cite: 3, 4].

Evidence Uncertainty

The TALAPRO-2 ctDNA and CTC findings come from an exploratory analysis rather than a primary trial endpoint, so prospective [planned forward-looking] studies are needed before using blood-marker shifts to change therapy[cite: 3, 4].

The Decipher analysis was retrospective [looking back at collected data] across STAMPEDE and real-world records rather than a randomized trial testing Decipher-guided treatment choices directly[cite: 3, 4].

64Cu-SAR-bisPSMA's lesion-detection advantage comes from a 50-patient phase II study, with phase III results awaited to confirm clinical impact[cite: 3, 4].

Modeling data on PSA screening in older men show that higher age and other health conditions increase the ratio of overdiagnosis to prevented deaths, and MRI-based pathways reduce—but do not completely remove—these trade-offs[cite: 3, 4].

What To Watch

Future clinical studies testing whether changing treatment based on ctDNA or CTC results improves patient outcomes[cite: 3, 4].

Further validation studies of Decipher and other genomic tools in deciding who benefits from treatment intensification[cite: 3, 4].

Phase III trial results (such as AMPLIFY and CLARIFY) for 64Cu-SAR-bisPSMA in recurrent prostate cancer[cite: 3, 4].

Long-term findings from organized PSA-to-MRI screening research and AI imaging studies[cite: 3, 4].
