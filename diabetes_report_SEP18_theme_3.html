CGM Evolves Beyond Glucose: Ketone Monitoring, Dialysis Utility, Faster Biomarkers, and the Education Gap

"Continuous glucose monitoring is expanding rapidly: the first combined glucose-ketone sensor (Abbott Libre Duo) gained FDA De Novo authorization, real-time CGM proved beneficial for adults on hemodialysis (improving time-in-range and hyperglycemia), and glycated albumin shows promise as a faster marker than A1C for early treatment response. However, a critical gap remains — many patients and clinicians lack education to interpret CGM data and act on ketone alerts, especially for euglycemic DKA risk with SGLT2 inhibitors."

FDA granted De Novo authorization [a pathway for novel devices] to Abbott Libre Duo 10 Day (August 25, 2026) — the first CGM [continuous glucose monitor] to continuously measure both glucose and ketones [acids that build up dangerously in diabetic ketoacidosis] for ages 2 and up; U.S. launch expected late 2026.
A randomized crossover trial published in Diabetes Care (2026): real-time CGM vs. fingerstick monitoring in adults with type 2 diabetes on hemodialysis [kidney dialysis]. Results: 63.4% vs. 54.5% time-in-range (70–180 mg/dL), mean glucose 173.6 vs. 187.7 mg/dL, less time above 180 and above 250 mg/dL; hypoglycemia was low in both groups.
Glycated albumin RCT (200 adults, newly diagnosed T2D): earlier intensification at week 4 if glycated albumin >16% did not meet primary A1C<7% endpoint (64% both arms), but secondary per-protocol analysis favored glycated albumin arm (40.5% vs 25.3% achieved A1C<6.5%).
Survey data: roughly 1 in 3 adults with type 1 diabetes at specialty centers were unfamiliar with the term 'DKA' [diabetic ketoacidosis]; 46% could not name a single symptom; 64% reported never testing ketones; only 18% had a home ketone monitor.
Ketones rise before glucose in euglycemic DKA (especially with SGLT2 inhibitors); continuous ketone monitoring could catch impending DKA earlier than glucose alone.
Hemodialysis distorts A1C (anemia, transfusions, ESA therapy) and causes glucose swings during/after sessions; CGM captures patterns fingersticks miss.
Glycated albumin reflects ~2–4 weeks glucose exposure (vs 2–3 months for A1C), potentially enabling faster treatment adjustments after medication changes.
CGM data in people without diabetes (Framingham Heart Study) found that higher average glucose and more time above 140 mg/dL were associated with higher risk of hypertension and abnormal cholesterol levels — a potential future prevention tool.
Libre Duo: People ages 2 and up on insulin pumps, those with recurrent DKA, pregnant patients with type 1 diabetes, and potentially others at high DKA risk. Expert commentary suggests these highest-risk groups should receive the technology first; broader access and coverage are still being defined.
CGM in dialysis: adults with T2D on maintenance hemodialysis where A1C unreliable and glucose patterns unpredictable.
Glycated albumin [a blood marker that reflects average glucose over about 2–4 weeks, faster than the 2–3 month A1C]: newly diagnosed or treatment-intensifying type 2 diabetes patients where early response assessment matters; not reliable in conditions that alter albumin metabolism (general medical guidance).
Education gap: all CGM users — especially those starting sensors without structured onboarding or ongoing support.
Ask your care team: "Do I qualify for the Libre Duo glucose-ketone sensor? Is it covered by my insurance?"
If on hemodialysis: request CGM prescription — cite Diabetes Care 2026 trial showing improved time-in-range and hyperglycemia reduction.
If starting or changing glucose-lowering medications: ask about glycated albumin testing to gauge early response. Note: the trial using this approach did not meet its primary endpoint, though secondary analyses were promising. This is not yet established standard practice (general medical guidance).
Request structured CGM education: schedule follow-up at 2 and 4 weeks after sensor start to review patterns, set alerts, and build a sick-day plan with ketone thresholds (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Libre Duo could prevent DKA hospitalizations; CGM standardizes dialysis glucose management; glycated albumin speeds treatment optimization.
  • Reality: Libre Duo cost/coverage unknown; ketone alert workflows not established; dialysis CGM practical barriers (sensor adhesion, fluid shifts, cost); glycated albumin not widely available or guideline-endorsed.

The Reality

  • Education crisis: "Sensor applied ≠ problem solved" — most users need ongoing coaching to translate data to action; clinician time/bandwidth limited.
  • Equity: Pharmacy-channel CGM access helps, but digital literacy, language, and tech support gaps persist.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Abbott Libre Duo 10 Day: glucose plus ketone monitoring; 10-day wear; De Novo cleared August 25, 2026; launch expected late 2026.

Current standard CGMs (such as Dexcom G7 and Abbott Libre 3 Plus) do not measure ketones (general medical guidance).

Glycated albumin assay: lab-based (not point-of-care); limited U.S. availability; standardization efforts are ongoing (general medical guidance).

SGLT2 inhibitors increase euglycemic DKA risk [ketoacidosis occurring at near-normal blood sugar levels]; ketone monitoring is now part of international sick-day guidance.

Evidence Uncertainty

Libre Duo: De Novo authorization based on analytical accuracy; clinical outcome studies (whether it reduces DKA rates and ER visits) have not yet been completed.

Dialysis CGM trial: Small crossover study; primary outcome (hypoglycemia) was not statistically significant due to low event rates. Glucose benefits were significant but observed over short 2-week monitoring periods; no long-term outcome data.

Glycated albumin trial: Primary endpoint was negative (64% achieved A1C below 7% in both groups). The secondary per-protocol analysis favoring the glycated albumin arm (40.5% vs. 25.3% achieving A1C below 6.5%) is prone to bias. The source explicitly states this trial does not establish routine glycated albumin testing as a replacement for current monitoring.

Non-diabetes CGM study: Cross-sectional (Framingham); association not causation; no intervention data.

What To Watch

Libre Duo real-world evidence: DKA rates, ER visits, ketone alert adherence, and payer coverage decisions (2027).

Larger and longer CGM trials in hemodialysis patients with hard kidney and cardiovascular outcomes.

Glycated albumin standardization progress and whether larger outcome trials support routine clinical use.

Framingham and other cohorts: longitudinal CGM data predicting type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular events in people without diabetes — a potential screening tool for the future (general medical guidance).
