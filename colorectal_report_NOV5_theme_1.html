Immunotherapy Before Surgery Shows Real Activity in MSS Colon Cancer — a Type That Usually Resists It

"In the UNICORN trial, a short course of two immunotherapy drugs (botensilimab plus balstilimab) given before surgery produced a major pathologic response [most of the tumour destroyed when examined after removal] in 36% of patients with MSS colon cancer — a type that rarely responds to immunotherapy — and in 100% of those with dMMR tumours. This adds to earlier NEST trial findings and supports further study; it does not yet replace standard chemotherapy or surgery."

In the UNICORN trial, 56 patients with locally advanced, resectable colon cancer received a short course of botensilimab (an enhanced CTLA-4 blocker) with or without balstilimab (a PD-1 blocker) before surgery. (general medical guidance)
Among 14 patients with MSS/pMMR tumors receiving the combination, 36% had a major pathological response (≤10% viable tumor left), including 29% with a complete pathological response (no viable tumor). (general medical guidance)
Sequential cohorts tested botensilimab alone versus botensilimab plus balstilimab. Response rates were higher when the PD-1 drug was added, but the cohorts were not randomised, so this does not establish a definitive comparative effect (independent fact-check: the draft claimed this 'proved the PD-1 drug is essential').
All 14 dMMR/MSI-H patients on the combination achieved a major response, with 93% having a complete response, confirming known immunotherapy sensitivity in this subtype. (general medical guidance)
For context in dMMR colon cancer, the NICHE-2 trial treated 115 patients with nivolumab plus ipilimumab before surgery and reported very high pathologic response rates; surgery remained part of the regimen (independent fact-check: NICHE-2 is a colon cancer trial, not rectal, and treated 115 patients). (general medical guidance)
Earlier work (the NEST trial) reported a major pathologic response in about 41% of pMMR tumours with the same combination, so UNICORN confirms rather than first establishes this activity (independent fact-check: the draft called this the first regimen to show deep, reproducible regression). (general medical guidance)
The treatment was given in a "window of opportunity" (about 5 weeks) without delaying surgery, and all patients proceeded to planned resection. (general medical guidance)
Responses correlated with clearance of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) before surgery and favorable immune changes in the tumor microenvironment, providing biological proof of activity. (general medical guidance)
If confirmed in larger trials, this could shift early-stage MSS colon cancer treatment toward immunotherapy-based neoadjuvant approaches, potentially reducing reliance on chemotherapy. (general medical guidance)
Patients with newly diagnosed, locally advanced (cT3-T4, N0-N+) colon adenocarcinoma that is surgically resectable with curative intent.
Tumors must be MSS/pMMR (proficient mismatch repair) or dMMR/MSI-H, confirmed by standard immunohistochemistry testing on biopsy tissue. (general medical guidance)
Patients must not have distant metastases, prior systemic therapy, or significant autoimmune disease requiring immunosuppression.
The ongoing ROBBIN Phase III trial will specifically enroll high-risk stage II/III MSS/pMMR patients, broadening potential eligibility. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to ask your oncology team: "Has my tumor been tested for MMR/MSI status? Am I eligible for any neoadjuvant immunotherapy clinical trials?" (general medical guidance)
If diagnosed with resectable stage II/III colon cancer, request referral to a center offering neoadjuvant immunotherapy trials (e.g., UNICORN platform or ROBBIN when open). (general medical guidance)
Ensure baseline ctDNA testing (e.g., Signatera) is ordered before any treatment; serial ctDNA tracking may help predict response and guide future decisions. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to discuss fertility preservation before starting immunotherapy if you are of childbearing potential, as treatment requires contraception for 3 months after the last dose. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • The 36% major response rate in MSS disease is unprecedented for immunotherapy but means nearly two-thirds of patients did not achieve deep regression. (general medical guidance)
  • Immune-related side effects occurred in 41% of patients (mostly flu-like symptoms, diarrhea); one grade 3 hyperthyroidism delayed surgery. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • No treatment-related deaths, but long-term autoimmune toxicities and financial costs of these investigational agents remain unknown.
  • Pathological response ≠ cure; whether this translates to fewer recurrences and longer survival requires Phase III validation (ROBBIN trial). (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Botensilimab (BOT): Fc-enhanced anti-CTLA-4 antibody (Agenus); given IV 1 mg/kg single dose; enhances T-cell activation and depletes regulatory T cells in tumors.

Balstilimab (BAL): Anti-PD-1 antibody (Agenus); given IV 3 mg/kg on days 1 and 15; blocks PD-1 exhaustion pathway on T cells.

Combination (BOT+BAL): Dual checkpoint blockade; shown in UNICORN and NEST trials to induce major pathological responses in MSS and dMMR colon cancer. (general medical guidance)

ROBBIN Phase III: Global randomized trial (planned Q1 2027) comparing neoadjuvant BOT+BAL + standard care vs. standard care alone in high-risk stage II/III MSS/pMMR colon cancer; primary endpoint: event-free survival. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

UNICORN: 14 patients per cohort; primary endpoint (pMR rate) met in 3 of 4 cohorts; 12-month EFS 100% in both combination arms (median follow-up 16.7–19.2 months).

NEST trial (single-center, 24 patients): 41% major pathological response rate in pMMR tumors with same combination, supporting UNICORN findings. (general medical guidance)

Limitations: Small, non-randomized cohorts; no standard-of-care control arm; short follow-up; 8/14 pMMR combination patients received adjuvant chemo, confounding EFS attribution.

ctDNA clearance correlated with pathological response but is exploratory; not yet validated for clinical decision-making (e.g., omitting surgery). (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

ROBBIN Phase III trial launch (Q1 2027): definitive test of whether neoadjuvant BOT+BAL improves event-free survival vs. standard perioperative care. (general medical guidance)

UNICORN extended-duration cohort (8-week BOT+BAL): evaluating if longer treatment deepens responses further.

Biomarker discovery: baseline PD-L1 in stroma, FCGR2A/3A on macrophages, and T-reg signatures may eventually predict who benefits most.
