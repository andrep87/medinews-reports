Stage III NSCLC Enters a Multi-Trial Era Challenging the PACIFIC Standard

"Durvalumab [an immunotherapy drug targeting PD-L1] after chemoradiation [combined chemotherapy and radiation] remains standard care for unresectable [cannot be surgically removed] stage III non-small cell lung cancer, and at least eight phase III trials are testing ways to improve outcomes—including ivonescimab [a bispecific antibody blocking PD-1 and VEGF] vs. durvalumab in HARMONi-10, ctDNA-guided [circulating tumor DNA, tumor genetic fragments in blood] strategies in ADAPT-C and IDEATION, new radiation approaches, and induction [pre-chemoradiation] treatments[cite: 1, 2]."

HARMONi-10 is a global phase III trial comparing ivonescimab against durvalumab as consolidation [follow-up therapy after initial treatment] after chemoradiation in unresectable stage III NSCLC, with progression-free survival as the primary endpoint [main study measure] and site activations slated for Q4 2026[cite: 1, 2].
This comes after the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter [a formal rejection letter] for ivonescimab plus chemotherapy in previously treated EGFR-mutated NSCLC (HARMONi), citing lack of statistically significant overall survival benefit and concerns that 93% of progression-free survival events occurred in patients in China[cite: 1, 2].
In the phase II ADAPT-C study, patients 1–8 months into durvalumab consolidation undergo ctDNA MRD [minimal residual disease, microscopic cancer traces] testing, and those who test positive receive four cycles of durvalumab plus tremelimumab [another immunotherapy drug] and platinum chemotherapy before finishing 12 months of durvalumab[cite: 1, 2].
In the phase II IDEATION study, patients after chemoradiation are randomized 2:1 to a ctDNA-guided group (where ctDNA-negative patients omit durvalumab and only MRD-positive patients receive it) versus a control group that receives durvalumab, with 12-month progression-free survival in the guided arm as the primary endpoint[cite: 1, 2].
NRG-LU008 (phase III) tests stereotactic body radiation [focused high-dose radiation] to the primary tumor before concurrent chemoradiation, while NRG/RTOG 1308 (phase III) compares proton versus photon radiation with results potentially reporting within a year[cite: 1, 2].
NEOLA (phase II) tests induction osimertinib before chemoradiation in EGFR-mutated disease, and SUNRAY-02 and KANDLELIT-015 (phase III) add KRAS G12C inhibitors (olomorasib and calderasib) to durvalumab consolidation[cite: 1, 2].
While durvalumab consolidation after chemoradiation is standard care, many patients still relapse, and having at least eight phase III trials in this space creates multiple potential paths to improve outcomes over time[cite: 1, 2].
Using ctDNA minimal residual disease testing is being studied as a way to tailor consolidation—potentially intensifying care when microscopic disease is detected or avoiding extra treatment when it is not—though clinical utility outside trials is still being evaluated[cite: 1, 2].
Ivonescimab combines PD-1 [programmed cell death protein 1, an immune checkpoint] and VEGF [vascular endothelial growth factor, a blood-vessel growth factor] blockade in one bispecific antibody, moving into an earlier, potentially curable stage III setting in HARMONi-10[cite: 1, 2].
Earlier chemotherapy-induction trials in stage III NSCLC were negative, whereas newer induction approaches such as NEOLA and MDT-BRIDGE are being tested—with MDT-BRIDGE reporting 92% of patients reaching definitive therapy and an 85% 12-month progression-free survival rate among those who went on to chemoradiotherapy[cite: 1, 2].

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Durvalumab (Imfinzi) consolidation after concurrent chemoradiation remains the established standard of care in unresectable stage III NSCLC (independent fact-check: the PACIFIC trial used 10 mg/kg every two weeks for up to 12 months, while 1,500 mg every four weeks is a standard alternative schedule in clinical practice; the week's sources stated standard durvalumab consolidation without specifying a dose)[cite: 1, 2].

Ivonescimab (SMT112/AK112) is a bispecific antibody targeting PD-1 and VEGF that was approved in China in 2024, is in 18 phase III trials globally, and is being tested against durvalumab in HARMONi-10[cite: 1, 2].

ADAPT-C tests adding four cycles of durvalumab, tremelimumab, and platinum chemotherapy when ctDNA is detected during consolidation[cite: 1, 2].

IDEATION tests omitting durvalumab if ctDNA is negative after chemoradiation and giving durvalumab only if ctDNA is positive[cite: 1, 2].

NEOLA evaluates induction osimertinib before chemoradiation in EGFR-mutated stage III NSCLC[cite: 1, 2].

Evidence Uncertainty

Durvalumab consolidation is backed by the phase III PACIFIC trial (independent fact-check: PACIFIC enrolled 713 randomized patients and reported 5-year overall survival of 42.9% vs. 33.4% [draft wrote 33.1%] and progression-free survival of 16.8–16.9 vs. 5.6 months, though those figures were not inside the three week-specific articles)[cite: 1, 2].

HARMONi-10 is just beginning site activations with no efficacy data yet; while ivonescimab's earlier phase III readouts in China were positive across reported trials, the FDA raised questions about applicability to U.S. patients after the HARMONi rejection[cite: 1, 2].

ADAPT-C and IDEATION are phase II trials (IDEATION uses 2:1 randomization), so whether ctDNA-guided consolidation improves long-term outcomes remains to be shown in larger studies[cite: 1, 2].

NEOLA and MDT-BRIDGE are phase II studies testing induction strategies in a setting where older chemotherapy-induction trials did not succeed[cite: 1, 2].
