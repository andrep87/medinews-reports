Genomic, Liquid, and Molecular Tests Increasingly Guide Prostate Cancer Decisions

"Several biomarker tests are reaching clinical use in prostate cancer. The Decipher genomic classifier helped identify which men benefit from adding hormone therapy to radiation. An AI-based pathology biomarker (Artera) guided hormone therapy and pelvic radiation decisions across diverse populations. Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis tracked treatment response. Several newer molecular markers remain in early research."

A Decipher genomic analysis [a 22-gene test on tumor tissue] within NRG/RTOG 0815 found that men with intermediate or high scores had an 8% absolute reduction in distant metastasis from adding short-term hormone therapy to radiation, while low-score men had only about 1% benefit — supporting its predictive value.
The Artera biomarker [an AI tool that reads biopsy slides] guided short-term hormone therapy decisions in the Australian ASTuTE trial and identified patients benefiting from whole-pelvis radiation in the Indian POP-RT trial. It was validated across African, African American, and European ancestry groups.
A ctDNA [circulating tumor DNA — tumor fragments in blood] analysis found that persistent ctDNA during hormone-pathway therapy predicted worse outcomes, and that radiographic progression could occur at very low PSA levels, supporting combined imaging and ctDNA monitoring.
Early laboratory research identified several potential new markers and targets, including a transfer-RNA change linked to treatment resistance, SPEN mutations, and soluble CD163 — all preclinical or observational at this stage.
Decipher and Artera move beyond uniform treatment: men with low genomic risk may avoid hormone therapy side effects, while those with higher risk can be identified for more intensive treatment (general medical guidance).
Artera's validation across multiple ancestry groups addresses historic underrepresentation in biomarker development.
ctDNA and emerging molecular markers may eventually allow earlier detection of treatment resistance than PSA or scans alone.
These tools support matching treatment intensity to individual tumor biology rather than clinical risk category alone (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Biomarkers increasingly match therapy to tumor biology, helping avoid both overtreatment and undertreatment.
  • Reality: Access to testing is uneven, results are not always acted on, and newer markers remain years from clinical use (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • ctDNA monitoring is not uniformly reimbursed, and genetic testing uptake remains low in many settings (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Decipher: A 22-gene tissue-based test providing prognostic information and, in this analysis, predicting benefit from hormone therapy with radiation.

Artera: An AI tool that reads standard pathology slides, studied for guiding hormone therapy and pelvic radiation decisions across diverse populations.

ctDNA assays: Blood tests that detect tumor DNA, used here to track treatment response; not yet standardized across guidelines (general medical guidance).

Emerging markers (a transfer-RNA change, SPEN mutations, soluble CD163): Preclinical or observational research without clinical assays yet available.

Evidence Uncertainty

Decipher: Supported by analysis within the NRG/RTOG 0815 trial; tissue adequacy can be a limitation (general medical guidance).

Artera: Studied prospectively in the ASTuTE and POP-RT trials, with validation across multiple ancestry groups.

ctDNA: Based on analysis within a single trial; assays vary and thresholds are not standardized, and prostate cancers shed relatively little ctDNA (general medical guidance).

The transfer-RNA, SPEN, and soluble CD163 findings are preclinical or observational only, with no clinical tests yet and years from potential use.

Germline testing uptake remains low nationally, limited by consent time, genetic counselor availability, and result tracking (general medical guidance).
