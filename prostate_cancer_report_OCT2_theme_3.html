New Strategies to Preserve Function and Reduce Treatment Burden

"Innovations in nerve-sparing surgery, shorter radiation courses, rectal spacers, lifestyle programs, and focal therapies are giving men more ways to cure or control prostate cancer while protecting urinary continence, sexual function, bowel health, and overall vitality—making treatment fit life, not the other way around."

Nerve graft during prostatectomy: Cleveland Clinic performed a cavernosal nerve allograft [specially processed donor nerve tissue used to bridge nerves that had to be removed] during robot-assisted prostate removal in a man whose cancer had invaded the erection nerves. At 3 months his functional recovery was ahead of the typical timeline. This is a single patient case report — the graft is FDA-approved for other nerve repairs but investigational in prostate surgery.
Hypofractionated post-prostatectomy radiation (HYPORT): NRG-GU003 phase 3 trial (296 patients) showed 25 fractions (5 weeks) provides similar cancer control and patient-reported urinary/bowel quality of life as 37 fractions (7 weeks), but with higher physician-reported grade 3 urinary toxicity (16% vs 7%, driven by cystitis and hematuria).
SpaceOAR Flex Hydrogel: FDA 510(k) cleared (Sept 2026); a pre-formed, CT-visible hydrogel spacer injected between prostate and rectum before radiation; single-syringe delivery with real-time adjustability; HYDROSPACE trial (NCT06451614) ongoing to confirm non-inferiority vs SpaceOAR Vue.
STAMINA lifestyle program: NHS trial (700 men on ADT across 15 hospitals) showed 12 months of supervised aerobic/resistance exercise, dietary advice, and behavioral support improved cancer-specific quality of life by 37%, reduced fatigue by 30%, and cut quality-of-life deterioration by 26%; cost-effective at £13,920/QALY; now rolling out to 8 NHS trusts.
HIFU [high-intensity focused ultrasound — sound waves that heat and destroy a targeted area of tissue]: A focal therapy treating only the cancerous portion of the prostate, with same-day discharge, no radiation and no incisions. Offered at select centers for carefully selected men with MRI-visible, one-sided disease.
The "cure at all costs" era is shifting to "cure with preserved self"; men live decades after treatment, so urinary, sexual, bowel, and metabolic health define daily quality of life.
Nerve grafts may turn a surgery that historically caused erectile dysfunction into one where recovery is measurable in months, not years—addressing the top fear for many men.
A shorter radiation course (5 weeks versus 7 weeks after surgery) reduces travel, work disruption, and fatigue — but the higher rate of severe urinary side effects reported by physicians in NRG-GU003 requires careful shared decision-making.
Rectal spacers are injected between the prostate and rectum to reduce radiation dose to the bowel. The HYDROSPACE trial is evaluating SpaceOAR Flex with a primary endpoint of 25% reduction in rectal radiation dose.
Exercise is not just general wellness — the STAMINA trial tested it as a prescribed intervention and found improvements in quality of life and fatigue for men on hormone therapy.

Shorter post-surgery radiation: Men needing radiation after prostatectomy who want a shorter course — but the higher rate of severe urinary side effects reported by physicians must be discussed.
Rectal spacer: Men receiving external beam radiation for prostate cancer. Ask your radiation oncologist whether a spacer is appropriate for you at your treatment planning session (general medical guidance).
Exercise programs: Any man starting or on hormone therapy. STAMINA studied a supervised program specifically — ask your care team what supervised options are available locally.
Focal therapy (HIFU): Men with low- or favorable intermediate-risk, MRI-visible, one-sided, organ-confined disease who want to avoid whole-gland treatment. Not appropriate for high-risk or multifocal disease.
If facing prostatectomy and MRI shows cancer near the nerves, ask: 'Do you offer nerve graft reconstruction, or can you refer me to a center that does?' Understand this is investigational with very limited data.
If you need radiation after surgery, ask: 'Am I a candidate for the shorter 5-week schedule, and what is my personal risk of severe urinary side effects?'
Before starting prostate radiation, ask: 'Will you place a rectal spacer to protect my rectum? If not, why?' (general medical guidance)
If starting hormone therapy, ask for referral to a supervised exercise program or a physical therapist with cancer experience. Discuss bone health monitoring and cardiovascular risk with your team (general medical guidance).
If you have low or favorable intermediate-risk, MRI-visible, one-sided disease, ask whether focal therapy such as HIFU is appropriate for you — and consider a second opinion at a center offering it (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: You can treat prostate cancer while protecting continence, sexual function, bowel health, and overall vitality.
  • Reality: Nerve grafting rests on a single case report, not established evidence. The shorter radiation schedule may increase severe urinary irritation. Spacers add a procedure. Exercise requires sustained commitment. Focal therapy may leave cancer untreated elsewhere in the gland.

The Reality

  • Access: Spacer placement requires a trained physician. Supervised exercise programs are not widely available outside integrated health systems. Focal therapy is concentrated at specialized centers (general medical guidance).
  • Insurance coverage varies considerably across these options, and some may require appeals (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Cavernosal nerve allograft: Specially processed donor nerve tissue, FDA-approved for peripheral nerve repair in other settings but investigational in prostate surgery. Currently supported only by individual case reports.

Shorter post-prostatectomy radiation: NRG-GU003 compared 25 sessions with 37 sessions, finding similar cancer control and patient-reported quality of life but higher physician-reported severe urinary toxicity.

SpaceOAR Flex: FDA 510(k) cleared September 2026. A hydrogel spacer injected between prostate and rectum before radiation, visible on CT with single-syringe delivery. The HYDROSPACE trial is comparing it with the previous generation.

STAMINA: Supervised exercise plus dietary and behavioral support, delivered through the NHS and now rolling out to additional trusts.

HIFU: A focal ablation technique offered at select centers for carefully selected patients. Long-term cancer outcome data compared with whole-gland treatment remain limited (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Nerve graft: This is a single case report. There are no randomized data, long-term erectile recovery rates are unknown, and availability is very limited.

Shorter post-surgery radiation (NRG-GU003): A randomized phase 3 trial, but physician-reported severe urinary toxicity was higher with the shorter course (16% vs 7%) even though patient-reported quality of life was similar. The reason for this discrepancy is unclear, and longer follow-up is needed.

SpaceOAR Flex: Cleared through the FDA 510(k) pathway based on similarity to existing devices. The HYDROSPACE trial is ongoing to evaluate its safety and effectiveness, with a primary endpoint of 25% reduction in rectal radiation dose.

STAMINA: The largest exercise trial in men on hormone therapy, using a pragmatic NHS design. It demonstrated quality-of-life and fatigue benefits. Whether the model transfers to healthcare systems with different structures needs testing (general medical guidance).

Focal therapy: Lacks long-term cancer control data compared with surgery or radiation. Cancer may recur in untreated areas of the prostate, and patient selection criteria continue to evolve (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Long-term urinary toxicity and cancer control from the shorter post-prostatectomy radiation schedule.

HYDROSPACE trial results confirming whether SpaceOAR Flex matches the rectal protection of earlier spacer generations.

Whether the STAMINA exercise model can be adapted and reimbursed in other healthcare systems.

Focal therapy registries reporting long-term cancer control and metastasis-free survival versus whole-gland treatment (general medical guidance).

Prospective multi-center trials of nerve grafting with validated erectile function endpoints — essential before this moves beyond case reports.
