Structured Exercise and Lifestyle Program (STAMINA) Cuts ADT Side Effects by One-Third at Low Cost

"The STAMINA trial — the largest of its kind — shows that a 12-month supervised exercise, diet, and behavioral support program delivered in community gyms reduces quality-of-life decline by up to one-third in men on androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [hormone therapy for prostate cancer]. The program costs £13,920–£19,077 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY), well below the NHS cost-effectiveness threshold of £30,000, making it one of the most cost-effective supportive care interventions in oncology. Over 80% adherence shows men will stick with it when it's prescribed as part of routine care."

700 men on ADT (median age 71.6, 34% metastatic, 19% also on ARPI or chemotherapy) were randomized 5:4 to the STAMINA program vs. enhanced usual care (clinician training plus behavioral prompts).
STAMINA: 12 months of supervised aerobic plus resistance exercise in community gyms (Nuffield Health), dietary advice, behavioral support, and free gym membership.
Primary outcome: FACT-P [a prostate cancer-specific quality-of-life questionnaire] improved by 4.5 points (p=0.0004); FACT-G [general quality of life] improved by 3.7 points — both exceed the 3-point threshold considered clinically meaningful.
Fatigue (FACIT-F [a fatigue questionnaire]) improved by 1.9 points (p=0.0068); adherence was over 80% at 12 months.
Cost-effectiveness: £13,920–£19,077 per QALY [quality-adjusted life year, a measure of how much health a treatment buys] vs. the £30,000 NICE threshold; high probability of cost-effectiveness for up to 10 years.
ADT causes fatigue, weight gain around the waist, muscle loss, bone thinning, cardiovascular risk, and psychological distress — affecting over 300,000 UK men and roughly 500,000+ U.S. men on ADT at any given time.
STAMINA is the first lifestyle intervention integrated into routine cancer care at scale across a national health system — designed for real-world delivery, not just a research setting.
Benefits were sustained across physical, functional, emotional, and social well-being for the full 12 months.
Cost per QALY is about half that of most cancer drugs — this is high-value care that improves daily living, not just survival.
Any man starting or continuing ADT for prostate cancer, regardless of stage — the trial included localized, biochemical recurrence, and metastatic patients.
Men experiencing ADT side effects: fatigue, weight gain, muscle weakness, low mood, hot flashes, sexual dysfunction.
Men with cardiovascular risk factors (high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol) — ADT worsens these; exercise directly counters them (general medical guidance).
Older men (median age 71 in trial) — the program was safe and effective in this age group. Only 3 intervention-related serious adverse events occurred, all resolved.

Use the 'talk test' — you should be breathing harder but able to speak (general medical guidance).

  • Reality: Community gyms need trained staff (clinical exercise specialists); Nuffield Health invested heavily in training — most gyms lack this expertise.
  • Reality: Cardiovascular risk on ADT is real — routine cardiology co-management is recommended but not standard in many systems.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

STAMINA program now rolling out via Nuffield Health Cancer Activity Programme across UK NHS trusts; 50,000 places funded by 2030, expanding to breast and bowel cancer.

U.S. equivalents: ACSM/ACS Cancer Exercise Trainer certification programs; some cancer centers offer 'Exercise Oncology' clinics (such as Dana-Farber, MD Anderson, Cleveland Clinic) (general medical guidance).

Pharmacologic adjuncts: denosumab/zoledronic acid for bone health; statins for cardiovascular risk (observational data suggest benefit); vitamin D/calcium supplementation (general medical guidance).

Ongoing trials: testing exercise combined with ARPI therapy, high-intensity interval training (HIIT) vs. moderate exercise, and home-based digital delivery models.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strength: Large RCT (n=700), active comparator (enhanced usual care), high adherence (over 80%), pre-specified cost-effectiveness analysis, multi-center NHS delivery.

Limitation: 97% White participants — generalizability to Black, Asian, and Hispanic men is unknown (these groups may have higher ADT complication rates).

Limitation: Open-label design with patient-reported outcomes (participants knew which group they were in). Fatigue improvement was at the edge of the minimal clinically important difference (MCID).

Limitation: Objective physical performance measures (grip strength, 6-minute walk test) were unchanged — quality of life improved without measurable fitness gains in this protocol.

Unknown: Long-term sustainability after the 12-month supervised period ends; optimal 'dose' of exercise for bone and metabolic protection (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

U.S. implementation: CMS coverage decisions for cancer rehabilitation and exercise oncology; NCCN guideline updates incorporating structured exercise for ADT.

STAMINA long-term follow-up: 5-year quality of life, cardiovascular events, bone fractures, and survival.

Digital and telerehab adaptations: app-based coaching, wearable monitoring, virtual group classes to reach rural and underserved men.

Combination trials: exercise plus novel agents (such as selective androgen receptor modulators for muscle, cardio-protective drugs) to amplify benefits.
