Treatment Intensification for High-Risk and Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer: Multiple Practice-Changing Phase 3 Results

"Four major trials (three phase 3 and one phase 2) reported at ESMO 2026 show that adding newer androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) — apalutamide, darolutamide, or abiraterone — to standard therapy significantly delays cancer progression in high-risk localized and metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. Benefits are large (hazard ratios 0.21–0.48), but overall survival data are still maturing, and side effects increase."

PROTEUS substudy (n=408): Perioperative apalutamide + ADT (6 months before + 6 months after radical prostatectomy) vs. surgery alone. 3-year event-free survival (EFS) 63% vs 41% (HR 0.38). PSMA PET-based MFS 87% vs 78% (HR 0.49). Positive margins 17% vs 56%.
DASL-HiCaP (n=1,106): Darolutamide + ADT + radiation (primary or salvage) vs. placebo + ADT + radiation in "very high-risk" localized disease. 4-year MFS (conventional imaging) 95% vs 88% (HR 0.42-0.48). Grade ≥3 adverse events 43% vs 36%. OS immature (HR 0.88, NS).
PEACE-1 (n=1,173): Abiraterone + ADT ± docetaxel ± prostate radiation in de novo metastatic hormone-sensitive disease. With docetaxel: median OS 6.2 vs 4.4 years (abiraterone vs none); 8-year OS 40.3% vs 27.1%. Hypertension grade ≥3: 21% vs 14%.
ARASEC (n≈160/arm): Darolutamide + ADT vs. historical CHAARTED ADT-alone control in metastatic hormone-sensitive disease (US patients). PFS HR 0.28 (high volume) and 0.21 (low volume). Consistent benefit across subgroups.
These trials extend the proven benefit of ARPIs (already standard in metastatic hormone-sensitive disease) into earlier, curable-intent settings: high-risk surgery and very high-risk radiation.
The magnitude of benefit is striking: 50-70% reductions in progression/metastasis risk. For men facing high-risk localized disease, this represents the first therapy to show such large improvements when added to surgery or radiation.
The PROTEUS and DASL-HiCaP designs test "total therapy intensification" — combining systemic ARPI with local therapy (surgery or radiation) — rather than sequential approaches.
PROTEUS: Men with high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer (Grade Group 4-5, PSA >20, or ≥T3a) planning radical prostatectomy, fit for 12 months of ADT + apalutamide.
DASL-HiCaP: Men with "very high-risk" localized disease: Grade Group 5 (Gleason 9-10) or pelvic nodal involvement; or Grade Group 4 (Gleason 8) plus one additional risk factor (T2b+, PSA >20, or radiological T3a+). Also includes post-prostatectomy salvage radiation patients with persistent/early rising PSA and high-grade or nodal disease.
PEACE-1/ARASEC: Men with newly diagnosed metastatic prostate cancer that is still hormone-sensitive (castration-sensitive), regardless of volume. PEACE-1 included men receiving docetaxel; ARASEC focused on darolutamide + ADT without chemotherapy.
If you have high-risk localized disease and are considering surgery, ask: "Am I a candidate for perioperative apalutamide + ADT as studied in PROTEUS, and what are the side effects of 12 months of hormone therapy?" (general medical guidance)
If you have very high-risk disease and are planning radiation, ask: "Does the DASL-HiCaP regimen (darolutamide + ADT + pelvic radiation) apply to me, and how does it compare to standard ADT + radiation?" (general medical guidance)
If you have metastatic hormone-sensitive disease, confirm you are receiving an ARPI (abiraterone, apalutamide, enzalutamide, or darolutamide) plus ADT — this is now unequivocal standard of care. Ask about adding docetaxel if you have high-volume disease and are fit. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to discuss fertility preservation and bone health (DEXA scan, calcium/vitamin D, denosumab or zoledronic acid) before starting long-term ADT. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: For the first time, men with high-risk localized disease have systemic therapy options that meaningfully reduce recurrence risk when combined with surgery or radiation — potentially curing more men.
  • Reality: 12 months of ADT + ARPI causes significant side effects: hot flashes (64% in PROTEUS), fatigue, rash, hypertension, bone loss, metabolic changes. Grade 3-4 events: 31% (PROTEUS) and 43% (DASL-HiCaP) vs ~22-36% in control arms.

The Reality

  • Cost: ARPIs are expensive oral drugs; 12 months of therapy adds substantial financial burden. Insurance coverage for off-label perioperative use may vary.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Apalutamide (Erleada): FDA-approved for non-metastatic CRPC and metastatic HSPC; not yet approved for perioperative use. PROTEUS results submitted for publication in European Urology.

Darolutamide (Nubeqa): FDA-approved for non-metastatic CRPC and metastatic HSPC; DASL-HiCaP tests it with radiation in very high-risk localized disease (not yet approved for this indication).

Abiraterone (Zytiga): FDA-approved for metastatic HSPC (with prednisone); PEACE-1 confirms benefit when added to ADT + docetaxel.

Enzalutamide (Xtandi): ENZARAD trial (ADT + radiation ± enzalutamide) was negative overall, but suggested benefit in patients receiving pelvic nodal radiation — highlighting that radiation field matters.

Evidence Uncertainty

PROTEUS was a surgery trial: men received ADT with or without apalutamide for six months before and six months after radical prostatectomy with pelvic lymph-node dissection. Radiotherapy was permitted only at the investigator's discretion as adjuvant or salvage treatment (independent fact-check: the draft described PROTEUS as using contemporary radiation doses and mandating pelvic radiation; it is not a radiotherapy trial).

Limitations: OS data immature for PROTEUS, DASL-HiCaP, and ARASEC. PROTEUS PSMA PET-based MFS is not a validated surrogate. DASL-HiCaP "very high-risk" definition differs from other trials. ARASEC used external historical control (CHAARTED) rather than concurrent randomization.

Key uncertainty: Whether the large MFS/EFS benefits translate into OS gains, and how to select patients who benefit most vs. those who only get added toxicity (hot flashes, fatigue, rash, hypertension, fracture risk).

What To Watch

OS follow-up from PROTEUS, DASL-HiCaP, ARASEC, and PEACE-1 (especially 8-year OS in PEACE-1 with abiraterone + docetaxel).

Biomarker studies to identify who needs intensification vs. who does well with local therapy alone (e.g., genomic classifiers, ctDNA, PSMA PET).

ENZARAD subgroup: prospective validation of benefit when pelvic nodal radiation is given.

Regulatory submissions for apalutamide (perioperative) and darolutamide (with radiation) in high-risk localized disease.
