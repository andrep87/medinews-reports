Blood Tests and New Tools Are Speeding Up Alzheimer's Diagnosis — For People Who Already Have Symptoms

"An FDA-cleared blood test (PrecivityAD2) can now help evaluate adults as young as 40 who already have memory or thinking symptoms — replacing the need for a spinal tap or PET scan as a first step. Researchers are also developing AI tools that read retinal photos and speech patterns, and a conversation-based risk score for lower-income countries. Important: these are tools to evaluate people WITH symptoms, not screening tests for people without them (independent fact-check: the draft described the blood test as detecting Alzheimer's years before symptoms appear; it is cleared only for symptomatic adults and is explicitly not a screening test)."

The FDA cleared PrecivityAD2, the first Alzheimer's blood test authorized for symptomatic adults as young as 40. It measures amyloid and tau proteins in blood to help identify amyloid pathology, and is intended to support — not replace — a clinical evaluation. It is not cleared for population screening or as a stand-alone diagnostic.
Researchers at Arizona State University and Mayo Clinic launched a $3.2 million NIH-funded project using AI to enhance routine retinal photos and combine them with blood biomarkers to flag preclinical Alzheimer's risk years before memory loss—potentially during a standard eye exam.
European researchers are building standardized databases of speech recordings analyzed by AI "phonebots" to detect subtle language changes that signal early Alzheimer's, addressing the need for scalable screening now that disease-slowing drugs (lecanemab, donanemab) are approved for early symptomatic stages.

The Hope

  • Hope: A simple blood draw, eye photo, or phone call could someday replace months-long specialist waits and invasive tests, getting people into treatment or prevention trials years earlier.
  • Reality: PrecivityAD2 is only for symptomatic people 40+—not a screening test for asymptomatic adults. Retinal AI, speech tools, and ARC score are years from clinical use. The 5-factor score is for research recruitment in specific regions. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Cost and access: Blood tests may not be covered by insurance for all indications; retinal AI requires equipment/software not yet in standard clinics; phonebot databases need massive standardization across languages/accents. (general medical guidance)
  • False reassurance or anxiety: A "negative" blood test doesn't guarantee no future dementia; "elevated risk" scores don't mean you will develop it. Results must be interpreted by a clinician who knows your full picture.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

PrecivityAD2 (C2N Diagnostics): FDA-cleared blood test measuring amyloid-beta and tau proteins via high-resolution mass spectrometry; indicated for symptomatic adults 40+ alongside clinical assessment—not a standalone screening test. (general medical guidance)

Lecanemab (Leqembi, Eisai/Biogen) and donanemab (Kisunla, Eli Lilly): FDA-approved (US) and EC-approved (EU) IV anti-amyloid antibodies for early symptomatic Alzheimer's; require confirmed amyloid pathology—driving demand for accessible diagnostic tools. (general medical guidance)

Retinal AI + blood biomarker combo (ASU/Mayo/NIH): 5-year research project using generative AI to fix poor-quality nondilated retinal photos, then deep learning to detect Alzheimer's patterns combined with blood tests (amyloid, tau, p-tau217). (general medical guidance)

ARC score (Stanford/Neuroimaging consortia): Machine learning model from resting-state fMRI distinguishing SuperAgers from Alzheimer's patients (85% accuracy); applied to 1,300+ cognitively normal adults 55–70 predicting 12-year outcomes.

Evidence Uncertainty

PrecivityAD2 validation: 1,142 symptomatic adults; dual cutoffs yielded 97.6% PPV (rule-in) and 93.1% NPV (rule-out) vs. amyloid PET/CSF; consistent across 12 common chronic diseases and increasing age; 17.3% "likely positive" zone with 77.3% PPV requires clinical judgment. (general medical guidance)

Retinal imaging: Still in research phase; nondilated photos are convenient but often blurry—AI enhancement must preserve true vascular details without inventing false signals; clinical use requires prospective validation, regulatory review. (general medical guidance)

5-factor LMIC risk score: Built from 11,000+ older adults across 7 sites (China, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, Venezuela, Mexico, Peru); AUC 0.75 (clinically useful threshold ≥0.70); designed for research recruitment, not general population screening; needs validation in South Asia, MENA, Sub-Saharan Africa. (general medical guidance)

ARC score: Derived from 180 SuperAgers (75+, memory of 45-year-olds) vs. 110 Alzheimer's patients; predicted decline in 1,300+ unimpaired adults 55–70 over 5–12 years; outperformed demographics, amyloid, tau, genetics; not yet diagnostic—requires intervention trials to test if shifting brain patterns changes outcomes.

What To Watch

Results from ASU/Mayo retinal AI trial (5-year timeline) and validation of enhanced nondilated images against amyloid PET in diverse community cohorts. (general medical guidance)

Expansion of phonebot speech databases across languages and integration into telehealth platforms for remote cognitive screening. (general medical guidance)

Validation of the 5-factor LMIC risk score in South Asia, Middle East/North Africa, and Sub-Saharan Africa—critical for global equity.

Stanford ARC score intervention trial: testing whether cognitive training or transcranial magnetic stimulation can shift brain activity toward SuperAger resilience patterns in at-risk unimpaired adults.
