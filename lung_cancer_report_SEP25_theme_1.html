Germline EGFR T790M Mutation: A Rare but Powerful Inherited Risk Factor for Lung Cancer, Especially in Never-Smokers

"Researchers discovered that a rare inherited mutation in the EGFR gene (called T790M) increases lung cancer risk 25-fold overall and more than 60-fold in people who never smoked, making it one of the strongest known cancer risk genes. This finding could lead to genetic testing to identify high-risk individuals for earlier, personalized screening."

Scientists analyzed genetic data from over 3.3 million 23andMe research participants to study a rare inherited variant in the EGFR gene called T790M.
They found 641 carriers of this variant and determined it increases overall lung cancer risk 25-fold compared to non-carriers.
The risk was dramatically higher in never-smokers (62-fold increase) than in smokers (10-fold increase), and the variant was not linked to 17 other common cancers.
The mutation traces back to British/Irish settlers and became enriched in the Southern Appalachian region due to a founder event ~200 years ago (carrier frequency 1 in 2,078 there vs 1 in 15,850 generally).
Current lung cancer screening guidelines rely almost entirely on smoking history, missing never-smokers who develop lung cancer due to genetics.
Identifying this mutation could allow personalized CT screening for high-risk carriers before cancer develops, catching it at a curable stage.
The variant's effect is specific to lung cancer, aligning with EGFR's role in lung epithelial biology, and is independent of polygenic risk scores.
This represents one of the strongest single-gene cancer risk associations ever documented, comparable to BRCA1/2 for breast cancer but with higher magnitude.
Individuals with a family history of lung cancer (especially multiple relatives or early-onset cases).
People diagnosed with multifocal lung cancer or multiple lung nodules.
Never-smokers with lung cancer or those with Southern Appalachian / Southeastern US ancestry.
Patients found to have EGFR T790M in tumor sequencing — germline testing is needed to distinguish inherited vs. tumor-only mutation.
Ask your doctor: "Should I consider germline genetic testing for EGFR T790M based on my family history or personal history of lung nodules?"
If you have a confirmed germline EGFR T790M mutation, discuss personalized low-dose CT (LDCT) screening with a lung cancer specialist, even if you never smoked.
Encourage relatives of known carriers to seek genetic counseling to assess their own risk and screening eligibility.
Avoid smoking entirely if you are a carrier — smoking remains a major risk factor and is not protective.

The Hope

  • Hope: This discovery opens the door to genetics-based screening, potentially saving lives in never-smokers currently excluded from guidelines.
  • Reality: The mutation is very rare; most lung cancer in never-smokers has other causes. Genetic testing access and counseling infrastructure are limited.

The Reality

  • Hope: Carriers can be identified before cancer develops, enabling earlier detection at a curable stage.
  • Reality: Screening guidelines have not yet changed; insurance coverage for LDCT [low-dose CT scan] in non-smokers based on genetics alone is uncertain until prospective data mature (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

No targeted prevention therapy exists yet; the immediate application is risk stratification and earlier detection via LDCT screening.

The INHERIT prospective study (GO2 for Lung Cancer) is ongoing to define lifetime risk, age-related penetrance, and optimal screening intervals for carriers.

Dr. Pasi Jänne's team (Dana-Farber) is developing a next-generation EGFR inhibitor targeting resistant NSCLC, currently advancing toward Phase 1 trials.

Standard EGFR-targeted drugs (like osimertinib) treat tumor-only EGFR mutations; their role in germline T790M carriers without cancer is unknown and not recommended (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Massive sample size (3.3M), robust odds ratio (25.18, p=1.9x10⁻²⁴), ancestry tracing, specificity to lung cancer, replication in polygenic models.

Limitations: Variant is rare (1 in 15,850 overall), so absolute number of carriers is small; most carriers never develop lung cancer (incomplete penetrance).

Unknowns: Why risk differs so dramatically by smoking status; what other genetic/environmental factors modify penetrance; lifetime absolute risk; cost-effectiveness of population screening.

What To Watch

Results from the prospective INHERIT study defining natural history and screening outcomes in germline T790M carriers.

Guideline updates (USPSTF, NCCN) on incorporating germline genetics into lung cancer screening eligibility.

Development of polygenic risk scores combined with EGFR T790M for broader risk stratification.

Whether preventive strategies (such as EGFR inhibitors) in high-risk germline carriers are studied in clinical trials (general medical guidance).
