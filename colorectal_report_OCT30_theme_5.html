Adjuvant Therapy Intensification Fails in High-Risk Colon Cancer — Novel Strategies Needed

"The phase III IROCAS trial definitively showed that intensifying adjuvant chemotherapy from mFOLFOX6 to mFOLFIRINOX (adding irinotecan) does not improve disease-free survival in high-risk stage III (T4/N2) colon cancer — 3-year DFS 65.6% vs 66.3% (HR 1.07). Meanwhile, high-dose omega-3 EPA after liver metastasis resection improved 5-year overall survival by 32% (HR 0.68) despite no PFS benefit. The message: empirical chemo escalation fails; we need biology-driven, not just dose-driven, approaches."

IROCAS (795 patients; France, Canada, Italy): adjuvant mFOLFIRINOX did NOT improve disease-free survival over standard mFOLFOX6 in high-risk stage III (T4 and/or N2) colon cancer — 3-year disease-free survival 65.6% vs 66.3% (HR 1.07; 95% CI 0.84-1.36; p=0.61) — a clearly negative result (independent fact-check: verified against ESMO 2026 LBA29).
EMT2 (418 patients, UK, 5-year follow-up): High-dose EPA (4g/day icosapent ethyl/Vascepa) started ≥1 day before liver metastasis resection and continued 2–4 years vs placebo. Primary endpoint PFS not met (13.5 vs 12.9 mo, HR 0.89, p=0.33). But 5-year OS 62.0% vs 45.1% (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.48–0.96, p=0.03) — 32% lower death hazard. EPA well tolerated, no excess bleeding/AF.
INCA33890 (bispecific TGF-βR2/PD-1 inhibitor) + chemo/bevacizumab showed promising activity in MSS metastatic CRC: 71% ORR in 1st-line (n=42) with FOLFOX/bev; 28% ORR in 3rd-line (n=39) with bev alone. No dose-limiting toxicities.
IROCAS closes a 20-year question: adding irinotecan to adjuvant fluoropyrimidine/oxaliplatin does not work in colon cancer — consistent with multiple prior negative trials. "High pathological risk identifies patients who need better outcomes, but does not tell us how to achieve them" (Bennouna).
EMT2 reveals a surprising OS/PFS dissociation: EPA didn't delay progression but extended survival — possibly by reducing cancer cachexia, improving immune surveillance, or altering host metabolism rather than directly killing tumor cells. A safe, cheap, oral supplement with potential survival benefit.
INCA33890 demonstrates that targeting the TGF-β pathway (which creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment in MSS tumors) can restore immunotherapy sensitivity — a biology-driven strategy vs empirical chemo escalation.
Together, these trials signal the end of "more chemo = better" in adjuvant colon cancer and the beginning of mechanism-based adjuvant and post-metastasectomy strategies.
Patients with resected high-risk stage III colon cancer (pT4 and/or pN2) — IROCAS proves mFOLFIRINOX offers no DFS advantage over mFOLFOX6 but adds more diarrhea (15.6% vs 3.8% grade 3+) and fatigue (7.3% vs 1.8%), requiring more G-CSF support (38% vs 19%).
Patients undergoing curative-intent liver metastasis resection for CRC — EMT2 supports considering 4g/day prescription EPA (icosapent ethyl) perioperatively and continuing for years, regardless of adjuvant chemo use (20% in each arm got chemo).
Patients with microsatellite-stable (MSS) metastatic colorectal cancer — the large majority of metastatic cases — for whom early-phase trials of TGF-beta/PD-1 bispecific antibodies (such as INCA33890) are exploring whether immunotherapy can be made to work (general medical guidance).
Not for dMMR/MSI-H patients — they should receive immunotherapy (dostarlimab, pembrolizumab) per current guidelines.
If you have high-risk stage III (T4/N2) colon cancer and are offered mFOLFIRINOX adjuvant therapy, you may wish to ask: "Given IROCAS showed no DFS benefit over mFOLFOX6 but more diarrhea and fatigue, why is mFOLFIRINOX recommended for me? Can I receive standard mFOLFOX6 instead?"
If you are scheduled for liver metastasis resection, you may wish to ask your surgeon/oncologist: "Should I start prescription icosapent ethyl (Vascepa) 4g daily before surgery and continue for 2–4 years per EMT2 data? Are there contraindications (bleeding risk, AFib)?"
If you have MSS metastatic CRC, you may wish to ask: "Are there clinical trials of TGF-β/PD-1 bispecific antibodies (like INCA33890) or other novel immunotherapy combinations I qualify for?"
You may wish to ask about ctDNA testing after surgery and after adjuvant chemotherapy, since persistent ctDNA can identify patients at higher risk of recurrence (see the ctDNA theme) (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: We stop giving toxic irinotecan to high-risk adjuvant patients who won't benefit. EPA offers a benign, inexpensive survival boost after liver surgery. TGF-β/PD-1 bispecifics may finally crack MSS CRC immunotherapy resistance.
  • Reality: mFOLFOX6 still leaves 35% 3-year recurrence risk in high-risk stage III — we need better adjuvant strategies (ctDNA-guided? immunotherapy combinations? targeted agents?). EPA OS benefit needs confirmation — not yet standard of care.

The Reality

  • INCA33890/Temab-A/petosemtamab are early-phase — years from approval, if they succeed. MSS CRC remains the "final frontier" for immunotherapy.
  • Cost and access: novel targeted and immunotherapy agents are expensive, and prescription EPA (icosapent ethyl) adds cost; coverage varies (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

mFOLFOX6 (standard adjuvant): Oxaliplatin 85 mg/m² + leucovorin 400 mg/m² + 5-FU bolus 400 mg/m² + 5-FU 2400 mg/m² 46-hr infusion q2wk × 12 cycles. Established standard per IDEA collaboration.

mFOLFIRINOX (failed intensification): Adds irinotecan 180 mg/m² to above. More GI toxicity, no DFS gain.

Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa, 4g/day): High-purity EPA ethyl ester; FDA-approved for CV risk reduction; EMT2 used it off-label for CRC post-hepatectomy survival. Low cost, generic available.

INCA33890 (Incyte): Bispecific TGF-βR2/PD-1 inhibitor; phase I showed 71% ORR 1st-line MSS mCRC + FOLFOX/bev; phase II/III trials planned.

Temab-A (ABBV-400, AbbVie): c-Met ADC + bevacizumab; phase I showed 30% ORR, 14.7 mo OS in 3rd-line vs 0% ORR, 10.2 mo OS for TAS-102/bev; phase III AndroMETa-CRC-560 ongoing.

Petosemtamab (Genmab): EGFR/LGR5 bispecific + chemo; 75% ORR 1st/2nd-line MSS mCRC; combining with KRAS G12D inhibitor.

Evidence Uncertainty

IROCAS: Definitive negative phase III (n=795, powered for DFS); excellent compliance (irinotecan relative dose intensity 91.8%); OS immature (55-month median follow-up, few deaths) — long-term OS benefit cannot be excluded but unlikely given DFS curves superimposable.

EMT2: Phase III, double-blind, placebo-controlled; OS benefit prespecified secondary endpoint — statistically significant but requires cautious interpretation given negative primary (PFS); no adjustment for post-progression therapies (unknown if balanced).

INCA33890: Phase I, non-randomized, small cohorts (n=6–42); impressive ORRs but no control arm; durability and OS unknown; TGF-β inhibition may cause cardiac toxicity (monitored).

All trials: Predominantly White/Asian populations; limited racial diversity; real-world comorbidity burden may affect tolerability.

What To Watch

IROCAS final OS analysis (additional follow-up needed — event rate low).

EMT2 confirmatory trial or meta-analysis — is OS benefit reproducible? Mechanism studies (cachexia, immune modulation).

INCA33890 phase II/III in 1st-line MSS mCRC (with FOLFOX/bev) and adjuvant settings.

Temab-A phase III AndroMETa-CRC-560 vs TAS-102/bev in refractory MSS CRC.

CIRCULATE-US adjuvant escalation arm (ctDNA-positive → intensified therapy) — now questioned post-IROCAS; may pivot to novel agents not irinotecan.

KRAS G12D inhibitor + petosemtamab + chemo trial (Genmab/Incyte) — expanding beyond RAS wild-type.

NCCN/ESMO guideline updates: Will mFOLFIRINOX be removed from high-risk adjuvant options? Will EPA be mentioned post-liver resection?
