FDA Approves Talazoparib Plus Enzalutamide for HRR-Mutated Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer

"The FDA approved talazoparib (Talzenna) combined with enzalutamide (Xtandi) for men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer harboring homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene mutations. In the TALAPRO-3 trial, this combination cut the risk of cancer progression or death by 52% versus enzalutamide alone, with median progression-free survival not yet reached after over 3 years."

On October 26, 2026, the FDA expanded the approval of talazoparib (a PARP inhibitor [a pill that blocks a DNA-repair enzyme, which is especially lethal to cancers with faulty DNA-repair genes]) plus enzalutamide (an androgen receptor inhibitor) to include men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer that carries an HRR gene mutation.
The approval is based on the Phase III TALAPRO-3 trial of 599 men randomly assigned to talazoparib 0.5 mg daily plus enzalutamide 160 mg daily, or placebo plus enzalutamide; all received ongoing ADT.
Talazoparib plus enzalutamide reduced the risk of radiographic progression or death by 52% (HR 0.48; 95% CI 0.36–0.65; P<0.0001); median progression-free survival was not reached in the combination arm versus 45.8 months with enzalutamide alone.
At 3 years, 77% of men on the combination remained progression-free versus 56% on enzalutamide alone; benefit was seen in both BRCA-mutated (HR 0.37) and non-BRCA HRR-mutated (HR 0.57) subgroups.
This is the first FDA-approved PARP inhibitor combination for metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, moving PARP inhibitors earlier in the treatment course where they may have greater impact.
Previously, PARP inhibitors (olaparib, rucaparib, talazoparib) were only approved for castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) after progression on hormonal therapy and/or chemotherapy.
The combination targets two key cancer pathways simultaneously: PARP inhibition blocks DNA repair in HRR-deficient tumors, while enzalutamide blocks androgen receptor signaling.
It is biomarker-driven: only men with a mutation in one of 12 HRR (homologous recombination repair) genes — DNA-repair genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2, and ATM — are eligible, identified by an FDA-authorized tissue or blood test.
Men with newly diagnosed metastatic prostate cancer (de novo mHSPC/mCSPC) or metastatic recurrence after prior local therapy, who have not yet received androgen pathway inhibitor therapy (or remain sensitive to it).
Must have a documented HRR gene mutation in at least 1 of 12 specified genes, detected by an FDA-authorized test (tissue or liquid biopsy).
Approximately 20–25% of men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer harbor HRR mutations; BRCA1/2 mutations account for about one-third of these.
Patients must be fit for combination therapy; those with significant baseline anemia, neutropenia, or thrombocytopenia may require dose adjustments or may not be eligible.
If you have metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, you may wish to ask your oncologist: "Have I had HRR gene testing (both inherited and tumor) with an FDA-authorized test?"
If HRR testing has not been done, you may wish to request it, since it determines eligibility for this combination and other targeted options (general medical guidance).
Discuss the talazoparib + enzalutamide combination with your doctor if you test positive for an HRR mutation; ask about expected side effects (especially anemia, fatigue, low blood counts) and monitoring plan.
Ensure you are on ADT (LHRH agonist/antagonist or surgical castration) as required with this regimen; ask about bone health monitoring (DEXA scan, calcium/vitamin D) during long-term ADT.

The Hope

  • Hope: Median progression-free survival not reached after >3 years suggests durable disease control; 77% progression-free at 3 years vs 56% with enzalutamide alone; oral regimen avoids chemotherapy.
  • Reality: Significant hematologic toxicity—over half develop Grade 3+ anemia requiring transfusions, dose reductions, or growth factor support; close blood count monitoring (monthly for first year) is essential.

The Reality

  • Not a cure: progression will eventually occur; subsequent treatment options after PARP inhibitor progression are an active area of research.
  • Cost and access: Dual branded oral therapy is expensive; requires companion diagnostic testing; insurance coverage may vary; patient assistance programs available through Pfizer.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Talazoparib (Talzenna, Pfizer): oral PARP inhibitor; dose 0.5 mg daily (0.35 mg for moderate renal impairment); combined with enzalutamide 160 mg daily until progression or unacceptable toxicity.

Enzalutamide (Xtandi, Astellas/Pfizer): oral androgen receptor signaling inhibitor; standard dose 160 mg daily.

Companion diagnostics: FoundationOne CDx (tissue-based NGS) and FoundationOne Liquid CDx (blood-based ctDNA NGS) are FDA-approved to identify HRR mutations for this indication.

Other PARP inhibitors in mHSPC: olaparib + abiraterone (PROpel trial, FDA-approved May 2024 for BRCA-mutated mCRPC, not yet in mHSPC); rucaparib + enzalutamide (TRITON3, ongoing).

TALAPRO-3 overall survival data immature (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.56–1.04 at interim); continued follow-up ongoing.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large randomized Phase III trial (599 patients), double-blind, placebo-controlled; prospectively mandated HRR testing; consistent benefit across BRCA and non-BRCA HRR subgroups; priority review under Project Orbis (FDA, Health Canada, Australian TGA).

Primary endpoint: investigator-assessed radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) by RECIST 1.1 and PCWG3 criteria—robust endpoint but not overall survival.

Overall survival data were immature at the main analysis (an early interim look); the overall-survival hazard ratio of 0.77 favored the combination but did not yet reach statistical significance, and longer follow-up is ongoing.

Safety: Grade 3+ adverse events 79% vs 41% (talazoparib arm vs placebo); Grade 3+ anemia 51% vs 3%; treatment discontinuation 19% vs 10%; two treatment-related deaths (pancytopenia, pulmonary fibrosis) in talazoparib arm.

What To Watch

Final overall survival results from TALAPRO-3 (expected when OS maturity reaches prespecified threshold).

Long-term safety and quality-of-life data from TALAPRO-3.

Real-world evidence on sequencing: optimal treatment after progression on talazoparib + enzalutamide (e.g., chemotherapy, radioligand therapy, immunotherapy).

Biomarker refinement: do specific HRR genes (e.g., BRCA2 vs ATM vs CDK12) predict magnitude of benefit?; role of HRR mutation allele burden/loss of heterozygosity.

Combination strategies: trials adding talazoparib + enzalutamide to docetaxel (triplet) or radioligand therapy in mHSPC.
