Testing Antibody-Drug Conjugates Before Surgery: One Trial Disappointed, Another Points Toward Chemo-Free Treatment

"Two early-stage trials tested whether antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) [drugs that carry chemotherapy directly to cancer cells using an antibody as a guide] could work before surgery. In the TALENT trial, trastuzumab deruxtecan alone produced a pathologic complete response [no remaining invasive cancer found at surgery] in only 7% of women with HR-positive/HER2-low early breast cancer — not enough to replace standard chemotherapy. In I-SPY2.2, pairing the same drug with an experimental immunotherapy reached a modelled 54% response in immune-positive HER2-negative disease, raising the possibility of curative treatment without traditional chemotherapy. Both are early results."

The phase II TALENT trial tested trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) before surgery in HR-positive/HER2-low early breast cancer. Pathologic complete response was modest: about 7% with T-DXd alone.
The I-SPY2.2 trial tested rilvegostomig — a bispecific antibody blocking both PD-1 and TIGIT, two brakes on the immune system — combined with T-DXd, reaching a modelled pathologic complete response of 54% in immune-positive HER2-negative early breast cancer.
TALENT did show biological activity: tumours shrank, but not often enough to clear the cancer entirely, which is what a pathologic complete response requires.
Investigators have suggested that quantitative measures of HER2 protein levels, rather than the current categories, might better identify who benefits — though that remains a hypothesis.
A negative result matters: it tells patients and clinicians that T-DXd alone is not ready to replace standard chemotherapy before surgery in HER2-low disease, despite its success in metastatic disease.
The contrast between the two trials suggests the ADC may need an immunotherapy partner to produce deep responses in early-stage disease, rather than working alone.
If the I-SPY2.2 signal holds, it could allow some women to avoid anthracycline chemotherapy — a class associated with heart damage and, rarely, secondary leukaemia (general medical guidance).
Pathologic complete response is used as an early signal of long-term benefit, but it is a surrogate: achieving it does not guarantee cure, and missing it does not guarantee recurrence (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: the I-SPY2.2 result raises a genuinely appealing prospect — curative-intent treatment without traditional chemotherapy for a defined group (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: TALENT is a reminder that a drug working well in metastatic disease does not automatically work before surgery; the settings are biologically different (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: both findings are early-phase and neither changes standard care today; standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains the comparator to beat (general medical guidance).
  • Honest framing: reporting the disappointing trial alongside the promising one is itself useful, since negative results rarely reach patients (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd): an established ADC in metastatic HER2-positive and HER2-low disease; TALENT tested whether that success transfers to the early-stage, pre-surgery setting (general medical guidance).

Rilvegostomig: an investigational bispecific antibody targeting PD-1 and TIGIT, studied in combination with T-DXd within the I-SPY2.2 platform.

I-SPY2.2 is an adaptive platform trial, which tests several regimens within a shared framework and uses modelling to estimate response rates — a design that generates signals faster but with more uncertainty than a conventional trial (general medical guidance).

Interstitial lung disease (lung inflammation) is a recognised risk of T-DXd and requires monitoring in any setting (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

TALENT is a phase II trial, and a 7% pathologic complete response with T-DXd alone is well below what standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy achieves in comparable settings.

The I-SPY2.2 figure of 54% is a MODELLED estimate produced within an adaptive platform, not an observed rate from a randomised comparison — it should be read as a signal for further testing.

Neither trial reports long-term outcomes; pathologic complete response is an early endpoint, and survival data are not yet available.

The suggestion that quantitative HER2 measurement could better select patients is a hypothesis arising from these results, not an established approach.

What To Watch

Whether the I-SPY2.2 signal is confirmed in a randomised trial with observed rather than modelled response rates (general medical guidance).

Whether quantitative HER2 measurement is developed into a usable test for selecting patients (general medical guidance).

Longer-term outcomes from TALENT, which would show whether tumour shrinkage without complete response still carries benefit (general medical guidance).

Further ADC-plus-immunotherapy combinations entering early-stage trials (general medical guidance).
