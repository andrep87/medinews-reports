First Potential Disease-Modifying Drug for Vascular Dementia Heads Toward Human Trials

"Vascular dementia — caused by damage to the brain's blood vessels from strokes, high blood pressure, or diabetes — has no FDA-approved treatments. A peptide called PNA5, developed over roughly 15 years at the University of Arizona, crosses the blood-brain barrier, reduces brain inflammation, and improved cognition in animal studies. A Phase 1 safety trial in healthy volunteers is planned, supported by a $9 million National Institute on Aging grant."

Researchers at the University of Arizona developed PNA5, a peptide designed to treat vascular dementia — a condition with no FDA-approved disease-modifying drugs.
In animal studies, PNA5 crossed the blood-brain barrier, reduced neuroinflammation, improved blood flow, and improved cognitive performance.
The work represents roughly 15 years of development.

The Reality
