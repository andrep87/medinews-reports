Precision Biomarkers Enabling Smarter Treatment Decisions: ctDNA, Imaging, and Genomic Tests

"Advances in circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) monitoring, MRI-based radiomics, genomic recurrence assays, and AI-powered pathology are transforming breast cancer from a "one-size-fits-all" approach to biologically guided therapy—identifying who needs treatment, who can safely avoid it, and when to switch before scans show progression."

SERENA-6 trial: Serial ctDNA monitoring during 1st-line aromatase inhibitor + CDK4/6 inhibitor detected emergent ESR1 mutations; switching to camizestrant (oral SERD) + CDK4/6 inhibitor at molecular progression (before scans) improved PFS from 9.2 to 16 months and delayed chemotherapy need.
Tumor Heterogeneity Index (THI) from DCE-MRI: In I-SPY2 trial, high THI predicted 56.1% pathologic complete response (pCR) with AKT inhibitor MK2206 + chemo vs low THI; outperformed standard IHC receptor testing.
CDK1 activity biomarker: High CDK1 protein activity (not just presence) predicts CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance across early and advanced ER+ breast cancer; detectable in biopsies and preclinical models.
Genomic assay evolution: OPTIMA trial (PAM50/ROR) showed ~2/3 of patients with up to 9 positive nodes avoided chemo with noninferior outcomes; TAILORx/RxPONDER/MINDACT continue refining chemo omission criteria.
AI/digital pathology: Clairity Breast (FDA-authorized) estimates 5-year risk from standard mammograms; Vanderbilt transcriptomic atlas reveals novel molecular subtypes; HER2DX assay predicts trastuzumab deruxtecan benefit.
ctDNA/MRD testing enables "molecular interception"—treating at the first sign of resistance (ESR1 mutation) rather than waiting for symptomatic progression, potentially extending endocrine therapy durability.
Imaging biomarkers like THI can predict drug response before treatment starts, sparing patients ineffective therapy (e.g., AKT inhibitors only for high-THI tumors) and reducing trial-and-error.
CDK1 activity testing could finally answer "will CDK4/6 inhibitors work for me?" and "have they stopped working?"—currently unanswerable questions for millions on these drugs.
Genomic assays are moving beyond "chemo yes/no" to "which endocrine therapy, for how long, with/without ovarian suppression"—personalizing duration and intensity. (general medical guidance)
AI pathology democratizes access: a digital slide image replaces shipping tissue to central labs, enabling faster, cheaper, globally accessible biomarker testing.
HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer on 1st-line aromatase inhibitor + CDK4/6 inhibitor: Eligible for ctDNA monitoring (e.g., Guardant360, FoundationOne Liquid) to detect ESR1 mutations for potential camizestrant switch.
Early-stage TNBC or HR+/HER2- considering neoadjuvant AKT inhibitor or chemo: May benefit from DCE-MRI with THI analysis (investigational) to predict pCR likelihood.
ER+ breast cancer patients on CDK4/6 inhibitors: Future CDK1 activity testing (IHC/functional assay) could guide initial selection and resistance detection.
Node-positive HR+/HER2- early breast cancer: OPTIMA/RxPONDER genomic assays (Oncotype DX, PAM50/Prosigna, MammaPrint) inform chemo omission decisions, especially postmenopausal.
Women <40 or with dense breasts: AI mammography (Clairity Breast) and blood-based RNA assays (genTRU-breast) may detect risk missed by standard models.
If on 1st-line AI + CDK4/6 inhibitor for metastatic HR+/HER2- disease: You may wish to ask your oncologist: "Do you offer serial ctDNA monitoring for ESR1 mutations? If an ESR1 mutation emerges, would we switch to camizestrant + CDK4/6 inhibitor per SERENA-6?"
Before starting neoadjuvant therapy for early breast cancer: Ask: "Is DCE-MRI with radiomic analysis (THI) available to predict if I'll benefit from specific agents like AKT inhibitors?"
If deciding on adjuvant chemotherapy for HR+/HER2- early breast cancer: You may wish to request genomic recurrence testing (Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, Prosigna) and ask: "Does my score support chemo omission? How does menopausal status and node count change this?"
For screening: If <40 or dense breasts, ask: "Am I eligible for AI-enhanced mammography (Clairity Breast) or blood-based RNA testing (genTRU-breast trial) to improve risk assessment?"
Discuss HER2DX or similar predictive assays if considering trastuzumab deruxtecan for HER2+ or HER2-low disease: "Can we test if my tumor is likely to respond?"

The Hope

  • Hope: We are moving toward "test before treat" for major decisions—chemo, endocrine duration, ADC selection, resistance switching—sparing toxicity and cost. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: Most biomarkers (THI, CDK1 activity, ctDNA for ESR1) are not yet standard-of-care; insurance coverage varies; turnaround times and lab access limit real-time use.
  • ctDNA false positives/negatives: Clonal hematopoiesis, low tumor fraction, and assay sensitivity can mislead; clinical decisions require confirmation. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Genomic assays don't capture all biology: TAILORx showed younger women may benefit from chemo despite low RS—age/menopause still modify genomic guidance. (general medical guidance)
  • Equity concern: Advanced testing (ctDNA serial, DCE-MRI, AI pathology) may widen disparities between academic centers and community/rural practices.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Camizestrant (AZD9833, AstraZeneca): Oral SERD; FDA-approved 2026 for ESR1-mutated HR+/HER2- advanced breast cancer after CDK4/6 inhibitor + AI; SERENA-6 established ctDNA-guided switching paradigm. (general medical guidance)

MK2206 (Merck): AKT inhibitor; I-SPY2 showed THI-high predicted pCR 56.1%; not yet approved—biomarker-guided development continues. (general medical guidance)

CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib): CDK1 activity may become companion diagnostic for selection and resistance monitoring.

Genomic assays: Oncotype DX (RS 0-100), MammaPrint (70-gene), Prosigna/PAM50 (ROR score)—guiding chemo decisions in early HR+/HER2- disease. (general medical guidance)

AI platforms: Clairity Breast (iCAD, FDA-authorized 5-yr risk from mammogram); genTRU-breast (Mammogen, blood RNA assay, KATELYN-ERIN trial); HER2DX (mRNA assay for T-DXd response). (general medical guidance)

Vanderbilt transcriptomic atlas: 7,600 tumors, 22 studies—redefining molecular subtypes (ER-low, neuroendocrine, basal-luminal progenitor) with therapeutic implications.

Evidence Uncertainty

SERENA-6: switching to camizestrant when an ESR1 mutation appeared in the blood (before scans showed worsening) prolonged the time without progression from about 9.2 to 16 months. Overall survival data are not yet mature.

Tumor Heterogeneity Index: an exploratory imaging analysis within the I-SPY2 platform; the AKT inhibitor studied is not approved, and prospective validation is needed.

CDK1: Preclinical models + 400+ patient biopsies + retrospective trial analysis; prospective clinical utility trial required; functional assay (activity not just protein) not commercially available.

OPTIMA: a large multicentre population, 3.9-yr median follow-up; noninferiority for genomic-guided chemo omission; premenopausal benefit uncertainty remains (chemo vs ovarian suppression).

AI pathology: Clairity Breast validated in a large screening population; genTRU-breast early trial; Vanderbilt atlas retrospective—prospective treatment-guided trials needed.

What To Watch

SERENA-6 OS and PFS2 maturity: Will early ESR1-switching translate to overall survival gain?

Prospective THI-guided trial: Will selecting AKT inhibitor only for THI-high patients improve overall neoadjuvant outcomes?

HER2DX prospective validation: Can mRNA assay identify HER2+ and HER2-low patients most likely to benefit from trastuzumab deruxtecan? (general medical guidance)

Trials clarifying whether chemotherapy benefit in premenopausal women reflects a direct effect on the cancer or its effect on ovarian function (general medical guidance).
