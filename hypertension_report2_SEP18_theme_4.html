Expanding Treatment Landscape — New Drug Classes, Better Tolerability Profiles, and Pipeline Innovations for Resistant and Pulmonary Hypertension

"The hypertension treatment arsenal is expanding beyond traditional pills. For pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) [high blood pressure in the lung arteries], sotatercept (Winrevair) — a first-in-class injectable that rebalances blood vessel growth signals — is now guideline-recommended as add-on therapy for intermediate/high-risk patients. For pulmonary hypertension with lung disease (PH-ILD), inhaled mosliciguat cut lung vessel resistance by half in Phase 2. For common hypertension, ARB+CCB [angiotensin receptor blocker + calcium channel blocker] combinations emerge as the best-tolerated regimen, while novel mechanisms (RNA interference, aldosterone synthase inhibition, antisense oligonucleotides) advance in late-stage trials."

European Respiratory Society (ERS) 2026 guidelines strongly recommend adding sotatercept (Winrevair, Merck) — a subcutaneous [under the skin] injection every 3 weeks — to background therapy for PAH patients at intermediate-low, intermediate-high, or high risk of death. Based on 4 RCTs, it improved 6-minute walk distance by ~35–41 meters, cut hospitalization risk by 81% (relative risk 0.19), and reduced clinical worsening by 72% (RR 0.28).
In PH-ILD [pulmonary hypertension caused by lung scarring], inhaled mosliciguat (Pulmovant) more than halved pulmonary vascular resistance [resistance in lung blood vessels] (-51% vs. +7% placebo) at 16 weeks in a 135-patient Phase 2 trial; Phase 3 (PHrontier, 375 patients) is underway.
A JAMA network meta-analysis of 716 trials found ARB+CCB combination had the lowest treatment discontinuation due to side effects — better than monotherapy or placebo. Four of the top five regimens contained an ARB [angiotensin receptor blocker, e.g., losartan, valsartan, olmesartan].
A 19.3-million-patient real-world study found telmisartan (an ARB with PPAR-gamma activity [a metabolic pathway that may improve insulin sensitivity]) did not significantly reduce new-onset diabetes compared to losartan, valsartan, or candesartan after propensity matching (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.88–1.08).
Pipeline: zilebesiran (RNA interference [gene-silencing] targeting liver angiotensinogen, biannual injection), lorundrostat (aldosterone synthase inhibitor, daily oral), tonlamarsen (antisense oligonucleotide [gene-targeting] against angiotensinogen), seralutinib (inhaled PDGFR/CSF1R/c-KIT inhibitor for PAH/PH-ILD) — all in Phase 2/3.
PAH and PH-ILD are rare but devastating conditions. Prior treatments targeted only 3 pathways (endothelin, nitric oxide, prostacyclin). Sotatercept adds a 4th pathway (activin signaling). Mosliciguat offers a novel inhaled approach for PH-ILD — both expand treatment options beyond vasodilation [vessel widening] alone.
For common hypertension, tolerability drives adherence. ARB+CCB's superior side-effect profile (less cough than ACE inhibitors, less swelling than CCB alone, fewer discontinuations) means more patients may stay on therapy — which is associated with better BP control (general medical guidance).
Telmisartan’s theoretical metabolic advantage did not materialize in practice — clinicians should choose ARBs based on dosing, cost, and patient factors, not presumed diabetes prevention.
Pipeline diversity (RNAi, ASO, enzyme inhibition, kinase inhibition) targets the RAAS [renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system] at multiple levels, offering hope for resistant hypertension [BP uncontrolled on 3+ drugs including a diuretic] and infrequent dosing to solve adherence.
PAH patients (WHO Group 1) on stable background therapy (ERA + PDE5i ± prostacyclin) who are intermediate/high risk per REVEAL 2.0 or ESC/ERS risk tables — discuss sotatercept add-on with your PH specialist.
PH-ILD patients (WHO Group 3) with symptomatic disease despite optimal lung disease management — ask about mosliciguat trial eligibility (PHrontier, NCT07816185) or compassionate access.
Newly diagnosed hypertensive patients: ARB+CCB is a well-tolerated combination option per the meta-analysis evidence — discuss with your doctor whether it's appropriate for your situation (general medical guidance).
Resistant hypertension patients (on 3+ drugs at max doses) — monitor trials of zilebesiran, lorundrostat, tonlamarsen; discuss referral to hypertension specialist for phenotyping [detailed testing to identify the mechanism driving your BP].
If you have PAH: Ask your PH team: “Am I intermediate/high risk? Should we add sotatercept?” Request monitoring plan for hemoglobin [red blood cell protein, sotatercept can raise it], platelets [clotting cells, can drop], and bleeding — especially if on blood thinners.
If you have PH-ILD: Ask your pulmonologist: 'Is mosliciguat an option for me, or can I join the PHrontier trial?' Inhaled delivery may offer convenience advantages for some patients (general medical guidance).
If starting BP meds or switching: Ask your doctor: “Is an ARB+CCB combination right for me?” — especially if you’ve had cough on ACE inhibitors or leg swelling on CCB alone.
If on telmisartan for diabetes prevention: Discuss with your doctor whether another ARB is equally effective and lower cost — the metabolic benefit is unproven in real-world use.
All patients: Report side effects promptly — dizziness, cough, swelling, electrolyte changes — so regimens can be adjusted before you stop taking them.

The Hope

  • Hope: First new PAH pathway in decades; inhaled PH-ILD option without IV pumps; biannual injections could solve adherence; ASO/RNAi offer durable RAAS suppression.
  • Reality: Sotatercept is a specialty drug with high cost; requires monitoring for hemoglobin elevation, platelet drops, and bleeding risk. Not curative. Mosliciguat is years from approval. ARB+CCB combinations use affordable generic drugs. Pipeline drugs face payer scrutiny versus cheap generics (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Access: Specialty drugs for PAH/PH-ILD are typically available through accredited centers. Resistant hypertension evaluation may require referral to a specialist. Global equity gaps in access to newer therapies persist (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Sotatercept (Winrevair): FDA-approved March 2024 for PAH; ERS 2026 guideline add-on for intermediate/high risk; SC injection 0.3→0.7 mg/kg q3wk; monitor Hb, platelets, bleeding; $ cost high, specialty pharmacy required.

Mosliciguat: Inhaled sGC stimulator; Phase 3 PHrontier enrolling; targets PH-ILD gap (only inhaled treprostinil approved); cough rate 12% vs. 18% placebo — favorable vs. other inhaled PAH drugs.

Zilebesiran: Alnylam/Roche RNAi therapeutic; biannual SC injection; Phase 3 ZENITH CVOT [cardiovascular outcomes trial] ongoing; targets angiotensinogen at the source.

Lorundrostat: Mineralys aldosterone synthase inhibitor; daily oral; Phase 3 Launch-HTN met primary endpoint in resistant/uncontrolled HTN; FDA NDA accepted, PDUFA Dec 22, 2026.

Tonlamarsen: Kardigan ASO against angiotensinogen; Phase 2 KARDINAL showed significant BP reduction; Phase 2b planned for acute severe HTN post-hospitalization.

Evidence Uncertainty

Sotatercept: Strong RCT evidence for functional improvement (walk distance, NT-proBNP [heart strain biomarker], time to worsening); long-term RV [right ventricle] effects unknown; low-risk PAH patients excluded — no recommendation; bleeding risk with anticoagulants (5.2% severe bleeding in SOTERIA, half on anticoagulants).

Mosliciguat: Phase 2 only (n=135); PVR reduction substantial but this is a surrogate endpoint; Phase 3 needed for clinical outcomes (exercise capacity, survival). Cough rate was 12% vs. 18% with placebo.

ARB+CCB tolerability: Network meta-analysis of short-term trials (4–26 weeks); cardiovascular outcomes not assessed; editorial warns against switching stable patients; CCB dose-dependency for edema persists.

Telmisartan: Large real-world data but observational; residual confounding possible; PPAR-gamma effect may be dose/duration dependent.

Pipeline: Many novel mechanisms but high attrition risk; RNAi/ASO require injection/infusion; cost and access unknown; CVOTs (zilebesiran ZENITH) will determine if BP lowering translates to event reduction.

What To Watch

Sotatercept: SOTERIA long-term safety (NCT07218029); ERS/ESC guideline update for low-risk PAH; real-world hemoglobin/platelet management protocols.

Mosliciguat: PHrontier Phase 3 results (primary endpoint: PVR at 24 weeks); FDA/EMA submission timeline.

Zilebesiran: ZENITH CVOT interim analyses (major adverse cardiovascular events); FDA review if positive.

Lorundrostat: FDA decision Dec 22, 2026; Launch-HTN subgroup data (CKD, resistant HTN).

Tonlamarsen: Phase 2b in acute severe hypertension; ASO platform safety across cardiovascular indications.

Future guideline integration: Whether ARB+CCB will be elevated to a preferred initial combination, and whether detailed blood pressure phenotyping will enter treatment algorithms (general medical guidance).
