Once-Weekly Basal Insulin (Efsitora/Onswik) FDA-Approved: 365 Injections Cut to 52 Per Year

"The FDA approved insulin efsitora alfa-gobe (Onswik) on September 24, 2026 — the second once-weekly basal insulin approved for type 2 diabetes, following insulin icodec (Awiqli) in March 2026. Phase 3 trials in more than 3,400 adults showed it matches daily insulins on A1c reduction while cutting yearly injections from about 365 to 52."

FDA approved Onswik (insulin efsitora alfa-gobe) as a once-weekly basal insulin for adults with type 2 diabetes, as an adjunct to diet and exercise.
Approval was based on the QWINT phase 3 program: four trials with more than 3,400 participants, comparing efsitora against daily insulin glargine U-100 or insulin degludec U-100.
The primary endpoint was met in each trial: non-inferior A1c reduction compared with daily basal insulin, across insulin-naive patients, those switching from daily basal insulin, and those also using mealtime insulin.
The safety profile was similar to daily basal insulins, with hypoglycemia [low blood sugar] remaining the main side effect. Onswik is not recommended for type 1 diabetes because of increased severe hypoglycemia risk.
Onswik is the second once-weekly basal insulin approved in the U.S., following insulin icodec (Awiqli) in March 2026 (independent fact-check).
Daily injections are a real barrier. Many people delay starting insulin or miss doses because of needle burden, stigma, or dependence on a caregiver (general medical guidance).
Weekly dosing can align with weekly injectable diabetes medications some patients already take, potentially consolidating the injection schedule (general medical guidance).
Two pen concentrations accommodate patients who need large weekly doses because of substantial insulin resistance.
Efsitora has a half-life of about 17 days, which is what allows weekly dosing — but also means dose changes take longer to take full effect than with daily insulin (independent fact-check).
Adults with type 2 diabetes who need to start basal insulin or intensify existing treatment.
People who struggle with daily injections — including older adults, those with needle anxiety or dexterity difficulties, and those who depend on a caregiver for injections (general medical guidance).
Not for type 1 diabetes. Onswik is not recommended in type 1 diabetes due to increased risk of severe hypoglycemia.
If you are on daily basal insulin, ask your clinician: 'Is once-weekly Onswik an option for me, and how would we make the switch?'
SWITCHING DOSE — ASK YOUR CLINICIAN TO CALCULATE THIS. The starting dose depends on your fasting blood glucose. In the trials, patients whose baseline fasting glucose was ABOVE 120 mg/dL received a one-time starting dose of their prior total daily basal dose multiplied by 7, then multiplied by 3. But patients whose baseline fasting glucose was 120 mg/dL OR LOWER did NOT receive that higher starting dose — they started at the prior daily dose multiplied by 7. Applying the higher starting dose to someone who does not need it could cause prolonged low blood sugar, because efsitora stays in the body for weeks. Do not calculate or adjust this yourself (independent fact-check).
Ask which pen concentration is right for your dose. The two concentrations deliver different maximum amounts per injection, and getting this wrong matters (general medical guidance).
Monitor your glucose closely after switching and report any low readings to your clinician promptly. Because efsitora has a roughly 17-day half-life, a dose that is too high cannot be corrected quickly (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: A substantial reduction in injection burden — a meaningful quality-of-life change for people who are injection-averse, and one that may improve willingness to start insulin and stay on it.
  • Reality: Cost and formulary placement are not yet known, and insurance prior authorization could delay access (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: The long half-life cuts both ways. Weekly dosing offers less room for day-to-day adjustment, and an error in dosing persists far longer than it would with daily insulin (independent fact-check).
  • Reality: The KwikPen will be available in U.S. pharmacies in the coming months rather than immediately.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Onswik (efsitora alfa-gobe, Eli Lilly): FDA approved September 2026; also approved in the European Union, Mexico, and Japan. Supplied in KwikPen in two concentrations.

Efsitora uses an Fc-fusion design that slows clearance from the body, producing its approximately 17-day half-life (independent fact-check).

Insulin icodec (Awiqli, Novo Nordisk) was approved in the U.S. in March 2026 and uses a different mechanism — albumin binding rather than Fc fusion (independent fact-check).

The QWINT program tested two titration approaches: a fixed stepwise escalation and a flexible schedule using a one-time higher starting dose.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large global phase 3 program across four trials, consistent non-inferiority in diverse patient populations, with two titration strategies tested.

Limitations: Trials compared efsitora only against daily basal insulins, not against newer non-insulin combinations. Long-term real-world adherence and safety are unknown. The trials were sponsored by the manufacturer.

The long half-life is a double-edged feature: it enables weekly dosing but means both reaching steady state and correcting an excessive dose take considerably longer than with daily insulin (independent fact-check).

Cardiovascular outcomes data were not required for approval and are not available.

What To Watch

Real-world effectiveness and adherence data following the U.S. launch.

How the two approved once-weekly insulins — efsitora and icodec — compare in practice, given their different mechanisms (general medical guidance).

Whether dosing errors emerge as a real-world safety signal, particularly around the conditional starting dose (general medical guidance).

Long-term cardiovascular outcomes data, which were not required for approval (general medical guidance).
