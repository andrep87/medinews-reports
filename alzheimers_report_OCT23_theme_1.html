Prevention Vaccines and Cell Therapies Enter Human Testing

"Two first-in-class prevention strategies—an amyloid-beta vaccine and a neuron-replacement cell therapy—have cleared regulatory hurdles and will begin human trials in 2027. These approaches aim to stop Alzheimer’s before symptoms start or replace neurons already lost, addressing gaps left by current amyloid-clearing drugs that only modestly slow decline."

Nuravax's AV-1959R vaccine [a shot that trains the immune system to make antibodies against amyloid-beta, a protein that builds up in Alzheimer's] received FDA Fast Track designation in October 2026 for preventing Alzheimer's in cognitively unimpaired adults who have biomarker evidence of amyloid buildup. In a Phase 1 trial of 16 people, all 12 vaccine recipients developed anti-amyloid antibodies after two doses (independent fact-check: verified against the Phase 1 publication and FDA Fast Track announcement).
S2N.Bio began transferring its cell therapy to a manufacturing facility in Melbourne, Australia, ahead of a planned first-in-human study in 2027. The therapy uses a patient's own hair-follicle-derived cells to try to replace neurons lost to neurodegeneration.
A 2022 veterinary study in five aged dogs with naturally occurring cognitive decline reported that four of five treated dogs improved, with two showing complete reversal; the dog model shares some features with human Alzheimer's.
Fast Track is an FDA program that allows more frequent agency interaction and potentially faster review for conditions with serious unmet need; it does not guarantee approval.
Current approved anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) only modestly slow cognitive decline and cannot restore function already lost. A vaccine aims to prevent amyloid buildup during the silent years before symptoms appear (general medical guidance).
S2N.Bio's cell therapy targets a different mechanism: replacing neurons that have already died, rather than clearing protein. No approved therapy currently does this.
An active vaccine, if it works, could in principle provide longer-lasting protection than repeated antibody infusions, though this is unproven in humans (general medical guidance).
Fast Track designation can speed development for serious conditions with unmet need, but efficacy still has to be proven in larger trials.
AV-1959R vaccine: cognitively unimpaired adults (around ages 55-80) with biomarker-confirmed amyloid (positive amyloid PET or blood test) but no symptoms. The planned AREVIA Phase 2 trial aims to enroll about 160 such people.
S2N.Bio cell therapy: initially, participants in a first-in-human safety trial in Australia (planned 2027). Future studies would likely focus on people who have lost significant neuronal function (general medical guidance).
These approaches are investigational and not for people with advanced dementia or those without biomarker evidence of Alzheimer's.
Participation in any of these requires meeting specific trial criteria confirmed by a specialist (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask your neurologist or memory specialist whether amyloid biomarker testing (blood test or PET scan) is appropriate to see if you might qualify for prevention trials such as AREVIA (general medical guidance).
If you have a family history of Alzheimer's and are roughly 55-80 with no cognitive symptoms, you may wish to discuss whether enrolling in a prevention trial is appropriate for you (general medical guidance).
You may wish to register with clinical-trial matching services (such as ClinicalTrials.gov or the Alzheimer's Association TrialMatch) to be notified when relevant trial sites open near you (general medical guidance).
For S2N.Bio's future trial, you may wish to ask your care team about Australian trial registries if you are willing to travel, since the first-in-human study is planned there (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: a vaccine could help stop Alzheimer's before it starts, and cell therapy could one day restore lost function; Fast Track and new trial designs may speed timelines.
  • Reality: prevention trials take several years to show clinical benefit. AV-1959R's Phase 2 is planned for 2027, with later-phase trials and any approval years beyond that. S2N.Bio's first study is a safety trial.

The Reality

  • Access: vaccines and especially cell therapies are likely to be expensive at first, and insurance coverage for prevention in people without symptoms is unresolved (general medical guidance).
  • No guarantee any will work in humans — most Alzheimer's drugs fail in mid- to late-stage trials. These are early steps, not imminent treatments.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

AV-1959R (Nuravax): an amyloid-beta vaccine. Phase 1 complete (all 12 vaccine recipients developed antibodies); the AREVIA Phase 2 trial (NCT07827768, about 160 participants) is planned to begin in early 2027; FDA Fast Track granted October 2026.

S2N.Bio cell therapy: hair-follicle-derived cells intended to become neurons; the manufacturing process is being transferred to a GMP facility in Melbourne, with a first-in-human Australian trial planned for 2027. Preclinical data come from the five-dog study.

Both approaches are early-stage; neither has human efficacy data in Alzheimer's.

Other prevention and neuron-supporting strategies are in development and are covered in later themes (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

AV-1959R Phase 1: 16 people (12 vaccine, 4 placebo); the main goal was immunogenicity (antibody response), which all vaccine recipients met, with no placebo response. There is no efficacy data yet — prevention trials take years.

S2N.Bio: preclinical only. The dog study was small (5 animals) and open-label; four improved and two reversed, but animal models do not reliably predict human outcomes. No human safety or efficacy data exist.

A key uncertainty is whether preventing amyloid buildup, or replacing neurons, will translate into meaningful clinical benefit in people.

Long-term safety of vaccination or cell transplantation in this setting is unknown (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

AREVIA Phase 2 launch (planned early 2027): enrollment of amyloid-positive, cognitively unimpaired adults, with safety, antibody response, and biomarker changes as endpoints.

S2N.Bio Australian first-in-human trial initiation (planned 2027): safety, feasibility, and early biomarker data from the neuron-replacement approach.

Longer-term global decisions on coverage and reimbursement for disease-modifying Alzheimer's therapies, which will shape access (general medical guidance).

Whether early vaccine antibody responses translate into reduced amyloid on scans over time (general medical guidance).
