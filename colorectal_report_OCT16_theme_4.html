Immunotherapy Is Being Tested in New Colorectal Cancer Settings and Tumor Types

"Immunotherapy drugs are being studied in more colorectal cancer settings, including earlier-stage disease and tumors once considered resistant. A Phase III trial is testing a bispecific antibody before and after surgery for MSI-H/dMMR colon cancer (tumors with high microsatellite instability or deficient mismatch repair), while other combinations show early promise in MSS metastatic disease (microsatellite-stable tumors that usually resist immunotherapy). These approaches are investigational and not yet approved for these new uses."

Akeso started a Phase III trial (COMPASSION-40) of cadonilimab [a bispecific antibody that blocks two immune checkpoints at once, PD-1 and CTLA-4] given before and after surgery (perioperative) for resectable MSI-H/dMMR colon cancer; the first patient was dosed in October 2026.
A Phase 1b study (FORTRESS) of NG-350A combined with chemoradiation finished enrolling patients with mismatch repair-proficient (pMMR) rectal cancer [tumors that can repair DNA errors and usually resist immunotherapy].
Early data show botensilimab plus balstilimab (two immunotherapy antibodies) may help some patients with microsatellite-stable (MSS) metastatic colorectal cancer, which has historically not responded to immunotherapy.
Several additional immune-based combinations and new drugs are entering early clinical trials for colorectal cancer.
MSI-H/dMMR tumors respond well to immunotherapy, but conventional chemotherapy given around surgery produces little tumor response in this group (a pathologic complete response in only about 7% of patients), so an immunotherapy option could fill a real gap.
MSS tumors make up most colorectal cancers and have historically resisted immunotherapy, so new combinations that work in MSS disease could help a much larger group of patients.
Perioperative treatment aims to shrink or clear the tumor before surgery and lower the chance it returns afterward, and in some cases could allow less extensive surgery.
No immunotherapy is yet approved anywhere for the perioperative treatment of localized MSI-H/dMMR colon cancer, so these trials address an unmet need (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: extending immunotherapy into earlier-stage disease and into MSS tumors could reach many more colorectal cancer patients, and a bispecific antibody might clear MSI-H/dMMR tumors before surgery (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: all of these approaches are investigational. COMPASSION-40 is just beginning, FORTRESS is early-phase, and the MSS data are small and preliminary, so none has yet changed standard care (general medical guidance).
  • Access is largely through clinical trials at specialized centers, which can involve travel and strict eligibility criteria (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Immunotherapy can cause immune-related side effects (such as inflammation of the colon, skin, or glands), and dual checkpoint blockade can raise that risk, so careful monitoring is needed (general medical guidance).
  • Not a cure: even if these strategies work, they are being studied to improve the chance of cure or control, not to guarantee it (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Cadonilimab (AK104, Akeso): a PD-1/CTLA-4 bispecific antibody; COMPASSION-40 is its Phase III perioperative colon cancer trial, supported by an earlier Phase II neoadjuvant signal in MSI-H/dMMR disease (general medical guidance).

NG-350A (Akamis Bio): an engineered oncolytic virus-based therapy being tested with chemoradiation in pMMR rectal cancer in the Phase 1b FORTRESS study.

Botensilimab plus balstilimab (Agenus): a next-generation CTLA-4 antibody combined with a PD-1 antibody, studied in MSS and MSI-H colorectal cancer (for example, the NEST and UNICORN trials) (general medical guidance).

Standard immunotherapy for colorectal cancer is currently limited mainly to MSI-H/dMMR metastatic disease (for example, pembrolizumab) and, newly, adjuvant atezolizumab for stage III dMMR disease (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

COMPASSION-40 has only just dosed its first patient, so it will take years to show whether perioperative cadonilimab improves outcomes; no efficacy results are available yet.

The supporting cadonilimab data come from an earlier, small Phase II neoadjuvant study, which needs confirmation in this randomized Phase III design.

NG-350A/FORTRESS has completed enrollment but is an early-phase (Phase 1b) study focused primarily on safety and feasibility, not proof of benefit.

Botensilimab-plus-balstilimab results in MSS disease come from small, early trials (for example, roughly 29% pathologic complete response in MSS tumors in one neoadjuvant study), and are not yet confirmed in large randomized trials (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Early safety and feasibility signals from the COMPASSION-40 perioperative cadonilimab trial (general medical guidance).

Results from the NG-350A/FORTRESS study in pMMR rectal cancer and whether they justify larger trials (general medical guidance).

Larger, randomized trials testing botensilimab-plus-balstilimab in MSS metastatic colorectal cancer (general medical guidance).

Whether any immunotherapy earns the first approval for perioperative localized MSI-H/dMMR colon cancer (general medical guidance).

Research on biomarkers that could identify which MSS patients are most likely to benefit from immunotherapy (general medical guidance).
