An Implantable Device That Burns Off Excess Blood Sugar — and Other Early-Stage Ideas

"Researchers have tested a first-in-class implantable device that uses an electrical-chemical reaction to convert excess blood glucose into harmless by-products, alongside work on repurposed drugs. These are early-stage concepts, not available treatments. Two once-weekly basal insulins approved earlier in 2026 (Awiqli and Onswik) remain the most practical near-term change for people on insulin."

ETH Zurich researchers created a 3-cm wireless implant: gold/platinum catalyst on graphite felt converts blood glucose to gluconate (excreted by kidneys) when activated by a wearable glucose sensor via smartphone.
In mice with "diabesity," the implant normalized glucose within 1 hour, maintained control for 6 weeks, reduced weight 10%, lowered triglycerides, and shifted metabolism to fat burning.
Context: two once-weekly basal insulins — insulin icodec (Awiqli) and insulin efsitora alfa (Onswik) — were approved in 2026, cutting background insulin injections from daily to weekly (covered in an earlier issue) (general medical guidance).
SITGEM-CARE trial: 22 advanced pancreatic cancer patients received sitagliptin + gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel (GnP); ORR 36.4%, median OS 12.5 months vs historical ~8–9 months with GnP alone.
Weekly insulin: Reduces 365 injections/year to 52; may overcome "psychological insulin resistance," improve adherence, especially in elderly, visually impaired, or low-health-literacy populations. (general medical guidance)
Implant: First drug-free, cell-free, gene-free glucose-lowering approach; directly removes glucose rather than enhancing insulin action; could avoid weight gain from insulin therapy. (general medical guidance)
Sitagliptin repurposing: Pancreatic cancer has <12-month median survival; sitagliptin inhibits ribonucleotide reductase (RRM1/2), resensitizing tumors to gemcitabine—cheap, oral, well-known safety profile. (general medical guidance)
Kyinsu (insulin icodec + semaglutide fixed-ratio combo) approved in Japan/China/Korea for T2D: Weekly dual therapy could further simplify regimens. (general medical guidance)
Weekly insulin: Adults with type 2 diabetes needing basal insulin; type 1 diabetes (Awiqli approved in some regions); patients struggling with daily injection burden, adherence, or needle anxiety. (general medical guidance)
Implant: Currently preclinical only; future candidates would be type 2 diabetes/obesity patients failing or intolerant to medications, or those seeking non-drug alternatives. (general medical guidance)
Sitagliptin + chemo: Advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma patients (stage III/IV) eligible for gemcitabine-based regimens; not yet standard of care—clinical trial context only. (general medical guidance)
Kyinsu: Type 2 diabetes patients needing both basal insulin and GLP-1 agonist; available in Japan, China, South Korea; not yet FDA-reviewed. (general medical guidance)
If on daily basal insulin: Ask your endocrinologist "Is once-weekly insulin (Awiqli or Onswik) appropriate for me? What monitoring changes are needed during transition?" (general medical guidance)
For weekly insulin switch: Expect dose conversion (e.g., daily dose ×7 = weekly dose); require closer glucose monitoring first 2–4 weeks; understand hypoglycemia risk with prolonged action during illness/fasting. (general medical guidance)
If pancreatic cancer patient: Ask your oncologist about clinical trials combining sitagliptin or other DPP-4 inhibitors with chemotherapy; do not self-add sitagliptin without trial oversight. (general medical guidance)
For implant curiosity: This is years from human use; focus on current effective therapies (GLP-1s, SGLT2, insulin) and lifestyle; ask about trial eligibility if interested in novel devices. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: Weekly insulin could be "insulin for everyone" by removing daily injection barrier; implant represents paradigm shift to device-based metabolic control; sitagliptin costs pennies vs $$$ novel cancer drugs. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: Weekly insulin pricing/reimbursement uncertain; Awiqli in India ~Rs 1,044/week vs daily insulin Rs 1,000–5,000/month—may not save money without adherence gains. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Implant: 5–10+ years to clinic if successful; requires surgery, battery/recharging, regulatory path for active implantable device; not a near-term option. (general medical guidance)
  • Sitagliptin: Pancreatic cancer trials need pharma sponsorship (generic drug, low profit); physician awareness low; gemcitabine use declining vs FOLFIRINOX in fit patients. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Awiqli (insulin icodec): 700 U/mL pen; weekly dosing; approved US (T2D), EU, China, Japan, India, Australia (T1D & T2D); Kyinsu (icodec + semaglutide) weekly combo approved Japan/China/Korea. (general medical guidance)

Onswik (insulin efsitora alfa): Weekly basal insulin; FDA approved Sep 2026 for T2D; Japan approval Sep 2026; longer half-life (~1 week) via Fc-fusion albumin binding. (general medical guidance)

Implant: ETH Zurich team planning pig/sheep studies; challenges: scaling catalyst capacity for human glucose load (250g carbs/day vs 2g in mice), gluconate kidney clearance safety, device longevity. (general medical guidance)

Sitagliptin: Generic, $4–$10/month; SITGEM-CARE used 100 mg daily; ongoing mechanistic studies to confirm RRM1/2 inhibition vs immune/DPP-4 effects; randomized confirmatory trial needed. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Weekly insulin: ONWARDS trials (icodec) and QWINT trials (efsitora) showed non-inferior HbA1c vs daily basal insulin; hypoglycemia rates comparable but longer duration of action requires new sick-day rules. (general medical guidance)

Implant: Mouse data only (n=8, 6 weeks); catalyst stable 3 months; no immune/inflammatory reaction; but human scaling, gluconate acid-base/electrolyte effects, and surgical implantation risks unknown. (general medical guidance)

Sitagliptin: Single-arm phase II (n=22), no control group; wide CIs (ORR 17–59%, OS 7.8 months–not reached); immature OS data (median follow-up 9.9 months < median OS 12.5 months).

Safety: Weekly insulin—rare prolonged hypoglycemia; Implant—electrolyte depletion risk; Sitagliptin—no added toxicity with chemo in trial, but. (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

FDA review of Kyinsu (weekly insulin + semaglutide combo) for US market—could redefine T2D intensification. (general medical guidance)

Real-world weekly insulin data: Adherence, persistence, hypoglycemia patterns, cost-effectiveness in diverse populations (especially APAC launch markets). (general medical guidance)

Implant large-animal studies (pig/sheep) results: Glucose clearance capacity, safety, device durability. (general medical guidance)

SITGEM-CARE follow-up: Randomized phase III design; sitagliptin + FOLFIRINOX exploration; biomarker development (RRM1/2 expression for patient selection).

Biosimilar weekly insulins: Development pipeline as patents expire; could dramatically lower global access barriers. (general medical guidance)
