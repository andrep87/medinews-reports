New FDA Approvals Expand Options for HER2-Positive and Hormone Receptor-Positive Metastatic Breast Cancer

"The FDA has approved several new drug combinations that extend the time patients live without their cancer worsening. These advances offer more choices for people with HER2-positive disease and for those with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative tumors that have developed specific mutations."

The FDA approved tucatinib (Tukysa) plus trastuzumab (Herceptin) and pertuzumab (Perjeta) as maintenance therapy for adults with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer whose disease had not progressed after initial chemotherapy. In the HER2CLIMB-05 trial, this triplet reduced the risk of progression or death by 36% (median progression-free survival 24.9 vs 16.3 months) [progression-free survival = time until the cancer grows].
Camizestrant (Etcamah), a new oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) [a pill that blocks and breaks down the estrogen receptor that fuels many breast cancers], received accelerated approval with a CDK4/6 inhibitor for ESR1-mutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. The SERENA-6 trial showed median progression-free survival of 16 months vs 9.2 months with standard aromatase inhibitor plus CDK4/6 inhibitor.
Inavolisib (Itovebi) plus palbociclib (Ibrance) and fulvestrant (Faslodex) was approved for PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer after recurrence on adjuvant endocrine therapy (INAVO120 trial).
Gedatolisib (Revtorpyk), an IV PI3K/mTOR pathway inhibitor, was approved for PIK3CA wild-type (non-mutated) HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer after progression on endocrine therapy (VIKTORIA-1 trial).
These approvals give doctors more tools to match treatment to a tumor's specific biology rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach. Treatment can now be targeted to HER2 status, ESR1 mutations, or PIK3CA status.
For HER2-positive disease, adding tucatinib to standard dual HER2 blockade extends the time before cancer grows, and the trial included patients with brain metastases, who were historically often excluded.
For hormone receptor-positive disease, camizestrant and inavolisib offer new options after resistance to standard CDK4/6 inhibitor plus aromatase inhibitor therapy, potentially delaying the need for chemotherapy (general medical guidance).
The camizestrant approval is notable because it allows switching therapy at the first sign of an ESR1 mutation in the blood, before scans show progression — the first FDA approval guided by a ctDNA resistance mutation detected before imaging progression.
Adults with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer whose disease has not progressed after 4-8 cycles of a taxane plus trastuzumab and pertuzumab may be candidates for tucatinib maintenance.
Adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer whose tumors develop an ESR1 mutation on ctDNA blood testing while on a CDK4/6 inhibitor plus aromatase inhibitor may qualify for camizestrant.
Patients with PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer who relapsed during or soon after adjuvant endocrine therapy may be eligible for inavolisib triplet therapy.
Patients with PIK3CA wild-type HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer who progressed on prior endocrine therapy may be candidates for gedatolisib-based regimens.
If you have HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer on first-line treatment, you may wish to ask your oncologist whether tucatinib maintenance is appropriate after you complete initial chemotherapy (general medical guidance).
If you have HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer on a CDK4/6 inhibitor plus aromatase inhibitor, you may wish to ask whether periodic ctDNA testing for ESR1 mutations is appropriate to detect resistance early, as studied in SERENA-6 (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask whether your tumor should be tested for PIK3CA mutations (tissue or blood) at diagnosis of metastatic disease, since this determines eligibility for inavolisib or alpelisib (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask your care team whether any clinical trials of newer agents are appropriate for you if standard options are exhausted (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • These drugs extend progression-free survival by months, not years, and are not cures. The gains over prior standards were about 8.6 months (tucatinib: 24.9 vs 16.3) and about 6.8 months (camizestrant: 16 vs 9.2).
  • Side effects require active management: tucatinib can cause diarrhea and liver-enzyme elevations; camizestrant requires heart-rhythm monitoring; inavolisib can cause mouth sores and rash; gedatolisib is IV and can raise blood sugar (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Cost and access can be barriers: oral targeted agents are expensive, and insurance prior authorizations can delay treatment; financial-assistance programs exist but require navigation (general medical guidance).
  • Resistance eventually develops. Research is exploring using these agents earlier or combining them with other drug classes to deepen and prolong responses (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Tucatinib (Tukysa, Seagen/Pfizer): an oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting HER2; approved in 2026 in a triplet maintenance regimen based on HER2CLIMB-05 (NCT05132582). (independent fact-check: corrected from the draft's 'Seagen/Merck'; Seagen is now part of Pfizer).

Camizestrant (Etcamah, AstraZeneca): an oral SERD; accelerated approval in 2026 based on SERENA-6 (NCT04964934) for ESR1-mutated disease, used with a CDK4/6 inhibitor; the Guardant360 CDx assay is the companion diagnostic (general medical guidance).

Inavolisib (Itovebi, Genentech/Roche): an oral PI3K-alpha inhibitor; approved in 2024 in a triplet with palbociclib and fulvestrant for PIK3CA-mutated disease (INAVO120, NCT04191499) (general medical guidance).

Gedatolisib (Revtorpyk, Celcuity): an IV pan-PI3K/mTOR inhibitor; approved in July 2026 for PIK3CA wild-type disease after endocrine-therapy progression (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

HER2CLIMB-05: 654 patients, double-blind, placebo-controlled; hazard ratio 0.64 for progression-free survival (95% CI 0.51-0.80, p<0.0001); overall survival data immature. Tucatinib carries warnings including liver toxicity; common side effects include diarrhea.

SERENA-6: 315 patients; median progression-free survival 16 vs 9.2 months; required a ctDNA assay (Guardant360 CDx) to detect the ESR1 mutation. The prescribing information includes a boxed warning for arrhythmia and warnings for bradycardia and embryo-fetal toxicity (independent fact-check: the draft listed 'QT prolongation'; the actual boxed warning is for arrhythmia).

INAVO120: the control arm did not include other PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibitors, so the relative benefit versus alpelisib or capivasertib is not directly known (general medical guidance).

Long-term overall survival data are still pending for these newer regimens, and the best sequence of therapies after progression on them is not yet defined (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Overall survival results from HER2CLIMB-05 and SERENA-6 as the data mature.

Trials of newer PI3K-pathway inhibitors designed to reduce high-blood-sugar side effects, and of other targeted combinations in resistant disease (general medical guidance).

Guideline updates incorporating camizestrant, inavolisib, and gedatolisib into treatment algorithms for specific biomarker subgroups (general medical guidance).

First-line HER2-positive metastatic trials of antibody-drug conjugates (such as trastuzumab deruxtecan), which may reshape frontline therapy (general medical guidance).
