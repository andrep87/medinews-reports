Two New First-Line Regimens Transform EGFR Exon 20 Insertion NSCLC: Amivantamab and Zipalertinib Combinations

"EGFR exon 20 insertion NSCLC [a historically hard-to-treat subtype resistant to older EGFR TKIs] now has two phase III-validated first-line regimens: amivantamab + chemotherapy (PAPILLON) and zipalertinib + chemotherapy (REZILIENT3). Both significantly improve progression-free survival, with amivantamab showing the longest reported median overall survival (34.3 months) despite crossover, and zipalertinib delivering a 6-month PFS advantage (14.5 vs 8.5 months)."

PAPILLON: 308 patients randomized to amivantamab [Rybrevant, a bispecific EGFR×MET antibody] + carboplatin/pemetrexed vs chemo alone. Median OS 34.3 vs 27.9 months (HR 0.87, p=0.307); 76% of chemo-arm patients crossed over to amivantamab at progression. Crossover-adjusted analysis showed 43% death risk reduction (HR 0.57). PFS2 [progression-free survival through second progression] was 28.3 vs 17.5 months (HR 0.59).
REZILIENT3: 279 patients randomized to zipalertinib [oral, next-gen EGFR TKI selective for exon 20 insertions] 100 mg twice daily + platinum/pemetrexed vs chemo alone. Median PFS 14.5 vs 8.5 months (HR 0.50, p=0.00015), consistent across subgroups including brain metastases (HR 0.38). ORR 65% vs 40.3%. Interim OS HR 0.72 (30% maturity).
Both regimens approximately double the historical median OS of 16–24 months for this subtype.
EGFR exon 20 insertions account for ~12% of EGFR mutations and were historically treated with chemotherapy alone (median OS 16–24 months, 5-year OS ~8%).
Amivantamab's bispecific design targets both EGFR and MET [a bypass resistance pathway], while zipalertinib is a mutation-selective oral TKI sparing wild-type EGFR [reducing classic TKI toxicities like diarrhea].
PAPILLON's crossover-adjusted OS benefit and strong PFS2 suggest durable disease control; REZILIENT3's PFS benefit was statistically significant and clinically meaningful at interim analysis.
Neither trial has shown statistically significant OS benefit in the primary ITT analysis yet, but both support a new standard of care over chemotherapy alone.
Patients with advanced/metastatic non-squamous NSCLC harboring EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations [identified by NGS or PCR-based testing].
Treatment-naive for advanced disease (prior adjuvant therapy allowed if completed >6–12 months before).
ECOG 0–1; stable/treated brain metastases allowed in both trials (REZILIENT3 allowed untreated asymptomatic ≤2 cm).
No prior EGFR TKI or EGFR/MET-directed therapy.
Ensure your tumor has undergone comprehensive NGS testing [not just EGFR hotspot testing] to detect exon 20 insertions specifically, as they require different treatment than classical EGFR mutations.
Ask your oncologist: "Do I have an EGFR exon 20 insertion mutation? If so, should I receive amivantamab + chemotherapy or zipalertinib + chemotherapy as first-line treatment?"
Discuss tolerability: amivantamab requires IV infusions (risk of infusion reactions, rash, paronychia [nail inflammation], neutropenia); zipalertinib is oral but has higher grade 3+ hematologic toxicity (87% vs 54% with chemo alone).
If choosing amivantamab, ask about the subcutaneous formulation and prophylactic strategies to reduce infusion reactions and rash (general medical guidance).
Inquire about clinical trials comparing these regimens head-to-head or sequencing strategies after progression.

The Hope

  • Hope: Median OS approaching 3 years (vs historical 1.5–2 years); some patients on treatment for years with durable control.
  • Reality: Amivantamab IV requires frequent infusions (weekly then every 3 weeks), infusion reactions (44% any grade), rash (58%), paronychia (60%); zipalertinib oral but high hematologic toxicity (neutropenia, anemia) especially first 4 cycles.

The Reality

  • Cost: Both are novel targeted agents with high price tags; insurance coverage and prior authorization may delay start.
  • Neither regimen is a cure; resistance develops, and subsequent therapy options are evolving (other EGFR TKIs, ADCs, chemotherapy).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Amivantamab (Rybrevant, J&J): IV bispecific EGFR×MET antibody + chemo; FDA-approved March 2024 for first-line exon 20 insertion NSCLC; subcutaneous version also available (general medical guidance).

Zipalertinib (CLN-081/TAS6417, Taiho/Cullinan): Oral irreversible EGFR TKI [a targeted drug that blocks EGFR signaling] selective for exon 20 insertions; investigational; regulatory filing planned based on REZILIENT3 PFS data.

Sunvozertinib (Zegfrovy, Dizal/AstraZeneca): Another oral EGFR exon 20 TKI with phase III data (not detailed in corpus).

Future: Head-to-head comparisons, sequencing studies, combination with immunotherapy or ADCs, earlier-stage trials (adjuvant/neoadjuvant).

Evidence Uncertainty

PAPILLON: Primary PFS met (HR 0.40); final OS ITT not significant due to 76% crossover; IPCW-adjusted HR 0.57 is hypothesis-generating (non-randomized comparison). Safety predates SC formulation and prophylactic strategies.

REZILIENT3: Primary PFS met at interim (HR 0.50); OS immature (30% maturity, HR 0.72); higher grade 3+ AEs (87% vs 54%) mainly hematologic; no new safety signals.

No head-to-head trial between amivantamab and zipalertinib regimens; choice currently based on toxicity preference, access, CNS efficacy, and patient factors.

Both trials enrolled predominantly in Asia-Pacific regions; generalizability to Western populations needs confirmation (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

PAPILLON final OS with longer follow-up and additional crossover-adjustment models.

REZILIENT3 final OS analysis (ongoing) to see if PFS benefit translates to OS.

Head-to-head trials or real-world comparative effectiveness studies between amivantamab and zipalertinib regimens.

Adjuvant/neoadjuvant trials for exon 20 insertion NSCLC (e.g., REZILIENT early-stage study mentioned by Jay Lee).

CNS efficacy comparisons: zipalertinib showed activity in brain metastases (HR 0.38 for PFS in brain mets subgroup); amivantamab's CNS data are less mature.
