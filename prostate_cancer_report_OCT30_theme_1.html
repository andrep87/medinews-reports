Darolutamide Plus ADT and Radiation Redefines Curative-Intent Treatment for Very High-Risk Localized Prostate Cancer

"Adding darolutamide (Nubeqa) to standard hormone therapy and radiation cut the risk of cancer spreading or death by 58% in men with very high-risk localized prostate cancer. At four years, 95% of men on darolutamide remained metastasis-free versus 88% on standard treatment alone, establishing a new treatment paradigm for patients undergoing curative-intent radiation."

The Phase III DASL-HiCaP trial enrolled 1,106 men with very high-risk localized prostate cancer or early recurrence after surgery across six countries.
All men received about 2 years (96 weeks) of androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [treatment that lowers testosterone, the hormone that fuels prostate cancer] plus radiation to the prostate (or the area where it was removed) and the pelvic lymph nodes; half also received darolutamide and half a placebo.
At a planned interim analysis after 3.8 years median follow-up, darolutamide reduced the risk of metastasis or death by 58% (hazard ratio 0.42; 95% CI 0.27–0.66; P=0.0001).
Four-year metastasis-free survival was 95% with darolutamide versus 88% with placebo; PSA progression-free survival was 91% versus 83%, and freedom from castration-resistant prostate cancer was 99% versus 95%.
This is the first large Phase III trial to show a clear benefit from adding a newer androgen receptor inhibitor [a pill that blocks the testosterone signal prostate cancer depends on] (darolutamide) to standard hormone therapy and radiation in very high-risk localized disease.
Previous trials (STAMPEDE with abiraterone, ENZARAD with enzalutamide) had conflicting results; DASL-HiCaP helps resolve this by showing benefit in patients with the highest-risk features (Gleason 9–10, pelvic nodal involvement).
The benefit was consistent across subgroups—whether men received primary or salvage radiation, had pelvic node involvement or not, and regardless of baseline PSMA PET use.
Adding darolutamide did not introduce new types of side effects; the most common were hot flashes (66% vs 64%) and fatigue (64% vs 57%), with similar rates of serious adverse events.
Men with newly diagnosed, very high-risk localized prostate cancer planned for curative-intent radiation therapy (external beam radiation to prostate and pelvic nodes).
Men with biochemical recurrence (PSA rising ≥0.1 ng/mL within 12 months) after radical prostatectomy who are planned for salvage radiation to the prostate bed and pelvic nodes.
High-risk features include: Gleason score 9–10 (Grade Group 5), or Gleason 8 (Grade Group 4) with clinical stage T2b–T4, PSA >20 ng/mL, or pelvic lymph node involvement (N1).
Patients must be fit for 2 years of ADT and radiation; median age in the trial was 70–71 years.
You may wish to ask your radiation oncologist or urologist: "Does my prostate cancer meet the 'very high-risk' criteria used in DASL-HiCaP, and would adding darolutamide to my hormone therapy and radiation be appropriate?"
You may wish to ask about HRR gene testing (inherited and tumor) [tests for mutations in DNA-repair genes such as BRCA] at diagnosis — not required for darolutamide here, but it can inform other treatment options (general medical guidance).
Discuss pelvic nodal radiation with your team; it was mandatory in DASL-HiCaP and may contribute to the benefit.
Plan for side effect management: hot flashes, fatigue, and potential bone health monitoring during 2 years of ADT plus darolutamide; consider a bone density scan (DEXA) at baseline and periodically.

The Hope

  • Hope: 95% of very high-risk patients remained metastasis-free at 4 years with this intensified regimen—a meaningful improvement over 88% with standard therapy; delays progression to castration-resistant disease and subsequent hormonal therapy.
  • Reality: This is not a cure; metastatic disease will eventually develop in some patients. Overall survival benefit not yet proven. Treatment requires 2 years of daily pills plus ADT injections and 6–8 weeks of radiation.

The Reality

  • Side effects: Hot flashes, fatigue, and potential cardiovascular risks (ischemic heart disease warning) are real; 43% had Grade 3+ adverse events vs 36% on placebo.
  • Access/cost: Darolutamide is expensive; insurance coverage for this new indication (pending FDA approval) may require prior authorization and documentation of very high-risk features.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Darolutamide (Nubeqa, Bayer/Orion): oral androgen receptor inhibitor; already FDA-approved for non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (nmCRPC) and metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) with or without docetaxel; this data supports a potential new indication for very high-risk localized disease.

Abiraterone (Zytiga, Janssen): showed MFS benefit in STAMPEDE high-risk localized population; remains an option but with different side effect profile (requires prednisone, more mineralocorticoid effects).

Enzalutamide (Xtandi, Astellas/Pfizer): did not show MFS benefit in ENZARAD trial when added to ADT + radiation in a somewhat lower-risk population.

Ongoing: ARASTEP trial (NCT05794906) testing darolutamide in high-risk biochemical recurrence with positive PSMA PET/CT; longer follow-up of DASL-HiCaP for overall survival.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large Phase III trial (1,106 patients), randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter, prespecified interim analysis with strict statistical threshold (P<0.012) met; consistent benefit across prespecified subgroups.

Primary endpoint: metastasis-free survival [time until the cancer spreads to other organs or the patient dies] measured by standard CT and bone scans — a measure that has reliably tracked with longer survival in localized disease (general medical guidance).

Overall survival data immature: 4-year OS 97% vs 95% (HR 0.88; 95% CI 0.49–1.59); prostate cancer-specific survival 99% in both arms; longer follow-up needed.

Grade 3+ adverse events higher with darolutamide (43% vs 36%), driven by known ARPI toxicities; no new safety signals.

What To Watch

Final overall survival analysis of DASL-HiCaP (planned at 130 MFS events; interim at 87 events).

FDA regulatory review for darolutamide in very high-risk localized prostate cancer indication.

Biomarker analyses from DASL-HiCaP: which patients benefit most? (e.g., HRR mutations, genomic classifiers, PSMA PET findings).

ARASTEP trial results: darolutamide in biochemically recurrent disease with PSMA PET-positive nodal disease.

Long-term quality of life and testosterone recovery data from DASL-HiCaP and PROTEUS trials.
