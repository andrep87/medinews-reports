New Alzheimer’s Treatments Expand Beyond Amyloid — Targeting Psychosis, Inflammation, and Cellular Cleanup

"While anti-amyloid drugs (Leqembi, Kisunla) [drugs that clear amyloid plaques from the brain] mark historic progress, 2026 brought a wave of therapies targeting other disease drivers: psychosis [hallucinations and delusions] (remlifanserin), senescent 'zombie' cells [damaged cells that won't die and harm surrounding tissue] (senolytics), cellular cleanup machinery (TML-6), and stress hormones (XANAMEM). For patients, this means a future of combination therapy — like cancer care — where treatments attack Alzheimer's from multiple angles."

Remlifanserin (Acadia): A 5-HT2A inverse agonist [a drug that reduces signaling from a specific brain receptor] for Alzheimer's psychosis. Phase 2 showed benefit on clinician-rated severity but the primary endpoint was not met (p=0.06). Acadia modified its Phase 3 trials (RADIANT) to use the 60mg dose only and adjusted the enrollment criteria.
Senolytics [drugs that clear damaged 'zombie' cells from the brain]: A Phase 2 trial of dasatinib + quercetin [a cancer drug combined with a plant compound] in mild cognitive impairment/early Alzheimer's has completed; data analysis is underway. About 2% of brain cells show signs of senescence [cellular damage], and clearing them may reduce inflammation.
XANAMEM (Actinogen): An oral cortisol synthesis inhibitor [a drug that reduces stress hormone production specifically in the brain]. The Phase 2b/3 trial (XanaMIA) enrolled 247 patients for 36 weeks with results expected November 2026.
TML-6 (Merry Life Biomedical): An oral autophagy-lysosomal enhancer [a drug that boosts the brain's cellular cleanup machinery]. Global Phase 2 enrolling 210 patients with early Alzheimer's, measuring cognitive change and p-tau217 levels over 52 weeks.
Leqembi (lecanemab) received FDA approval for a subcutaneous starting-dose regimen [injections under the skin] in July 2026, allowing weekly self-injection at home — removing the previous requirement for 18 months of IV infusions before switching.
GAL-101 (Galimedix): Oral amyloid aggregation modulator; Phase 1 showed safety + CSF penetration (proves brain entry); Phase 2a (SAPHARY) preparing; distinguished SAB formed.
Leqembi SubQ: FDA approved at-home weekly auto-injector starting dose (July 2026) — first anti-amyloid therapy patients can start at home, removing 18-month IV prerequisite.
Anti-amyloid drugs alone slow decline by ~27–35% but don’t stop progression; ~30% of Alzheimer’s patients have psychosis (hallucinations/delusions) with no FDA-approved treatment — remlifanserin could fill this gap.
Senescent “zombie” cells accumulate with age, spew inflammatory proteins, and harbor tau tangles; clearing them in mice halts neurodegeneration — a fundamentally different mechanism from amyloid removal.
Autophagy-lysosomal system (TML-6) is the cell’s recycling machinery; its failure lets toxic proteins pile up — restoring it could complement amyloid clearance.
Safety data: A meta-analysis found no increased ARIA risk [brain swelling/bleeding] with oral blood thinners during anti-amyloid treatment — reassuring for patients with atrial fibrillation [an irregular heartbeat] who need both treatments.
Subcutaneous Leqembi starting dose is a delivery breakthrough: reduces infusion burden, enables combination regimens, and mirrors cancer care’s shift to scalable, chronic therapy.
Remlifanserin: Alzheimer’s patients with distressing hallucinations/delusions (psychosis) — ~30% of 7M+ US patients; not for agitation without psychosis.
Senolytics: Early symptomatic (MCI/mild dementia) with evidence of high senescent cell burden — future biomarkers needed to identify who benefits.
TML-6 / GAL-101 / XANAMEM: Early Alzheimer’s (MCI/mild dementia) with confirmed amyloid positivity; trials enrich for faster progressors (p-tau181/217 elevation).
Leqembi SubQ: Patients eligible for Leqembi (early Alzheimer’s, amyloid+) who prefer home dosing or live far from infusion centers; requires training/caregiver support.
PROTECT-Cog prevention: Cognitively normal adults at high risk (amyloid+, APOE ε4, family history) — tests lifestyle + GLP-1 drug combo before symptoms start.
If your loved one has Alzheimer’s with hallucinations/delusions causing fear or danger: ask their neurologist/psychiatrist about remlifanserin trial eligibility or off-label options (current antipsychotics have black-box warnings in dementia).
For early Alzheimer’s on Leqembi: ask “Am I eligible for the new at-home weekly auto-injector instead of monthly IV infusions? What training is needed?”
Track blood biomarkers during treatment: WashU study shows 34 plasma proteins shift with lecanemab — some track amyloid clearance, others track cognitive trajectory. Ask your clinic if they participate in biomarker monitoring programs.
Consider trial enrollment: XanaMIA (Actinogen) topline Nov 2026; TML-6 (Merry Life) enrolling at 20 sites; senolytics Phase 3 planning; PROTECT-Cog prevention trial launching globally.
Discuss GLP-1 medications (semaglutide, tirzepatide) with your doctor if you have diabetes/obesity + cognitive risk — PROTECT-Cog will test if they add benefit to lifestyle intervention.

The Hope

  • Hope: Multiple mechanisms in the pipeline increase the odds that at least one approach works beyond amyloid clearance. Combination therapy could yield additive benefits. Home dosing with Leqembi improves sustainability.
  • Reality: Most novel drug mechanisms fail in Alzheimer's — the historical failure rate is extremely high. Even successful drugs are likely to add months of independent function, not years.

The Reality

  • Psychosis treatment: Remlifanserin's modest effect may not justify cost and side effects if Phase 3 barely crosses statistical significance. Non-drug approaches (redirection, validation, environmental modification) remain first-line for behavioral symptoms (general medical guidance).
  • Prevention: PROTECT-Cog tests drugs in healthy people — the safety bar is extremely high for treating people who may never develop the disease (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Remlifanserin (Acadia): Phase 3 RADIANT trials enrolling for Alzheimer's psychosis. Phase 2 showed benefit on clinician assessment but missed primary endpoint (p=0.06). FDA granted Fast Track designation.

Senolytics (dasatinib + quercetin): Phase 2 completed in mild cognitive impairment/early Alzheimer's; results pending. AD-SMART (metformin-focused Phase 3) is also in planning.

XANAMEM (Actinogen): Phase 2b/3 XanaMIA trial (n=247, 36 weeks) results expected November 2026. Targets brain-specific cortisol [stress hormone] reduction.

TML-6 (Merry Life): Phase 2 enrolling 210 patients globally. Targets autophagy-lysosomal pathway [the brain's cellular waste-clearing system]. Primary endpoint: CDR-SB [a standard Alzheimer's cognitive test] at 52 weeks.

Leqembi subcutaneous: Now available for at-home weekly self-injection starting dose. May improve treatment adherence by reducing clinic visits.

PROTECT-Cog (Alzheimer's Association): A global prevention trial announced at AAIC 2026, testing combined lifestyle intervention and a GLP-1 receptor agonist [a diabetes/weight loss drug] in cognitively healthy at-risk adults.

Evidence Uncertainty

Remlifanserin: Phase 2 primary endpoint missed (p=0.06); effect size modest. Phase 3 modified accordingly, but success not guaranteed. Long-term safety in frail older adults unknown.

Senolytics: Only 48 patients in Phase 2. Dasatinib is a cancer chemotherapy drug with potential off-target effects. Quercetin has variable absorption. Whether this clears enough senescent cells to matter in humans is unknown.

XANAMEM: The cortisol-reduction hypothesis is plausible but unproven in Alzheimer's. Phase 2b/3 results (November 2026) will be the first real test.

TML-6: Phase 2 may not be large enough (n=210) to detect clinical change on cognitive testing; the study is also measuring p-tau217 as a mechanistic biomarker.

Leqembi subcutaneous: Approved based on pharmacokinetic bridging [showing blood levels match the IV version], not new efficacy data. Real-world adherence and injection-site reactions need monitoring.

What To Watch

XanaMIA topline results (November 2026) — a positive result would validate the cortisol hypothesis and enable rapid regulatory progress.

RADIANT Phase 3 remlifanserin enrollment and detailed Phase 2 data presentation.

Senolytics Phase 2 data analysis — do cleared senescent cells translate to cognitive or biomarker benefit?

TML-6 Phase 2 readout (expected 2027) — p-tau217 trajectory over 52 weeks is the key mechanistic signal.

Leqembi subcutaneous real-world data — adherence, ARIA rates, and caregiver experience versus IV infusions.

PROTECT-Cog trial site activation and enrollment progress.
