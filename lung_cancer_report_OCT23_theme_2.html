KRAS-Targeted Therapy Enters a New Era — From G12C to G12D and Rational Combinations

"KRAS G12C inhibitors are moving beyond monotherapy into chemo- and immunotherapy combinations, while the first KRAS G12D inhibitor (zoldonrasib) enters phase III testing in the first-line setting alongside standard chemoimmunotherapy."

Sotorasib (Lumakras) and adagrasib (Krazati) received accelerated FDA approval for previously treated KRAS G12C-mutated NSCLC based on ~40% response rates and ~6–7 month median PFS.
Divarasib (GDC-6036) showed statistically significant PFS and OS improvement over sotorasib or adagrasib in the Krascendo 1 phase III trial (n=338); detailed results expected at ESMO 2026.
Multiple next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitors (fulzerasib, glecirasib, garsorasib, sosimerasib, olomorasib, divarasib, opnurasib) report ORRs of 39–53% in phase I/II studies.
Combination strategies advancing: sotorasib + chemo (CodeBreaK 101/SCARLET), adagrasib + pembrolizumab (KRYSTAL-7), olomorasib + pembrolizumab (LOXO-RAS-20001), fulzerasib + cetuximab (KROCUS, ORR 80%).
Zoldonrasib (RMC-9805), a KRAS G12D-selective inhibitor, launched the global Phase III RASolve 308 trial adding it to pembrolizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy in first-line metastatic non-squamous NSCLC (about 430 patients planned; primary endpoint progression-free survival) (independent fact-check: corrected from the draft's '~600' per the trial registry).
KRAS mutations occur in ~30% of NSCLC; G12C (~14%) and G12D (~4%) are the two most common variants, together affecting ~18% of patients.
Monotherapy responses are modest (median PFS 6–7 months) and resistance emerges rapidly via new KRAS mutations, bypass pathways (MET, EGFR, FGFR, PI3K), or histologic transformation.
Combinations aim to deepen and prolong responses: chemotherapy adds cytotoxic pressure, immunotherapy may reverse immune evasion from KRAS signaling, anti-EGFR (cetuximab) blocks a key escape route.
KRAS G12D has had no targeted therapy until now; RASolve 308 tests whether adding a RAS inhibitor to standard first-line chemoimmunotherapy improves outcomes without replacing immunotherapy.
Patients with metastatic non-squamous NSCLC harboring KRAS G12C mutation (approx. 12–14% of NSCLC) who have progressed on prior chemoimmunotherapy.
Patients with KRAS G12C mutation considering first-line clinical trials combining a KRAS inhibitor with chemoimmunotherapy (SUNRAY-01: olomorasib + chemoimmunotherapy).
Patients with KRAS G12D mutation (approx. 4% of NSCLC) with previously untreated metastatic non-squamous disease eligible for RASolve 308 (NCT07777822).
Those with co-mutations (STK11, KEAP1, TP53) that may influence immunotherapy benefit and chemotherapy sensitivity.

If KRAS G12C and previously treated, you may wish to discuss sotorasib, adagrasib, or a clinical trial with your oncologist, and ask about liver-function monitoring (general medical guidance).
If KRAS G12D and treatment-naive, you may wish to ask about RASolve 308 eligibility (NCT07777822).
You may wish to ask about co-mutation testing (STK11, KEAP1, TP53), which can affect immunotherapy response (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask about liquid biopsy (ctDNA) at progression to help detect emerging resistance (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: First direct KRAS inhibitors after 40 years of "undruggable" status; divarasib may offer superior efficacy; combinations show high response rates (70–100%); G12D finally targeted.
  • Reality: Current approved drugs (sotorasib, adagrasib) extend PFS by months, not years; hepatotoxicity requires monitoring; combinations increase toxicity (liver, GI, hematologic); resistance remains inevitable.

The Reality

  • KRAS G12D population is small (~4%); RASolve 308 tests "add-on" strategy, not replacement of chemoimmunotherapy — if negative, no approved G12D drug exists.
  • Cost and access: oral KRAS inhibitors and combination regimens are expensive, and access disparities are likely (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Sotorasib (Lumakras, Amgen): oral; CodeBreaK 200 improved PFS vs docetaxel; combination studies with chemotherapy ongoing (general medical guidance).

Adagrasib (Krazati, BMS): oral; KRYSTAL-1 provided the accelerated-approval data; KRYSTAL-7 (with pembrolizumab) and KRYSTAL-12 (vs chemotherapy) are later-stage trials (general medical guidance).

Divarasib (GDC-6036, Genentech/Roche): oral; the Krascendo 1 Phase III compared it with sotorasib/adagrasib, with detailed results expected at ESMO 2026 (general medical guidance).

Zoldonrasib (RMC-9805, Revolution Medicines): a KRAS G12D-selective RAS(ON) inhibitor; RASolve 308 is its first-line Phase III with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy (general medical guidance).

Fulzerasib (IBI351, Innovent): studied with cetuximab (KROCUS), with rash as a main side effect (general medical guidance).

Other next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitors (such as glecirasib, garsorasib, olomorasib, opnurasib) report response rates in the ~40-50% range in early trials (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Monotherapy evidence: CodeBreaK 100 (sotorasib n=126, ORR 41%, mPFS 6.3 mo), KRYSTAL-1 (adagrasib n=116, ORR 43%, mPFS 6.9 mo); both accelerated approvals based on ORR, not OS.

Krascendo 1 (divarasib): Positive top-line PFS/OS vs approved agents; hazard ratios and magnitude not yet public; safety described as manageable.

Combination data mostly phase I/II: SCARLET (sotorasib+chemo 1L ORR 82–100%), KRYSTAL-7 (adagrasib+pembro 1L better in PD-L1 high), LOXO-RAS-20001 (olomorasib+pembro ORR 70% 1L), KROCUS (fulzerasib+cetuximab ORR 80%).

RASolve 308: the Phase III has just begun enrolling; the company reported encouraging preliminary activity from an early combination cohort, but specific numerical results from the randomized trial are not yet available.

Major unknowns: Whether any combination improves OS over sequential therapy; optimal sequencing after combo progression; long-term toxicity of chronic KRAS inhibition + immunotherapy.

What To Watch

ESMO 2026: Krascendo 1 full divarasib data (magnitude of PFS/OS benefit, CNS activity, safety details).

RASolve 308 enrollment and first interim PFS analysis (expected 2027–2028).

SUNRAY-01 (olomorasib + chemoimmunotherapy 1L) and KROCUS expansion (fulzerasib + cetuximab ± chemo).

CodeBreaK 200 OS maturity, KRYSTAL-12 readout, KontRASt-02/06 results.

PP2A molecular glue RPT04402 IND filing and first-in-human KRAS-mutant NSCLC trial.

Biomarker-driven sequencing: ctDNA-guided therapy switches at molecular progression vs radiographic progression.
