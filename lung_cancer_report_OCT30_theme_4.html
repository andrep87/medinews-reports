Antibody-Drug Conjugate Sacituzumab Tirumotecan Replaces Chemotherapy in First-Line PD-L1-Negative Lung Cancer

"In a landmark trial, the targeted drug sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT) combined with pembrolizumab markedly improved progression-free survival compared to standard chemo-immunotherapy for PD-L1-negative advanced lung cancer — without increasing severe side effects. This is the first phase III proof that an antibody-drug conjugate can replace platinum chemotherapy as the backbone of first-line treatment."

In patients with advanced PD-L1-negative lung cancer [a group that typically responds less well to immunotherapy alone], the antibody-drug conjugate sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT) [a 'guided missile' that carries chemotherapy directly to cancer cells] plus pembrolizumab markedly improved the time before the cancer worsened, compared with standard chemotherapy plus immunotherapy — offering a potential chemotherapy-free first option (these are the strongest Phase III data yet, not a definitive replacement of chemotherapy).
Median PFS: 15.0 months with sac-TMT vs 5.7 months with chemo (51% lower progression risk, HR 0.49, p<0.0001).
At 1 year, 51% remained progression-free on sac-TMT vs 23% on chemo.
Tumor shrinkage rates were similar (~50% both arms), but responses lasted nearly twice as long with sac-TMT (13.6 vs 6.8 months).
Early OS trend favored sac-TMT (HR 0.58, 12-month OS 78% vs 66%), but data immature (only 91 deaths total).
PD-L1-negative NSCLC (no checkpoint protein expression) cannot use pembrolizumab alone; chemo has been mandatory.
Sac-TMT is an antibody-drug conjugate: an antibody targeting TROP2 (on cancer cells) linked to a chemotherapy payload.
It delivers chemo directly to tumor cells, sparing healthy tissue — potentially more effective and less toxic.
This trial provides strong evidence an ADC can replace platinum chemo, not just add to it, reshaping first-line treatment architecture.
Advanced nonsquamous NSCLC (stage IIIB/IIIC or IV) with no prior systemic therapy.
PD-L1 tumor proportion score <1% by central 22C3 assay.
No EGFR sensitizing mutations or ALK rearrangements (tested locally or centrally).
ECOG 0–1; adequate organ function; measurable disease.
Trial conducted in China — global applicability needs confirmation in diverse populations.

Evidence Badges
1 Phase III Trial (OptiTROP-Lung06, NCT06711900) Published in New England Journal of Medicine Presented at ESMO 2026 Presidential Symposium 6 Independent Sources

The Hope

  • PFS doubling (15 vs 5.7 months) is remarkable but does not equal cure; median OS not yet reached in either arm.
  • Sac-TMT not yet approved anywhere for lung cancer; regulatory pathway depends on global confirmatory trials.
  • IV infusion every 2 weeks vs chemo every 3 weeks — similar clinic burden but different toxicity profile.

The Reality

  • Cost of ADC + immunotherapy likely high; access in lower-resource settings uncertain.
  • TROP2 expression not used for selection — all patients enrolled regardless of TROP2 level; predictive biomarker unknown.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT, Kelun-Biotech/MSD): TROP2-directed ADC with topoisomerase I inhibitor payload.

OptiTROP-Lung05 (phase III): sac-TMT + pembrolizumab vs pembrolizumab alone in PD-L1-positive NSCLC — also positive (HR 0.35).

OptiTROP-Lung04 (phase III): sac-TMT vs chemo in EGFR-mutant NSCLC post-TKI — positive PFS and OS (HR 0.60).

TroFuse-007 (phase III): sac-TMT + pembrolizumab in PD-L1-high NSCLC (results expected 2028).

Broad MSD/Kelun phase III program across gynecologic, breast, GI, GU cancers.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Randomized phase III; large PFS benefit (HR 0.49); consistent investigator-assessed PFS (HR 0.52); favorable safety (grade 3+ TEAEs 48% vs 61%); less hematologic toxicity.

Limitations: China-only population (generalizability); interim analysis (OS immature, 20% events); no sac-TMT monotherapy arm (cannot isolate ADC vs immunotherapy contribution); open-label.

Safety: Grade 3+ stomatitis 2.2%; grade 3+ neutropenia 20% vs 29% chemo; grade 3+ anemia 6% vs 20% chemo; no new safety signals.

OS not yet statistically tested — final analysis needed to confirm survival benefit.

What To Watch

Final OS analysis of OptiTROP-Lung06 (primary endpoint PFS met, OS secondary).

Global phase III trials (TroFuse program) confirming efficacy/safety in diverse populations.

OptiTROP-Lung05 final OS (PD-L1-positive setting) — may support pan-PD-L1 label.

Head-to-head vs amivantamab-based regimens or other ADCs (datopotamab deruxtecan) in biomarker-negative NSCLC.

Biomarker studies: does TROP2 expression, TROP2 splicing, or ctDNA predict benefit?
