Perioperative Apalutamide Improves Outcomes for High-Risk Localized Prostate Cancer Surgery

"The phase 3 PROTEUS trial showed that adding apalutamide to ADT for 12 months (6 months before and 6 months after radical prostatectomy) reduced the risk of metastasis or death by 20% and increased the rate of little-to-no residual cancer at surgery nearly 10-fold (8.9% vs 1.0%) in high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer. This could establish a new option for men choosing surgery."

PROTEUS randomized 2,109 men with high-risk or locally advanced localized prostate cancer to apalutamide [a next-generation androgen receptor inhibitor, a potent hormone-blocking pill] plus ADT versus placebo plus ADT for 12 months (6 months before and 6 months after radical prostatectomy).
A dual primary endpoint was met: a 20% reduction in the risk of metastasis or death (hazard ratio 0.80; 95% CI 0.67-0.96; p=0.02). Five-year metastasis-free survival was 78.2% versus 73.5%.
Pathologic complete response or minimal residual disease (little to no cancer left at surgery) occurred in 8.9% versus 1.0% (odds ratio 10.17; 95% CI 5.27-19.64).
Time to the first additional (salvage) therapy was delayed from a median of 41.5 months to 74.2 months; event-free survival and time to distant metastasis also improved significantly.
This is the first phase 3 trial to show that giving androgen receptor inhibition around the time of surgery improves cancer outcomes in high-risk localized disease.
A nearly 10-fold increase in little-to-no residual cancer at surgery is a notably large degree of downstaging for a perioperative prostate cancer trial.
Delaying the next line of treatment by about 3 years can mean years before many men face additional treatment side effects.
It offers a surgery-based alternative to radiation plus long-term ADT for high-risk patients who prefer prostatectomy (general medical guidance).
Men with newly diagnosed high-risk or locally advanced localized prostate cancer choosing radical prostatectomy. (High-risk is generally defined by features such as high Gleason score, high PSA, or locally advanced stage) (general medical guidance).
Generally, men well enough for 12 months of systemic therapy (general medical guidance).
Not for metastatic disease or low/intermediate-risk disease.

Evidence Badges
Phase 3 Randomized Trial (PROTEUS, 2,109 patients) Published in the New England Journal of Medicine (2026); ASCO 2026 plenary FDA submission anticipated; not yet approved 1 Independent Source (plus the trial publication)

The Hope

  • Hope: potential to improve cure rates for high-risk surgical patients, with a multi-year interval before the next therapy and a large degree of pathologic downstaging.
  • Reality: it is a 12-month daily pill with a meaningful rash risk, testosterone recovery is uncertain for some men, and the absolute survival benefit so far is modest; cost of 12 months of apalutamide is substantial (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Not every high-risk patient needs this — some are cured by surgery alone — and genomic classifiers may eventually help identify who benefits most (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Apalutamide (Erleada): a next-generation androgen receptor inhibitor taken once daily; already used in other prostate cancer settings (general medical guidance).

ADT backbone: GnRH agonists or antagonists are standard; agents differ in how quickly testosterone recovers after stopping (general medical guidance).

Other biomarker-directed combinations (such as PARP-inhibitor or AKT-inhibitor regimens) are approved in metastatic disease, and trials are exploring them in high-risk localized disease (general medical guidance).

Neoadjuvant/adjuvant trials are also exploring PARP inhibitors, immunotherapy, and radioligands in localized high-risk settings (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: a large multicenter trial with hard clinical endpoints (metastasis-free survival), prespecified pathologic endpoints, and consistent secondary benefits.

Limitations: overall survival data are not yet mature; the 20% relative reduction corresponds to a roughly 4.7-percentage-point absolute gain in 5-year metastasis-free survival.

Toxicity: grade 3/4 adverse events were higher with apalutamide, driven largely by rash (general medical guidance).

Testosterone recovery after treatment is an endpoint of interest; not all men recover testosterone, and recovery timing varies (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Any FDA decision and label for perioperative apalutamide, which is not yet approved in this setting (independent fact-check: the draft's "FDA approval imminent" was not supported by sources).

Longer-term overall survival and quality-of-life data from PROTEUS (general medical guidance).

Trials combining apalutamide with other agents in neoadjuvant/adjuvant settings (general medical guidance).

Whether genomic classifiers can identify patients who need only surgery, or only ADT plus apalutamide (general medical guidance).

Real-world adoption, including insurance and formulary coverage and patient acceptance of a 12-month perioperative course (general medical guidance).
