New Strategies Aim to Overcome Immunotherapy Resistance

"Some lung cancers stop responding to immunotherapy, but new drugs and approaches are being tested to reverse this resistance. One trial combines a drug targeting immune cells called macrophages with standard immunotherapy, while other research explores how cancer hides from the immune system and how to make it visible again. These approaches are early-stage."

A new phase 1/2 trial called BLAZE began enrolling patients to test bexmarilimab combined with nivolumab, aiming to reverse immunotherapy resistance in metastatic lung cancer and melanoma.
Researchers are presenting phase 2 data on imneskibart combined with avelumab for lung cancers that no longer respond to checkpoint inhibitors [a type of immunotherapy that releases the brakes on immune cells] at the ESMO 2026 congress.
A review article describes how lung tumors can change their characteristics (a process called epithelial-mesenchymal transition) to escape immune detection and resist treatment.
A small study is testing psilocybin-assisted therapy to help lung cancer patients cope with existential distress and depression.

The Hope

  • Hope: reversing immunotherapy resistance could reopen effective treatment for patients who have run out of standard options, and several distinct strategies (macrophage-targeting, IL-2 redirection, and biomarker-guided combinations) are now being tested (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: all of these approaches are early-stage. BLAZE is just beginning, the imneskibart data are not yet presented, and the immune-escape work is mechanism research — none has yet shown it extends survival in people (general medical guidance).
  • Access is limited: these options are mainly available through clinical trials at specialized centers, which can involve travel and strict eligibility criteria (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Supportive care matters alongside treatment: approaches like psilocybin-assisted therapy for existential distress remain investigational and are offered only in controlled research settings, not as standard care (general medical guidance).
  • Not a cure: even if these strategies work, they are being studied to extend disease control, not to eliminate advanced lung cancer (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Bexmarilimab targets a protein called Clever-1 on macrophages to try to reduce immune suppression; it is being studied in the BLAZE phase 1/2 trial.

Nivolumab is a standard anti-PD-1 immunotherapy used in lung cancer (general medical guidance).

Imneskibart is designed to direct IL-2 (an immune-signaling molecule) toward activating immune cells; phase 2 data with avelumab are being presented at ESMO 2026.

Psilocybin is a psychedelic compound given in a controlled setting with therapy support, not a take-home medication; it is being studied for cancer-related distress.

Evidence Uncertainty

BLAZE is an early-phase (phase 1/2) trial just reaching first patients, so it is gathering safety and preliminary signals rather than proof of benefit.

The imneskibart phase 2 data are being presented at ESMO 2026 and have not yet established a new standard of care.

The review article summarizes mechanisms of immune escape (such as epithelial-mesenchymal transition); this is background science, not evidence from a treatment trial (independent fact-check: the full source text could not be independently verified, but the underlying mechanism is well established in the literature) (general medical guidance).

The psilocybin study is small and early, so its findings should be considered preliminary.
