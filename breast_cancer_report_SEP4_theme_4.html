Estrogen Receptor Expression Is a Spectrum, Not a Binary Switch—Refining Treatment for "ER-Positive" Disease

"A landmark Swedish study of 75,211 women reveals that estrogen receptor (ER) expression behaves as a continuous spectrum, not a simple positive/negative category. Tumors with low-to-moderate ER levels (10–59%) behave more like ER-negative disease and derive greater benefit from adding chemotherapy to endocrine therapy, challenging the one-size-fits-all approach to "ER-positive" breast cancer."

Researchers studied 75,211 Swedish women with HER2-negative breast cancer (diagnosed 2007–2023). They split tumors into five groups by ER percentage: 0%, 1–9%, 10–29% (called ER-mild), 30–59% (ER-moderate), and 60–100% (ER-strong).
ER-mild tumors (10–29% ER) had survival and molecular features very similar to ER-low (1–9%) and ER-zero tumors. Both did significantly worse than ER-strong tumors (≥60% ER).
For both ER-mild and ER-moderate tumors, adding chemotherapy to endocrine (hormone) therapy improved survival compared to endocrine therapy alone.
Molecular testing of about 4,800 tumors confirmed clear biological differences across the ER range, supporting the idea that ER is a continuum, not a simple on/off switch.
Current guidelines call any tumor with ≥1% ER (or ≥10% in some countries) "ER-positive." This groups biologically different tumors together and may lead to undertreatment of low/moderate ER cancers. (general medical guidance)
Patients with ER 10–59% tumors might miss out on chemotherapy benefit because they are labeled "ER-positive" and often given only endocrine therapy. (general medical guidance)
Seeing ER as a spectrum allows more precise prognosis talks and personalized treatment—chemotherapy benefit varies across "ER-positive" disease.
This challenges the long-standing binary (positive/negative) ER classification used to guide breast cancer treatment.

Evidence Uncertainty

Limitations: Observational (not randomized); treatment not randomly assigned; confounding by indication possible (sicker patients may have received more chemo).

The 10% and 60% cutoffs are analytical thresholds, not biologically proven boundaries; the underlying relationship appears continuous. (general medical guidance)

Sweden uses a 10% ER positivity threshold versus 1% internationally; findings may not directly apply to populations using different cutoffs. (general medical guidance)
