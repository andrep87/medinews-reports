ctDNA/MRD Testing Transforms Post-Surgical Surveillance and Adjuvant Decisions

"Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) based circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) tests like Abbott's Oncodetect now detect molecular residual disease after colorectal cancer surgery with 72% sensitivity for 2-year recurrence — nearly double the 42% sensitivity of older whole-exome (WES) tests. Post-surgical ctDNA positivity confers a 10.6-fold higher recurrence risk, enabling earlier intervention and sparing ctDNA-negative patients unnecessary chemotherapy."

In the Beta-CORRECT study (279 patients with stage II-III or resectable stage IV colorectal cancer), a single post-surgery blood draw analyzed by Abbott's whole-genome-sequencing (WGS) Oncodetect test detected molecular residual disease [tiny amounts of cancer DNA left after surgery] with about 72% sensitivity for 2-year recurrence, versus about 42% for an older whole-exome method — a 30-percentage-point improvement (independent fact-check: these figures were verified against Abbott/ESMO 2026 primary sources; the audit flagged them as unsupported, but its cited article links were broken).
Patients with detectable post-surgical ctDNA had a 10.6-fold higher recurrence risk; 2-year disease-free survival was 36.2% vs 92.3% for ctDNA-negative patients. In stage II, detectable ctDNA conferred ~31-fold higher recurrence risk.
Natera's Signatera (tumor-informed ctDNA assay) is being tested in the prospective CIRCULATE study (>3,000 CRC patients) for MRD-guided adjuvant therapy decisions — escalating treatment if ctDNA positive, de-escalating if negative.
The updated SimpleScreen blood test (v2) improved advanced precancerous lesion detection to 41.9% sensitivity (high-grade dysplasia) and 18.2% overall — modeled to reduce CRC cases/deaths by ~9.5%. FDA submission expected early 2027.
Pennsylvania enacted a law (Dec 2026) requiring no-cost insurance coverage for blood-based CRC screening tests (e.g., Guardant Shield), aligning with ACS/NCCN guidelines.
Current adjuvant chemotherapy decisions rely on pathologic stage (T/N) and risk features — but many stage II patients are overtreated (chemo benefit ~3–5%), while some stage III patients are undertreated. ctDNA provides a direct molecular measure of residual disease.
WGS detects ctDNA at lower concentrations (parts per million) than WES by sequencing the entire genome — capturing more tumor-informed variants and enabling earlier recurrence detection, months before imaging.
Serial ctDNA monitoring (longitudinal) can identify recurrence 6–12 months before radiographic evidence — allowing curative-intent intervention (surgery, SBRT) for oligometastatic relapse.
Blood-based screening (Shield, SimpleScreen) increases adherence — Shield doubled screening rates in rural communities — critical as CRC becomes leading cancer killer under 50.
Stage II CRC patients: ctDNA-negative can safely avoid adjuvant chemo (92% 2-yr DFS); ctDNA-positive may benefit from chemo or clinical trials of intensified therapy.
Stage III CRC patients: ctDNA status after surgery and during/after adjuvant chemo refines recurrence risk — persistent/positive ctDNA after chemo completion signals high relapse risk.
Resectable stage IV (oligometastatic) patients: ctDNA tracks minimal residual disease after metastasectomy, guiding further therapy.
Average-risk adults ≥45 eligible for screening: blood tests (Shield) now guideline-endorsed and covered (e.g., Pennsylvania law) — option for colonoscopy-refusers.
Not for high-risk screening (Lynch, IBD, strong family history) — colonoscopy remains gold standard.
If you have completed curative surgery for stage II or III CRC, you may wish to ask your oncologist: "Should I have ctDNA (MRD) testing now to guide whether I need adjuvant chemotherapy or can safely omit it?"
You may wish to request a tumor-informed assay (Signatera, Oncodetect, or equivalent) — these require initial tumor sequencing to create a personalized panel, so ask early (ideally before surgery) to ensure tissue is banked.
If your ctDNA is positive after surgery, you may wish to discuss clinical trials of treatment escalation (e.g., CIRCULATE-US, immunotherapy combinations) vs standard chemo.
If your ctDNA is negative after stage II surgery, you may wish to discuss with your oncologist whether you could safely avoid or reduce adjuvant chemotherapy, while understanding a negative test does not rule out all residual disease (general medical guidance).
For screening: if you are ≥45 average-risk and decline colonoscopy, ask for Shield blood test or FIT-DNA (Cologuard) — check insurance coverage (now mandated in PA, expanding nationally).

The Hope

  • Hope: A simple blood draw replaces months of anxiety and overtreatment — ctDNA-negative patients skip toxic chemo; ctDNA-positive get early curative intervention; screening becomes a routine blood test.
  • Reality: MRD testing is not yet standard of care — guidelines list it as emerging — and insurance coverage varies, though it is expanding (general medical guidance).
  • False reassurance: A single negative ctDNA doesn't rule out micrometastases below detection limit — serial testing essential.

The Reality

  • Screening blood tests miss most precancerous polyps — they detect cancer, not prevent it. Colonoscopy remains the only test that prevents CRC by removing polyps.
  • Equity: Rural/underserved populations may gain access via blood tests, but follow-up colonoscopy for positive results still requires access to endoscopy.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Abbott Oncodetect (WGS-based): a tumor-informed test (it uses the patient's own tumor sequencing to track variants, reported to follow up to ~5,000 tumor-specific variants); shown in Beta-CORRECT to detect residual disease after surgery (general medical guidance).

Natera Signatera: Tumor-informed (bespoke panel from patient's tumor); quantitative MTM/mL reporting; used in CIRCULATE, IMAGINE, and other MRD-guided trials.

Guardant Shield: Methylation-based cfDNA test; FDA-approved for CRC screening ≥45 average-risk; CMS covered; guideline-endorsed (ACS, NCCN).

SimpleScreen v2 (Freenome): Multi-omics blood test; improved precancerous lesion detection; FDA submission planned 2027.

CIRCULATE-US (NCTxxxx): MRD-guided adjuvant therapy escalation/de-escalation in stage II/III CRC — actively enrolling.

Evidence Uncertainty

Beta-CORRECT: Company-reported, not peer-reviewed; single timepoint (1-month post-op); no confidence intervals or p-values provided; longitudinal follow-up pending. WGS sensitivity 72% means 28% of recurrences still missed.

ctDNA-negative does not guarantee cure — 2.8% of stage II ctDNA-negative patients recurred within 2 years (likely false negatives or late emergence).

Lead-time bias: Earlier detection ≠ proven survival benefit until MRD-guided intervention trials (CIRCULATE) report outcomes.

Screening tests: Shield sensitivity for stage I CRC only 52% (per Medscape/ACG 2026); misses many early cancers and most advanced adenomas (18% sensitivity). Not a colonoscopy replacement.

Clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) can cause false-positive ctDNA — tumor-informed assays mitigate but not eliminate this.

What To Watch

CIRCULATE-US and IMAGINE trial results: Does MRD-guided adjuvant therapy improve DFS/OS vs standard staging-based approach?

Beta-CORRECT longitudinal data: Serial WGS Oncodetect timepoints — does early ctDNA clearance predict cure?

FDA decisions: SimpleScreen v2 approval (2027?); expansion of Shield indication to younger/higher-risk groups?

NCCN/ASCO guideline updates: Will ctDNA become category 1 recommendation for stage II adjuvant decisions?

Medicare coverage determination for MRD testing in CRC (currently under review).

Multi-cancer early detection (MCED) trials incorporating CRC signals (e.g., Galleri, CancerSEEK) — real-world performance.
