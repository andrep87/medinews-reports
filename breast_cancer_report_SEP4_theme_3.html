Antibody-Drug Conjugates Break Free from HER2 Expression Boundaries

"A new generation of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) is showing effectiveness in breast cancer subtypes previously considered ineligible for targeted therapy. EGFR/HER3-bispecific ADCs work in HER2-negative (cancer without high HER2 protein) disease, and novel HER2-bispecific agents are advancing. These drugs use surface proteins as "delivery addresses" for potent chemotherapy, expanding options regardless of traditional biomarker thresholds."

The phase III PANKU-Breast01 trial met its main goal (progression-free survival) for izalontamab brengitecan (iza-bren), an EGFR×HER3 bispecific antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), compared to doctor's choice chemotherapy in 383 patients with HR+/HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer who had prior chemotherapy.
Earlier phase I data (121 patients) showed similar response rates in HER2-zero (IHC 0) and HER2-low tumors (about 42% in both), suggesting ADC efficacy may not require HER2 expression. However, phase I data are not randomized and cannot prove this definitively.
The DESTINY-Breast05 trial showed trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is better than T-DM1 as adjuvant (after surgery) treatment for HER2-positive early breast cancer with residual disease, moving ADCs earlier in treatment.
New HER2-bispecific agents are advancing: zanidatamab (Ziihera) is a HER2-bispecific antibody (not an ADC), TQB2102 (Rolditamig Deuderuxtecan) is a HER2-bispecific ADC, and evorpacept is a CD47-targeted combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy. These are being studied in HER2-positive and HER2-low disease.
Traditional ADCs needed a target highly expressed on cancer cells (like HER2). Bispecific ADCs (targeting two proteins) and non-HER2 targets (EGFR, HER3) expand the treatable tumor population. (general medical guidance)
For HR+/HER2-negative patients who have used up endocrine and CDK4/6 inhibitor options, iza-bren offers a new way to deliver chemotherapy via EGFR/HER3 instead of HER2.
In HER2-positive disease, T-DXd moving to first-line metastatic and adjuvant settings means patients get the most potent ADC earlier, when they are healthier and more likely to benefit.
Strategies for after T-DXd progression (like evorpacept plus trastuzumab plus chemotherapy) are being developed for the growing group of patients who progress on T-DXd.
HR+/HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer patients who have had at least one line of chemotherapy (the PANKU-Breast01 population for iza-bren).
HER2-positive early breast cancer patients with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant therapy (the DESTINY-Breast05 adjuvant T-DXd indication).
HER2-positive metastatic patients whose cancer progressed on T-DXd, who may be eligible for evorpacept or other post-T-DXd trials.
Patients with HER2-low expression who may benefit from the HER2-bispecific ADC TQB2102 currently in development.
If you have HR+/HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer and have had chemotherapy, ask your oncologist about iza-bren (BL-B01D1) clinical trial availability or expected approval timeline.
For HER2-positive early breast cancer with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, confirm whether adjuvant T-DXd (Enhertu) has been discussed as standard of care per DESTINY-Breast05.
If your HER2-positive metastatic cancer has progressed on T-DXd, ask about trials evaluating evorpacept combinations or other new post-T-DXd agents.
Request comprehensive biomarker testing (HER2 IHC/ISH, HER2-low quantification, EGFR, HER3) to understand which ADC targets your tumor expresses.

The Hope

  • ADCs are not cures; in metastatic disease they extend survival by months, not years, and resistance eventually develops. (general medical guidance)
  • Toxicity profiles differ: iza-bren shows mainly blood-related side effects (low neutrophils, anemia, low platelets); T-DXd carries lung inflammation (ILD) risk; evorpacept adds CD47-related considerations.

The Reality

  • Payload cross-resistance (e.g., topoisomerase I inhibitors used in multiple ADCs) may limit the benefit of using ADCs sequentially—this is an unanswered clinical question.
  • Access and cost: Newer ADCs are expensive; insurance coverage for off-label or post-progression use can be difficult; global access varies widely. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Iza-bren (BL-B01D1, Sichuan Biokin): EGFR×HER3 bispecific ADC with topoisomerase I inhibitor payload; phase III PANKU-Breast01 positive (topline results); phase III PANKU-Breast02 positive in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu, Daiichi Sankyo/AstraZeneca): HER2 ADC; approved for first-line metastatic use (with pertuzumab) and adjuvant residual disease (DESTINY-Breast05); HER2-low metastatic approval not explicitly documented in this week's sources.

Zanidatamab (Ziihera, Jazz/BeOne): HER2 bispecific antibody (not an ADC); phase 1b/2 data in 24 patients with HER2-positive breast cancer; no FDA approval or ongoing breast trials reported in this week's sources.

TQB2102 (Rolditamig Deuderuxtecan, SBP Group/Cipla): HER2 bispecific ADC binding ECD II and ECD IV; Breakthrough Therapy Designation in China; licensed for India, South Africa, and other emerging markets.

Evorpacept (ALX148, ALX Oncology): CD47 blocker combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in the ASPEN-09-03 trial for HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer after T-DXd progression.

Evidence Uncertainty

PANKU-Breast01: Only topline results released; no hazard ratio, median PFS, overall survival, response rate, safety details, or subgroup analyses yet. Phase I data (121 patients) showed 42% response rate, median PFS 6.9 months, similar across HER2-zero and HER2-low.

DESTINY-Breast05: 1,635 patients; 3-year invasive disease-free survival 92.4% vs 83.7% (hazard ratio 0.47); interstitial lung disease (ILD) 9.6% with 2 deaths; Japanese patients had higher ILD rate (14.9%).

ASPEN-09-03: Single-arm phase II; primary endpoint is response rate in HER2-positive and CD47-expressing subgroup; interim data expected Q3 2026.

No head-to-head comparisons between different ADC platforms exist; optimal sequencing is unknown.
