Personalized mRNA Vaccine (Intismeran) Plus Pembrolizumab Sets New Standard After Surgery for High-Risk Melanoma

"A first-of-its-kind personalized mRNA cancer vaccine, intismeran, cut the risk of melanoma returning or causing death by 42% when added to standard immunotherapy (pembrolizumab) after surgery. At two years, 82% of patients receiving the combination remained recurrence-free versus 70% with immunotherapy alone. This is the first Phase 3 proof that a vaccine built from each patient’s unique tumor mutations can improve outcomes in any solid cancer."

In the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial, 1,137 patients with surgically removed stage IIB-IV melanoma were randomly assigned (2:1) to intismeran autogene — a personalized mRNA vaccine [a shot custom-built from the genetic 'fingerprint' of each patient's own tumor, encoding up to 34 neoantigens] — plus pembrolizumab, or to placebo plus pembrolizumab (independent fact-check: figures verified against the ESMO 2026 Presidential Symposium presentation and company releases).
Each vaccine was custom-made by sequencing the patient’s tumor, identifying up to 34 unique neoantigens (cancer-specific proteins), and encoding them in mRNA to train the immune system to recognize and attack any remaining melanoma cells.
After a median follow-up of 24.6 months, the combination reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 42% (hazard ratio 0.58, p<0.001). Two-year recurrence-free survival was 82.2% vs 70.0%.
The combination also reduced the risk of distant metastasis or death by 38% (hazard ratio 0.62, p=0.002). Two-year distant metastasis-free survival was 88.5% vs 80.2%.
Until now, adding a second immunotherapy drug to pembrolizumab after surgery had repeatedly failed in Phase 3 trials. Intismeran works differently—it gives the immune system a personalized “wanted poster” of the patient’s own tumor, while pembrolizumab releases the brakes on T cells.
The benefit appeared after the first 6 months (when the vaccine had time to prime an immune response), with a 58% reduction in recurrence risk thereafter. This delayed effect matches how a therapeutic vaccine is expected to work.
Benefit was consistent across stage IIB–IIIC and stage IIIC–IV subgroups, suggesting broad applicability for high-risk resected melanoma.
This is the first Phase 3 success for any personalized cancer vaccine, validating the mRNA neoantigen platform and opening the door for similar vaccines in lung, bladder, kidney, and other cancers.
Adults with completely resected (surgically removed) stage IIB, IIC, III, or IV cutaneous melanoma who have not received any prior systemic therapy.
Patients must have adequate tumor tissue for next-generation sequencing and vaccine manufacturing (approximately 17% of screened patients lacked sufficient tissue).
Patients with ECOG performance status 0–1 (fully active or restricted in strenuous activity only).
The trial did not include uveal, mucosal, or acral melanoma subtypes, nor patients with prior systemic treatment.
If you have recently been diagnosed with stage IIB–IV melanoma and surgery is planned, ask your oncology team: “Should my tumor be sent for next-generation sequencing now so a personalized vaccine could be an option after surgery?”
Discuss with your surgeon and medical oncologist whether your treatment center participates in INTerpath-001 follow-up studies or has early-access programs for intismeran.
Request a copy of your pathology report and confirm that tumor tissue is being preserved (frozen or formalin-fixed) for potential future vaccine manufacturing.
Ask your doctor: “Based on my stage and risk factors, what is my estimated recurrence risk with pembrolizumab alone, and how might intismeran change that?” — this frames a shared decision-making conversation for when the vaccine gains approval.

The Hope

  • Hope: 12.2% absolute improvement in 2-year recurrence-free survival (82% vs 70%) is clinically meaningful. For patients, more time melanoma-free translates to fewer surgeries, less anxiety, and potentially longer life. The vaccine’s side effects (fever, injection-site pain, fatigue) are mostly low-grade and transient.

The Reality

  • Reality: OS benefit not yet proven. Vaccine manufacturing fails for some patients (insufficient tissue, though 99% success once manufacturing starts). Turnaround time means pembrolizumab starts alone for ~2 months. Cost will be high; insurance coverage and global equity are unresolved. Not yet FDA/EMA approved—regulatory review may take 1–2 years. Benefit in non-cutaneous melanomas (uveal, mucosal) unknown.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Intismeran autogene (mRNA-4157/V940) – Personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccine (Moderna/Merck). Administered intramuscularly 1 mg every 3 weeks for 9 doses, starting ~9 weeks after pembrolizumab initiation. Manufacturing takes 6–8 weeks.

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda) – Anti–PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor given IV 400 mg every 6 weeks for 9 cycles (1 year). Standard adjuvant therapy for resected stage IIB–IV melanoma.

KEYNOTE-942 (the earlier Phase 2b study) supported this approach, reporting a substantial reduction in recurrence or death with the vaccine plus pembrolizumab (general medical guidance).

The individualized neoantigen therapy platform is being tested across several other tumor types in additional trials (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large (1,137 patients), randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial. Primary endpoint (RFS) met with high statistical significance (p<0.001). Consistent benefit across prespecified subgroups. Safety profile manageable—grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events 14.2% vs 13.4% (combo vs pembro alone); no new safety signals.

Limitations: Overall survival (OS) data immature (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.45–1.31, p=0.33). Longer follow-up needed to confirm survival benefit. 17% of screened patients excluded due to inadequate tumor tissue. Manufacturing complexity (6–8 weeks) delays vaccine start. Cost and global access unknown. No head-to-head comparison with neoadjuvant ipilimumab + nivolumab (NADINA regimen) for macroscopic stage III/IV disease.

What To Watch

Longer follow-up from INTerpath-001 for melanoma-specific and overall survival, which the investigators note is still needed (general medical guidance).

Regulatory: FDA and EMA submission timelines; potential approval as early as 2027 per expert commentary.

Publication: Full peer-reviewed manuscript in The New England Journal of Medicine (target mid-November 2026).

Long-term follow-up: OS maturity, durability of RFS/DMFS benefit at 3–5 years, late immune-related toxicities.

Positioning trials: Studies comparing adjuvant intismeran + pembro vs neoadjuvant ipilimumab + nivolumab (NADINA-like regimens) for resectable stage III/IV disease.
