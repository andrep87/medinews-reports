Novel Resistance Mechanisms and Next-Generation Therapies for Advanced Prostate Cancer

"Researchers are uncovering why prostate cancer becomes resistant to hormone therapy — including tRNA-driven lineage plasticity [cancer cells changing identity to escape treatment], NCOA6/EGFR pathway activation, steroid-synthesis bypasses involving CYP51, and PKA signalling problems. These are laboratory discoveries that point toward future drug targets; none has yet been tested in people with prostate cancer, so they are years from clinical use."

tRNA-driven lineage plasticity (Nature): Depletion of tRNA Arg (UCU)-1 acts as a molecular switch allowing prostate cancer cells to lose androgen receptor dependence and transition to neuroendocrine-like, treatment-resistant state. Restoring this tRNA or its target SMARCC2 re-sensitizes resistant cells to AR inhibitors. (general medical guidance)
NCOA6 loss + EGFR overexpression (Nature Communications, n=1,719): Loss of nuclear coactivator NCOA6 correlates with aggressive disease and high EGFR. Low NCOA6/high EGFR tumors had worse survival. Suggests EGFR inhibitors (e.g., erlotinib) may work in this molecularly defined subset.
Steroid synthesis bypass (Sharifi lab): CYP51 enzyme converts oxysterols directly to androgens, bypassing CYP17 (target of abiraterone) and CYP11. HSD3B1 germline variants ("fast synthesizers") linked to faster progression and mortality. (general medical guidance)
PKA holoenzyme disassembly (Nature): In high-grade tumors, regulatory subunit RIβ is lost from basal layer while catalytic PKACβ redistributes to tumor cells, driving pathway activation independent of cAMP.
BET + DNMT inhibition (JCI Insight): Combined bromodomain and DNA methyltransferase inhibitors reversed transdifferentiation in TP53/RB1-null models, suppressing growth at low doses. (general medical guidance)
Bavdegalutamide (ARV-110): First-in-class PROTAC AR degrader. Phase 1/2 in mCRPC: 420 mg daily safe; PSA declines, soft tissue regressions, pain improvement. Greater effect in T878/H875 AR mutations.
JNJ-6143: First-in-class RIPTAC (AR-BRD4 degrader). Phase 1 in heavily pretreated mCRPC: PSA50 77%, PSA90 48%, ORR 69% (measurable disease) at 50 mg dose.
Alpha-emitter radioligands: Ac-225-PSMA-617 (PSMAction, AcTFirst phase 3), Pb-212-PSMA (phase 3), Ac-225-PSMA-I&T, Ac-225-J591 antibody. Deep PSA responses but durability unknown; dry mouth major toxicity.
Immunotherapy combo: GI-102 (CD80-IL2 fusion) + pasritamig (CD3×KLK2 T-cell engager) phase 1b/2 in mCRPC (JET trial, 9 sites US/Korea). (general medical guidance)
Resistance to ARPIs (enzalutamide, apalutamide, darolutamide) and taxanes is nearly universal in metastatic disease. Understanding mechanisms reveals new druggable targets. (general medical guidance)
tRNA modulation and BET/DNMT inhibition offer reversible epigenetic approaches — unlike DNA mutations, RNA/protein dosage changes can potentially be corrected.
NCOA6/EGFR defines a biomarker-selected population for EGFR inhibitors, which previously failed in unselected prostate cancer.
PROTACs and RIPTACs degrade the AR protein itself rather than just blocking it — potentially overcoming mutations and splice variants that cause resistance to current ARPIs.
Alpha emitters deliver higher energy over shorter distances, potentially killing resistant clones with less collateral damage — but long-term toxicity (salivary, marrow, kidney) must be defined.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Bavdegalutamide (ARV-110): PROTAC AR degrader. Phase 2 ongoing. ARV-766 (broader mutation coverage) in phase 1/2.

JNJ-6143: RIPTAC AR-BRD4 degrader. Phase 1 dose optimization complete; phase 2 planning.

Alpha radioligands: Ac-225-PSMA-617 (Novartis: PSMAction post-Lu-177, AcTFirst pre-Lu-177), Pb-212-PSMA (Persist), Ac-225-PSMA-I&T (Vector), Ac-225-J591 (Convergent). Adaptive dosing for dry mouth toxicity.

Non-PSMA targets: ACP3 (imaging/therapy trials starting), DLL3 (neuroendocrine), KLK2 (pasritamig T-cell engager), STEAP1/2. (general medical guidance) — these are investigational targets in early trials, not available as standard care (general medical guidance).

Epigenetic combos: BET inhibitors (e.g., ZEN-3694) + DNMT inhibitors (azacitidine/decitabine) — early phase.

tRNA therapeutics: Synthetic tRNA mimics or small molecules to boost tRNA Arg (UCU)-1 — preclinical. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Mechanistic studies in patient samples, organoids, and xenografts. Bavdegalutamide and JNJ-6143 show unprecedented PSA/tumor responses in heavily pretreated patients. Alpha emitters demonstrate deep PSA responses (90% decline in 91% of chemo-naive mCRPC in AcTION).

Limitations: Most mechanistic data preclinical or correlative. Bavdegalutamide/JNJ-6143 phase 1/2 — small numbers, no control arm, short follow-up. Alpha emitter durability unknown (AcTION: median 3 cycles, progression-driven discontinuation). tRNA/NCOA6 findings need prospective clinical validation. (general medical guidance)

Key uncertainty: Will these novel mechanisms translate into OS benefit? Resistance to PROTACs/RIPTACs will emerge. Alpha emitter long-term toxicity (salivary, renal, marrow) in longer-surviving patients undefined.
