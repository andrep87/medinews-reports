T-Cell Engager Immunotherapy Breaks Through in Advanced Prostate Cancer

"First-in-human data for dual-antigen T-cell engagers (pasritamig + JNJ-9401) show unprecedented 73% PSA50 and 47% PSA90 response rates in heavily pretreated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), with minimal cytokine release syndrome — launching Phase III trials and reviving immunotherapy hope for "cold" prostate tumors."

At SITC 2026, Johnson & Johnson presented Phase I data (NCT06095089) for pasritamig (KLK2×CD3) + JNJ-9401 (PSMA×CD28), the first dual-tumor-antigen bispecific T-cell engager regimen in prostate cancer. (general medical guidance)
At the recommended Phase 2 regimen (15 patients, median 4 prior lines, 25% visceral disease): 73% achieved PSA ≥50% decline (PSA50), 47% achieved PSA ≥90% decline (PSA90), and 4 patients (27%) reached undetectable PSA (<0.2 ng/mL) — all 4 remained on treatment >6 months without progression.
Safety was remarkable: no grade 3+ treatment-related AEs, no dose-limiting toxicities, no treatment discontinuations for toxicity; cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurred in only 20% (all grade 1 fever, no tocilizumab needed). (general medical guidance)
Pasritamig monotherapy (Phase I, 33 patients at RP2D) had shown 42% PSA50 and median rPFS 7.85 months with 9% grade 1 CRS; adding JNJ-9401's CD28 costimulation deepened and sustained responses without adding significant toxicity. (general medical guidance)
Prostate cancer has been notoriously resistant to checkpoint inhibitors (PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4) due to low tumor mutational burden, poor T-cell infiltration, and immunosuppressive microenvironment — T-cell engagers bypass these barriers by physically linking T cells to tumor cells. (general medical guidance)
The dual-antigen design (KLK2 + PSMA) requires co-expression on the same cell for full T-cell activation (signal 1 + signal 2), reducing on-target/off-tumor toxicity to normal tissues expressing only one antigen. (general medical guidance)
Responses are deep (PSA90 in nearly half) and durable (>6 months in all PSA90 responders) in patients who have exhausted standard therapies (chemotherapy, ARPIs, radioligand therapy) — a population with median survival of 13–31 months.
Outpatient every-6-week dosing (subcutaneous JNJ-9401 + IV pasritamig after 2 step-up doses) is feasible in community settings, unlike CAR-T or high-CRS bispecifics requiring hospitalization. (general medical guidance)
Currently: men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) who have progressed on at least one ARPI and one taxane chemotherapy (docetaxel/cabazitaxel), and often also — the Phase I population.
Future (Phase III): KLK2-comPAS Part 2 will test pasritamig ± JNJ-9401 in later-line non-visceral mCRPC; KLK2-PASenger tests pasritamig + docetaxel in earlier mCRPC; XALute tests xaluritamig (STEAP1×CD3) vs standard care in post-chemo mCRPC. (general medical guidance)
Patients must have adequate organ function (no active infection, controlled cardiac disease) and tumor expression of target antigens — KLK2 and PSMA testing not yet standardized for selection. (general medical guidance)
Visceral metastases (liver, lung) appeared to have less deep responses in Phase I; non-visceral disease is the focus for Phase III combination arm.
If you have mCRPC progressing on standard therapies, ask your oncologist: "Am I eligible for any T-cell engager clinical trial (pasritamig, xaluritamig, or similar)?" — Phase III trials are enrolling now. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to request PSMA PET imaging if not done recently: it confirms PSMA expression (required for JNJ-9401) and assesses disease distribution (visceral vs. bone/nodal). (general medical guidance)
You may wish to discuss KLK2 expression testing: not yet clinically available, but emerging as a potential biomarker for pasritamig eligibility; ask if your center has access to research assays. (general medical guidance)
Prepare for trial logistics: screening involves labs, imaging, and often a fresh biopsy; the every-6-week schedule means ~8–9 visits per year — plan for travel and time off work. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: For the first time, immunotherapy shows deep, durable responses in prostate cancer with a safety profile allowing outpatient community administration — potentially a new class of therapy for a disease where checkpoint inhibitors failed. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: These are Phase I data in 15 patients; Phase III trials will take 2–4 years to read out; many promising Phase I immunotherapies fail in larger studies. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Antigen escape is a real concern: tumors can downregulate KLK2 or PSMA under selective pressure; dual targeting helps but may not prevent all resistance. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Pasritamig (JNJ-78278343): KLK2×CD3 bispecific T-cell engager; IV every 6 weeks after 2 weekly step-up doses; Phase III KLK2-comPAS (NCT07164443) and KLK2-PASenger (NCT07225946) ongoing. (general medical guidance)

JNJ-9401 (JNJ-87189401): PSMA×CD28 costimulatory bispecific; subcutaneous every 6 weeks; no single-agent activity; enables dual-antigen targeting with pasritamig. (general medical guidance)

Xaluritamig (AMG 509, Amgen): STEAP1×CD3 bispecific; Phase I: 49% PSA50, 24% partial response, 72% CRS (mostly grade 1–2); Phase III XALute (NCT06691984) vs cabazitaxel/ARPI in post-chemo mCRPC. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Phase I pasritamig + JNJ-9401: n=15 at RP2R; 73% PSA50, 47% PSA90, 27% undetectable PSA; median rPFS not reached for non-visceral disease (7.4-month median follow-up). All grade 3+ AEs unrelated to treatment.

Phase I pasritamig monotherapy: n=33 at RP2D; 42% PSA50, median rPFS 7.85 months; 9% grade 1 CRS. (general medical guidance)

Xaluritamig Phase I: n=97; 49% PSA50, 24% RECIST partial response; 72% CRS (grade 1–2 predominant), managed with step-up dosing. (general medical guidance)

Major limitations: Very small sample sizes; no randomized comparison yet; PSA response may not correlate perfectly with clinical benefit in immunotherapy; antigen heterogeneity (KLK2 loss in visceral mets, PSMA negativity) may limit durability. (general medical guidance)

These are Phase I results in very small numbers (15 patients at the recommended regimen), with no randomised comparison — PSA response is an early signal, not proof of longer survival.

What To Watch

KLK2-comPAS Part 2 enrollment (pasritamig + JNJ-9401 vs pasritamig alone vs placebo in later-line non-visceral mCRPC; OS primary endpoint). (general medical guidance)

KLK2-PASenger readout (pasritamig + docetaxel vs docetaxel alone in mCRPC; rPFS primary). (general medical guidance)

XALute Phase III (xaluritamig vs investigator's choice in post-chemo mCRPC; OS primary, estimated completion 2029).

Biomarker development: KLK2/PSMA co-expression assays, T-cell fitness markers, cytokine profiles predicting CRS/response; liquid biopsy monitoring of antigen loss. (general medical guidance)
