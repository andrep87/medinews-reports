Novel Immunotherapy Strategies Targeting Resistance Mechanisms

"Several early-phase immunotherapy trials are targeting distinct resistance mechanisms in NSCLC — adding LAG-3 blockade to PD-1 and radiation, redirecting IL-2, reprogramming macrophages, engineering next-generation CTLA-4 agents, and neutralizing GDF-15. All are investigational, with early signals in refractory disease."

The TRIPL trial launched, combining thoracic radiotherapy with cemiplimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) and fianlimab (a LAG-3 inhibitor) for locally advanced NSCLC.
Aulos presented phase 2 data: imneskibart (an IL-2 redirector) plus low-dose IL-2 and avelumab showed activity in checkpoint-inhibitor-refractory NSCLC.
The BLAZE trial opened, testing bexmarilimab (an anti-Clever-1 antibody) followed by nivolumab to reverse macrophage-mediated immunotherapy resistance.
In PRESERVE-003, gotistobart (a next-generation CTLA-4 agent) showed an overall survival signal versus docetaxel in squamous NSCLC after checkpoint inhibitor and chemotherapy (median OS 18.5 vs 10.0 months, HR 0.56), though this was a nominal result not formally tested (audit clarification).
CatalYm is advancing visugromab (an anti-GDF-15 antibody) plus nivolumab, with or without docetaxel, in second-line non-squamous NSCLC.
Most NSCLC patients eventually develop resistance to PD-1/PD-L1 therapy; these trials target distinct resistance mechanisms — T-cell exhaustion, regulatory T-cell suppression, macrophage blockade, CTLA-4 pathways, and GDF-15.
Combining radiotherapy with dual checkpoint blockade may deepen responses in locally advanced disease (general medical guidance).
Squamous NSCLC has had fewer precision options, so gotistobart's survival signal in this histology is notable.
GDF-15 neutralization is of interest for both immunotherapy resistance and cancer-related weight loss (cachexia).

The Hope

  • Hope: Targeting several independent resistance mechanisms raises the chance that one approach helps a given patient.
  • Reality: All are early-phase, combination toxicities can be severe, and trials typically exclude sicker patients (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Hope: Gotistobart and visugromab address squamous NSCLC and cancer-related weight loss, which have had fewer options.
  • Reality: CTLA-4 agents require proactive management of immune-related side effects, and GDF-15 inhibition's effects on normal physiology are still being studied (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Cemiplimab plus fianlimab: A PD-1 inhibitor plus a LAG-3 inhibitor, tested with radiotherapy in the TRIPL trial for locally advanced NSCLC.

Imneskibart (Aulos): An IL-2 redirector designed to direct IL-2 toward effector cells rather than regulatory T cells; phase 2 with avelumab in checkpoint-refractory NSCLC.

Bexmarilimab (Faron): An anti-Clever-1 antibody intended to counter macrophage-mediated resistance; tested with nivolumab in BLAZE.

Gotistobart: A next-generation CTLA-4 agent tested against docetaxel in squamous NSCLC (PRESERVE-003).

Visugromab (CatalYm): An anti-GDF-15 antibody tested with nivolumab, with or without docetaxel, in second-line non-squamous NSCLC.

Evidence Uncertainty

TRIPL: Built on earlier single-arm radiation-plus-immunotherapy data; additive toxicity (such as pneumonitis or esophagitis) is not yet established (general medical guidance).

Imneskibart: Phase 2 data presented at a conference but not yet published; the mechanism is novel and the safety advantage is theoretical.

BLAZE: Early-phase; Clever-1 targeting is unproven in large cohorts.

Gotistobart: The squamous NSCLC overall survival signal was nominal, not formally tested; grade 3 or higher adverse events were similar to docetaxel (audit clarification).

Visugromab: Earlier-phase data showed responses in checkpoint-refractory tumors; the phase 2b trial is just beginning.
