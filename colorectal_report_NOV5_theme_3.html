Rectal Cancer: Immunotherapy and Organ Preservation Expand the Options

"Rectal cancer care is shifting. The UNION trial in China found that adding immunotherapy to a short course of radiation and chemotherapy improved 3-year event-free survival, the FDA granted accelerated approval to dostarlimab for dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer — letting some patients avoid surgery — and a first patient has been treated in a Danish study testing an oncolytic virus [a virus engineered to infect and kill tumour cells] to make 'cold' MSS rectal tumours respond to immunotherapy."

UNION trial (China, 231 patients with locally advanced rectal cancer): a short course of radiation (25 Gy in 5 sessions) plus the immunotherapy camrelizumab and CAPOX chemotherapy before surgery, followed by more chemotherapy afterwards, improved 3-year event-free survival compared with standard chemoradiotherapy.
OV-PRIME-R (Denmark): the first patient has been treated in a study combining an oncolytic virus injected into the tumour with immunotherapy, aiming to make MSS rectal tumours — which usually resist immunotherapy — responsive. This is a first-in-human step, not an available treatment.
Standard total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) still leaves ~30% recurrence risk; UNION's immunotherapy addition cut events by 67% in a mostly pMMR population (93%). (general medical guidance)
Dostarlimab approval is the first immunotherapy for rectal cancer and the first regimen where "watch and wait" after complete clinical response is a regulated pathway. (general medical guidance)
Oncolytic virus strategy addresses the core problem: most rectal cancers are MSS and immunotherapy-resistant; viral injection may create inflammation to enable checkpoint inhibitors. (general medical guidance)
Together, these move rectal cancer toward personalized, organ-preserving, immunotherapy-based curative strategies. (general medical guidance)
UNION regimen: Locally advanced rectal adenocarcinoma (cT3-T4 or N+), mostly pMMR; camrelizumab not yet approved in US/EU.
Dostarlimab: Adults with stage II/III dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer; requires biomarker testing at diagnosis. (general medical guidance)
OV-PRIME-R: Early-phase trial for localized rectal cancer; eligibility evolving; aims to expand immunotherapy to MSS majority. (general medical guidance)
If newly diagnosed with rectal cancer: Demand MMR/MSI testing on the biopsy—it determines eligibility for dostarlimab, immunotherapy trials, and non-operative management. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to ask your multidisciplinary team: "Is short-course radiation + immunotherapy an option for me? Am I a candidate for 'watch and wait' if I have a complete response?" (general medical guidance)
If MSS: Ask about referral to centers offering novel neoadjuvant immunotherapy trials (e.g., oncolytic virus, BOT/BAL, other combinations). (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: Some dMMR patients may avoid surgery, radiation, and chemo entirely—preserving bowel, bladder, and sexual function. (general medical guidance)
  • UNION regimen adds immunotherapy toxicity (immune-related AEs, 1 septic death attributed to chemo) to standard CRT side effects. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Most rectal cancers are MSS; none of these immunotherapy approaches are yet standard for them—OV-PRIME-R and BOT/BAL are early steps. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Camrelizumab: Anti-PD-1 (Hengrui); approved in China; not in US/EU; UNION used it with short-course RT + chemo.

Oncolytic virus (OV-PRIME-R): Engineered virus injected into tumor to lyse cells, release antigens, and stimulate immune response; combined with systemic immunotherapy. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

UNION: Positive interim EFS (HR 0.33), but control arm used older TNT (only 2 pre-op CAPOX cycles); OS immature (15 deaths); camrelizumab not globally available.

Dostarlimab: Accelerated approval based on surrogate endpoints (pathological response); confirmatory trials needed for full approval; long-term non-operative outcomes unknown. (general medical guidance)

OV-PRIME-R: First patient only; safety and efficacy completely unknown; long path to potential approval. (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

Dostarlimab confirmatory trials and real-world non-operative management registries (sustained complete response rates, salvage surgery outcomes). (general medical guidance)

OV-PRIME-R expansion and similar oncolytic virus/immunotherapy combinations in MSS rectal cancer. (general medical guidance)
