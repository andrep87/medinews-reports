New Frontline Options Extend Survival in Metastatic Prostate Cancer

"Several advances are reshaping treatment for metastatic prostate cancer. In hormone-sensitive disease, adding lutetium-177-PSMA-617 to standard hormone therapy (PSMAddition) reduced progression or death by 28%, leading to FDA approval in July 2026. A separate combination (radium-223 plus enzalutamide, PEACE-3) extended survival — but in castration-resistant, not hormone-sensitive, disease. Treatment is increasingly matched to tumor biology."

PSMAddition trial: Adding lutetium-177-PSMA-617 [a targeted radiation drug] to standard hormone therapy (ADT plus an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor) in 1,144 patients with PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer reduced the risk of radiographic progression or death by 28% (HR 0.72). The FDA approved this combination on July 31, 2026 (independent fact-check).
TALAPRO-3: Talazoparib [a PARP inhibitor] plus enzalutamide in metastatic hormone-sensitive disease with HRR gene alterations met its primary endpoint; the combination is under FDA priority review with a decision expected in Q4 2026.
PEACE-3: Radium-223 plus enzalutamide improved median overall survival to 38.2 vs 32.6 months (HR 0.76) — but this was in metastatic CASTRATION-RESISTANT prostate cancer with bone metastases, a different setting from the hormone-sensitive disease above. A bone-protecting agent was required (audit correction: the draft incorrectly placed this in the hormone-sensitive frontline triplet category).
A real-world study of 9,141 men found that androgen receptor pathway inhibitor plus ADT was associated with longer progression-free survival than docetaxel plus ADT, though overall survival data were not yet available.
Men with metastatic hormone-sensitive disease now have options that can be matched to tumor biology — PSMA status for radioligand therapy, HRR mutations for PARP inhibitors.
The PSMAddition approval brings radioligand therapy earlier in the disease course, before castration resistance develops (general medical guidance).
The real-world data suggest that androgen receptor pathway inhibitor doublets performed well relative to chemotherapy, allowing choice based on side effects, other health conditions, and preference (general medical guidance).
It is important to note that radium-223 plus enzalutamide (PEACE-3) applies to castration-resistant disease, not the hormone-sensitive setting.
PSMAddition: Newly diagnosed metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer with PSMA-positive disease on PET imaging.
TALAPRO-3: Metastatic hormone-sensitive disease with homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene alterations such as BRCA1/2, identified by genetic testing. FDA decision pending.
PEACE-3: Castration-resistant disease with bone metastases — not the hormone-sensitive setting. A bone-protecting agent is required (audit correction).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Lutetium-177-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto) plus standard hormone therapy: FDA-approved July 2026 for PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive disease, based on PSMAddition.

Talazoparib plus enzalutamide: Under FDA priority review for HRR-altered metastatic hormone-sensitive disease, with a decision expected Q4 2026.

Radium-223 plus enzalutamide: Studied in castration-resistant disease with bone metastases (PEACE-3), where a bone-protecting agent was required.

Androgen receptor pathway inhibitor doublets (abiraterone, apalutamide, enzalutamide plus ADT): Established options for metastatic hormone-sensitive disease (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

PSMAddition: Radiographic progression-free survival benefit (HR 0.72); overall survival data were not yet mature and were affected by control-arm patients crossing over to the drug.

TALAPRO-3: The primary endpoint was met, but full efficacy and survival data are not yet public, and the FDA decision is pending.

PEACE-3: The overall survival benefit was in castration-resistant disease, not hormone-sensitive disease. Sequencing after other PSMA-targeted therapy is not established.

The real-world study was based on insurance claims, which lack clinical detail such as PSA, Gleason score, and disease volume, and did not evaluate overall survival.
