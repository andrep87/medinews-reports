New First-Line Option for CLL: Pirtobrutinib Approved for Untreated Patients Without a 17p Deletion

"The FDA has approved pirtobrutinib (Jaypirca) as a first-line treatment for adults with previously untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) or small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) who do not have a 17p deletion. It is a non-covalent BTK inhibitor [a pill that blocks a signal leukemia cells use to grow, binding in a reversible way], giving a new oral option that can still work against mutations that make older BTK inhibitors stop working."

The FDA approved pirtobrutinib (brand name Jaypirca) for adults with previously untreated CLL or SLL who do not have a 17p deletion [a missing piece of chromosome 17 that signals higher-risk disease].
Approval was based on the phase III BRUIN CLL-313 trial, which compared pirtobrutinib with bendamustine plus rituximab (a chemoimmunotherapy combination) in 282 previously untreated patients.
At 28 months of median follow-up, median progression-free survival [the time patients lived without the disease getting worse] was not reached with pirtobrutinib versus 33.5 months with chemoimmunotherapy — an 80% lower risk of progression or death.
Pirtobrutinib is the first and only FDA-approved non-covalent (reversible) BTK inhibitor for first-line use, meaning it binds differently from older covalent BTK inhibitors such as ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, and zanubrutinib.
Patients have a new first-line option that works through reversible binding, so it can stay active against BTK C481 mutations — changes that cause resistance to older covalent BTK inhibitors.
The overall response rate was 94% with pirtobrutinib versus 81% with chemoimmunotherapy, with similar overall survival between groups at this early follow-up.
An oral, once-daily targeted pill can be an appealing alternative to intravenous chemotherapy, especially for older patients.
NCCN guidelines list pirtobrutinib as a Category 2A option for untreated CLL/SLL without a 17p deletion, particularly for older patients with heart-related conditions who may need only one line of treatment.
Adults newly diagnosed with CLL or SLL who need treatment. (Not everyone needs immediate therapy; some are monitored with "watch and wait") (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: an 80% lower risk of progression, a high response rate (94%), once-daily oral dosing, and activity against resistance mutations that limit older BTK inhibitors.
  • Reality: it is continuous daily therapy with no fixed stopping point, and like other BTK inhibitors it requires monitoring; the trial reported serious adverse reactions in 28% of patients (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Not a cure: CLL remains a chronic disease; treatment controls it but rarely clears it completely, and resistance can eventually develop (general medical guidance).
  • Not for patients with a 17p deletion: this higher-risk group was not the trial population and generally needs venetoclax-based or clinical-trial options (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Pirtobrutinib (Jaypirca, Eli Lilly): a non-covalent BTK inhibitor, taken as 200 mg by mouth once daily until the disease progresses or side effects become unacceptable; now approved for first-line CLL/SLL without a 17p deletion (Oct 2026) and, earlier, for relapsed or refractory disease after a covalent BTK inhibitor and a BCL-2 inhibitor.

Covalent BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib/Imbruvica, acalabrutinib/Calquence, zanubrutinib/Brukinsa): bind BTK irreversibly; they remain first-line standards but can lose activity against C481 mutations (general medical guidance).

Venetoclax (Venclexta) plus obinutuzumab (Gazyva): a time-limited BCL-2 inhibitor regimen used for many patients, including some with a 17p deletion (general medical guidance).

Other agents, including additional non-covalent BTK inhibitors, BTK degraders, next-generation BCL-2 inhibitors, and cell-based therapies, are in development but were not covered by this week's sources (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: a randomized phase III trial (282 patients) with progression-free survival assessed by an independent review committee, a hazard ratio of 0.20 (95% CI 0.11-0.37, p<0.0001), and a safety profile consistent with earlier pirtobrutinib studies. Serious adverse reactions occurred in 28% of pirtobrutinib patients.

Limitations: median follow-up was only 28 months, and overall survival data are immature (median not reached in either group).

The comparison arm was bendamustine plus rituximab, a chemoimmunotherapy that targeted therapies have largely replaced in current practice, which complicates how the results apply today (general medical guidance).

There are no head-to-head data versus other first-line BTK inhibitors or venetoclax-based regimens, and long-term durability and resistance patterns with first-line pirtobrutinib remain unknown (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Head-to-head trials comparing pirtobrutinib with covalent BTK inhibitors or venetoclax-based regimens in first-line CLL/SLL (general medical guidance).

Longer follow-up of BRUIN CLL-313 for overall survival and for resistance mutations at progression.

Real-world evidence on sequencing: what works best after first-line pirtobrutinib (general medical guidance).

Results from trials testing triplet combinations (a BTK inhibitor plus a BCL-2 inhibitor plus an anti-CD20 antibody) that may reshape first-line therapy (general medical guidance).
