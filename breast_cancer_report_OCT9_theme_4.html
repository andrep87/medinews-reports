Shorter, Lighter Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy Works for Many HER2+ Early Breast Cancers, Guided by Tumor Biomarkers

"The CompassHER2-pCR trial found that 43.8% of patients with stage II–IIIA HER2-positive breast cancer achieved a pathologic complete response (no invasive cancer found at surgery) after 12 weeks of taxane plus trastuzumab and pertuzumab, without anthracyclines or carboplatin. Response rates varied sharply by hormone receptor status. This was a single-arm trial with no comparison group, so it does not establish that the lighter regimen performs as well as more intensive chemotherapy."

CompassHER2-pCR enrolled 2,175 patients with stage II–IIIA HER2+ breast cancer; 2,141 started 12 weeks of THP (weekly paclitaxel + trastuzumab + pertuzumab).
43.8% achieved pathologic complete response (pCR: no invasive cancer in breast or nodes at surgery); these patients skipped further chemo, continuing HER2 therapy alone for 1 year.
pCR rate was 63.7% in ER-negative vs 32.4% in ER-positive tumors.
HER2DX genomic test (569 patients): high pCR score predicted 68% pCR vs 19% for low score (49% absolute difference); independent predictor after adjusting for clinical factors.
The trial tested whether many HER2-positive patients can avoid anthracyclines [chemotherapy associated with heart damage risk] and carboplatin. Because there was no comparison group, the trial shows what this regimen achieves but does not prove it matches more intensive chemotherapy (audit correction).
Pathologic complete response is widely used as an early marker in HER2-positive disease, but the trial's survival endpoint is still ongoing — so the long-term meaning of these response rates is not yet established.
HER2DX, a genomic test, identified patients far more likely to achieve pathologic complete response, offering a potential tool for tailoring how much chemotherapy is given before surgery.
Hormone receptor status mattered substantially: 63.7% response in ER-negative tumors versus 32.4% in ER-positive tumors.
Newly diagnosed stage II or IIIA HER2-positive (IHC 3+ or ISH+) breast cancer patients considering neoadjuvant therapy.
Particularly relevant for ER-negative/HER2+ tumors (63.7% pCR rate with THP alone).
Patients with high HER2DX scores, where testing is available, were considerably more likely to achieve a complete response in this trial.
Patients with ER+ disease, low HER2DX scores, or high clinical risk may still need more intensive regimens.
If you are newly diagnosed with HER2-positive breast cancer, you may wish to ask your oncologist what neoadjuvant [before surgery] regimen they recommend and why, given your tumor's hormone receptor status.
You may wish to ask whether HER2DX testing is available at your center and whether it would inform your treatment plan.
If you achieve a pathologic complete response at surgery, ask what the plan is for the remainder of your HER2-targeted therapy.
If you do not achieve a pathologic complete response, ask what additional treatment options are recommended in your situation (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Many patients may be able to avoid anthracycline heart toxicity and carboplatin side effects with a shorter chemotherapy course, with a biomarker to help personalize the decision.
  • Reality: 56% did not achieve a pathologic complete response and would need additional treatment after surgery. HER2DX is not universally available or covered.

The Reality

  • ER-positive, HER2-positive patients were substantially less likely to achieve a complete response with this regimen alone.
  • Long-term recurrence-free survival data are not yet mature. Pathologic complete response is a surrogate marker, not proof of cure.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

THP regimen: Weekly paclitaxel plus trastuzumab and pertuzumab for 12 weeks, followed by surgery, then continuing HER2-targeted therapy to complete one year.

HER2DX: A genomic test that predicted pathologic complete response in this trial. In 569 patients, a high score predicted 68% response versus 19% for a low score.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu): China's regulator approved it for high-risk early HER2-positive breast cancer in the adjuvant setting following neoadjuvant therapy, based on DESTINY-Breast05 (audit correction: the draft described this as a neoadjuvant approval and claimed approval in the US and other countries, which the sources do not support).

Whether more patients could avoid chemotherapy entirely with antibody-drug conjugate-based approaches is under study (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

CompassHER2-pCR: 2,175 patients enrolled, 2,141 started treatment. The primary endpoint is 3-year recurrence-free survival, which is still ongoing.

Strengths: Prospective, multi-center, with centralized HER2DX testing blinded to outcomes.

Key limitation: This was a single-arm trial with no randomized comparison to anthracycline or carboplatin-containing regimens. It cannot establish that the shorter regimen is equivalent.

HER2DX requires validation in broader populations and is not yet standard of care in all guidelines; access and cost vary (general medical guidance).
