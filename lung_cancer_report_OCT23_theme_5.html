Early-Stage and Locally Advanced NSCLC — Curative-Intent Strategies Evolving Beyond One-Size-Fits-All

"Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy shows survival advantage over adjuvant therapy, adjuvant targeted therapy (osimertinib, alectinib, selpercatinib) is proving curative in biomarker-defined subgroups, and radiation techniques are advancing to reduce long-term lung damage."

NCDB analysis (n=2,815): Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy associated with 29% lower death risk vs adjuvant (HR 0.71); median OS not reached vs 79 months; benefit strongest in cN2 disease.
pCR rates with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy: 25–29% overall; similar in patients ≥75 years (25% vs 29%, p=0.051) — elderly not excluded from benefit.
Adjuvant targeted therapy milestones: ADAURA (osimertinib, EGFR-mutant), ALINA (alectinib, ALK-positive), LIBRETTO-432 (selpercatinib, RET fusion) all show significant event-free survival benefit.
LAURA trial: osimertinib after chemoradiation in unresectable stage III EGFR-mutant NSCLC markedly extended median progression-free survival (about 39.1 vs 5.6 months; hazard ratio about 0.16), redefining the stage III standard (independent fact-check: corrected from the draft's 'HR 0.10,' which is the lower confidence bound; the published point estimate is 0.16).
S1914 phase III: Atezolizumab + SBRT did NOT improve OS/PFS vs SBRT alone in medically inoperable early-stage NSCLC; more grade 3+ toxicity (12% vs 3%).
Proton therapy registry (Japan, n=156): 5-year OS 43.7% in unresectable stage III; feasible for patients unsuitable for photon therapy.
Radiation biology breakthrough: IFN-beta drives protective ILC2→inflammatory ILC1 transformation causing lung fibrosis; blocking this pathway reduces damage in mice.
Early-stage NSCLC (I–III) is potentially curable; treatment intensity and sequencing directly impact cure rates and long-term quality of life.
Neoadjuvant immunotherapy leverages intact tumor antigen presence to prime systemic immunity against micrometastases — a biological rationale supported by superior OS in observational data.
Adjuvant targeted therapy for oncogene-driven disease (EGFR, ALK, RET) extends the curative paradigm beyond chemotherapy; CNS protection (ALINA HR 0.22 for CNS recurrence) is critical.
LAURA proves molecularly selected stage III patients benefit more from targeted maintenance than immunotherapy consolidation (PACIFIC regimen).
SBRT + immunotherapy negative result prevents overtreatment in early-stage; proton therapy offers option for photon-ineligible patients.
Radiation toxicity reduction could expand eligibility for curative radiation in patients with COPD or prior lung disease.
Resectable stage II–III NSCLC patients considering neoadjuvant vs adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy — neoadjuvant preferred per NCDB and biological rationale.
Stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutant (ADAURA), ALK-positive (ALINA), RET fusion-positive (LIBRETTO-432) patients post-resection — adjuvant targeted therapy indicated.
Unresectable stage III EGFR-mutant NSCLC — osimertinib after chemoradiation (LAURA) is new standard.
Medically inoperable early-stage NSCLC — SBRT alone remains standard; avoid adding atezolizumab (S1914 negative).
Patients with COPD or pulmonary fibrosis needing thoracic radiation — future IFN-beta pathway inhibitors may reduce fibrosis risk.
Elderly patients (≥75) — neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy pCR rates comparable to younger; age alone should not deny curative-intent therapy.

The Hope

  • Hope: Cure rates improving — neoadjuvant pCR 25–30%, adjuvant targeted therapy DFS >90% at 2 years for ALK, >80% for EGFR/RET; LAURA transforms stage III EGFR-mutant from incurable to long-term control.
  • Reality: Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy adds 3–4 months of treatment before surgery; 10–20% cannot complete surgery due to progression/toxicity; surgical delays risk upstaging.
  • Adjuvant targeted therapy: 3 years of daily oral TKI (osimertinib/selpercatinib) or 2 years twice-daily (alectinib) — adherence, cost, chronic side effects (rash, diarrhea, lipid abnormalities, QTc).

The Reality

  • Stage III EGFR-mutant disease: osimertinib is continued after chemoradiation, which raises questions of cumulative toxicity, financial burden, and the unknown optimal duration of therapy (general medical guidance).
  • These are major gains, but recurrence can still occur, and longer follow-up (including overall survival) is needed to confirm durability (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy: Multiple regimens (KEYNOTE-671 pembrolizumab+chemo→surgery→pembro; CheckMate 77T nivolumab+chemo→surgery→nivo; AEGEAN durvalumab+chemo→surgery→durva).

Adjuvant osimertinib (Tagrisso): ADAURA phase III, stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutant, 3 years, DFS HR 0.20; CNS recurrence reduced.

Adjuvant alectinib (Alecensa): ALINA phase III, stage IB–IIIA ALK-positive, 2 years, DFS HR 0.24; CNS HR 0.22.

Adjuvant selpercatinib (Retevmo): LIBRETTO-432 phase III, stage IB–IIIA RET fusion, 3 years, EFS HR 0.172; 91.5% vs 61.1% event-free at 24 mo.

LAURA: Osimertinib post-chemoradiation stage III EGFR-mutant unresectable; mPFS 39.1 vs 5.6 mo; OS immature.

Proton therapy: Registry data support for photon-ineligible; randomized trials needed.

Radiation toxicity mitigation: IFN-beta/ILC pathway inhibitors in preclinical development (City of Hope).

Evidence Uncertainty

Neoadjuvant OS evidence: NCDB retrospective (HR 0.71), but no phase III OS maturity yet (KEYNOTE-671, CheckMate 77T, AEGEAN DFS positive, OS not mature).

Adjuvant targeted therapy: ADAURA/ALINA/LIBRETTO-432 EFS/DFS positive; OS not mature; optimal duration unknown (3 years osimertinib, 2 years alectinib).

LAURA: PFS dramatic (HR ~0.16); OS not mature; long-term toxicity of indefinite osimertinib after chemoradiation unknown.

S1914: Definitive negative for atezolizumab + SBRT; futility stopped early; 2 grade 5 respiratory events in combo arm.

pCR as a surrogate: neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy produces pathologic complete response in roughly 25-30% of patients; whether adjuvant immunotherapy adds benefit after a pCR remains uncertain, and some analyses are underpowered.

Socioeconomic disparities: Lower income associated with higher pCR but worse access to multimodal care; biology equitable, access not.

What To Watch

Overall survival maturity from the adjuvant targeted-therapy trials (ADAURA for osimertinib, ALINA for alectinib, LIBRETTO-432 for selpercatinib), since current results are based mainly on disease-free/event-free survival (general medical guidance).

Overall survival data from LAURA (osimertinib after chemoradiation in stage III), where the progression-free survival benefit is already dramatic (general medical guidance).

Mature overall-survival readouts from the Phase III neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy trials (such as KEYNOTE-671, CheckMate 77T, and AEGEAN), to confirm the survival advantage suggested by retrospective data (general medical guidance).

Randomized trials of proton therapy versus photon-based radiation for patients who are not candidates for standard radiation (general medical guidance).

Progress of radiation-toxicity-reduction research (such as the IFN-beta/immune-cell pathway driving lung fibrosis), which is currently preclinical (general medical guidance).

Guideline updates on neoadjuvant versus adjuvant sequencing and on the optimal duration of adjuvant targeted therapy (general medical guidance).
