FDA Approves Immunotherapy Plus Chemotherapy for Early-Stage Colon Cancer

"The FDA has approved atezolizumab, an immunotherapy drug, combined with chemotherapy for certain patients with stage III colon cancer after surgery. Based on a large trial, the combination cut the risk of cancer returning or death by half compared to chemotherapy alone. Patients with dMMR tumors may now have a stronger chance of cure."

The FDA approved atezolizumab plus chemotherapy for adults with stage III colon cancer whose tumors are dMMR.
dMMR means the tumor has trouble fixing DNA errors, making it easier for the immune system to spot and attack cancer cells.
The approval follows the ATOMIC trial, which enrolled 712 patients whose cancer had spread to lymph nodes but was removed by surgery.
Patients who received immunotherapy plus chemotherapy lived longer without cancer returning than those who received chemotherapy alone.
Previously, immunotherapy was mainly used after colon cancer had spread or returned.
Now it can be used earlier, right after surgery, to try to prevent the cancer from coming back.
This could mean more patients are cured and fewer need additional treatment later.
It marks a shift toward using the body's immune system in the curative setting for a subset of colon cancer patients.
Adults (and children aged 2 and older) with stage III colon cancer that has been surgically removed.
Patients whose tumors test positive for dMMR (deficient DNA mismatch repair).
dMMR is found in roughly 15% of colon cancers overall (somewhat less in stage III), including some cases linked to Lynch syndrome [an inherited condition raising cancer risk] (general medical guidance).
Younger adults are included; more than a quarter of trial participants were under age 50.
You may wish to ask your surgeon or oncologist whether your tumor has been tested for dMMR or MSI status [lab tests showing whether the tumor struggles to repair DNA errors].
You may wish to ask if atezolizumab plus chemotherapy is an option for your stage III colon cancer treatment plan.
You may wish to ask about clinical trials if your tumor is not dMMR, since immunotherapy may not help all colon cancer types (general medical guidance).
It can help to keep all follow-up appointments and scans after surgery to watch for any signs of recurrence (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • This is not a cure for everyone; it applies only to the dMMR subgroup of stage III colon cancer.
  • Immunotherapy can cause unique side effects that require monitoring and sometimes steroids.

The Reality

  • Cost and insurance approval may delay access for some patients.
  • The benefit is measured in reducing recurrence risk, not necessarily guaranteeing a cure.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Atezolizumab (brand name Tecentriq, from Genentech/Roche) is an immune checkpoint inhibitor [a drug that releases a brake on the immune system so it can attack cancer].

It blocks the PD-L1 protein (not PD-1) to help immune cells recognize and attack cancer cells (independent fact-check: the draft stated it blocks PD-1; atezolizumab is a PD-L1 inhibitor).

It is given alongside oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy (such as mFOLFOX6) after surgery, per the ATOMIC regimen.

Other immunotherapies are being tested in earlier stages of colon and rectal cancer (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

In the ATOMIC trial (712 patients; NCT02912559), 3-year disease-free survival was 86.3% with immunotherapy plus chemotherapy versus 76.2% with chemotherapy alone, at a median follow-up of about 41 months.

This represents a 50% relative reduction in the risk of recurrence or death (hazard ratio 0.50; 95% CI 0.35-0.73; P=0.0001).

Side effects can include immune-related inflammation of the skin, intestines, or other organs, sometimes requiring steroids (general medical guidance).

Overall survival data are still maturing; disease-free survival is the primary endpoint reported so far.

What To Watch

The regimen is already in NCCN guidelines (Version 1.2026 lists FOLFOX or CAPEOX plus atezolizumab as preferred adjuvant options for stage III dMMR/MSI-H colon cancer); watch for further guideline refinements (independent fact-check: the draft implied NCCN inclusion was still pending).

Results from similar trials in stage II and rectal cancer (general medical guidance).

Longer follow-up data from the ATOMIC trial, including overall survival.

FDA decisions on other immunotherapies in early-stage colorectal cancer (general medical guidance).
