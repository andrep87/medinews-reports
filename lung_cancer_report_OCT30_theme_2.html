Tarlatamab Plus Durvalumab Maintenance Redefines First-Line Treatment for Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer

"Adding the immune-engaging drug tarlatamab to standard durvalumab maintenance after initial chemotherapy nearly doubles survival for patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer. Median overall survival reached 21.1 months — an unprecedented 8.4-month improvement over durvalumab alone — establishing a new standard of care."

The DeLLphi-305 trial randomized 563 patients with extensive-stage SCLC who had not progressed after 4 cycles of platinum-etoposide plus durvalumab.
Half received tarlatamab added to durvalumab maintenance; half continued durvalumab alone.
Median overall survival: 21.1 months with the combination vs 12.7 months with durvalumab alone (58% lower risk of death, HR 0.42).
Progression-free survival more than tripled: 7.4 months vs 1.9 months (66% lower risk, HR 0.34).
At 1 year, 79% of combination patients were alive vs 53% on durvalumab alone.
SCLC is aggressive; most patients relapse quickly after initial chemo-immunotherapy and few survive to receive second-line therapy.
Tarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engager that binds DLL3 on cancer cells and CD3 on T cells, pulling immune cells to the tumor.
Moving tarlatamab from second-line (after progression) to first-line maintenance attacks residual disease before resistance fully develops.
The magnitude of benefit (8+ month OS gain) is unprecedented in a disease where progress has been measured in about a year historically.
Adults with newly diagnosed extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (cancer spread widely in chest or beyond).
Patients who completed 4 cycles of platinum-etoposide chemotherapy with durvalumab without progression.
ECOG performance status 0–1 (fully active or restricted in strenuous activity only).
Includes patients with treated or untreated asymptomatic brain metastases (~20% of trial) and liver metastases (~33%).
Excludes symptomatic brain metastases and primary refractory disease (progression during initial chemo).
If you have extensive-stage small cell lung cancer and have finished initial chemotherapy plus durvalumab without the cancer growing, you may wish to ask your oncologist about the DeLLphi-305 results — while understanding that tarlatamab is currently FDA-approved for later-line (second-line and beyond) use, and its use as first-line maintenance is not yet approved and would be off-label or trial-based (general medical guidance) (independent fact-check: DeLLphi-305 was published in NEJM and is practice-relevant, but the 1L-maintenance indication is not yet approved).
You may wish to ask whether a clinical trial of tarlatamab in the maintenance setting is available to you (general medical guidance).
Because tarlatamab can cause cytokine release syndrome (CRS) [an immune over-reaction causing fever and low blood pressure], you may wish to ask how your center monitors for and manages it, especially in the first cycles (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask your care team to explain the expected schedule of infusions and monitoring so you can plan (general medical guidance).
Evidence Badges
1 Phase III Trial (DeLLphi-305, NCT06211036) Published in New England Journal of Medicine Presented at ESMO 2026 Presidential Symposium 10 Independent Sources

The Hope

  • Median OS 21 months is a major advance but still means half of patients pass away within ~2 years of maintenance start.
  • CRS requires hospital monitoring for first two infusions (6–8 hours in US/EU); logistics may challenge rural patients.
  • Taste changes (dysgeusia) affect ~50% — can impact nutrition and quality of life during long maintenance.

The Reality

  • Not all patients qualify: those progressing during initial chemo-immunotherapy (primary refractory) are excluded.
  • Cost and access: tarlatamab is a specialized therapy requiring monitoring for CRS; access and coverage for a maintenance indication would depend on future approval (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Tarlatamab (Imdelltra, Amgen): DLL3×CD3 bispecific T-cell engager; approved 2024 for 2L+ ES-SCLC; DeLLphi-305 supports 1L maintenance label expansion.

Durvalumab (Imfinzi, AstraZeneca): PD-L1 inhibitor; standard 1L with chemo and maintenance in ES-SCLC.

DeLLphi-303 (phase Ib): tarlatamab + atezolizumab or durvalumab maintenance — median OS 37.9 months with 30.8-month follow-up.

DeLLphi-304 (phase III): tarlatamab vs chemo in 2L ES-SCLC — sustained OS benefit (13.6 vs 8.4 months, HR 0.62).

DeLLphi-306/312/315: testing tarlatamab in limited-stage SCLC, upfront combo with chemo, and subcutaneous formulation.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large randomized phase III (n=563); primary OS endpoint met at interim analysis (HR 0.42, p<0.001); consistent benefit across all subgroups including brain/liver mets; published in NEJM.

Limitations: Interim analysis (53% OS maturity); control arm was durvalumab alone, not the newer lurbinectedin+atezolizumab (IMforte) maintenance; selected population (only patients who didn't progress during induction).

Safety: CRS in 50% (mostly grade 1–2, median onset 22.5 hours); ICANS in 1.1% (no grade ≥3); grade 3+ TRAEs 21% vs 11%; discontinuation due to TRAEs low (5.7% for tarlatamab).

OS measured from maintenance randomization, not diagnosis — adds ~4 months induction time to total survival estimate.

What To Watch

FDA/EMA supplemental approval for tarlatamab in 1L maintenance (Amgen submitting urgently).

DeLLphi-312: tarlatamab + durvalumab + chemo as induction/maintenance — could further improve outcomes.

DeLLphi-306: tarlatamab after chemoradiation in limited-stage SCLC — may cure more early-stage patients.

Head-to-head vs lurbinectedin+atezolizumab (IMforte) maintenance — needed to define optimal 1L maintenance.

Subcutaneous tarlatamab (DeLLphi-308/315) — may reduce CRS and allow home administration.
