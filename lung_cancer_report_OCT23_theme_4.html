Precision Oncology Infrastructure — Biomarker Testing, Liquid Biopsy, and AI Diagnostics Closing Gaps

"New diagnostic tools — RNA-based immunotherapy predictors, AI pathology screening, and ctDNA liquid biopsy — are addressing critical gaps in biomarker testing access, turnaround time, and early recurrence detection for lung cancer patients."

OncoPrism-NSCLC (Cofactor Genomics): Multidimensional RNA biomarker validated in PREDAPT trial (17 US health systems); high-score patients had doubled median OS on immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) vs low-score; predictive for ICI benefit specifically, not prognostic for chemo; adds value even in PD-L1-high (TPS ≥50%) patients.
Tempus AI pathology panel: Analyzed 60,459 advanced NSCLC H&E slides; predicted 12 genomic/IHC biomarkers with >80% sensitivity (several >90%); designed to prioritize downstream molecular testing and conserve tissue.
Liquid biopsy (ctDNA, whole-genome sequencing): NYU Langone study showed positive ctDNA after surgery detects minimal residual disease months before imaging recurrence; negative ctDNA strongly correlates with sustained remission in stage I–II NSCLC.
PD-L1 testing variability: ALCHEMIST study found 36% discordance between central and local lab PD-L1 categories; pathologist re-review showed only 35% agreement across different tumor blocks.
Pulmonologist-led biomarker testing gaps: 43% of providers start therapy before biomarker results; minority patients less likely to receive testing; pulmonologists critical for adequate tissue acquisition.
Biodesix + Vanderbilt: Multimodal platform combining blood biomarkers, CT radiomics, and AI for lung nodule risk stratification; STRATIFY prospective trial launching Q4 2026; commercial target 2028.
PD-L1 alone is an imperfect predictor — OncoPrism identifies ICI benefit beyond PD-L1, sparing low-score patients from ineffective immunotherapy toxicity and cost.
AI pre-screening can triage limited biopsy tissue: instead of running all tests sequentially, labs can prioritize based on AI predictions, accelerating actionable results.
ctDNA surveillance transforms post-surgical monitoring: detects recurrence at molecular level when curable intervention (surgery, radiation, targeted therapy) is still possible.
PD-L1 discordance means treatment decisions may hinge on which lab processes the sample — standardization and AI-assisted scoring needed.
Pulmonologists are the "gatekeepers" of tissue; early involvement ensures sufficient material for broad NGS, RNA, and future tests.
All newly diagnosed advanced NSCLC patients considering first-line immunotherapy (OncoPrism-NSCLC predicts ICI benefit regardless of PD-L1).
Patients with limited biopsy tissue where sequential testing risks exhaustion (AI triage preserves tissue for confirmatory NGS/IHC).
Stage I-II NSCLC patients after surgery or definitive therapy — ctDNA monitoring may detect recurrence earlier than CT scans (the cited study focused on early-stage disease).
Patients with discordant or borderline PD-L1 results — awareness of inter-lab variability informs treatment discussions.
Underserved populations (rural, minority, uninsured) disproportionately affected by testing gaps; pulmonologist engagement and patient advocacy critical.

The Hope

  • Hope: RNA/AI/ctDNA tools democratize precision oncology — better patient selection, earlier recurrence detection, tissue conservation.
  • Reality: OncoPrism not yet widely available/commercial for NSCLC; AI pathology tools are "pre-screening" only, not diagnostic; ctDNA monitoring not standard of care or universally reimbursed.
  • PD-L1 variability persists across labs, and there is no universal central-testing requirement for community patients; standardization is an ongoing need (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Disparities: patients in underserved communities are less likely to receive comprehensive biomarker testing; pragmatic programs and patient advocacy can help close gaps (general medical guidance).
  • Integration challenge: Clinicians need workflows to incorporate AI predictions, RNA scores, and ctDNA results into real-time decisions.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

OncoPrism-NSCLC (Cofactor Genomics): CAP/CLIA-validated, Medicare-covered for HNSCC; NSCLC validation published npj Precision Oncology 2026; predicts ICI-specific benefit via tumor-immune microenvironment RNA signature.

Tempus AI Pathology Panel: Pre-screening tool (not replacement for NGS/IHC); evaluates 12 biomarkers from routine H&E; Tempus Preview delivers predictions ~24 hours post-sample receipt.

Paige Predict: Pan-cancer AI biomarker prediction (123 biomarkers across tumor types) for tissue-limited cases.

ctDNA MRD assays (C2i Genomics WGS, others): Detect tumor-specific ctDNA signatures; NYU study shows sensitivity for minimal residual disease in early-stage NSCLC.

Biodesix Nodify Lung: Blood-based biomarker panel for lung nodule risk; adding Vanderbilt radiomics/AI for multimodal nodule triage (RSNA 2026 preview, STRATIFY trial Q4 2026).

Evidence Uncertainty

OncoPrism: PREDAPT retrospective-prospective validation; doubled median OS in high-score ICI-treated patients; not prognostic for chemo; needs prospective randomized utility trial.

Tempus AI: 60,459 cases, >80% sensitivity for 12 biomarkers; clinical utility (does it improve testing rates/outcomes?) not yet proven; regulatory pathway for diagnostic aid vs replacement unclear.

ctDNA MRD: NYU study demonstrates lead time over imaging; clinical trial needed to prove ctDNA-guided intervention improves survival vs standard imaging surveillance.

PD-L1 discordance: 36% category mismatch central vs local; pathologist agreement only 35% across blocks; AI standardization promising but unvalidated in prospective trials.

Testing gaps: 43% treatment-before-testing rate (IASLC 2024 survey); structural barriers (cost, access, tissue) persist despite guideline mandates.

What To Watch

A prospective randomized trial of RNA-score-guided immunotherapy selection (versus PD-L1 alone) (general medical guidance).

Prospective utility studies of AI pathology triage — does it increase testing completion and reduce time to treatment? (general medical guidance).

ctDNA-guided intervention trials (acting on a positive ctDNA result versus waiting for imaging) (general medical guidance).
