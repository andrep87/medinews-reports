When Standard Treatment Stops Working: New Options for Brain Metastases and After ADCs Fail

"Research is moving toward patients with the fewest remaining options. Datopotamab deruxtecan, a TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate, showed meaningful activity against brain metastases in metastatic triple-negative breast cancer — important because many drugs do not cross into the brain. A phase 3 trial is testing an off-the-shelf cell vaccine in women whose disease has progressed after antibody-drug conjugates, a group with very limited options. And the FDA has accepted an application for gedatolisib, a dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor, for PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive disease."

Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd, Datroway), an antibody-drug conjugate directed at the TROP2 protein, demonstrated significant activity inside the brain in metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, including improved time before brain disease progressed.
BriaCell's phase 3 trial is testing Bria-IMT — an off-the-shelf whole-cell vaccine — combined with a PD-1 inhibitor in heavily pretreated metastatic patients who have already exhausted antibody-drug conjugates.
The FDA accepted a supplemental application for gedatolisib, a dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor, combined with fulvestrant with or without palbociclib, for PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive/HER2-negative advanced disease (independent fact-check: acceptance of an application means the FDA will review it, not that it is approved).
Sacituzumab govitecan combined with pembrolizumab is an approved first-line option for PD-L1-positive metastatic triple-negative disease (general medical guidance).
Brain metastases are a major problem in triple-negative disease, and many effective drugs do not reach the brain in useful concentrations — so an ADC with intracranial activity addresses a real gap (general medical guidance).
Dato-DXd also offers a non-immunotherapy route for patients who cannot receive checkpoint inhibitors — for example because of autoimmune disease, prior immunotherapy, or access barriers (general medical guidance).
Patients whose disease progresses after antibody-drug conjugates currently have very few options, which is why a trial aimed specifically at that group matters.
PIK3CA mutations are common in HR-positive disease, and a dual PI3K/mTOR mechanism is a different approach from existing options (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: these developments target people with the fewest options — brain metastases, post-ADC progression — rather than adding another option where several already exist (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: the vaccine trial has no results yet, and historically most trials in heavily pretreated populations do not succeed (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: ADCs carry real toxicity, including interstitial lung disease, and require monitoring (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: access varies — trial participation may require travel to a specialist centre (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Datopotamab deruxtecan: a TROP2-directed ADC, with the brain-activity finding being the distinguishing feature here (general medical guidance).

Bria-IMT: an off-the-shelf (not patient-specific) whole-cell vaccine being tested with a PD-1 inhibitor in a phase 3 trial after ADC failure.

Gedatolisib: a dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor under FDA review for PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive advanced disease (general medical guidance).

Sacituzumab govitecan: a TROP2-directed ADC already approved in combination with pembrolizumab for first-line PD-L1-positive metastatic triple-negative disease (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

The brain-activity finding for Dato-DXd comes from analyses within metastatic triple-negative trials; intracranial endpoints are harder to measure than systemic ones (general medical guidance).

The Bria-IMT phase 3 trial is ongoing — no efficacy results are available, and a trial in a heavily pretreated population faces a high bar.

FDA acceptance of the gedatolisib application means a review is underway; it carries no implication about the outcome.

Most of these options are for metastatic disease, where treatment extends time without progression rather than offering cure (general medical guidance).
