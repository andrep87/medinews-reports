Osimertinib Plus Savolitinib Establishes New Standard for EGFR-Mutant Lung Cancer With MET-Driven Resistance

"For patients whose EGFR-mutant lung cancer stops responding to osimertinib due to MET overexpression or amplification, adding the MET inhibitor savolitinib to continued osimertinib significantly extends survival compared to chemotherapy. This all-oral, biomarker-directed combination is now the first globally proven standard for this specific resistance mechanism."

For patients whose EGFR-mutant lung cancer [cancer driven by a mutation in the EGFR gene] stopped responding to osimertinib because of a second change called MET amplification or overexpression [the cancer switches on a backup growth pathway], adding the MET-blocking pill savolitinib to continued osimertinib worked better than switching to chemotherapy.
Osimertinib (80 mg daily) + savolitinib (300 mg twice daily) vs platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy (4 cycles then pemetrexed maintenance).
Median PFS: 7.6 vs 5.4 months (45% lower progression risk, HR 0.55, p<0.001); 12-month PFS 36% vs 10%.
Median OS: 17.8 vs 12.9 months (32% lower death risk, HR 0.68, p=0.010); 12-month OS 70% vs 53%.
Tumor shrinkage: 51% vs 27%; responses lasted longer (9.7 vs 5.7 months).
~34% of EGFR-mutant NSCLC develop MET-driven resistance after osimertinib; historically treated with chemotherapy only.
When MET becomes overactive, it appears to give the cancer an alternative way to keep growing even while EGFR is blocked; targeting both pathways at once aims to overcome this (general medical guidance).
SAFFRON is the first global phase III to prove dual EGFR+MET inhibition beats chemotherapy in this biomarker-selected group.
Continuing osimertinib (the "backbone") while adding savolitinib targets both the original driver and the escape pathway.
Advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC (exon 19 del, L858R, ± T790M) progressed on 1st or 2nd line osimertinib.
MET overexpression (IHC 3+ in ≥90% tumor cells) AND/OR MET amplification (FISH ≥10 copies) on post-progression biopsy.
ECOG 0–1; stable/treated brain metastases allowed; adequate organ function.
Requires fresh tissue biopsy at progression for central MET testing — liquid biopsy not sufficient for this trial's criteria.
If your cancer progressed on osimertinib (Tagrisso), ask: "Has my tumor been re-biopsied for MET overexpression or amplification?"
You may wish to request MET testing by both IHC (protein) and FISH (gene copies) on new biopsy — both criteria identify eligible patients.
Discuss with your oncologist whether osimertinib + savolitinib is available via clinical trial, compassionate use, or off-label (savolitinib approved in China as Orpathys, not yet US/EU).
Monitor for peripheral edema (swelling, 49% with combo) — report rapid weight gain or shortness of breath.

The Hope

  • Median OS 17.8 months is a major gain over chemo (12.9 months) but still under 2 years for most.
  • Savolitinib not yet approved in US/EU — access depends on regulatory timeline, compassionate use, or trials.
  • Edema, GI effects, and liver enzyme changes require ongoing monitoring and dose adjustments.

The Reality

  • Not all EGFR resistance is MET-driven; this only helps the ~34% with high MET protein or gene copies.
  • Tissue re-biopsy at progression is mandatory but can be invasive; liquid biopsy MET detection not yet validated for this regimen.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Osimertinib (Tagrisso, AstraZeneca): 3rd-gen EGFR TKI; standard 1L and 2L (T790M+) for EGFR+ NSCLC.

Savolitinib (Orpathys, AstraZeneca/HUTCHMED): selective oral MET TKI; approved in China for MET-exon14 skipping NSCLC; US/EU approval pending SAFFRON.

Amivantamab (Rybrevant, J&J): EGFR×MET bispecific antibody + chemo (MARIPOSA-2) — alternative post-osimertinib option not directly compared in SAFFRON.

SAVANNAH (phase II): established activity of osimertinib+savolitinib; informed SAFFRON biomarker thresholds.

SACHI (phase III, China): osimertinib+savolitinib vs chemo in similar population — positive, supported SAFFRON.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Global phase III (25 countries); pre-specified biomarker thresholds from SAVANNAH; blinded central review for PFS; statistically significant OS at 53% maturity; consistent benefit in MET-overexpressed and MET-amplified subgroups.

Limitations: Open-label; no direct comparison to amivantamab-based regimens (current US standard post-osimertinib); stringent MET cutoffs may exclude patients with lower MET levels; CNS-specific efficacy not separately reported.

Safety: Grade 3+ treatment-related AEs lower with combo (40% vs 52% chemo); but more serious AEs (41% vs 28%) and discontinuations (14% vs 8%); 12 fatal AEs (7%) in combo arm (1 treatment-related pneumonitis, 9 with documented progression).

Peripheral edema (49% vs 8%) is hallmark MET inhibitor toxicity requiring proactive management.

What To Watch

FDA/EMA approval decisions for savolitinib (Orpathys) in this indication (AstraZeneca pursuing based on SAFFRON).

Head-to-head trials vs amivantamab+chemo (MARIPOSA-2 regimen) to define optimal sequencing.

Lower MET expression/amplification thresholds — could broader populations benefit?

CNS efficacy analysis — critical given high brain metastasis rate in EGFR+ NSCLC.

Long-term OS follow-up (53% maturity) and subsequent therapy patterns after progression on the combo.
