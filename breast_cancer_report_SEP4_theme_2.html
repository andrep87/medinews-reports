Circulating Tumor DNA Testing Moves Toward Guiding Real-Time Treatment Decisions in Early and Metastatic Breast Cancer

"Multiple studies now show that tracking circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA—cancer DNA in the bloodstream) during and after treatment can provide earlier insight into treatment response and relapse risk than imaging alone. While not yet recommended for routine clinical use, ctDNA-guided therapy switching (as in the SERENA-6 trial) and neoadjuvant (treatment before surgery) response monitoring (CIPHER study) are demonstrating the potential to personalize treatment in real time."

The CIPHER study enrolled 37 patients with stage II–III triple-negative or HER2-positive breast cancer; 30 had evaluable baseline ctDNA. Clearing ctDNA within six weeks of starting neoadjuvant chemotherapy strongly predicted a favorable surgical outcome (pathologic complete response or minimal residual disease under 1 cm with no nodal involvement) and postoperative MRD negativity (95% vs 40%, P=0.0128).
The phase III SERENA-6 trial tested switching from an aromatase inhibitor to the oral SERD camizestrant *while continuing the CDK4/6 inhibitor* upon ctDNA detection of an ESR1 mutation (molecular progression) before scans showed growth. This early switch improved median progression-free survival to almost 17 months versus about 9 months with continued aromatase inhibitor plus CDK4/6 inhibitor.
ASCO's 2026 guideline update explicitly states ctDNA testing is not recommended for routine recurrence monitoring outside clinical trials due to unproven clinical utility.
A separate cfDNA nucleosome analysis study (150 samples) distinguished primary from recurrent breast cancer with an AUC of 0.826 using chromatin packaging patterns.
Traditional monitoring (scans, tumor markers) detects progression only after macroscopic disease appears; ctDNA can signal molecular relapse months earlier, offering a window for intervention.
In the neoadjuvant setting, early ctDNA clearance may eventually help identify patients who need less therapy (de-escalation) or those who need more (escalation) while treatment is still ongoing (general medical guidance).
The SERENA-6 strategy—switching therapy at molecular progression rather than radiographic progression—represents a paradigm shift toward "response-adapted" treatment.
However, detecting recurrence earlier does not automatically improve survival; prospective trials must prove that acting on ctDNA changes outcomes.
Patients with early-stage triple-negative or HER2-positive breast cancer undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy (CIPHER population).
Patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer on first-line aromatase inhibitor plus CDK4/6 inhibitor who develop ESR1 mutations detectable in blood (SERENA-6 population).
Patients completing curative-intent treatment where postoperative ctDNA positivity (MRD) indicates high relapse risk.
Currently, these applications are primarily in clinical trials or specialized centers; routine use is not yet standard of care.
Ask your oncologist whether ctDNA testing (such as Signatera, Guardant360, or similar assays) is available and clinically appropriate for your specific situation.
If you have HR-positive metastatic breast cancer on an aromatase inhibitor, discuss whether periodic ctDNA monitoring for ESR1 mutations is part of your monitoring plan or available via trial.
After completing neoadjuvant therapy and surgery, inquire about postoperative ctDNA testing to assess molecular residual disease (MRD) status, especially if you had residual disease at surgery.
Understand that a positive ctDNA result does not automatically mean treatment change; discuss the implications and uncertainties with your care team before acting.

The Hope

  • ctDNA offers a "liquid biopsy" window into tumor biology without invasive procedures, but it is not yet a replacement for scans or pathology.
  • False positives/negatives occur; ctDNA may not detect brain-only metastases due to blood-brain barrier shedding limitations.

The Reality

  • Insurance coverage for serial ctDNA monitoring varies; out-of-pocket costs can be significant without established clinical utility (general medical guidance).
  • The emotional burden of "molecular relapse" detection without clear actionable steps is real; shared decision-making is essential.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Signatera (Natera): Tumor-informed MRD assay used in CIPHER and multiple trials; personalized to each patient's tumor mutations.

Guardant360 (Guardant Health): Plasma-based comprehensive genomic profiling used in SERENA-6 for serial ESR1 mutation detection.

Camizestrant (AstraZeneca): Oral SERD being studied for ctDNA-triggered switching; approved in Europe; FDA action pending additional data post-ODAC vote.

Evidence Uncertainty

CIPHER: Small pilot (30 evaluable patients); early ctDNA clearance associated with 86% favorable surgical outcomes vs 60% for late/no clearance (P=0.0128); short follow-up (7 months), few relapse events.

SERENA-6: Large phase III (>3,300 screened); PFS benefit clear but debate persists over PFS1 vs PFS2 interpretation; ODAC voted with concerns; FDA requesting additional data before US approval.

ASCO guideline: No randomized trials prove ctDNA-guided management improves survival; potential harms include anxiety, overtreatment, cost.

cfDNA nucleosome study: Retrospective, limited cohort; requires prospective validation across subtypes and clinical settings.
