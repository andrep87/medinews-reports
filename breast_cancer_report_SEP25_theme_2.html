Enhertu (Trastuzumab Deruxtecan) Expansion: From Metastatic HER2-Low to Curative-Intent Early HER2+

"Enhertu (trastuzumab deruxtecan) [an antibody-drug conjugate that delivers chemotherapy directly to HER2-expressing cancer cells] continues to redefine HER2-targeted therapy. The EU's CHMP [the European medicines advisory committee] issued a positive opinion recommending approval for adjuvant treatment [after-surgery treatment to reduce recurrence risk] of early HER2+ breast cancer with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy [pre-surgery treatment]. Based on DESTINY-Breast05: 53% recurrence risk reduction (HR 0.47), 3-year invasive disease-free survival 92.4% vs. 83.7% compared to T-DM1 [the prior standard drug]. NICE [the UK health technology body] in England recommended Enhertu for HER2-low metastatic breast cancer after a commercial pricing agreement. Enhertu is approved in the US, Brazil, Canada, and India for adjuvant HER2+; EU Commission final decision is pending."

EU CHMP positive opinion: Recommended Enhertu as monotherapy for adjuvant treatment of resected HER2+ early breast cancer with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant taxane + HER2-targeted therapy. Based on DESTINY-Breast05: 53% reduction in invasive disease recurrence or death (HR 0.47, p<0.0001); 3-year IDFS 92.4% vs 83.7% with T-DM1. European Commission final decision is pending.
NICE approval for England (Sept 2026): Recommended Enhertu for HER2-low metastatic breast cancer after a commercial solution raised the cost-effectiveness threshold to £35,000/QALY. Based on DESTINY-Breast04: median PFS 9.9 vs 5.1 months (HR 0.50); median OS 23.4 vs 16.8 months. Available on NHS immediately. Scotland approved Dec 2023; Wales/Northern Ireland pending.
US approvals already in place: Adjuvant HER2+ with residual disease (DESTINY-Breast05, approved 2025); metastatic HER2-low (DESTINY-Breast04, approved 2022). Also approved in Brazil, Canada, India for adjuvant HER2+.
DESTINY-Breast05 safety: ILD/pneumonitis 9.6% (mostly grade 1-2; two grade 5 deaths). Common AEs: nausea 71%, constipation 32%, neutropenia 32%, vomiting 31%. Consistent with known profile.
Curative-intent shift: Enhertu is the first ADC to show superiority over T-DM1 (the prior adjuvant standard) in early HER2+ breast cancer with residual disease—a high-risk population where recurrence risk is ~2-3x higher. This moves ADCs from metastatic to curative settings.
HER2-low as a new treatable entity: ~50-60% of "HER2-negative" breast cancers are HER2-low (IHC 1+ or 2+/ISH-). DESTINY-Breast04 proved Enhertu nearly doubles PFS vs chemotherapy in this group. NICE approval after 2+ years of rejection (only breast cancer drug NICE couldn't recommend since 2018) signals payer acceptance of HER2-low as a distinct biomarker-defined population.
One drug, multiple biomarkers, multiple stages: Same ADC (trastuzumab deruxtecan, DXd payload) works in HER2+ (amplified), HER2-low (low expression), and now adjuvant (curative) and metastatic (life-extending) settings. This challenges the old binary HER2+ vs HER2- classification.
ILD/pneumonitis remains the key safety concern: ~10% incidence, mostly low-grade, but fatal cases occur (0.2% in DESTINY-Breast05). Requires baseline imaging, patient education, and prompt steroid treatment for new respiratory symptoms. This risk is acceptable in metastatic disease but weighs heavier in adjuvant/curative settings.
Adjuvant early HER2+: Adults with surgically removed HER2-positive early breast cancer who have residual invasive disease [cancer still found in tissue] after pre-surgery chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy. Approved in US, Brazil, Canada, India; CHMP positive opinion in EU (final decision pending).
Metastatic HER2-low: Adults with HER2-low [cancers with low but detectable HER2 protein, scored IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH-] unresectable or metastatic breast cancer. Recommended by NICE in England; approved in Scotland. Source does not confirm approval status in other regions for this indication.
Patients should have HER2 status confirmed on a recent biopsy or metastatic site — HER2 expression can change over time, and accurate scoring is critical for eligibility (general medical guidance).
If you have early HER2+ breast cancer with residual disease after pre-surgery treatment: Ask your oncologist 'Am I eligible for adjuvant Enhertu based on the DESTINY-Breast05 results?'
If you have HER2-low metastatic breast cancer: Ask 'Is Enhertu available to me? Has my tumor's HER2 status been confirmed on a recent biopsy?' HER2 expression can change, so retesting matters.
Before starting Enhertu, request a baseline chest CT scan and understand the warning signs of ILD [interstitial lung disease, a serious lung inflammation]: new or worsening cough, fever, or shortness of breath. Report these to your care team immediately.
Ask about the monitoring schedule during treatment and what to do if you develop breathing symptoms between visits (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Enhertu significantly improves outcomes across the HER2 spectrum — from after-surgery treatment (HER2+) to life-extending therapy for metastatic disease (HER2-low). Patients who previously had no targeted option (HER2-low) now have one.
  • Reality: Enhertu is an IV infusion [given through a vein at a clinic], not a pill. Requires regular clinic visits. ILD surveillance is essential — this side effect can be life-threatening. Cost is substantial; NICE required a commercial pricing agreement to achieve cost-effectiveness (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: NICE took over 2 years to recommend Enhertu for HER2-low after initial rejection. Access in Wales and Northern Ireland is expected to follow. Scotland has had access since December 2023.
  • Reality: Not a cure for metastatic disease. Resistance develops over time. Treatment options after Enhertu progression are still being defined (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

DESTINY-Breast05 (adjuvant HER2+): Phase III trial showing 53% recurrence risk reduction vs. T-DM1 [the prior standard after-surgery drug] (HR 0.47). Basis for CHMP positive opinion and US/Brazil/Canada/India approvals.

DESTINY-Breast04 (metastatic HER2-low): Phase III trial showing PFS [time before cancer worsens] 9.9 vs. 5.1 months (HR 0.50), overall survival 23.4 vs. 16.8 months. Basis for NICE recommendation in England.

Enhertu safety profile: ILD [lung inflammation] occurred in 9.6% of patients (mostly mild to moderate; two fatal cases reported). Most common side effects: nausea (71%), constipation (32%), low white blood cells (32%), vomiting (31%). Requires ongoing lung monitoring throughout treatment.

HER2 expression retesting: Tumor biology can change over time and between different sites of disease. Retesting at progression is recommended to confirm ongoing eligibility for HER2-targeted therapies (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

DESTINY-Breast05: CHMP opinion based on Phase III data (HR 0.47, 3-year invasive disease-free survival 92.4% vs. 83.7%). Safety: ILD in 9.6%, two fatal cases. Source does not provide detailed sample size, follow-up duration, or subgroup analyses.

DESTINY-Breast04: Median PFS 9.9 vs. 5.1 months, overall survival 23.4 vs. 16.8 months per NICE/source data. Longer-term data from broader populations are still accumulating.

ILD risk: 9.6% is significant. Requires baseline chest CT, patient education on symptoms (cough, fever, shortness of breath), and rapid access to steroids if ILD is suspected. Two fatal cases reported in DESTINY-Breast05.

HER2 testing variability: Source notes that HER2 scoring can vary between laboratories, which affects who qualifies for treatment. AI-assisted scoring methods are being explored to improve consistency (general medical guidance).
