Beta Bionics Mint: Second Tubeless Insulin Patch Pump Cleared by FDA, Bringing Choice and New Dosing Algorithms

"The FDA cleared Beta Bionics' Mint, a tubeless, smartphone-controlled insulin patch pump that holds 200 units of insulin, wears for 3 days (plus 12-hour grace), requires no recharging, and integrates with Dexcom G7 and Abbott Libre 3 Plus CGMs. It launches Q1 2027 exclusively through pharmacies, not DME, broadening access. A new "3D Intelligence" algorithm with three clinician-selectable dosing profiles (Original, Optimized, Conservative) is under FDA review to power both Mint and the tubed iLet pump."

FDA cleared the Mint insulin patch pump (Beta Bionics) in September 2026; full U.S. launch planned for Q1 2027 via pharmacy channel only.
Device: 2-piece design — reusable controller plus disposable patch; 3-day wear plus 12-hour grace period; 200-unit reservoir; IPX8 waterproof [can be submerged 5 feet for 60 minutes]; no recharging needed (2-year battery).
Smartphone app control (iOS/Android); integrates with Dexcom G7, G7 15-Day, Abbott Libre 3 Plus; Senseonics Eversense 365 integration planned.
Separate 510(k) submitted for "3D Intelligence" next-gen algorithm with three dosing profiles; iLet 3D expected late 2026, Mint 3D with Q1 2027 launch.
Only second tubeless patch pump on U.S. market (vs Insulet Omnipod 5); smaller, no recharging, pharmacy access removes DME hurdles for many patients.
3D Intelligence offers clinician choice: Original (current iLet algorithm), Optimized (real-world data from 40,000+ users, aims to reduce hypoglycemia), Conservative (gentler high-glucose corrections for elderly/high-risk).
Pharmacy-channel model could improve affordability and access — at least 40% of payers expected to cover at launch.
Automated insulin delivery (AID) without manual basal rates, carb ratios, or correction factors — meal announcement only — reduces burden.
People with diabetes who use insulin and prefer tubeless, patch-based delivery.
Those previously limited by DME coverage requirements — pharmacy benefit may open access.
Patients wanting algorithm-driven automation without complex pump settings; not for those wanting full manual control.
Compatible with Dexcom G7, G7 15-Day, and Abbott Libre 3 Plus at launch; Senseonics Eversense 365 integration planned for later.
Ask your endocrinologist: "Is the Mint patch pump appropriate for me? How does it compare to my current pump or Omnipod 5?"
Verify pharmacy benefit coverage with your insurance plan for Q1 2027; ask about prior authorization requirements and copay tier.
If on iLet tubed pump, ask about 3D Intelligence software update (expected late 2026) — three algorithm choices without hardware change.
Discuss CGM compatibility: ensure your current CGM (Dexcom G7 or Libre 3 Plus) works at launch; plan transition if using other CGM.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Mint (Beta Bionics): patch pump, 3-day disposable, 200U reservoir, no recharge, smartphone app, CGM integration; U.S. launch Q1 2027.

iLet Bionic Pancreas (Beta Bionics): tubed AID pump since 2023; same meal-announcement-only algorithm; 3D Intelligence update pending.

3D Intelligence algorithm: 3 selectable profiles (Original, Optimized, Conservative); 510(k) under review; targets late 2026 (iLet) / Q1 2027 (Mint).

Competitors: Insulet Omnipod 5 (tubeless, integrated with Dexcom G6/G7); other tubeless options in development (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Clearance based on substantial equivalence to existing devices; no pivotal randomized trial outcomes published yet for Mint specifically.

iLet real-world data: the company has reported outcomes from over 40,000 users, including a 25% improvement in time-in-range presented at ADA 2026 — not yet peer-reviewed.

3D Intelligence algorithm: no clinical trial data released; FDA clearance pending; performance vs. current algorithm unknown.

Manufacturing target: 1.5 million disposable units in 2027; supply chain execution risk for a new product launch.
