Anti-Amyloid Drugs in Real-World Use: One Failure, New Safety-Monitoring Tools

"Two anti-amyloid drugs (lecanemab, donanemab) are now approved in the US and Europe for early Alzheimer's, but a third (sabirnetug) just failed its Phase 2 trial, and companies are racing to deploy AI imaging tools that monitor dangerous brain swelling and bleeding side effects. Combination trials are launching to boost efficacy and reduce harms."

Lecanemab and donanemab are in clinical use for early Alzheimer's (the cited sources document European Commission approval); they modestly slow decline rather than stopping or reversing it (general medical guidance).
Acumen Pharmaceuticals halted development of sabirnetug (ACU193), an antibody targeting toxic amyloid-beta oligomers, after its Phase 2 ALTITUDE-AD trial (542 participants, 18 months) missed the primary endpoint on the iADRS cognitive/functional scale at both 35 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg doses—stock dropped 90%.
Temple University launched a Phase 1/2 trial combining acetazolamide (a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor used for altitude sickness, glaucoma) with anti-amyloid antibodies to reduce ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities—brain swelling/bleeding) and further improve memory/cognition; funded by Alzheimer's Association Part the Cloud ($2M).
Neurophet and RapidAI partnered to integrate AI-powered MRI analysis (Neurophet AQUA AD Plus) into 2,500+ US hospital workflows via the Rapid Enterprise Platform—automatically quantifying microbleeds, superficial siderosis, and edema to monitor ARIA safety during anti-amyloid treatment.
Anti-amyloid drugs are the first to modestly alter Alzheimer's trajectory, but their benefit is measured in months of delayed decline, not cure or improvement. Sabirnetug's failure shows targeting amyloid oligomers alone may not be enough. (general medical guidance)
ARIA (brain swelling/hemorrhage) occurs in 10–25% of treated patients, often asymptomatic but sometimes serious—requiring rigorous MRI monitoring every few months, which strains radiology capacity and limits access in community settings. (general medical guidance)
Acetazolamide targets brain vascular health and mitochondrial function—addressing a different mechanism (cerebrovascular dysfunction, energy metabolism) that may complement amyloid removal and protect vessels from ARIA. (general medical guidance)
AI imaging automation (Neurophet AQUA AD Plus) can standardize ARIA detection, reduce radiologist workload, and enable safer prescribing in memory clinics without dedicated neuroradiology teams. (general medical guidance)
Adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or mild dementia due to Alzheimer's who have confirmed brain amyloid pathology (via PET, CSF, or now blood test) and no contraindications (e.g., anticoagulants, significant cerebrovascular disease). (general medical guidance)
Patients currently on lecanemab or donanemab who need regular MRI surveillance for ARIA—AI monitoring tools aim to make this safer and more accessible at local imaging centers. (general medical guidance)
People with vascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes) who may be at higher ARIA risk and could benefit from combination approaches targeting vascular health. (general medical guidance)
Clinicians prescribing anti-amyloid therapies who need reliable, efficient ARIA monitoring—RapidAI/Neurophet integration targets memory disorder centers first. (general medical guidance)
If diagnosed with MCI or mild Alzheimer's, ask your neurologist: "Do I meet criteria for lecanemab or donanemab? What amyloid confirmation test will you use (PET, spinal fluid, blood)?" Request the PrecivityAD2 or p-tau217 blood test if available. (general medical guidance)
Before starting anti-amyloid treatment, ask: "What is the MRI monitoring schedule for ARIA? Will my scans be read by a neuroradiologist or AI-assisted system? What happens if ARIA is found?" (general medical guidance)
If you have vascular risk factors (high blood pressure, diabetes), ask: "Would I be a candidate for the acetazolamide + anti-amyloid combination trial (NCT pending) or similar vascular-protection strategies?" (general medical guidance)
For care partners: Learn the symptoms of symptomatic ARIA (new/worsening headache, confusion, dizziness, nausea, vision changes, seizures) and report them immediately—most ARIA is asymptomatic on MRI, but symptoms require urgent evaluation. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: Two approved drugs that modestly slow decline, with a third (donanemab) allowing treatment cessation once amyloid cleared; combination trials targeting vascular health may improve benefit/risk; AI monitoring could democratize safe prescribing. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: Benefits are small (months of delay), costs high (~$26,000–$32,000/year drug alone, plus infusions, MRIs, monitoring), access limited to specialty centers. ARIA monitoring every 1–3 months for 18+ months is burdensome. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Sabirnetug's failure reminds us that amyloid-targeting alone has limits—tau, inflammation, vascular, mitochondrial, and synaptic pathways also drive progression. (general medical guidance)
  • Most patients with moderate/severe dementia, significant vascular disease, or on blood thinners are excluded—these drugs help a narrow early population.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Sabirnetug (ACU193, Acumen): IV every 4 weeks targeting amyloid oligomers; Phase 2 ALTITUDE-AD (NCT06335173, 542 pts) missed primary endpoint (iADRS at 18 mo) at both doses; development halted. (general medical guidance)

Acetazolamide + anti-amyloid (Temple/Alzheimer's Assoc): Phase 1/2 trial launching; acetazolamide (oral, FDA-approved for other uses) enhances vascular compliance, mitochondrial function, may reduce ARIA and boost cognition. (general medical guidance)

Neurophet AQUA AD Plus (AI MRI): Quantifies microbleeds (hypointense), siderosis, edema (hyperintense) automatically; integrated into RapidAI platform (2,500+ hospitals); supports ARIA monitoring for Leqembi/Kisunla prescribing centers. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

ARIA risk: Higher in ApoE4 carriers (especially homozygotes), with anticoagulants, or significant microbleeds at baseline; most cases asymptomatic and resolve with treatment pause, but symptomatic ARIA can be serious (seizures, hospitalization, rare deaths). (general medical guidance)

Sabirnetug failure: Despite promising Phase 1 biomarker data (amyloid reduction), clinical endpoint missed—raises questions about oligomer-targeting sufficiency, dose selection, or trial population (early symptomatic only). (general medical guidance)

Acetazolamide combo: Preclinical data only so far (improved vascular function, reduced tau, better cognition in models); human safety with anti-amyloid antibodies unknown; trial just starting. (general medical guidance)

AI monitoring: Neurophet AQUA AD Plus validated against expert reads; real-world performance in diverse MRI scanners/protocols pending; FDA clearance status for ARIA quantification not specified in sources. (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

CMS and private insurer coverage policies for anti-amyloid drugs with blood test confirmation (vs. PET/CSF)—will expand or restrict access. (general medical guidance)

Temple acetazolamide + anti-amyloid Phase 1/2 trial enrollment and safety data (ARIA rates, cognitive outcomes). (general medical guidance)

Neurophet/RapidAI real-world deployment metrics: ARIA detection sensitivity/specificity, radiologist time savings, adoption in community hospitals. (general medical guidance)

Long-term extension data for lecanemab/donanemab: durability of effect, late ARIA incidence, conversion rates to moderate dementia. (general medical guidance)

Next-generation anti-amyloid (subcutaneous formulations, higher brain penetration) and tau-targeting combination trials (e.g., anti-amyloid + anti-tau). (general medical guidance)
