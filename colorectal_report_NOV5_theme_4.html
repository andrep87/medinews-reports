More Treatment Isn't Always Better: Four Studies Where Adding or Extending Therapy Didn't Help

"Four studies delivered useful negative or reassuring results: extending the gap between surveillance colonoscopies from 3 to 5 years after high-risk polyp removal was as safe as the shorter interval; aspirin after liver-metastasis surgery did not prevent recurrence; intensifying adjuvant chemotherapy with irinotecan did not improve survival in high-risk stage III colon cancer; and a dual immunotherapy combination failed to beat standard options in MSS metastatic disease."

EPoS II (10,799 patients): after removal of high-risk adenomas [precancerous polyps], waiting 5 years for the next surveillance colonoscopy was non-inferior to waiting 3 years (colorectal cancer rate 0.77% vs 0.82%); more than 99% remained cancer-free at 5 years.
EPoS III (1,861 pts): 5-year CRC incidence 1.0% after serrated polyp removal; supports 5-year interval for these lesions too.
ASAC trial (466 pts): Aspirin 160 mg daily for 3 years after curative liver mets resection did not reduce recurrence; numerically lower OS and more serious adverse events in aspirin arm.
IROCAS trial (795 pts): mFOLFIRINOX (adding irinotecan to mFOLFOX6) showed no DFS benefit (3-yr 65.6% vs 66.3%, HR 1.07) but more severe diarrhea (15.6% vs 3.8%).
KEYFORM-007 (441 patients): combining two immunotherapy drugs (favezelimab plus pembrolizumab, blocking PD-1 and LAG-3) did not improve overall survival compared with standard later-line options in PD-L1-positive MSS metastatic colorectal cancer (hazard ratio 0.98).
Chemotherapy escalation fails: Adding irinotecan increases toxicity without benefit; future adjuvant strategies must use biomarkers (ctDNA, molecular subtypes) not just TNM stage. (general medical guidance)
Aspirin not universal: Observational data suggested benefit, but randomized trial in liver mets setting showed harm; biology differs by stage and metastatic site.
Surveillance de-escalation safe: 5-year intervals after high-risk polyps spare patients procedures, reduce costs, and free endoscopy capacity for screening. (general medical guidance)
Immunotherapy resistance confirmed: Even dual checkpoint blockade in biomarker-selected MSS CRC fails; need strategies beyond releasing T-cell brakes.

The Reality

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

mFOLFOX6: Remains standard adjuvant for stage III colon cancer (oxaliplatin + leucovorin + 5-FU); 12 cycles every 2 weeks. (general medical guidance)

Aspirin: Cheap, accessible, but ASAC shows no benefit post-liver mets; ALASCCA trial suggested benefit only in PI3K-altered localized CRC.

Surveillance intervals: US Multi-Society Task Force likely to update guidelines toward 5-year intervals after high-risk polyps. (general medical guidance)

PROSIT signature: 7-gene "immune exclusion" signature (high TGF-β, low immune activation) predicts worse DFS in stage II; needs validation for adjuvant escalation trials. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

IROCAS: Underpowered for DFS (fewer events than planned); OS immature; subgroup biomarker benefits not excluded.

ASAC: 466 pts, 3-year follow-up; lower OS with aspirin concerning but not pre-specified; registry data previously suggested benefit—RCT trumps observation. (general medical guidance)

EPoS: Large, multinational, randomized; but only 65-70% adherence to assigned surveillance; inverse probability weighting used for missing data. (general medical guidance)

What To Watch

PROSIT validation: Prospective testing of immune exclusion signature for stage II adjuvant decisions. (general medical guidance)

EPoS 10-year results: Will confirm long-term safety of 5-year surveillance intervals. (general medical guidance)

ASAC/ALASCCA molecular analyses: Defining which subgroups (if any) benefit from aspirin. (general medical guidance)
