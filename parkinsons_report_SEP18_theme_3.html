Incisionless Brain Surgery and Precision Neuromodulation Expand Options for Advanced Parkinson’s

"High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) now offers a dual-target, incisionless procedure that immediately improves tremor, stiffness, and slowness in advanced Parkinson’s patients who cannot have deep brain stimulation (DBS). Meanwhile, FDA-cleared AI brain-mapping software (Quicktome DB) personalizes DBS lead placement, and a preclinical “masked antibody” technology promises to deliver biologics across the blood-brain barrier."

CU Anschutz performed the first dual-target HIFU procedures in the Rocky Mountain region: two advanced PD patients received back-to-back ablation of the dentatorubral thalamic tract (DRTT) for tremor and pallidothalamic tract (PTT) for rigidity/slowness in a single 90-minute outpatient session.
The female patient achieved ~70% tremor reduction after DRTT, then near-complete resolution of tremor plus ~70% improvement in stiffness and movement speed after PTT; the male patient (with dementia) had zero rigidity, minimal slowness, and no tremor post-procedure.
FDA cleared Omniscient Neurotechnology’s Quicktome Deep Brain (DB) software, which uses a patient’s MRI to map implanted DBS leads, target structures (e.g., subthalamic nucleus), and connecting neural pathways for personalized programming.
Researchers from Cornell, Tel Aviv University, and Technion developed “masked antibodies” encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) that cross cell membranes and the blood-brain barrier, delivering anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies inside neurons in preclinical models.
Dual-target HIFU treats both tremor AND bradykinesia/rigidity [slowness/stiffness] in one session, whereas prior HIFU only addressed tremor (DRTT); this expands eligibility to the 80–90% of advanced PD patients with mixed symptoms.
HIFU is incisionless, avoids hardware implantation (no battery, wires, infection risk), and provides immediate feedback during MRI-guided ablation—critical for patients too frail or elderly for DBS surgery.
Quicktome DB solves the “lead location vs. network effect” problem: DBS outcomes depend on how the lead connects to the patient’s unique brain network, not just coordinates; shared surgeon/neurologist map optimizes programming.
Masked antibody/LNP platform could eventually enable intravenous delivery of biologics (antibodies, enzymes) to intracellular targets like alpha-synuclein aggregates, overcoming the blood-brain barrier that blocks 98% of large molecules.
HIFU dual-target: Advanced PD patients with medication-refractory tremor plus stiffness/slowness who are not candidates for DBS surgery due to cognitive impairment, blood thinner use, or other surgical risk factors; currently performed on one brain hemisphere at a time (general medical guidance).
Quicktome DB: Any patient undergoing DBS implantation or programming; the software uses the patient's MRI to map leads and brain connections.
Masked antibody/LNP: Preclinical only; future application would be PD patients with confirmed alpha-synuclein pathology (via SAA or biopsy) who need disease-modifying biologic therapy.
If you have advanced PD with troublesome tremor AND stiffness/slowness despite medications, ask your movement disorder specialist: “Am I a candidate for dual-target HIFU, and is there a center near me offering PTT ablation?”
If you are considering DBS, ask the surgical team: “Do you use connectomic imaging (like Quicktome) to plan lead placement and guide programming?”
If you are on blood thinners or have memory concerns that may exclude you from DBS, ask about HIFU as an alternative — it is an incisionless procedure (general medical guidance).
For any advanced therapy discussion, bring a care partner to take notes; ask for a written summary of expected benefit, risks, durability data, and follow-up schedule.

The Hope

  • Hope: HIFU gives immediate, dramatic relief without brain surgery — an important option for patients who cannot undergo DBS.
  • Reality: Available at a limited number of centers; insurance coverage varies; the lesion is permanent — unlike DBS, it cannot be adjusted if side effects emerge (general medical guidance).
  • Hope: Quicktome DB could standardize DBS programming, reducing trial-and-error visits and side effects.

The Reality

  • Reality: Software adds cost and workflow complexity; neurologists need training; DBS remains an invasive surgical procedure (general medical guidance).
  • Hope: Masked antibodies could eventually enable IV delivery of large-molecule drugs to brain cells.
  • Reality: Years from first human trial; previous alpha-synuclein antibody approaches have not yet succeeded in Phase 3 (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

HIFU (Insightec Exablate Neuro): FDA-approved for tremor (DRTT target) and rigidity/slowness (PTT target); CU Anschutz performing dual-target procedures with reported immediate motor benefit; bilateral PTT approved but requires staged procedures months apart.

DBS (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott): Standard for medication-refractory PD; Quicktome DB adds brain-connectivity-based guidance for lead placement and programming (general medical guidance).

Masked antibody/LNP (Cornell/Tel Aviv University/Technion): Preclinical; demonstrated intracellular delivery and alpha-synuclein aggregate reduction in neuron models; further animal testing and safety studies needed before human trials.

Note: Focused ultrasound for blood-brain barrier opening (a separate lower-intensity technique) is being explored in other neurodegenerative diseases but is not yet studied specifically for Parkinson's drug delivery (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

HIFU dual-target: Only 2 cases reported; no control group; durability unknown beyond months; cognitive effects of dual-tract ablation not yet studied; the lesion is irreversible (unlike adjustable DBS).

Quicktome DB: FDA clearance based on anatomical accuracy, not clinical outcome improvement; no prospective trial showing better DBS results vs. standard methods.

Masked antibody/LNP: No in vivo primate or human data; biodistribution, immune reactions, and repeated-dosing safety unknown; the antibody target (alpha-synuclein) has not yet shown clinical benefit in Phase 3 trials of other anti-synuclein antibodies.

All neuromodulation is symptomatic — it does not alter disease progression. Benefits may wane as neurodegeneration spreads beyond the targeted brain circuits (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

CU Anschutz dual-target HIFU case series expansion and longer-term motor and cognitive follow-up data.

Quicktome DB real-world evidence from early-adopting centers on programming efficiency and outcomes.

Preclinical safety studies for masked antibody/LNP platform; timeline for potential first-in-human filing.

Broader focused ultrasound research: whether low-intensity techniques for drug delivery are explored in Parkinson's.
