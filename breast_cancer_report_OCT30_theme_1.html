Antibody-Drug Conjugates Are Reshaping Treatment Across Breast Cancer Subtypes

"New antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) trial results are changing the standard of care for HER2-positive early breast cancer, HER2-low metastatic disease, and triple-negative breast cancer. These "guided missile" drugs link cancer-targeting antibodies to potent chemotherapy, delivering treatment directly to tumor cells while sparing more healthy tissue."

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd, Enhertu) won European approval for HER2-positive early breast cancer with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, cutting recurrence risk by 53% versus T-DM1 (Kadcyla) in the DESTINY-Breast05 trial.
Trastuzumab rezetecan (Hengrui) showed a 73% reduction in progression risk in HER2-low metastatic breast cancer in the China-only HORIZON-Breast06 trial, with median progression-free survival of 16.4 months versus 6.8 months for chemotherapy.
Trastuzumab pamirtecan (BioNTech/DualityBio) met its primary endpoint in the DYNASTY-Breast02 trial but with only a modest PFS improvement and higher rates of interstitial lung disease, leading developers to question its clinical value in HER2-low disease.
Sacituzumab tirumotecan (Sac-TMT) reduced death risk by 40% in heavily pretreated triple-negative breast cancer in the OptiTROP-Breast01 trial, while datopotamab deruxtecan showed overall survival of 23.7 months versus 18.7 months for chemotherapy in untreated advanced TNBC.
ADCs work like guided missiles: an antibody finds cancer cells (via HER2, TROP2, or other targets), enters the cell, and releases chemotherapy inside, sparing more healthy tissue than traditional IV chemotherapy.
For HER2-positive early breast cancer, T-DXd now offers a more effective post-surgery option than T-DM1 for patients with residual disease, directly impacting curative-intent treatment.
In HER2-low metastatic disease (previously untreatable with HER2-targeted drugs), multiple ADCs now provide meaningful progression-free survival gains, though toxicity profiles differ significantly.
For triple-negative breast cancer, new ADCs are extending survival in settings where options were historically limited to chemotherapy alone.
HER2-positive early breast cancer patients with residual invasive disease in breast or lymph nodes after neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus HER2-targeted therapy.
Metastatic breast cancer patients with HER2-low tumors (IHC 1+ or 2+/ISH-negative); HORIZON-Breast06 included patients who had received prior therapy as well as some who were chemotherapy-naive in the advanced setting.
Triple-negative breast cancer patients in advanced or metastatic settings, including those who have received prior chemotherapy and immunotherapy.
Patients with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative metastatic disease may benefit from newer bispecific ADCs targeting EGFR/HER3 (izalontamab brengitecan) after CDK4/6 inhibitor and chemotherapy failure.
Ask your oncologist: "Is my tumor HER2-low (IHC 1+ or 2+/ISH-negative)? If so, am I eligible for an ADC like trastuzumab deruxtecan or trastuzumab rezetecan?"
If you have HER2-positive early breast cancer and had neoadjuvant therapy, ask: "Did I achieve a pathologic complete response? If not, should I receive T-DXd instead of T-DM1 after surgery?"
For triple-negative breast cancer, ask: "Have I received or am I eligible for sacituzumab govitecan or datopotamab deruxtecan? What line of therapy are they approved for in my situation?"
Request testing for HER2 expression level (including "ultralow" categories) and TROP2 status if considering ADC options, as these biomarkers guide eligibility.

The Hope

  • Hope: ADCs are converting previously untreatable subtypes (HER2-low, TNBC) into diseases with multiple effective options; some patients achieve years of disease control.
  • Reality: These drugs are not cures for metastatic disease; median PFS gains range from 4-10 months. ILD risk requires vigilant monitoring and can force treatment discontinuation.

The Reality

  • Cost and access: ADCs are expensive, and insurance prior authorizations and co-pay assistance programs vary; global access is uneven (general medical guidance).
  • Not all patients respond: Biomarkers like HER2-low, TROP2, or EGFR/HER3 expression are imperfect predictors; some patients derive little benefit despite target expression.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu, AstraZeneca/Daiichi Sankyo): Approved for HER2+ early BC with residual disease (EU), HER2-low metastatic BC, HER2-ultralow metastatic BC; multiple ongoing trials in earlier lines.

Trastuzumab rezetecan (SHR-A1811, Hengrui): Phase III HORIZON-Breast06 positive in HER2-low metastatic breast cancer, conducted in China; published in JAMA (general medical guidance).

Sacituzumab tirumotecan (Sac-TMT, Kelun-Biotech/MSD): Phase III OptiTROP-Breast01 positive in pretreated TNBC; trials ongoing in HR+/HER2- MBC (TroFuse-010) and TNBC (TroFuse-011).

Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd, AstraZeneca/Daiichi Sankyo): TROP2 ADC showing OS benefit in untreated advanced TNBC; not yet approved.

Izalontamab brengitecan (Iza-bren): First-in-class bispecific EGFR/HER3 ADC; Phase III PANKU-Breast01 positive in heavily pretreated HR+/HER2- MBC.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strongest evidence: DESTINY-Breast05 (T-DXd vs T-DM1 in residual HER2+ early BC) — HR 0.47 for IDFS, 3-year IDFS 92.4% vs 83.7%, n=1,635. HORIZON-Breast06 (trastuzumab rezetecan vs chemo in HER2-low MBC) — HR 0.27 for PFS, median PFS 16.4 vs 6.8 months, n=551.

Key uncertainty: Overall survival data immature for most new ADCs; long-term benefit versus chemotherapy not yet proven.

Safety: interstitial lung disease (ILD/pneumonitis) is a recognized class effect of HER2-directed ADCs such as trastuzumab deruxtecan, which can rarely be fatal and requires monitoring (chest imaging and prompt evaluation of new respiratory symptoms) (general medical guidance).

Trastuzumab pamirtecan met its primary endpoint in DYNASTY-Breast02 but with a more modest progression-free survival benefit and immature overall survival data, raising questions about its place in a crowded HER2-low landscape.

What To Watch

Mature overall survival data for the newer HER2-low and TNBC ADCs (general medical guidance).

Head-to-head ADC comparisons (currently none exist; experts call for trials comparing Enhertu vs newer ADCs).

T-DXd in adjuvant HER2+ early BC without residual disease (DESTINY-Breast05 only studied residual disease population).

Sac-TMT and datopotamab deruxtecan regulatory filings in US/EU for TNBC indications.

Bispecific ADCs (iza-bren, zanidatamab combinations) expanding into breast cancer after gastroesophageal success.
