FDA Approves Sevabertinib as First-Line Treatment for HER2-Mutant Non-Squamous NSCLC

"The FDA has expanded approval of sevabertinib (Hyrnuo) to include previously untreated patients with advanced non-squamous NSCLC [a type of non-small cell lung cancer] whose tumors carry HER2 tyrosine kinase domain mutations. This oral targeted therapy now offers a first-line option for a molecular subset historically linked to poor outcomes and brain metastases, with a 75% response rate and durable responses in the SOHO-01 trial."

On September 9, 2026, the FDA gave accelerated approval to sevabertinib (Hyrnuo) for adults with advanced non-squamous NSCLC whose tumors carry HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations [specific genetic changes that drive cancer growth]. This expands its earlier approval (November 2025) for previously treated patients to include those who have not yet received any treatment.
Approval was based on the open-label, single-arm SOHO-01 trial. Among 69 previously untreated patients who took sevabertinib 20 mg twice daily, 75% saw their tumors shrink (95% CI 64–85%). Of those who responded, 73% maintained their response for at least 6 months and 38% for at least 12 months.
The FDA reviewed the application under Project Orbis alongside the UK medicines regulator (MHRA), and granted Priority Review, Breakthrough Therapy, and Orphan Drug designations.
Sevabertinib is the second HER2-targeted pill approved for first-line use in this setting, following zongertinib (Hernexeos, Boehringer Ingelheim), which received accelerated approval in February 2026.
HER2 mutations occur in roughly 2–6% of NSCLC cases and are linked to aggressive disease, including higher rates of brain metastases and historically poor outcomes with standard chemotherapy.
Until now, patients with HER2-mutant NSCLC typically received platinum-based chemotherapy first, with targeted therapy reserved for later use. This approval moves a highly active, brain-penetrant oral drug to the front line where it can do the most good.
The 75% response rate is roughly double the typical chemotherapy response rate (about 30–40%) and offers a convenient oral alternative to intravenous antibody-drug conjugates like trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu), which requires infusion center visits and carries interstitial lung disease risk (general medical guidance).
Comprehensive molecular testing at diagnosis (tissue biopsy or blood-based liquid biopsy) is now essential to identify eligible patients early, as these specific HER2 mutations require specialized genomic testing for detection.
Adults with advanced (unresectable stage III or stage IV) non-squamous NSCLC whose tumors test positive for HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations via an FDA-authorized test.
Patients who have not received prior systemic therapy for advanced disease. Those who completed adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy more than 6 months prior may be eligible based on the trial criteria.
The approval covers non-squamous histology (mostly adenocarcinoma); squamous cell lung cancers were excluded from the pivotal trial.
HER2 mutations are more common in never/light smokers, younger patients, and Asian populations, but testing is recommended for all non-squamous NSCLC regardless of clinical features (general medical guidance).
If newly diagnosed with advanced non-squamous NSCLC, ask your oncology team: 'Has my tumor been tested for HER2 tyrosine kinase domain mutations using a comprehensive genomic panel or FDA-approved liquid biopsy?' Request testing if not yet done.
If you have a known HER2 mutation and have not started treatment, discuss with your doctor whether sevabertinib is appropriate as first-line therapy, and whether clinical trial options should also be considered.
Ask about a brain MRI at baseline if not already done, since HER2-mutant NSCLC has a high tendency to spread to the brain and sevabertinib has shown activity against brain metastases.
Ask about patient assistance programs through Bayer for help with out-of-pocket costs, as oral targeted therapies can be expensive even with insurance. Confirm whether coverage is through your pharmacy benefit or medical benefit.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Sevabertinib (Hyrnuo, Bayer): Oral HER2-selective targeted drug, approved under accelerated approval for both first-line and previously treated HER2 mutation-positive NSCLC. The confirmatory Phase 3 SOHO-02 trial (NCT06452277) is ongoing, comparing sevabertinib to standard chemotherapy in treatment-naive patients.

Zongertinib (Hernexeos, Boehringer Ingelheim): The first HER2-selective targeted drug approved for first-line HER2-mutant NSCLC (February 2026 accelerated approval). Its confirmatory Phase 3 Beamion LUNG-2 trial is enrolling.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu, AstraZeneca/Daiichi Sankyo): An antibody-drug conjugate [a targeted drug carrying chemotherapy directly to cancer cells] approved for previously treated HER2-mutant NSCLC. The DESTINY-Lung04 Phase 3 trial is testing it in the first-line setting.

Earlier HER2-targeting drugs (pyrotinib, neratinib, poziotinib) showed some activity in HER2-mutant NSCLC but had higher side effect rates and have largely been superseded by these newer selective agents.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: 75% tumor shrinkage rate (95% CI 64–85%) in 69 treatment-naive patients; 73% of responders maintained response for at least 6 months; intracranial responses observed. The large effect size supports accelerated approval despite the single-arm design.

Limitations: Single-arm trial without a comparison group; overall survival data not yet mature; median follow-up not reported; no randomized progression-free or overall survival data yet. The confirmatory SOHO-02 trial is required for full approval.

Safety: Grade 3 or higher treatment-related side effects in about 30% of patients. Notable risks include diarrhea, liver toxicity, interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis (about 3–5%), heart function changes, eye problems, pancreatic enzyme elevation, and harm to a developing fetus.

Unknowns: Best treatment sequence after sevabertinib stops working; how well it works against specific HER2 mutation subtypes (for example, exon 20 insertions vs. point mutations); potential combination strategies with chemotherapy or immunotherapy.

What To Watch

SOHO-02 Phase 3 confirmatory trial results (sevabertinib vs. chemotherapy in first-line HER2-mutant NSCLC) — primary completion estimated 2027–2028.

DESTINY-Lung04 Phase 3 results (trastuzumab deruxtecan vs. chemotherapy in first-line HER2-mutant NSCLC) — will help define how to choose between a targeted pill and an antibody-drug conjugate.

Real-world evidence on sevabertinib sequencing, brain metastasis outcomes, and resistance mechanisms.

Potential FDA conversion to regular approval depending on SOHO-02 overall survival benefit.
