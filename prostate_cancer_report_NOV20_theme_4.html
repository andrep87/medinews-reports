Novel Therapeutic Frontiers Target Aggressive, AR-Indifferent Prostate Cancer

"Early research is exploring new approaches for aggressive AR-indifferent [less dependent on male-hormone receptor signaling] prostate cancers—including neuroendocrine [having nerve- and hormone-like features] and double-negative [lacking both androgen receptor and PSA] subtypes—such as targeting NUAK2 RNA splicing [how genetic messages are cut and pasted into proteins] with trilaciclib, designing multiplexed [multi-target or multi-payload] ADCs [antibody-drug conjugates, targeted antibodies linked to cancer-killing drugs], and developing a PSMA-targeted 4-1BB bispecific antibody [an engineered antibody that binds two targets at once] (AM109)[cite: 3, 4]."

Researchers reported that the NUAK2 enzyme [a protein that speeds up chemical reactions] helps regulate pre-mRNA splicing in aggressive AR-indifferent prostate cancer, with higher NUAK2 levels linked to more advanced disease, including neuroendocrine prostate cancer[cite: 3, 4].
In laboratory and animal models, trilaciclib (a CDK4/6 inhibitor [a drug targeting cell-division proteins]) engaged NUAK2, altered splicing, slowed tumor growth, and increased the effect of platinum chemotherapy[cite: 3, 4] (independent fact-check: trilaciclib is FDA-approved to reduce chemotherapy-induced bone-marrow suppression in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, though that specific approval was not in this week's prostate cancer articles)[cite: 3, 4].
Researchers are exploring multiplexed ADC designs—such as antibodies targeting more than one marker, carrying dual payloads, or combining ADCs—to address uneven target expression across tumor cells[cite: 3, 4].
AbClon reported follow-up funding for AM109, a PSMA-by-4-1BB bispecific antibody built to activate 4-1BB immune signaling conditionally upon binding PSMA, aiming to limit liver toxicity seen with broader 4-1BB stimulation[cite: 3, 4].
In the UK COSTAR pilot study of 21 men (median age 74.8) with high-risk or locally advanced prostate cancer and urinary symptoms, UroLift [a device-based prostatic urethral lift procedure] was feasible as a day-case step before radiotherapy, with 83% of 12 evaluable men reporting symptom improvement at six weeks[cite: 3, 4].
Aggressive AR-indifferent subtypes such as neuroendocrine and double-negative prostate cancer have few effective treatments today, so targeting NUAK2 and RNA splicing offers a new biological angle independent of hormone receptors[cite: 3, 4].
Uneven protein expression across cancer cells can limit single-target ADCs, making multiplexed strategies an active area of design to broaden coverage[cite: 3, 4].
Conditional activation with AM109 is designed to focus 4-1BB immune activation where PSMA is present while reducing off-target liver toxicity, based on preclinical rationale[cite: 3, 4].
In the COSTAR pilot, all 21 men completed their planned radiotherapy, highlighting how urinary symptom management before radiation can support treatment completion[cite: 3, 4].

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Trilaciclib is in preclinical study for NUAK2-driven aggressive prostate cancer, including combinations with platinum chemotherapy[cite: 3, 4].

Multiplexed ADC approaches—including dual-target and dual-payload concepts—are in early research and design stages to overcome tumor heterogeneity[cite: 3, 4].

AM109 (a PSMA-by-4-1BB bispecific candidate from AbClon) is advancing in preclinical optimization and development following follow-up funding[cite: 3, 4].

UroLift before radiotherapy was evaluated in the COSTAR pilot study (NCT05840549), with researchers calling for larger prospective comparative studies[cite: 3, 4].

Evidence Uncertainty

NUAK2 and trilaciclib findings come from preclinical models (such as cell, organoid, and animal experiments), with no human clinical trial results in prostate cancer yet and potential overlap with trilaciclib's CDK4/6 activity[cite: 3, 4].

Multiplexed ADC strategies are conceptual and early-stage, with no approved prostate cancer ADC yet and technical challenges ahead[cite: 3, 4].

AM109 is at the preclinical stage with follow-up funding, so human safety and effectiveness remain to be tested[cite: 3, 4].

COSTAR was a small (21-patient), single-group pilot feasibility study—with 33% needing temporary catheterization and 83% of 12 evaluable men showing symptom improvement at six weeks—and did not test long-term cancer control or survival[cite: 3, 4].
