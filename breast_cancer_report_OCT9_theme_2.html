Giredestrant Plus Everolimus Significantly Improves Outcomes in ER+/HER2- Advanced Breast Cancer After CDK4/6 Inhibitors

"A phase III trial showed that combining the novel oral drug giredestrant with everolimus nearly doubles progression-free survival compared to standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus in patients with ER+/HER2- advanced breast cancer, especially those with ESR1 mutations. This all-oral regimen offers a new chemotherapy-free option after CDK4/6 inhibitor failure."

The phase III evERA trial randomized 373 patients with ER+/HER2- advanced breast cancer progressed on prior CDK4/6 inhibitor + endocrine therapy to giredestrant + everolimus vs investigator-choice endocrine therapy + everolimus.
In the overall population, median PFS was 8.8 months with giredestrant vs 5.5 months with standard therapy (HR 0.56, 44% risk reduction).
In the 55% of trial participants with ESR1 mutations, median progression-free survival was 10.0 months versus 5.5 months (HR 0.38) — reported across sources as a 62–63% reduction in the risk of progression or death (audit correction: sources differ slightly on the rounded percentage).
Giredestrant is a next-generation oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) that destroys the estrogen receptor; everolimus blocks the mTOR resistance pathway.
Provides a potent all-oral, chemotherapy-free option after CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance, where previous options offered only brief disease control.
The near-doubling of PFS in ESR1-mutated disease (10 vs 5.5 months) addresses a major unmet need for this common resistance mechanism.
Overall survival data trending favorably (HR 0.62 in ESR1-mutated, 0.69 overall) though not yet statistically mature.
Represents a shift toward biomarker-directed combinations targeting both ER degradation and PI3K/AKT/mTOR resistance pathways simultaneously.
Patients with ER+/HER2- advanced or metastatic breast cancer.
Disease progression on or after a CDK4/6 inhibitor combined with endocrine therapy (first-line setting).
Particularly relevant for patients whose tumors carry ESR1 mutations. In this trial 55% of participants had them, though the trial population was selected in a way that may not reflect prevalence in general practice (audit correction).
Patients must be able to tolerate everolimus (monitoring for stomatitis, hyperglycemia, infections, pneumonitis).
If your ER-positive, HER2-negative metastatic cancer has progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor, you may wish to ask your oncologist whether ESR1 mutation testing is appropriate for you.
Everolimus has a distinct side effect profile including mouth sores, high blood sugar, and infection risk. Ask your care team how these would be monitored and managed if this combination becomes an option (general medical guidance).
This combination is not yet FDA approved. An FDA decision is expected by December 18, 2026, so availability before then would be through a clinical trial.
If you are not a candidate for everolimus, you may wish to ask what other options or trials are open to you (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Oral regimen avoids chemotherapy; meaningful PFS gain in ESR1-mutated disease; OS trend positive; tolerable with proactive management.
  • Reality: Everolimus requires dose reductions/interruptions in many; stomatitis, hyperglycemia, fatigue, infection risk; drug interactions (CYP3A4).

The Reality

  • Not yet FDA approved for this combination (decision pending Dec 2026); access may be limited until formal approval.
  • ESR1 testing is not uniformly standardized, and tissue and blood tests can disagree. Not all patients with ESR1 mutations respond equally (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Giredestrant (Genentech/Roche): An investigational oral selective estrogen receptor degrader [a drug that breaks down the estrogen receptor] studied in the evERA trial.

Everolimus: An mTOR inhibitor [a drug that blocks a cell growth and survival pathway], used here as the partner agent in both trial arms.

The combination targets two mechanisms at once — degrading the estrogen receptor and blocking the mTOR resistance pathway.

An FDA decision on this combination for ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer is expected December 18, 2026.

Evidence Uncertainty

evERA: 373 patients; primary endpoint was investigator-assessed progression-free survival, stratified by prior fulvestrant use, ESR1 status, and visceral disease.

Overall survival data are not yet mature. The reported trends (HR 0.62 in ESR1-mutated patients, 0.69 overall) have confidence intervals crossing 1.0, meaning a survival benefit is not established.

Safety: everolimus discontinuation was higher in the giredestrant arm (17% vs 12%). Mouth sores, diarrhea, and anemia were the most common adverse events.

The control arm predominantly used exemestane plus everolimus, with fulvestrant plus everolimus less represented, which affects how the comparison generalizes.
