Targeted Therapy Breakthroughs Redefine Care for Molecular Subsets of NSCLC

"Three advances highlight precision care for specific genetic changes in non-small cell lung cancer: osimertinib plus savolitinib improved progression-free and overall survival after osimertinib resistance with MET [a cell-growth signaling protein] alterations; neladalkib (Nelkarmi) received accelerated approval for previously treated ALK-positive [anaplastic lymphoma kinase gene rearrangement] disease, with activity noted in the brain; and elticertinib received FDA Fast Track [an expedited review process] designation for EGFR [epidermal growth factor receptor] exon 20 insertion mutations after prior chemotherapy and amivantamab[cite: 1, 2]."

In the SAFFRON phase III trial of 338 patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC and MET overexpression or amplification after osimertinib [a targeted EGFR pill], adding savolitinib to osimertinib improved median progression-free survival [time without cancer worsening] to 7.6 vs. 5.4 months (HR [hazard ratio, a measure of relative risk over time] 0.55, P<0.001) and median overall survival [length of time patients live] to 17.8 vs. 12.9 months (HR 0.68, P=0.010) compared with platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy[cite: 1, 2].
The FDA granted accelerated approval [conditional approval based on early clinical markers] to neladalkib (Nelkarmi) for previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC after alectinib (ORR [overall response rate, percentage of patients whose tumors shrink] 49%, 12-month DOR [duration of response] 52%) or after at least two prior ALK TKIs [tyrosine kinase inhibitors, targeted blocking pills] plus platinum chemotherapy (ORR 32%, 12-month DOR 51%), with intracranial [in the brain] activity also reported (independent fact-check: outside sources also report a 55% intracranial response rate in alectinib-pretreated patients with measurable brain metastases, though this figure was not in the week's supplied sources)[cite: 1, 2].
The FDA granted Fast Track designation to elticertinib (PFL-721) for EGFR exon 20 insertion–mutated NSCLC after platinum chemotherapy and amivantamab, supported by phase I/II data showing a 54% ORR and 79% disease control rate at doses of 360 mg daily or higher[cite: 1, 2].
In a subset analysis of the phase II VISION trial of tepotinib in MET exon 14 skipping NSCLC, 8.3% of 313 patients had response durations over 36 months (up to nearly 79 months), including four patients who stopped treatment early for side effects and still had responses lasting up to 52 months off therapy[cite: 1, 2].
In preclinical [laboratory/animal] studies of KRAS-mutant [a common lung cancer gene mutation] NSCLC, blocking MYC [a cancer-driving protein] with OMO-103 shrank tumors directly and brought immune T cells into tumors, with higher response rates in mice when paired with OX40 or 4-1BB immune-stimulating antibodies[cite: 1, 2].
SAFFRON is reported as the first study after osimertinib progression to show both progression-free and overall survival gains with an all-oral targeted combination over chemotherapy in a biomarker-selected group[cite: 1, 2].
Because MET alterations contribute to resistance in roughly 34% of cases after osimertinib, targeted combination therapy offers an important potential option; in SAFFRON, progression-free survival also favored the combination in patients with baseline brain metastases (HR 0.50)[cite: 1, 2].
Neladalkib adds a newer ALK inhibitor option designed to enter the brain and reduce certain neurologic side effects seen with earlier drugs (independent fact-check: external sources and trial records also identify the first-line ALKAZAR phase III study [NCT06765109] and TRK-sparing / G1202R design details, which were not present in the week's supplied sources)[cite: 1, 2].
EGFR exon 20 insertion NSCLC has historically seen limited benefit and tolerability challenges with standard options after platinum chemotherapy and amivantamab, making new targeted candidates like elticertinib notable[cite: 1, 2].
Long-lasting responses in some tepotinib-treated patients point to underlying tumor biology worth studying to help more patients stay well longer[cite: 1, 2].
Adults with advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC whose cancer progressed on prior osimertinib and tested positive for MET overexpression or amplification on post-progression testing in the SAFFRON trial[cite: 1, 2].
Adults with advanced ALK-positive NSCLC who previously received alectinib, or who received two or more prior ALK inhibitors plus platinum-based chemotherapy, for FDA-approved neladalkib[cite: 1, 2].
Adults with advanced EGFR exon 20 insertion–mutated NSCLC whose disease progressed after platinum chemotherapy and amivantamab, for investigational elticertinib[cite: 1, 2].
Adults with MET exon 14 skipping NSCLC, where tepotinib is used and favored first-line by clinical experts over immunotherapy given older patient age and retrospective data showing weaker immunotherapy responses even with high PD-L1 [programmed death-ligand 1, an immune-checkpoint protein][cite: 1, 2].
If your EGFR-mutated NSCLC progressed on osimertinib, you may wish to ask your oncologist: "Has repeat testing been done to check for MET overexpression or amplification, and could I be a candidate for osimertinib plus savolitinib or a related trial?"[cite: 1, 2]
If you have ALK-positive NSCLC and have been treated with alectinib or prior ALK inhibitors and chemotherapy, you may wish to ask: "Does neladalkib fit my treatment history, and what lab or eye checks will we do for liver, kidney, and vision side effects?"[cite: 1, 2]
If you have an EGFR exon 20 insertion mutation after chemotherapy and amivantamab, you may wish to ask about clinical trial options, including the phase I/II elticertinib study (NCT06043817)[cite: 1, 2].
When starting any targeted therapy, you may wish to ask your care team for a written summary of expected side effects—such as swelling (peripheral edema) with savolitinib or liver test changes with neladalkib—and who to call urgently for new symptoms[cite: 1, 2].

The Hope

  • Hope: SAFFRON showed longer median overall survival (17.8 vs. 12.9 months) and higher one-year progression-free survival (36% vs. 10%) than chemotherapy in this post-osimertinib group[cite: 1, 2].
  • Reality: Savolitinib is not yet FDA-approved in the U.S. pending review of the September 2026 application, and out-of-pocket costs or insurance rules can still affect access[cite: 1, 2].

The Reality

  • Hope: Neladalkib expands oral options for previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC, including patients with central nervous system involvement[cite: 1, 2].
  • Reality: Because approval is accelerated, continued U.S. approval requires confirming clinical benefit in follow-up studies, and regular monitoring for side effects such as liver, kidney, and eye changes is part of care[cite: 1, 2].

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Osimertinib (Tagrisso) plus savolitinib (oral once-daily osimertinib with twice-daily savolitinib): a New Drug Application was submitted to the FDA in September 2026, and the combination is approved in China for EGFR-mutated, MET-amplified NSCLC after prior EGFR TKI therapy[cite: 1, 2].

Neladalkib (Nelkarmi): received FDA accelerated approval in November 2026 as a 150 mg once-daily oral pill for previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC, with confirmatory clinical trial follow-up required and first-line study work underway[cite: 1, 2].

Elticertinib (PFL-721/STX-721): holds FDA Fast Track designation and is being studied in an ongoing phase I/II trial (NCT06043817) as an irreversible [permanently binding] inhibitor selective for EGFR and HER2 [human epidermal growth factor receptor 2] exon 20 insertions[cite: 1, 2].

Tepotinib (Tepmetko): approved for MET exon 14 skipping NSCLC, with swelling often managed by holding treatment for a couple of weeks and restarting at a lower dose; four patients in the VISION subset maintained responses off therapy for up to 52 months[cite: 1, 2].

OMO-103: a MYC inhibitor in the phase I MYCure trial, with preclinical mouse studies showing strong antitumor activity when combined with OX40 or 4-1BB agonists [activating antibodies] and human combination trials still needed[cite: 1, 2].

Evidence Uncertainty

SAFFRON was a randomized, controlled phase III study (n=338) with central MET testing that met its progression-free survival goal and showed an overall survival trend at 53% data maturity, while noting peripheral edema (49%), lower grade 3 or higher treatment-related side effects than chemotherapy (40% vs. 52%), and one fatal pneumonia case[cite: 1, 2].

ALKOVE-1 for neladalkib was a single-arm study (n=222) without a control group, meaning long-term survival comparisons are not established and accelerated approval depends on confirmatory trial results[cite: 1, 2].

Elticertinib's reported 54% response rate (95% CI [confidence interval, a statistical range of likely values] roughly 34–73%) and 79% disease control rate come from 28 patients at doses of 360 mg or higher in a small, early-phase, uncontrolled study without survival data yet[cite: 1, 2].

The VISION long-term responder analysis looked at a subset of 313 patients (8.3% with response over 36 months), and why a small number of patients stayed in response off treatment is not yet understood[cite: 1, 2].
