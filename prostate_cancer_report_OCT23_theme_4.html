Bavdegalutamide (ARV-110): First-in-Class Androgen Receptor Degrader Shows Promise in Mutant mCRPC

"Bavdegalutamide, a novel PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera) drug that destroys the androgen receptor rather than just blocking it, demonstrated meaningful antitumor activity in a phase I/II trial of men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) whose tumors harbor specific androgen receptor mutations (T878/H875). This represents a new mechanistic class for overcoming resistance to standard hormonal therapies."

A phase I/II open-label study enrolled 239 men with mCRPC who had progressed on at least one androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) such as enzalutamide, abiraterone, or apalutamide.
Bavdegalutamide (ARV-110) is a PROTAC — a molecule that recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase to tag the androgen receptor for degradation (destruction) by the cell's own disposal system, rather than merely blocking its activity.
In phase II (144 patients at 420 mg daily), efficacy was analyzed in molecular subgroups based on androgen receptor (AR) alterations: T878/H875 mutations, wild-type/other AR, L702H/splice variant 7 (AR-V7), and a less-pretreated subgroup (one prior ARPI, no chemo).
Patients with AR T878 and/or H875 mutations had the highest response rates: in the primary analysis (25 such patients), about 56% achieved a PSA decline of at least 50%; median radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) was 11.1 months (independent fact-check: the draft's '60% PSA50 / 70% PSA30 / n=20' figures were corrected to the primary-source values of 56% PSA50 in 25 patients; the data cutoff was December 2022).
Response rates were low in the wild-type/other AR group (about 4.3% PSA50; rPFS 4.2 months) and the AR-V7/L702H group (about 3.1% PSA50; rPFS 5.1 months).
Safety was manageable: most side effects were grade 1–2 (nausea 56%, fatigue 36%, vomiting 34%); grade 3 in 17%; 11% dose reductions, 11% discontinuations; no grade 4+ events.
Standard ARPIs (enzalutamide, abiraterone, apalutamide, darolutamide) eventually fail because cancer cells develop AR mutations or splice variants (like AR-V7) that render the drugs ineffective.
Bavdegalutamide's degradation mechanism can eliminate mutated AR proteins that resist blocking drugs, potentially overcoming a major resistance mechanism.
The striking activity in T878/H875 mutants (common mutations that confer resistance to older ARPIs) suggests a biomarker-selected niche where this drug could fill a critical unmet need.
As a first-in-class AR degrader, it validates PROTAC technology in prostate cancer, opening the door for next-generation degraders with broader mutation coverage.
Men with mCRPC who have progressed on at least one ARPI (enzalutamide, abiraterone, apalutamide, or darolutamide).
Specifically, men whose tumors harbor AR T878 and/or H875 mutations (detected by tissue or liquid biopsy genomic testing) — this subgroup derived the most benefit.
Men with AR-V7 expression or L702H mutations did not benefit significantly; they need alternative strategies (chemotherapy, radioligand therapy, clinical trials).
Men who are chemotherapy-naïve or have received only one prior ARPI (less-pretreated subgroup had 21% PSA50 rate and 11.1 month rPFS).
If you have mCRPC progressing on an ARPI, you may wish to ask your oncologist about comprehensive genomic profiling (tissue or liquid biopsy) to test for AR mutations, including T878, H875, L702H, and AR-V7 status (general medical guidance).
If you have T878/H875 mutations, you may wish to ask whether a bavdegalutamide trial or expanded-access program is available (general medical guidance).
If you have AR-V7 or L702H, you may wish to discuss chemotherapy, PSMA radioligand therapy (if PSMA-PET positive), or clinical trials with your team (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask about repeat tissue or liquid biopsy at progression, since AR mutation status can change over time (general medical guidance).
Evidence Badges
Phase I/II trial (NCT03888612); published in the Journal of Clinical Oncology (2026) Data cutoff December 2022 (originally presented at ESMO 2023) Sponsored by Arvinas 1 Independent Source (ASCO Post) plus the primary trial abstract

The Hope

  • Hope: A new drug class that works after all current hormonal therapies fail, especially for men with specific AR mutations.
  • Reality: only a minority of mCRPC patients have T878/H875 mutations, so most men with AR-V7 or wild-type AR progression would not be expected to benefit (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Hope: PROTAC technology can be optimized — next-gen degraders (ARV-766) aim to cover AR-V7 and broader mutations.
  • Reality: Bavdegalutamide is not yet FDA-approved; phase III trials needed; access currently limited to clinical trials; cost unknown but likely high for novel targeted therapy.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Bavdegalutamide (ARV-110, Arvinas): a first-in-class oral AR PROTAC degrader; phase I/II completed, with a phase III planned in the biomarker-selected population (general medical guidance).

Other AR degraders are in development (such as next-generation degraders aiming for broader mutation coverage) (general medical guidance).

Standard ARPIs (enzalutamide, abiraterone, apalutamide, darolutamide) remain first-line for mCSPC and mCRPC; bavdegalutamide targets the post-ARPI resistance setting.

Combination strategies: AR degraders + PARP inhibitors (for HRR-mutated), + AKT inhibitors (for PTEN loss), + radioligand therapy — all in early trials.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strength: Clear biomarker-defined response (T878/H875); manageable safety; proof-of-concept for AR degradation in humans; 11.1-month rPFS in a heavily pretreated population is notable.

Limitation: phase I/II, non-randomized, no control arm; small subgroup sizes (about 25 patients with T878/H875); no overall survival data; the data cutoff was December 2022, so these are not brand-new results.

Limitation: No activity in AR-V7 or L702H subgroups — these are common resistance mechanisms not addressed by this degrader.

Limitation: Nausea/vomiting may affect quality of life and adherence; long-term safety of chronic AR degradation unknown.

What To Watch

Phase III trial initiation for bavdegalutamide in T878/H875-mutant mCRPC (likely vs standard of care chemotherapy or ARPI).

Development of ARV-766 and other next-gen AR degraders with activity against AR-V7 and L702H.

Combination trials: bavdegalutamide + PARP inhibitor (talazoparib), + AKT inhibitor (capivasertib), + Lu-PSMA.

Liquid biopsy monitoring of AR mutation dynamics during bavdegalutamide treatment to understand acquired resistance.

Regulatory pathway: FDA fast track/breakthrough therapy designation potential given unmet need in biomarker-defined population.
