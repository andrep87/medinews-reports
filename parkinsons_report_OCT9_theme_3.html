Solengepras: A Non-Dopamine Drug That Reduced "Off" Time in a Phase 3 Trial

"In the Phase 3 ARISE trial, solengepras (a once-daily GPR6 inhibitor [a drug that blocks a non-dopamine brain receptor]) reduced daily "off" time by about 0.61 hours (roughly 37 minutes) versus placebo, and increased "on" time without troublesome dyskinesia. It also improved daytime sleepiness and quality-of-life scores. As a non-dopamine add-on, it offers a new type of option for motor fluctuations, and the maker plans to discuss next steps with the FDA."

The ARISE trial randomized 341 patients who had motor fluctuations on levodopa to solengepras 75 mg, 150 mg, or placebo once daily for 12 weeks. At the start, patients averaged about 5.65 hours of "off" time per day.
The 150 mg dose cut average daily "off" time [periods when Parkinson's symptoms return as medication wears off] by 1.56 hours from baseline versus 0.95 hours with placebo — a 0.61-hour difference (about 37 minutes, p=0.035) — with benefit seen as early as week 2.
"On" time without troublesome dyskinesia [smoother, symptom-controlled time without disruptive involuntary movements] rose 1.58 versus 0.98 hours, a 0.60-hour difference (p=0.0468).
On daily-living scores (MDS-UPDRS Part II), the 150 mg group improved 2.05 versus 0.14 points (p=0.0008). Daytime sleepiness (Epworth scale) improved at a nominal p=0.009, and quality of life (PDQ-39) at a nominal p=0.012 (one source reports PDQ-39 as p=0.0012).
There were no serious adverse events at 150 mg; common side effects were headache, urinary tract infection, insomnia, and nausea. Discontinuations due to side effects were about 3.5% across all groups, similar to placebo.
It uses a first-in-class, non-dopamine mechanism: it blocks the GPR6 receptor on "indirect-pathway" neurons to help rebalance movement circuits without acting on dopamine.
It reduced "off" time with little added dyskinesia (4.4% at 150 mg versus 1.8% with placebo), addressing a major unmet need, since dopamine-based drugs can worsen dyskinesia over time.
It showed benefits across several areas — movement, daytime sleepiness, and quality of life — in a single once-daily add-on pill.
Based on its mechanism, it may offer an option for people who prefer or need a non-dopamine approach, though how it fits alongside existing drugs and devices will become clearer with time (general medical guidance).
People with Parkinson's who have about 3 or more hours of daily "off" time despite a stable, optimized medication regimen.

People looking for a non-dopamine add-on who may want to delay dyskinesia or device-based therapies such as DBS or infusion pumps, based on the drug's mechanism (general medical guidance).

Evidence Badges
Phase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trial (ARISE, NCT06553027, 341 patients) Primary endpoint met (p=0.035); key secondary endpoints met Presented at MDS Congress 2026 (Seoul) — conference presentation, full peer-reviewed publication pending FDA meeting planned for regulatory path

The Hope

  • Hope: a once-daily pill that reduced "off" time without worsening dyskinesia, and also improved alertness and quality-of-life scores — a meaningful daily-life improvement.
  • Reality: the effect size is modest (about 37 minutes less "off" time), and the drug is an add-on — it is not a cure and was not shown to be disease-modifying.

The Reality

  • As an investigational drug, its eventual cost, coverage, and access are not yet known (general medical guidance).
  • Longer-term safety and effects on thinking over years are still unproven and would need post-marketing study (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Solengepras (Cerevance/CVN424): Phase 3 ARISE positive; the company plans to meet with the FDA to discuss next steps. (An earlier Phase 2 monotherapy study in untreated patients had missed its primary motor endpoint, while an adjunctive Phase 2 study reduced off time.)

It is described as a potential first-in-class, non-dopamine (GPR6) approach for motor fluctuations.

The data were presented at the 2026 MDS Congress in Seoul; a full peer-reviewed publication is the next step for independent scrutiny of all endpoints.

Other Parkinson's drug classes and device-based therapies exist, but the cited sources focus on solengepras, so specific competitor details are not listed here (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: a randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 design with high completion, benefits seen across motor and non-motor measures, and a clean safety profile at 150 mg.

Limitations: the trial ran only 12 weeks, so long-term durability, effects on thinking, and any disease-modifying potential are unknown.

The 75 mg dose results were not fully reported in these sources, and the data so far come from a conference presentation rather than a full peer-reviewed paper.

The trial enrolled people with motor fluctuations; how the drug performs in older patients or those with other conditions will need post-approval, real-world data (general medical guidance).
