Structured Exercise and Lifestyle Program Proven to Preserve Quality of Life During Long-Term ADT

"The STAMINA randomized trial (700 men, 15 NHS centers) shows that a 12-month supervised exercise, dietary advice, and behavioral support program significantly reduces fatigue and preserves prostate cancer-specific quality of life during androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [hormone therapy that lowers testosterone] — and is cost-effective for health systems."

The STAMINA trial randomized 700 men on ADT (median age 71.6, 34% metastatic) to a structured lifestyle intervention vs. 'Optimised Usual Care' [an enhanced control group that received clinician training, educational materials, and behavioral prompts].
The intervention included: supervised aerobic plus resistance exercise twice weekly for 13 weeks (then tapered), dietary advice, behavioral support, and free 12-month gym membership. 95% of participants attended at least one session; the median was 28 sessions.
At 12 months: FACT-P [a prostate cancer-specific quality-of-life questionnaire] improved by 4.5 points favoring STAMINA (p=0.0004); FACIT-F [a fatigue questionnaire] improved by 1.9 points (p=0.0068); FACT-G [general quality of life] improved by 3.7 points — exceeding the 3-point threshold considered clinically meaningful.
Health utility (EQ-5D) was 0.79 vs. 0.76 (p=0.0001); leisure-time physical activity was sustained at 12 months.
ADT causes significant side effects: bone loss, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular risk, fatigue, hot flashes, sexual dysfunction, and weight gain.
STAMINA demonstrates a deliverable, scalable exercise model using community gyms and cancer center partnerships. The source notes that exercise is widely recommended but structured programs are rarely available (general medical guidance).
Benefits exceeded the 'minimal clinically important difference' — meaning patients felt the difference, not just statistical significance.
Cost-effective: £13,920–19,077 per QALY [quality-adjusted life year, a measure of how much health a treatment buys], with greater than 70–80% probability of cost-effectiveness — well below standard health-system thresholds.
Any man starting or currently on ADT — the trial included men with localized, biochemical recurrence, and metastatic disease (34% metastatic, 19% on ARPI or chemotherapy).
Age is not a barrier: trial participants' median age was 71.6, and the program was tailored to individual fitness levels — starting one-to-one before moving to small groups.
Men experiencing ADT side effects: fatigue, weight gain, muscle weakness, low mood.
Men with cardiovascular risk factors — ADT worsens metabolic and cardiovascular health; exercise directly counters these effects (general medical guidance).
Ask your oncology team: 'Do you have a supervised exercise program for men on ADT? Can you refer me to a clinical exercise specialist or cancer rehabilitation program?' (general medical guidance)
If no supervised program is available: The trial tested supervised exercise specifically, so unsupervised exercise has not been shown to produce the same results. However, any physical activity is better than none — discuss with your doctor what is safe and appropriate for you (general medical guidance).
Discuss bone health with your care team: Ask whether bone density testing, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, and bone-protective medications are appropriate for your situation (general medical guidance).
Oncology nurses and advanced practice providers play a key role in exercise referral, symptom monitoring, and adherence support — ask your nursing team about resources.
Evidence Badges
Phase III RCT: STAMINA trial (ISRCTN46385239), n=700, 15 NHS centers Published in The Lancet Oncology (September 4, 2026) Primary endpoints: FACT-P (quality of life) and FACIT-F (fatigue) at 12 months 2 Independent Sources (duplicate Oncodaily entry removed)

The Hope

  • Hope: A non-drug intervention with meaningful quality-of-life benefit; empowers patients; improves physical function and metabolic health. Three serious adverse events occurred but all resolved.
  • Reality: Requires a time commitment (twice weekly supervised sessions for 13+ weeks); transportation, scheduling, and gym access can be barriers — especially for rural or low-income patients (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Reality: The trial was conducted in the UK NHS. Structured cancer exercise programs are not widely available in many healthcare systems; access and insurance coverage vary (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: Fatigue and other ADT side effects make starting exercise difficult — but the trial's high adherence with supervised initiation suggests that structured support overcomes this barrier.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

STAMINA model: Embedded in NHS cancer services, delivered by clinical exercise specialists (not personal trainers), with gym partnership for sustainability.

Nursing-led implementation: Oncology nurses and advanced practice providers are key for exercise referral, symptom monitoring, and adherence support.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Large RCT (n=700), active enhanced control (not passive usual care), high adherence, patient-reported outcomes, economic evaluation, and 12-month follow-up.

Limitations: 97% White participants — generalizability to Black, Asian, and Hispanic men is unknown. The 'Optimised Usual Care' was better than what most patients receive in real-world practice, so the real-world benefit of the STAMINA program may actually be larger than the trial measured.

Limitation: Dietary adherence was not measured.

Uncertainty: Long-term sustainability after the 12-month supervised period ends; how the program interacts with newer combination therapies is unknown.

Important: The source reports 3 serious adverse events related to the intervention (transient loss of consciousness, leg pain/weakness, back pain) — all resolved. The program is safe but not risk-free.

Gap: No data on cancer-specific survival — quality-of-life and surrogate endpoints only.

What To Watch

STAMINA long-term follow-up: Quality of life, fatigue, physical function, fracture rates, and healthcare utilization beyond 12 months.

Whether the STAMINA model is adopted or adapted in other healthcare systems, including potential US implementation (general medical guidance).

Whether future studies test the STAMINA approach in patients receiving newer triplet therapies, where toxicity management is particularly important (general medical guidance).
