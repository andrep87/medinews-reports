Perioperative Apalutamide Plus ADT Outperforms Surgery Alone for High-Risk Localized Prostate Cancer

"The PROTEUS substudy showed that adding 12 months of apalutamide (Erleada) plus ADT before and after radical prostatectomy reduced the risk of cancer recurrence or death by 62% compared to surgery alone in high-risk localized prostate cancer. At 3 years, 63% of men on the perioperative regimen remained event-free versus 41% with surgery alone, and metastasis risk was cut by 51%."

The PROTEUS substudy randomized 408 men with high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer (in a 1:3 ratio) to either perioperative apalutamide [a strong hormone-blocking pill] plus ADT (given before and after surgery) or surgery alone, to test whether adding hormone therapy around the time of radical prostatectomy (prostate removal) improves outcomes.
At about 38 months of follow-up, event-free survival [time without a rising PSA, imaging relapse, or death] was significantly better with apalutamide plus ADT than with surgery alone.
3-year EFS rates: 63% (apalutamide + ADT) vs 41% (surgery alone); metastasis-free survival HR 0.49 (95% CI 0.26–0.90); time to subsequent therapy HR 0.29 (95% CI 0.20–0.43).
Local/regional recurrences: 4 vs 51 patients; distant metastases: 4 vs 28 patients.
This is the first randomized trial directly comparing perioperative systemic therapy (apalutamide + ADT) plus surgery versus surgery alone for high-risk localized prostate cancer.
The main PROTEUS trial compared perioperative apalutamide + ADT versus ADT alone (active comparator); this substudy fills the critical gap by using the true standard of care—surgery alone—as the control.
Benefits persisted after testosterone recovery (median 20.7 months to testosterone ≥200 ng/dL, ~9 months after treatment completion), suggesting durable disease control beyond hormonal suppression.
Fewer men needed later salvage radiation or additional systemic therapy, which could help preserve quality of life (the exact rates are still being analyzed) (general medical guidance).
Men with newly diagnosed high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer (clinical stage T3a–T4, Gleason 8–10, PSA >20 ng/mL, or N1 nodal involvement) who are candidates for radical prostatectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection.
Men fit for both major surgery and 12 months of hormone therapy; participants were often in their mid-60s (general medical guidance).
Not for men with metastatic disease (M1) or very low-risk disease where active surveillance is appropriate.
Shared decision-making essential: some men prefer surgery, others prefer radiation; DASL-HiCaP (radiation-based) and PROTEUS (surgery-based) now offer intensified options for both paths.
If you are considering surgery for high-risk prostate cancer, you may wish to ask your urologist and oncologist: "Should I receive apalutamide plus ADT before and after surgery, as in PROTEUS?" (general medical guidance).
You may wish to request a multidisciplinary consultation (urology, radiation oncology, medical oncology) to compare surgery-based and radiation-based intensified approaches (general medical guidance).
You may wish to discuss fertility preservation and sexual-health counseling before starting ADT plus apalutamide, since testosterone will be suppressed for many months (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask about side-effect monitoring during treatment, including rash, fatigue, and fall/fracture risk (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: 62% reduction in recurrence/death risk; 51% reduction in metastasis; many patients avoid subsequent radiation and systemic therapy; potential for cure in subset with pathologic complete response.
  • Reality: this adds 12 months of daily pills plus ADT injections on top of major surgery, with side effects that can include hot flashes, fatigue, rash, and a higher fall risk (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Not all patients benefit equally: those over 75 may have less benefit (per main PROTEUS analysis); patient selection crucial.
  • Long-term OS benefit unproven; EFS is a surrogate endpoint; follow-up continues.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Apalutamide (Erleada): an androgen receptor inhibitor taken by mouth; in PROTEUS it was given for 12 months around the time of surgery (general medical guidance).

ADT: LHRH agonist/antagonist for 12 months concurrent with apalutamide.

Other trials are studying hormone therapy or newer agents given before and after surgery for high-risk disease; most are still in progress (general medical guidance).

Biomarker studies planned: genomic classifiers, ctDNA clearance, pathologic complete response (pT0) as surrogate endpoints.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: this substudy compared the regimen against true standard of care (surgery alone), with a clear event-free and metastasis-free survival benefit and a large comparison group from the 3:1 randomization.

Limitations: Substudy not powered for OS; OS data immature; 3:1 randomization means fewer patients in experimental arm (102 vs 306); EFS includes PSA recurrence which may not always translate to clinical benefit.

Quality of life data not yet reported—critical for a treatment that adds 12 months of systemic therapy to surgery.

Grade 3–4 adverse events higher with apalutamide + ADT (30.7% vs 21.7%); rash, fatigue, hypertension, fracture risk noted.

What To Watch

Overall survival results from PROTEUS main study and substudy (planned at later timepoints).

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes from PROTEUS substudy.

ctDNA dynamics: does clearance after neoadjuvant therapy predict long-term outcome?

Comparison of perioperative surgery-based (PROTEUS) vs radiation-based (DASL-HiCaP) intensification strategies in real-world practice.

Neoadjuvant/adjuvant trials with novel agents: PARP inhibitors, immunotherapy, radioligand therapy.
