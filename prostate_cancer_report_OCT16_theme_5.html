CRPC Treatment Sequencing Evolves with PARP Inhibitors, Radioligands, and Aggressive-Variant Recognition

"Castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) care is increasingly biomarker-driven: PARP inhibitors for BRCA2-mutated disease, Pluvicto for PSMA-positive disease, and radium-223 plus enzalutamide for bone-predominant disease (studied in PEACE-3). Clinicians also watch for aggressive-variant features (such as visceral spread, low PSA with bulky disease, or loss of RB1/TP53/PTEN) that may call for platinum-based chemotherapy."

For BRCA2-mutated CRPC after an ARPI, a PARP inhibitor is generally preferred; BRCA1, CDK12, and PALB2 alterations may also benefit, while ATM and CHEK2 generally do not (general medical guidance).
For PSMA-positive disease, Pluvicto improved outcomes versus switching ARSI (PSMAfore) and showed an overall survival benefit after chemotherapy (VISION); the TheraP trial compared Pluvicto directly with cabazitaxel chemotherapy.
For bone-predominant metastases, radium-223 plus enzalutamide showed an overall survival benefit in the PEACE-3 trial (studied in ARPI-naive patients with minimal symptoms) and is an NCCN Category 1 recommendation; a bone-protecting agent is needed to reduce fracture risk.
After docetaxel in PSMA-negative disease, cabazitaxel remains a standard option (general medical guidance).
For aggressive-variant features (visceral metastases, low PSA with high-volume disease, neuroendocrine features, or RB1/TP53/PTEN loss), adding platinum chemotherapy is often considered (general medical guidance).
Sequence matters: PARP inhibitors tend to work best before chemotherapy in BRCA2 disease, and Pluvicto may be better tolerated than cabazitaxel while preserving chemotherapy for later (general medical guidance).
Cognitive side effects differ between hormone-blocking pills: data from the ARACOG comparison suggest darolutamide causes less cognitive decline than enzalutamide, which can matter for patients who need to preserve cognition.
Recognizing aggressive-variant disease changes therapy from AR-targeted pills to platinum-based chemotherapy; missing it can lead to rapid progression on ineffective hormone therapy (general medical guidance).
Simply switching between abiraterone and enzalutamide generally offers limited benefit and is usually avoided unless there are no other options (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: multiple effective lines of therapy can extend life; biomarker matching improves the benefit-to-toxicity balance; Pluvicto and PARP inhibitors offer non-chemotherapy options; and a cognitive-sparing hormone option (darolutamide) is available (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: there is no curative therapy for CRPC, and each line typically adds months; costs are high and access to radioligand centers and timely genetic testing is uneven; and side effects can accumulate (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Aggressive-variant and neuroendocrine disease carry a poor prognosis despite platinum chemotherapy (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

PARP inhibitors (for example, olaparib, rucaparib, talazoparib, niraparib): differ in dosing, side effects (such as anemia and fatigue), and approved combinations (general medical guidance).

Pluvicto (177Lu-PSMA-617): a PSMA-targeted radioligand given by IV over several cycles at an authorized site; side effects can include dry mouth, low blood counts, and rarely kidney injury (general medical guidance).

Radium-223 (Xofigo): a bone-targeted alpha-emitter for bone-predominant disease; a bone-protecting agent reduces fracture risk (general medical guidance).

Cabazitaxel (Jevtana): a chemotherapy option after docetaxel; side effects include low blood counts, neuropathy, and diarrhea (general medical guidance).

Emerging approaches include new bispecific T-cell engagers (targeting PSMA or STEAP1), antibody-drug conjugates, actinium-based radioligands, and bipolar androgen therapy, all investigational (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

For PARP inhibitors, the clearest overall survival benefit is in BRCA2 disease; benefit in non-BRCA HRR alterations is less certain (general medical guidance).

For Pluvicto, VISION showed an overall survival benefit after chemotherapy; in the pre-chemotherapy setting (PSMAfore) progression-free survival improved but overall survival interpretation is complicated by crossover (general medical guidance).

For radium-223 plus enzalutamide, PEACE-3 showed an overall survival benefit, with fracture risk a key consideration unless a bone-protecting agent is used (general medical guidance).

For aggressive-variant disease, platinum benefit comes largely from smaller studies rather than large phase 3 trials (general medical guidance).

What To Watch
