Managing the Side Effects of Hormone Therapy — Cardiovascular, Bone, Cognitive, and Sexual Health

"Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) remains central to prostate cancer treatment but causes cardiovascular, bone, cognitive, and sexual side effects. New data point toward structured survivorship care — cardiovascular risk management, bone density screening, and attention to cognitive and sexual health. Several interventions are promising but many remain early-stage."

An FDG PET/MRI study (43 men) found that ADT caused progressive arterial inflammation over 6 months, pointing to a direct vascular mechanism for cardiovascular risk. This was a small, single-center study using an imaging marker, not clinical events (audit correction).
A VA study (2,487 patients) found that systematic cardiology referral improved cholesterol and blood pressure but did not reduce major cardiovascular events over 5.8 years.
The HERO trial found that relugolix [an oral GnRH antagonist taken by mouth] was associated with more than a 50% lower rate of major cardiovascular events compared with leuprolide, particularly in men with prior cardiovascular events (audit correction: a specific hazard ratio stated in the draft was not found in the provided sources).
Vortioxetine, an approved antidepressant, reversed ADT-related cognitive deficits in animal models without compromising cancer control. This is preclinical work; the sources note human testing could move quickly because the drug is already approved (audit correction: the draft stated human trials were planned as fact).
A quality-of-life analysis found that adding ADT to salvage radiation worsened sexual and hormonal quality of life at 6 weeks and 1 year, but not at 5 years.
Cardiovascular disease is a major cause of death in men with metastatic prostate cancer, and ADT can accelerate metabolic and vascular changes within months (general medical guidance).
Routine primary care screening may miss bone and cardiovascular risks in men on ADT, suggesting a role for structured survivorship care (general medical guidance).
Cognitive effects of ADT may be distinct from normal aging, and the sources raise the possibility they could be addressed with existing drugs — though this is not yet established in humans.
Sexual side effects are common but, according to the quality-of-life analysis, may lessen over time for many men.
Men starting ADT for localized high-risk, recurrent, or metastatic prostate cancer.
Men with prior cardiovascular events or multiple risk factors, who may be most relevant to the relugolix cardiovascular findings.
Older men and those with baseline low bone density, who face higher fracture risk on ADT (general medical guidance).
Men experiencing hot flashes, cognitive changes, or sexual dysfunction on ADT.
When starting ADT, you may wish to ask your care team about cardiovascular risk assessment and monitoring, including cholesterol and blood pressure (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask whether bone density testing is appropriate before or during ADT, and whether bone-protective measures are warranted in your situation (general medical guidance).
If you have prior cardiovascular disease, you may wish to ask whether an oral GnRH antagonist such as relugolix is appropriate for you, given the HERO cardiovascular findings (general medical guidance).
If you experience hot flashes, cognitive changes, or sexual difficulties on ADT, raising these with your care team is reasonable — several are treatable (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: Structured survivorship care addressing cardiovascular, bone, cognitive, and sexual health can be delivered with existing tools, and relugolix may offer a lower-cardiovascular-risk option for some men.
  • Reality: Many oncology practices lack dedicated survivorship clinics, and coordination across specialties is uncommon (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Cognitive and sexual side effects are often under-discussed, and structured screening is not routine (general medical guidance).
  • Cost and insurance coverage differ across these interventions (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Relugolix (Orgovyx): An oral GnRH antagonist associated with a lower rate of major cardiovascular events than leuprolide in the HERO trial (general medical guidance).

Bone-protective agents: Used to reduce fracture risk during ADT; your care team can advise on whether and when these apply (general medical guidance).

Vortioxetine: An approved antidepressant that reversed ADT-related cognitive deficits in animal models; human testing has not yet been established (audit correction).

Several approaches to hot flashes and metabolic effects of ADT are described in the sources as options to discuss with a clinician (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

Arterial inflammation study: Observational, used an imaging marker rather than clinical events, and was single-center with 43 patients — it needs prospective validation (audit correction).

Relugolix cardiovascular benefit: The HERO finding is important, but the absolute risk reduction in the overall population was modest, and adherence to a daily oral drug matters (audit correction: specific adherence-failure figures in the draft were not sourced).

The cardiovascular referral trial did not reduce major cardiovascular events, in part because event rates were lower than expected.

Vortioxetine evidence is from animal models only; human dose, timing, and effect are unknown (audit correction).

The quality-of-life analysis found that sexual and hormonal detriment from ADT was largely transient, though individual experiences vary.

What To Watch

Extension of the arterial inflammation imaging work to larger studies (general medical guidance).

Publication of cognitive data comparing different hormone-pathway drugs (general medical guidance).

Whether human trials of vortioxetine for ADT-related cognitive effects are initiated (audit correction).

Development of structured survivorship care models for men on ADT (general medical guidance).

Guideline updates incorporating cardiovascular and bone health in ADT survivorship (general medical guidance).
