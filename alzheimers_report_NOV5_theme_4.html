Care Quality Crisis—Antipsychotic Overuse, Racial Disparities, and the Push for Person-Centered Alternatives

"Despite decades of warnings, antipsychotic drugs are still widely used as "chemical restraints" in US nursing homes—often without trying non-drug approaches first. Black and Hispanic dementia patients are significantly less likely to access specialized dementia care units. New federal reports expose hidden prescribing, while the DICE framework and caregiver-support research offer practical, evidence-based alternatives that treat behaviors as communication of unmet needs."

Two 2026 federal OIG reports found US nursing homes still routinely use antipsychotics as "chemical restraints" for dementia behaviors (agitation, aggression) without first attempting required non-pharmacological interventions; some facilities hide true rates by misdiagnosing residents with schizophrenia (excluded from quality metrics). National prescribing data: Antipsychotic use in adults 65+ increased 52% (2015–2024); long-term use (>4 months) rose 65%; >8 million older Americans received at least one prescription. FDA black-box warning (2005) and Five-Star rating penalties reduced use temporarily, but it rebounded during COVID and remains high (~17% of long-stay residents).
JAMA Network Open study (1.4M Medicare beneficiaries with dementia, 2018–2020): Only 17.9% accessed Alzheimer's Special Care Units (ASCUs); Black patients (1.01%) and Hispanic patients (0.71%) were half as likely as White patients (1.46%) to be admitted to ASCUs—hospital discharge planning explained much of the disparity. Antipsychotics in dementia: Marginal benefit (barely better than placebo for agitation), serious risks (death, stroke, falls, accelerated decline). They sedate but don't address root causes—pain, infection, hunger, fear, boredom, sensory overload.
"Behaviors are communication": Wandering may mean hunger; resisting bath may mean pain or cold; shouting may mean fear. The DICE approach (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate) teaches caregivers to decode this language. (general medical guidance)
ASCU disparity: Specialized units offer trained staff, tailored environments, structured activities—denying access to Black/Hispanic patients perpetuates inequity in care quality and outcomes. Hospital discharge planners are key gatekeepers. (general medical guidance)
Caregiver health = patient health: 40–60% of spousal caregivers have insomnia; only children lack sibling support; couple sleep is bidirectional. Supporting caregivers reduces nursing home placement, hospitalizations, and mortality for patients. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

The DICE approach (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate) for behavioural symptoms was developed by Helen Kales at UC Davis (independent fact-check: the draft attributed it to UCSF/University of Toronto) (general medical guidance).

Non-drug modalities with evidence: Sensory (music, massage, outdoor), exercise, cognitive stimulation, reminiscence therapy—reduce agitation/depression as well or better than drugs in meta-analyses.

Auvelity (dextromethorphan/bupropion): FDA-approved April 2026 for Alzheimer's agitation; less sedating than antipsychotics, but experts warn broad "agitation" definition may expand medicating instead of investigating. (general medical guidance)

ASCU model: Specialized nursing home units with dementia-trained staff, secure environments, therapeutic programming—JAMA study shows access disparity; state funding (LA, AZ) supports specialist navigation roles. (general medical guidance)

Mizzou trials: (1) Online CBT-I for spousal caregivers; (2) Couple sleep-cognition dyadic analysis (ELSA data); (3) Only-child vs. sibling caregiver longitudinal study with inflammatory/CV biomarkers. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Disparity study: Observational (Medicare claims); hospital discharge planning mediates disparity—suggests systemic bias, not patient preference; ASCU definition requires state designation, not all specialized care captured. (general medical guidance)

DICE/WeCareAdvisor: App RCT improved caregiver distress/well-being but not behavior frequency/severity at 3 months; implementation fidelity in real-world settings variable; requires staff buy-in and time. (general medical guidance)

Caregiver interventions: CBT-I gold standard for insomnia; dyadic sleep-cognition link novel but observational; only-child caregiver data scarce—Mizzou study filling gap. (general medical guidance)

Auvelity: Approved based on phase 3 trials; long-term safety in frail elderly unknown; agitation scales in trials include pacing/restlessness—may lower threshold for prescribing.

What To Watch

CMS response to Project PAUSE lobbying: Will antipsychotic measure stay in Five-Star ratings? Will minimum staffing standards be reinstated? (general medical guidance)

DICE implementation science: Scaling from research to routine nursing home practice—training, fidelity measures, reimbursement for non-drug care time. (general medical guidance)

Mizzou trial results: CBT-I efficacy for spousal caregivers; dyadic sleep-cognition findings; only-child caregiver biomarker trajectories. (general medical guidance)
