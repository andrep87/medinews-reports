Breaking the Immunotherapy-Resistant Barrier in MSS Metastatic Colorectal Cancer: Novel Combinations and Targets

"Most metastatic colorectal cancers are microsatellite stable (MSS) [the common type that does not respond well to standard immunotherapy]. New strategies — a TGF-beta/PD-1 bispecific (INCA33890), IL-1-alpha blockade (vilamakitug), a KRAS G12D inhibitor (QTX3034), and a PD-1/IL-15 fusion (SAR445877) — are being tested to make these 'cold' tumors respond, with early signals of activity. Most are early-stage, though one (INCA33890) has a phase III trial underway."

INCA33890 (a TGF-beta/PD-1 bispecific antibody [a single molecule that hits two targets at once to switch the immune system back on]): an early-phase study showed a 15% response rate in heavily pretreated MSS metastatic colorectal cancer. A phase III trial (NEXERA-303) is now enrolling first-line patients.
Vilamakitug (anti-IL-1α antibody): TASKIN phase II (refractory mCRC, n=159) met primary efficacy criterion — 61% alive at 12 months vs 56% control. Striking benefit in right-sided tumors: 62% reduction in death risk (HR 0.38), linked to high baseline IL-1α expression.
QTX3034 (KRAS G12D inhibitor): FDA Fast Track granted for QTX3034 + cetuximab ± FOLFOX in KRAS G12D-mutated CRC. Phase I: 40% ORR in CRC cohort (n=5) at 1200 mg BID + cetuximab; >90% ctDNA reduction in 57% of patients at that dose.
SAR445877 (PD-1/IL-15 fusion): First-in-class molecule designed to activate T cells via PD-1 blockade and IL-15 cytokine stimulation — targeting "cold" MSS tumors; early-phase trials ongoing.
Regorafenib (Stivarga) remains standard later-line oral therapy for refractory mCRC (CORRECT: OS 6.4 vs 5.0 mo, HR 0.77); tolerability managed via dose modulation (160→120→80 mg).
3D models (organoids, bioprinting, tumor-on-chip) now incorporate stroma/immune components to better predict chemo/immunotherapy resistance — but not yet clinically validated for individual prediction.
Hidden antigen discovery: MSS tumors present comparable numbers of tumor antigens as MSI tumors, but from different sources (non-canonical reading frames); could enable vaccines/personalized immunotherapy for MSS.
MSS mCRC represents ~85% of metastatic cases and has historically derived minimal benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors alone — these novel agents target distinct resistance mechanisms (TGF-β immunosuppression, IL-1α inflammation, KRAS signaling, T-cell exhaustion).
Right-sided tumors (historically worse prognosis, less responsive to EGFR inhibitors) show particular sensitivity to IL-1α blockade — a biomarker-defined subset for the first time.
KRAS G12D is a common KRAS mutation in colorectal cancer; QTX3034 plus cetuximab offers a targeted combination for a group that has lacked a mutation-directed option (general medical guidance).
Combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy/bevacizumab (NEXERA-303) tests whether "priming" the microenvironment converts cold tumors to hot — a rational combinatorial strategy.
Patients with metastatic colorectal cancer that is MSS/pMMR (microsatellite stable/proficient mismatch repair) — the majority of mCRC.
Specifically: Right-sided primary tumor patients (vilamakitug benefit); KRAS G12D-mutated patients (QTX3034 trials); heavily pretreated patients progressing on standard chemo/biologics (regorafenib, TAS-102, clinical trials).
First-line MSS mCRC patients eligible for NEXERA-303: Stage IV, measurable disease, ECOG 0–1, no prior metastatic treatment, no BRAF V600E mutation, no MSI-H/dMMR.
Patients interested in novel mechanisms: Those who have exhausted standard options and seek trials targeting TGF-β, IL-1α, KRAS G12D, or T-cell activation pathways.

The Hope

  • Hope: Multiple novel mechanisms (TGF-β, IL-1α, KRAS G12D, IL-15) simultaneously in late-stage development — unprecedented for MSS mCRC.
  • Hope: Right-sided tumor benefit with vilamakitug addresses a historically poor-prognosis subset with a biologically rational target.
  • Reality: most are early-phase (though INCA33890 has a phase III trial); phase III success rates in MSS CRC have been low (many failed immunotherapy combinations).

The Reality

  • Reality: Toxicity of combinations (e.g., INCA33890 + chemo/bevacizumab, QTX3034 + cetuximab anaphylaxis risk) may limit tolerability in frail, heavily pretreated patients.
  • Reality: Biomarker-driven subsets (right-sided, KRAS G12D) fragment an already small population — trial enrollment and commercial viability challenging.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

INCA33890 (Incyte): TGF-β/PD-1 bispecific; NEXERA-303 phase III (first-line + FOLFOX/bevacizumab) enrolling; monotherapy ORR 15% in pretreated MSS.

Vilamakitug (XBiotech/Natrunix): Anti-IL-1α mAb; TASKIN phase II positive in refractory mCRC + TAS-102/bevacizumab; right-sided biomarker signal; advancing to registrational trials.

QTX3034 (Quanta Therapeutics): Oral KRAS G12D inhibitor; phase I + cetuximab ± mFOLFOX6; FDA Fast Track; ESMO 2026 data presentation (Abstract 790RO).

SAR445877 (Sanofi): PD-1/IL-15 fusion protein; phase I in MSS CRC; novel dual immune-activation mechanism.

Regorafenib (Stivarga, Bayer): Approved 3rd-line+ mCRC; standard dose 160 mg days 1–21/28; dose reduction to 120/80 mg for toxicity; monitor LFTs, BP, skin, bleeding.

BRICKET (ESMO 2026): ctDNA-guided retreatment with encorafenib/cetuximab in BRAF V600E mCRC after prior benefit.

ECLYPse/TRIDENTE (ESMO 2026): Continuation/rechallenge strategies for BRAF V600E mCRC on encorafenib/cetuximab.

Evidence Uncertainty

INCA33890: Phase I n=105, ORR 15% (monotherapy); NEXERA-303 phase III ongoing — no efficacy data yet for combination; exclusion of BRAF V600E limits generalizability.

Vilamakitug (TASKIN): 159 patients, a phase II trial that met its primary survival criterion (patients alive at 12 months); other endpoints were less clear-cut, and a larger confirmatory trial is needed.

QTX3034: Phase I n=71 total, CRC combination cohort n=5 at target dose — very small; ORR 40% but unconfirmed responses included; ctDNA reduction promising but surrogate.

SAR445877: Preclinical/early clinical rationale only; no human efficacy data yet in CRC.

3D models: Organoids showed 100% sensitivity/93% specificity for targeted therapy prediction in 21-patient study (Vlachogiannis 2018) — small, selected cohort; bioprinted models used cell lines, not patient-derived.

What To Watch

NEXERA-303 enrollment completion and PFS readout (first-line MSS + INCA33890).

Vilamakitug registrational trial design and launch (likely right-sided enriched).

QTX3034 phase II expansion data (ESMO 2026 Abstract 790RO; further cohorts with mFOLFOX6).

SAR445877 phase I dose escalation/expansion results in MSS CRC.

CheckMate 8HW final OS (ESMO 2026 LBA32): Nivolumab + ipilimumab vs chemo/nivo in MSI-H mCRC — sets benchmark for immunotherapy combinations.

IROCAS adjuvant mFOLFIRINOX vs mFOLFOX6 in high-risk stage III (ESMO 2026 LBA29) — may influence early-stage MSS treatment intensity.

3D model clinical validation studies: Can PDO/bioprinted drug screening prospectively guide patient therapy?
