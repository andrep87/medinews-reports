Neoadjuvant Immunotherapy Combinations Reshape Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer Treatment

"Multiple phase III and II trials now show that adding immunotherapy (PD-1 inhibitors) to short-course radiotherapy and chemotherapy before surgery dramatically improves outcomes in locally advanced rectal cancer — even in the majority mismatch repair-proficient (pMMR) population. The UNION trial reported a 67% reduction in recurrence/death risk at 3 years, while organ preservation rates exceed 75% with long-course chemoradiation, offering a path to cure with less mutilating surgery."

The UNION trial (China, 231 patients) randomized locally advanced rectal cancer (cT3-4/N+) to short-course radiotherapy (SCRT, 25 Gy in 5 fractions) plus camrelizumab (a PD-1 inhibitor) and CAPOX chemotherapy, versus standard long-course chemoradiation plus chemotherapy. The primary endpoint — pathologic complete response (no cancer found at surgery) — was significantly higher with the immunotherapy regimen (39.8% vs 15.3%); the 3-year event-free survival (a secondary endpoint) reported at ESMO 2026 also favored it (independent fact-check: the draft described event-free survival as the primary endpoint; the primary endpoint was pathologic complete response).
STAR-TREC (Europe, 503 patients, early/intermediate stage) showed long-course chemoradiation (LCCRT) achieved 78.5% organ preservation (no surgery, no stoma, no local recurrence) at 12 months vs 60.6% with SCRT (HR 1.90) — establishing LCCRT as superior for organ preservation.
NEOASIS has opened a dMMR rectal cancer organ-preservation cohort testing 6 weeks of botensilimab+balstilimab before surgery — building on 72% pCR in colon cohort.
ASTRO 2026 presentations: STELLAR II (sintilimab + SCRT + CAPOX in pMMR) doubled complete response (27%→52%); TORCH-iTNT (toripalimab ± SCRT) showed SCRT boosted complete response from 37% to 62% when IO given in both arms.
Historically, immunotherapy only worked in dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancer (~5%); UNION and STELLAR II prove PD-1 inhibitors can boost responses in pMMR/MSS tumors (95% of cases) when combined with SCRT — radiation may "heat up" cold tumors by releasing antigens.
SCRT (5 days) + immunotherapy + chemo is logistically simpler and faster than 5.5 weeks of daily chemoradiation — patients reach surgery sooner (median 72 vs 113 days in UNION) yet achieve higher pathologic complete response rates (39.8% vs 15.3%).
Organ preservation (watch-and-wait) is now a validated strategy: STAR-TREC proves standardized LCCRT + surveillance spares the rectum in ~80% of early-stage patients at 1 year without compromising cancer control.
Total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) — giving all chemo/radiation/immunotherapy before surgery — is becoming the new standard, allowing response assessment and treatment de-escalation.
Patients with locally advanced rectal adenocarcinoma (cT3–4 or node-positive, within 10–12 cm of anal verge) — UNION required cT3–4/N+; STAR-TREC included mrT1–3bN0 early-stage.
All mismatch repair statuses — UNION and STELLAR II enrolled predominantly pMMR/MSS patients; NEOASIS rectal cohort focuses on dMMR; AZUR-1 established dostarlimab for dMMR/MSI-H.
Patients fit for combined modality therapy (ECOG 0–1, adequate organ function) and willing to undergo intensive surveillance (MRI, endoscopy, DRE q3–4mo) if pursuing organ preservation.
Not for metastatic disease — these are curative-intent neoadjuvant strategies for resectable or potentially resectable locally advanced disease.
If newly diagnosed with locally advanced rectal cancer, you may wish to ask: "Will my case be reviewed by a multidisciplinary tumor board?" (general medical guidance).
You may wish to request MMR/MSI and biomarker testing on your diagnostic biopsy, since results determine eligibility for immunotherapy-containing regimens (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask your radiation oncologist whether short-course radiotherapy (about 5 days) is an option for you, and whether it could be combined with immunotherapy as in these trials (general medical guidance).
If early-stage, you may wish to ask whether organ preservation (chemoradiation plus close surveillance, as in STAR-TREC) could be appropriate for you (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask for a clear written surveillance plan if you pursue organ preservation (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: A future where most rectal cancer patients receive 5 days of radiation + immunotherapy + chemo, achieve complete response, and keep their rectum — avoiding permanent colostomy, sexual dysfunction, and chronic bowel issues.
  • Reality: Grade 3+ toxicity remains ~50% with intensive neoadjuvant regimens; immune-related adverse events (colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies) add complexity; surveillance burden is high (frequent MRI/endoscopy for years).

The Reality

  • Access: UNION regimen uses camrelizumab (not FDA-approved); US trials testing pembrolizumab/nivolumab + SCRT needed. Organ preservation requires specialized centers with strict surveillance protocols — not universally available.
  • Resistance: even with immunotherapy added, many pMMR/MSS patients still do not achieve a complete response (roughly half did not in these trials), so biomarkers to predict benefit and salvage strategies are needed (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Camrelizumab (SHR-1210, Jiangsu Hengrui): Anti-PD-1; approved in China for multiple cancers; used in UNION at 200 mg IV q3w through neoadjuvant/adjuvant phases.

Sintilimab (Tyvyt, Innovent): Anti-PD-1; STELLAR II phase 2/3 in pMMR LARC with SCRT+CAPOX; 3-yr DFS primary endpoint pending.

Toripalimab (Loqtorzi, Junshi): Anti-PD-1; TORCH-iTNT showed SCRT added to CAPOX+toripalimab boosted complete response.

Dostarlimab (Jemperli, GSK): FDA-approved for dMMR/MSI-H LARC; AZUR-1 established monotherapy efficacy.

Botensilimab/Balstilimab (Agenus): Fc-enhanced CTLA-4 + PD-1; NEOASIS rectal organ-preservation cohort enrolling.

Evidence Uncertainty

UNION: Phase III but open-label; benefit combines SCRT schedule change + camrelizumab — cannot isolate immunotherapy contribution; EFS benefit clear but OS immature; safety similar between arms (grade 3+ AE ~50% both).

STAR-TREC: Phase 2/3, patient-preference design in phase 3 (not fully randomized for organ preservation); 12-month organ preservation primary endpoint — long-term oncologic equivalence to surgery not yet proven.

STELLAR II/TORCH-iTNT: Phase 2/3 and phase 2; cross-trial comparisons invalid; complete response includes clinical complete response (cCR) not just pathologic (pCR); organ preservation allowed.

All trials predominantly Asian (UNION, STELLAR II, TORCH) or European (STAR-TREC) populations — generalizability to diverse US populations needs confirmation.

What To Watch

UNION overall survival final analysis (the primary endpoint, pathologic complete response, was met; longer-term survival data are maturing).

STELLAR II phase 3 primary endpoint: 3-year DFS in pMMR LARC with sintilimab + SCRT + CAPOX.

STAR-TREC long-term (3–5 year) local recurrence and survival data for organ preservation vs surgery.

NEOASIS rectal cohort results: Can 6 weeks of BOT+BAL achieve high cCR/pCR in dMMR rectal cancer?

US cooperative group trials (NRG, Alliance) testing FDA-approved PD-1 inhibitors + SCRT in pMMR LARC.

Biomarker-driven trials: ctDNA clearance during neoadjuvant therapy as early surrogate for organ preservation success.
