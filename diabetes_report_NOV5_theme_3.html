Health Equity Gaps Persist in Diabetes Access, Diagnosis, and Treatment

"Critical mismatches exist between who needs diabetes care and who receives it. Asian Americans develop diabetes at lower BMIs but are denied GLP-1 coverage until BMI 30. Medicare is expanding CGM coverage for non-insulin type 2 diabetes, but prescribing of heart/kidney-protective SGLT2/GLP-1 drugs lags guidelines. Food insecurity drives uncontrolled diabetes, and postpartum breastfeeding support fails women with higher BMI."

Asian American physician (BMI 26.5) screened for diabetes per ADA guidelines (BMI ≥23) but denied GLP-1 coverage because FDA label requires BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with comorbidity); trials excluded Asians using Western obesity thresholds.
CMS expected to decide by year-end 2026 on expanding Medicare CGM coverage to type 2 diabetes not on insulin—Dexcom's NEJM-published study showed benefit; Abbott cites slow growth without it.
JAMA Network Open study: After 2019 ADA guidelines prioritized SGLT2/GLP-1 for heart/kidney protection, prescribing rose but gaps remained; clinicians with higher knowledge scores prescribed more guideline-concordantly.
Local analyses show that Food insecurity (by ZIP code) strongly associated with uncontrolled type 2 diabetes; data now used to target interventions.
Women with T2D had lower breastfeeding rates (24% full vs 64% in normal-BMI controls); difference disappeared when matched for BMI, suggesting BMI not diabetes drives the gap.
BMI thresholds ignore body composition: Asians carry more visceral fat at lower BMI; South Asians reach same diabetes risk as white adults at BMI 23.9 vs 30. Denying treatment at BMI 26.5 withholds effective therapy from high-risk patients. (general medical guidance)
CGM without insulin: Real-time glucose data helps non-insulin users understand food/activity effects, reduce hypoglycemia fear, and engage in self-management—currently a coverage gap. (general medical guidance)
SGLT2/GLP-1 underuse: These drugs reduce heart failure hospitalization and kidney decline; eligible patients missing out face preventable complications.
Food insecurity → uncontrolled diabetes → ER visits/hospitalizations: Addressing nutrition access is direct diabetes care, not just social service. (general medical guidance)
Breastfeeding support must account for metabolic health: Women with higher BMI need tailored lactation support, not assumptions about diabetes itself. (general medical guidance)

The Hope

  • Hope: Policy levers exist—payers can adopt ethnicity-adjusted BMI today; CMS can expand CGM; clinics can integrate food security screening/referral. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: Prior authorization bureaucracy delays/denies care; CGM expansion faces cost concerns ($200–$400/month); SGLT2/GLP-1 cost remains barrier even with indications. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Structural drivers: Food insecurity requires housing, wage, transport solutions beyond healthcare; breastfeeding support needs paid leave, lactation consultants, pump access. (general medical guidance)
  • Patient advocacy: Individual appeals work but systemic change needs collective action (e.g., ADA/ASN joint statements, congressional hearings on drug pricing).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Pharmacy-led models: Pharmasave/Diabetes Canada provide free virtual education + pharmacist CGM support + monitoring products—scalable community access model. (general medical guidance)

Evidence Uncertainty

Asian BMI disparity: Supported by WHO 2004, iCARDIO 2025, STEP 11 trial (semaglutide effective at BMI ≥25 in Korea/Thailand); but FDA labels and payer policies lag. (general medical guidance)

CGM in non-insulin T2D: Dexcom RCT (NEJM 2026) showed HbA1c reduction; but long-term adherence, cost-effectiveness, and optimal user phenotype need study. (general medical guidance)

Prescribing gap study: Observational, US Medicare data only; cannot prove guidelines caused changes; NHS applicability uncertain (different formulary, NICE guidance). (general medical guidance)

Food insecurity link: Ecological (ZIP-code level), not individual; association not causation; intervention trials needed to test if food provision improves diabetes control. (general medical guidance)
