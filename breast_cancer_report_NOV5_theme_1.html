Trastuzumab Deruxtecan (Enhertu) Reshaping HER2-Positive Breast Cancer Treatment Across All Stages

"Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd/Enhertu) has secured major regulatory approvals and practice-changing trial data across early and metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer, establishing a new standard that significantly reduces recurrence risk after surgery and improves progression-free survival in advanced disease, though it requires careful monitoring for lung toxicity."

The European Commission approved trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu) as a single-agent treatment after surgery for adults with HER2-positive early breast cancer who still had cancer remaining after pre-surgery (neoadjuvant) treatment (independent fact-check: the EU approval confirmed in sources covers this post-neoadjuvant setting).
In DESTINY-Breast05, three-year invasive disease-free survival reached 92.4% with trastuzumab deruxtecan versus 83.7% with trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), a 53% relative reduction in the hazard of invasive recurrence or death (HR 0.47, p<0.0001).
The FDA previously approved trastuzumab deruxtecan for the same post-neoadjuvant indication (May 2026), plus separate neoadjuvant and first-line metastatic approvals supported by DESTINY-Breast11 (pCR 67.3%) and DESTINY-Breast09 (median PFS 40.7 vs 26.9 months with pertuzumab combination).
Trastuzumab deruxtecan is now authorized across the entire HER2-positive treatment continuum: neoadjuvant, post-neoadjuvant, first-line metastatic, and later lines, representing a fundamental shift in how this antibody-drug conjugate is used.
For patients with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy—historically a high-risk group—trastuzumab deruxtecan offers a substantially more effective post-surgery option than the previous standard T-DM1, potentially sparing more women from recurrence.
The magnitude of benefit (8.7% absolute improvement in 3-year invasive disease-free survival) is clinically meaningful because it builds on an already effective standard (T-DM1), not chemotherapy alone.
In the metastatic setting, the DESTINY-Breast09 regimen (trastuzumab deruxtecan + pertuzumab) delays progression by a median of nearly 14 months compared to the prior standard (trastuzumab + pertuzumab + taxane), offering a chemotherapy-free first-line option.
This expansion reflects a broader oncology trend: moving highly active drugs from advanced disease into earlier, curative-intent settings where they may prevent recurrence entirely.
Adults with HER2-positive early breast cancer (IHC 3+ or ISH-positive) who have undergone neoadjuvant taxane-based chemotherapy plus HER2-targeted therapy and have residual invasive disease in the breast or lymph nodes at surgery.
Patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer eligible for first-line treatment, particularly those seeking a chemotherapy-free regimen (trastuzumab deruxtecan + pertuzumab).
The post-neoadjuvant approval specifically studied a high-risk population (initially inoperable or node-positive after neoadjuvant therapy), though the regulatory label is broader.
HER2-low or HER2-ultralow patients are not included in these specific approvals; separate indications exist for hormone receptor-positive HER2-low metastatic disease after endocrine therapy.
If you have HER2-positive early breast cancer and are completing neoadjuvant therapy, ask your surgeon and medical oncologist: "Will my pathology report show residual invasive disease? If so, am I a candidate for adjuvant trastuzumab deruxtecan instead of T-DM1?"
You may wish to request a baseline pulmonary function assessment and discuss a monitoring plan for interstitial lung disease (ILD) before starting trastuzumab deruxtecan, as adjudicated drug-related ILD occurred in 9.6% of DESTINY-Breast05 patients (0.9% grade 3, two fatal cases).
If you have metastatic HER2-positive disease and have not started first-line treatment, ask: "Am I eligible for trastuzumab deruxtecan plus pertuzumab as a chemotherapy-free option, and how does the side effect profile compare to taxane-based regimens?"
Ensure your care team coordinates radiotherapy timing with trastuzumab deruxtecan if postmastectomy radiation is planned; DESTINY-Breast05 showed no major ILD difference with concurrent vs sequential radiation, but close pulmonary monitoring remains essential.

The Hope

  • Hope: Trastuzumab deruxtecan offers a genuine chance to cure more women with high-risk early HER2-positive disease by eliminating microscopic residual cancer after surgery; metastatic patients gain a highly effective, chemotherapy-free first-line option with impressive PFS.
  • Reality: The 8.7% absolute IDFS improvement means ~9 out of 100 women avoid recurrence at 3 years who would have recurred on T-DM1—but 7-8% still recur on trastuzumab deruxtecan. It is not a guarantee.

The Reality

  • Access: EU approval ≠ immediate reimbursement; national health systems may delay availability. US patients face prior authorization hurdles and high out-of-pocket costs despite FDA approval. (general medical guidance)
  • Toxicity trade-off: ILD risk (including death) is real and requires lifelong vigilance; patients must weigh this against recurrence fear, especially in adjuvant setting where many are already disease-free after surgery.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu, Daiichi Sankyo/AstraZeneca): HER2-directed ADC with topoisomerase I inhibitor payload; approved in EU/US for post-neoadjuvant residual disease, neoadjuvant, and first-line metastatic (with pertuzumab) HER2-positive breast cancer. (general medical guidance)

Trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla/T-DM1, Roche): Previous post-neoadjuvant standard after KATHERINE trial; now superseded by trastuzumab deruxtecan for eligible patients based on DESTINY-Breast05 superiority.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan + pertuzumab (Perjeta, Roche): FDA-approved December 2025 for first-line metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer per DESTINY-Breast09 (median PFS 40.7 vs 26.9 months, HR 0.56). (general medical guidance)

WSG ADAPT-HER2-IV trial (presenting SABCS 2026): Testing neoadjuvant trastuzumab deruxtecan monotherapy vs standard multi-drug regimens in stage I-II HER2-positive disease; could further de-escalate chemotherapy use.

Evidence Uncertainty

DESTINY-Breast05: 1,635 patients randomized; primary endpoint IDFS HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.34–0.66); 30-month median follow-up; OS immature (2.9% deaths); open-label design; high-risk enrollment criteria (node-positive or initially inoperable) may overestimate benefit in lower-risk residual disease.

DESTINY-Breast09: PFS HR 0.56; OS data not mature; comparator was taxane/trastuzumab/pertuzumab, not trastuzumab deruxtecan-based regimens.

ILD/pneumonitis remains the critical safety concern: 9.6% incidence in adjuvant setting, including fatal cases; requires proactive monitoring (baseline imaging, patient education, prompt symptom evaluation).

Long-term durability of benefit, impact on subsequent treatment options after recurrence, and optimal sequencing with other ADCs remain unknown.

What To Watch

Mature overall survival data from DESTINY-Breast05 and DESTINY-Breast09—will early recurrence reduction translate to longer life?

WSG ADAPT-HER2-IV results at SABCS 2026 (GS3-5): Can trastuzumab deruxtecan monotherapy replace intensive neoadjuvant chemo in early HER2+ disease?

DESTINY-Breast11 long-term follow-up: Neoadjuvant trastuzumab deruxtecan pCR 67.3%—does this translate to event-free survival benefit?

Optimal sequencing after trastuzumab deruxtecan exposure: If recurrence occurs, do subsequent HER2-directed ADCs (T-DM1, tucatinib combinations, novel ADCs) retain efficacy?

Real-world ILD incidence and management strategies as adoption expands into community practice.
