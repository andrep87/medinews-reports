Tarlatamab Plus Durvalumab Shows Survival Benefit in First-Line Extensive-Stage SCLC

"The Phase 3 DeLLphi-305 trial met its primary endpoint: adding tarlatamab (Imdelltra), a DLL3-targeted bispecific T-cell engager [a drug that physically bridges immune cells to cancer cells], to durvalumab (Imfinzi) maintenance significantly improved overall survival in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer after initial chemo-immunotherapy. This is the first Phase 3 survival win for a BiTE therapy in frontline SCLC. Full data (including the size of the survival benefit) have not yet been publicly released."

In the global, randomized, open-label DeLLphi-305 trial, 563 patients with extensive-stage SCLC [the most advanced form of small cell lung cancer] who did not progress after 4 cycles of induction therapy (durvalumab + platinum chemotherapy + etoposide) were randomized 1:1 to maintenance durvalumab alone vs. durvalumab plus tarlatamab.
At a pre-specified interim analysis, the combination met the primary endpoint of statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in overall survival. Progression-free survival and objective response rate were also positive. No specific hazard ratios, median survival figures, or p-values have been publicly released yet — only the topline result that the primary endpoint was met.
Safety was consistent with the known profiles of each drug; no new safety signals emerged. Post-infusion monitoring time for tarlatamab was reduced to 1–2 hours (compared to 22–24 hours required in the current US label for second-line use).
Amgen and AstraZeneca plan to present full data at an upcoming medical congress and submit to regulatory authorities for first-line maintenance approval.
ES-SCLC is aggressive: median survival is about 12 months with current standard treatment (durvalumab + chemotherapy followed by durvalumab maintenance). Only about 40% of patients survive long enough to receive second-line therapy (general medical guidance).
Moving tarlatamab (currently approved for second-line and beyond) into first-line maintenance attacks the disease earlier, when tumor burden is lower and the immune system may be more capable of responding.
The combination pairs two complementary immune approaches: durvalumab (a PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor [releases the brakes on immune cells]) while tarlatamab (a DLL3×CD3 BiTE [physically drags immune T cells to DLL3-expressing cancer cells]). DLL3 is found on the surface of SCLC cells in about 85–96% of patients.
If approved, this would be the first new frontline overall survival improvement in ES-SCLC since CASPIAN (durvalumab + chemotherapy) in 2020, and the first BiTE therapy to demonstrate a survival benefit in any solid tumor first-line setting.
Adults with newly diagnosed extensive-stage SCLC (stage IV, or stage III too extensive for curative chemoradiation) who have completed 4 cycles of platinum/etoposide + durvalumab induction without disease progression.
Patients with treated or untreated asymptomatic brain metastases were eligible in the trial — important, as SCLC frequently spreads to the brain.
Adequate organ function and performance status (ECOG 0–1) required.
DLL3 expression testing is not required for enrollment (DLL3 is expressed in the vast majority of SCLC), though future biomarker studies may identify patients who benefit most.
If newly diagnosed with ES-SCLC, confirm your treatment plan includes 4 cycles of platinum (carboplatin or cisplatin) + etoposide + durvalumab followed by durvalumab maintenance — this is the current standard of care.
Ask your oncologist: 'Am I a candidate for the DeLLphi-305 regimen (adding tarlatamab to durvalumab maintenance) once it receives FDA approval, or can I access it via clinical trial now?'
Request brain MRI at baseline and every 2–3 months during maintenance, as SCLC has a high risk of spreading to the brain. Early detection allows targeted radiation while continuing systemic therapy (general medical guidance).
Discuss cytokine release syndrome (CRS) [an immune overreaction that can cause fever, low blood pressure, and breathing difficulty] and neurotoxicity monitoring: tarlatamab requires step-up dosing (starting low and increasing over the first cycle) with 1–2 hour observation after early infusions.

The Hope

  • Hope: This may be the most important survival advance in frontline SCLC in years; earlier intervention before patients become too frail for further therapy could meaningfully change outcomes.
  • Reality: SCLC remains incurable for most patients. The exact size of the survival benefit is not yet public. Only about 50–60% of patients may reach the maintenance phase. CRS and neurotoxicity require specialized center expertise. Tarlatamab is unavailable in most countries outside the US (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • Cost: Tarlatamab and durvalumab are both expensive immunotherapies. Combined maintenance costs will be substantial; insurance coverage for first-line maintenance use is pending approval (general medical guidance).
  • Practicality: Step-up dosing and post-infusion monitoring require infusion center visits every 3 weeks; this may be challenging for patients in rural areas. Pharmacist-led CRS protocols are needed (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Tarlatamab (Imdelltra, Amgen): First-in-class DLL3×CD3 BiTE; accelerated approval May 2024 (second-line), traditional (full) approval November 2025 based on DeLLphi-304 (40% reduction in risk of death; median overall survival 13.6 vs 8.3 months compared to chemotherapy); now in first-line maintenance (DeLLphi-305).

Durvalumab (Imfinzi, AstraZeneca): PD-L1 inhibitor [a drug that helps the immune system recognize cancer]; approved for first-line ES-SCLC with chemotherapy (CASPIAN) and after chemoradiation in limited-stage SCLC (ADRIATIC).

DeLLphi-306: Phase 3 testing tarlatamab + durvalumab after chemoradiation in limited-stage SCLC (earlier-stage disease).

DeLLphi-312: Phase 3 testing tarlatamab + durvalumab in first-line ES-SCLC induction and maintenance.

Competing approaches: B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugates, CAR-T cell therapies, trispecific antibodies, and other BiTE agents are in various stages of development for SCLC.

Evidence Uncertainty

Strengths: Randomized Phase 3 design; 563 patients; met the primary overall survival endpoint at interim analysis; consistent progression-free survival and response rate benefit; safety consistent with each drug's known profile; included patients with brain metastases.

Limitations: Only topline results released as of this writing. Full data — including hazard ratios, median survival, subgroup analyses, and detailed safety — have not yet been made public. Follow-up duration and crossover design details are not described.

Key unknowns: The magnitude of the survival benefit (specific numbers not yet available); benefit in subgroups (platinum-sensitive vs resistant, brain metastases, DLL3 expression levels); long-term toxicity of combining a BiTE with a checkpoint inhibitor; the added cost of combining two expensive immunotherapies.

Resistance: DLL3 loss on tumor cells, T-cell exhaustion, and the immunosuppressive tumor environment are potential resistance mechanisms, though no published resistance data exist yet for this combination.

What To Watch

Full DeLLphi-305 data presentation — expected at WCLC 2026 (Seoul, September 12–15) or ESMO 2026. This will be critical for understanding the actual magnitude of survival benefit, subgroup results, and safety details.

FDA regulatory submission and review timeline (could be 6–10 months with Priority Review); potential label expansion to first-line maintenance.

DeLLphi-306 (limited-stage SCLC) and DeLLphi-312 (first-line induction + maintenance) readouts — will define the broader role of this combination.

Global access: Amgen's plans for approvals outside the US; tiered pricing; compassionate use programs.
