Novel Approaches to Overcome Endocrine and Targeted Therapy Resistance in Metastatic HR+/HER2- Disease

"Resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors and endocrine therapy remains the central challenge in metastatic HR+/HER2- breast cancer. New biomarkers (CDK1 activity), next-generation selective CDK4/2 inhibitors, oral SERDs (camizestrant), immunotherapy combinations, and resistance pathway targeting (S100-RAGE, PI3K/AKT) are emerging to extend endocrine-based treatment durability and delay chemotherapy."

CDK1 biomarker discovery: High CDK1 activity (functional, not just expression) predicts CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance across early and advanced ER+ breast cancer; reactivates after ~10 months of CDK4/6 inhibitor exposure in resistant cells.
AVZO-023 (selective CDK4 inhibitor) + fulvestrant: Phase I ORION-1 preliminary results at SABCS 2026; designed to spare CDK6 (avoiding neutropenia) and combine with CDK2 inhibitor AVZO-021 to target resistance.
Camizestrant (oral SERD) + CDK4/6 inhibitor: FDA-approved 2026 for ESR1-mutated HR+/HER2- advanced disease after CDK4/6 inhibitor; SERENA-6 showed ctDNA-guided switch at molecular progression improved PFS 9.2→16 months.
KEYNOTE-B49: adding pembrolizumab (an immunotherapy) to chemotherapy for PD-L1-positive HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer after endocrine therapy significantly improved the time without progression (hazard ratio 0.74; 95% CI 0.58-0.95; P=.0089) — but the actual difference in median time was small, 6.7 versus 6.0 months, and overall survival was NOT improved (independent fact-check: verified against the ESMO 2026 presentation; the lead investigator noted the hazard ratio looks strong while the median difference is small).
S100-RAGE pathway: Mediates early resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in metastatic breast cancer; blocking it restores T-DXd sensitivity in preclinical models.
Elinzanetant (OASIS-4): Non-hormonal NK3 receptor antagonist significantly reduces vasomotor symptoms in HR+ breast cancer on endocrine therapy—critical for adherence (hot flashes drive discontinuation).
CDK4/6 inhibitors + endocrine therapy are standard 1st-line for metastatic HR+/HER2- disease, but nearly all patients progress within 2-3 years; no validated biomarker exists to predict who benefits or when resistance emerges—CDK1 could be the first.
Current CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib) inhibit CDK4/6 indiscriminately, causing neutropenia (CDK6-mediated); selective CDK4 inhibitors (AVZO-023) may enable higher dosing, better combinations, and less hematologic toxicity.
ESR1 mutations drive ~40% of endocrine resistance; camizestrant + CDK4/6 inhibitor switching at molecular (ctDNA) progression—not radiographic—represents a paradigm shift: treat resistance before it becomes clinical.
Immunotherapy (pembrolizumab) shows modest but real benefit in PD-L1+ HR+/HER2- disease, especially CPS≥10; TroFuse-010 testing sacituzumab tirumotecan + pembrolizumab may improve this further.
S100-RAGE identifies a targetable resistance mechanism to T-DXd, now moving into earlier lines—combination strategies could extend ADC durability.
Hot flash management (elinzanetant) is not just comfort—it's adherence therapy; uncontrolled vasomotor symptoms are a leading cause of endocrine therapy discontinuation, which worsens survival.
Metastatic HR+/HER2- breast cancer patients on 1st-line CDK4/6 inhibitor + aromatase inhibitor: Future CDK1 testing could guide initial selection and serial monitoring for resistance.
Patients progressing on CDK4/6 inhibitor + endocrine therapy with ESR1 mutation detected in ctDNA: Eligible for camizestrant + CDK4/6 inhibitor switch per SERENA-6/FDA approval.
PD-L1+ (CPS≥1, especially ≥10) HR+/HER2- metastatic patients after endocrine/CDK4/6 progression: Candidates for pembrolizumab + chemo (KEYNOTE-B49) or future ADC + immunotherapy combinations.
Patients developing early resistance to T-DXd (HER2+ or HER2-low): S100-RAGE pathway inhibition may restore sensitivity—investigational.
Any HR+ breast cancer patient on endocrine therapy (tamoxifen, AI) with disruptive hot flashes: Elinzanetant offers non-hormonal option when SSRIs/gabapentin fail; preserves endocrine adherence.
If starting 1st-line CDK4/6 inhibitor + AI for metastatic HR+/HER2- disease: Ask: "Do you monitor for ESR1 mutations via ctDNA? If one emerges, will we switch to camizestrant + CDK4/6 inhibitor per SERENA-6?"
If progressing on CDK4/6 inhibitor + endocrine therapy: You may wish to request ctDNA testing (Guardant360, FoundationOne Liquid) for ESR1 mutations—if positive, discuss camizestrant + continued CDK4/6 inhibitor.
If PD-L1 testing not done on recent metastatic biopsy: Ask: "Should we test PD-L1 CPS? If ≥10, pembrolizumab + chemo is an option per KEYNOTE-B49; if ≥1, still eligible."
If experiencing severe hot flashes on tamoxifen/AI: You may wish to ask specifically for elinzanetant (OASIS-4 data) or referral to menopause specialist—do not accept "just deal with it"; adherence depends on symptom control.
If on T-DXd and progressing early: You may wish to ask about clinical trials targeting S100-RAGE or other resistance pathways (preclinical but advancing).
Premenopausal with early HR+/HER2- disease: You may wish to discuss ovarian function suppression (OFS) intensity/duration with your oncologist—tailor to recurrence risk, not one-size-fits-all.

The Hope

  • Hope: resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors and endocrine therapy is the central problem in HR-positive metastatic disease, and for the first time there are several distinct strategies being tested at once — a predictive biomarker (CDK1), a more selective next-generation inhibitor, blood-test-guided switching, immunotherapy for a defined subgroup, and symptom control to help people stay on treatment.
  • Reality: most of these are early. The CDK1 biomarker and the S100-RAGE pathway are research-stage; AVZO-023 is in Phase I. Only camizestrant (approved) and the KEYNOTE-B49 regimen are near-term options (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: the gains are measured in months, not cures. KEYNOTE-B49 improved median time without progression by under a month and did not extend overall survival, so benefit must be weighed against added chemotherapy and immunotherapy side effects.

The Reality

  • Reality: blood-test-guided switching (as in SERENA-6) requires repeated testing and specialist coordination, and is not yet standard practice everywhere (general medical guidance).
  • Managing side effects matters: menopausal symptoms are a common reason people stop endocrine therapy early, so non-hormonal options like elinzanetant may help people stay on treatment that works (general medical guidance).
  • Access and cost: newer oral agents, repeated blood testing, and immunotherapy all carry cost and coverage considerations that vary by plan and country (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

CDK1 activity biomarker: based on laboratory models plus analysis of stored patient tumor samples. It measures how active the protein is, not just whether it is present, which requires a functional test that is not yet routinely available. A prospective trial is needed before it can guide treatment.

AVZO-023 (selective CDK4 inhibitor): Phase I ORION-1 is an early-stage, small, non-randomized study focused mainly on safety and dose-finding. The idea of sparing CDK6 to reduce low-white-blood-cell counts is biologically reasonable but not yet proven in patients.

Camizestrant and SERENA-6: the strategy of switching treatment when an ESR1 mutation appears in the blood — before scans show worsening — prolonged time without progression (about 9.2 to 16 months), but overall survival data are not yet mature, and how often to test is not standardized (general medical guidance).

KEYNOTE-B49: the hazard ratio of 0.74 was statistically significant, but the median gain was under one month and overall survival did not improve, so the clinical meaningfulness for an individual patient is debatable.

S100-RAGE resistance pathway: preclinical only (laboratory and animal models). No drug targeting this pathway has been tested in people with breast cancer.

Elinzanetant (OASIS-4): reduces hot flushes and night sweats in women on endocrine therapy. It treats a side effect rather than the cancer, and its effect on whether patients stay on endocrine therapy long-term has not been established (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Prospective trials testing whether the CDK1 activity biomarker can actually guide treatment choices, rather than just predict resistance retrospectively (general medical guidance).

Further ORION-1 data for AVZO-023, and whether a selective CDK4 inhibitor genuinely reduces low-white-blood-cell counts while remaining effective (general medical guidance).

Mature overall survival results from SERENA-6, which will show whether switching early on a blood test translates into living longer, not just longer before progression (general medical guidance).

Whether regulators and guideline bodies adopt the KEYNOTE-B49 regimen for PD-L1-positive HR+/HER2- disease, given the small median gain and lack of survival benefit (general medical guidance).

Whether any drug targeting the S100-RAGE pathway advances from laboratory work into first-in-human testing to restore sensitivity to trastuzumab deruxtecan (general medical guidance).

Longer-term data on whether better menopausal-symptom control actually improves how long people stay on endocrine therapy (general medical guidance).
