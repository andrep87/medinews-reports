New Treatment Advances for Advanced Colorectal Cancer — Immunotherapy Combinations, KRAS Inhibitors, Precision Aspirin, and Robotic Surgery Parity

"For patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (MSS, the 95% without high microsatellite instability), three novel approaches advanced: the FDA extended review of zanzalintinib + atezolizumab (first chemo-free immunotherapy combo showing survival benefit in MSS mCRC); a pan-KRAS inhibitor (KST-6051) received Fast Track designation; and pooled data confirmed aspirin's benefit is restricted to tumors with PIK3CA exon 9/20 mutations. Meanwhile, robotic and laparoscopic rectal cancer surgery after chemoradiation showed equivalent long-term outcomes, and a robotic colonoscopy system (Triton 1) gained FDA clearance."

Zanzalintinib + atezolizumab: FDA extended PDUFA date to March 3, 2027 (3-month extension) after Exelixis submitted updated safety/efficacy data from STELLAR-303 (n=901). The combo (TKI + PD-L1 inhibitor) showed median OS 10.9 vs 9.4 months vs regorafenib (HR 0.80, P=0.0045) in pretreated MSS mCRC — the first phase III immunotherapy combo with statistically significant OS benefit in this population. Grade 3+ adverse events: 59% vs 37%.
KST-6051: Oral pan-KRAS inhibitor (active against GTP- and GDP-bound KRAS) received FDA Fast Track for KRAS-mutant solid tumors (PDAC, CRC, NSCLC). First-in-human FALCON trial (NCT07458347) dosing since April 2026; expansion cohorts planned.
Aspirin precision: Pooled analysis of ASCOLT, SAKK 41/13, ALASCCA (471 patients with PIK3CA exon 9/20 mutations) showed 39% disease-free survival improvement (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.39-0.96). Broader PI3K-pathway mutations did not show a consistent benefit. The authors note that NCCN currently recommends aspirin for any PI3K-pathway mutation, but argue the data only support the narrower exon 9/20 subgroup.
ARISTOTLE trial (UK, 75 hospitals): Adding irinotecan to preoperative chemoradiotherapy (capecitabine + RT) for high-risk rectal cancer did *not* improve survival and increased toxicity — confirming current standard (capecitabine + RT) remains optimal.
Robotic vs laparoscopic rectal surgery: 463-patient propensity-matched cohort after neoadjuvant chemoradiation — 5-year DFS 89.8% vs 82.3% (NS), OS 96.8% vs 86.8% (attenuated after adjustment, HR 0.23, P=0.052). Operative time longer with robot; ileostomy rate similar.
Triton 1 robotic colonoscopy: FDA 510(k) cleared Sept 2026. CARE 1 study (n=50): 100% cecal intubation, 0 adverse events, 54.2% adenoma detection rate (vs 35% benchmark), 67% lower ergonomic burden (NASA-TLX). Indicated for screening, surveillance, EMR, ESD.
Zanzalintinib + atezolizumab: If approved, offers a chemotherapy-free option for heavily pretreated MSS mCRC [microsatellite stable metastatic colorectal cancer, the ~95% that don't respond to standard immunotherapy] — a population with very limited effective therapies. The comparator arm (regorafenib) had median OS of 9.4 months. The TKI [a targeted drug] targets MET, VEGFR, and TAM kinases to potentially make the tumor more responsive to immunotherapy.
KST-6051: KRAS mutations are common in CRC. Current approved KRAS inhibitors only target the G12C variant, which is a small fraction. A pan-KRAS inhibitor [a drug that blocks multiple KRAS variants] could treat a much larger group of patients — a major unmet need (general medical guidance).
Aspirin: Cheap, accessible — but the benefit appears restricted to the approximately 11% of CRC patients with PIK3CA exon 9/20 mutations (based on the ASCOLT translational cohort). Testing is already routine on NGS panels [comprehensive genetic tests]; acting on exon 9/20 results adds near-zero cost.
Surgical equivalence: Patients can choose robotic or laparoscopic surgery based on surgeon expertise and availability — no survival penalty was found. Robotic surgery may offer ergonomic benefits for surgeons, though this was not specifically studied in the source (general medical guidance).
Triton 1: Robotic colonoscopy could reduce missed polyps (instability is a known cause of post-colonoscopy cancer) and surgeon injury — potentially raising quality ceiling for screening.
Zanzalintinib + atezolizumab: Patients with MSS mCRC who have progressed on fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin, irinotecan, and (if RAS wild-type) anti-EGFR therapy — i.e., 3rd-line+.
KST-6051: Patients with advanced KRAS-mutant CRC (any variant) who have exhausted standard therapies — FALCON trial enrolling now (NCT07458347).
Aspirin: Patients with resected stage II/III CRC whose tumor has PIK3CA exon 9/20 mutation — ask for specific exon-level results, not just 'PIK3CA mutated.'
Rectal cancer surgery: Patients with locally advanced rectal cancer (cT3–4 or N+) undergoing neoadjuvant chemoradiation — both robotic and laparoscopic TME are oncologically equivalent.
Screening/surveillance patients: Triton 1 has received FDA clearance; commercial launch timeline has not been announced. Ask if your endoscopy unit is evaluating robotic platforms.
If you have MSS mCRC progressing after standard chemotherapy, ask: 'Is the zanzalintinib + atezolizumab combination available via clinical trial while awaiting FDA decision?' Reference STELLAR-303 (NCT05425940).
If you have KRAS-mutant advanced CRC, ask: "Am I eligible for the FALCON trial of KST-6051 (NCT07458347) or other pan-KRAS inhibitor studies?"
If you have resected stage II/III CRC, request PIK3CA exon-specific testing (exons 9 and 20) on your tumor block — if positive, discuss adding low-dose aspirin with your oncologist (per pooled data; NCCN recommends aspirin for PI3K-pathway mutations but the authors argue data only support exon 9/20 specifically).
If facing rectal cancer surgery after chemoradiation, ask your surgeon: "Do you perform both robotic and laparoscopic TME? What is *your* personal outcome data with each?" — surgeon experience matters more than platform.
For screening colonoscopy: Ask your gastroenterologist if they track their adenoma detection rate (ADR) [the percentage of screenings where at least one polyp is found]. The Triton 1 achieved 54.2% ADR in its pivotal study versus a 35% industry benchmark.

The Hope

  • Zanzalintinib combo: Hope — first immunotherapy breakthrough in MSS mCRC. Reality — adds ~1.5 months median OS, significant toxicity, likely high cost, not curative. Access pending FDA approval + coverage.
  • Pan-KRAS inhibitor: Hope — finally targeting the "undruggable" majority of KRAS mutations. Reality — years from approval; toxicity unknown; resistance inevitable (as with all targeted monotherapies).
  • Aspirin: Hope — pennies a day, reduces recurrence in a molecularly defined subset. Reality — only helps ~15% of patients; bleeding risk (though low at 200 mg); not yet standard of care.

The Reality

  • Robotic surgery: Hope — better ergonomics, potential for wider adoption of minimally invasive TME. Reality — longer OR time, higher cost, no proven patient survival advantage.
  • Robotic colonoscopy: Hope — fewer missed lesions, less physician burnout. Reality — early stage; cost/access unknown; learning curve; not yet proven to reduce interval cancer rates.

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Zanzalintinib (XL092, Exelixis): Oral multitargeted TKI (MET, VEGFR1–3, TYRO3, AXL, MER). + Atezolizumab (Tecentriq, Genentech/Roche): IV PD-L1 inhibitor. NDA under FDA review (PDUFA March 3, 2027).

KST-6051 (Kestrel Therapeutics): Oral pan-KRAS inhibitor. Fast Track. Phase 1 FALCON (NCT07458347) enrolling KRAS-mutant solid tumors. AbbVie has exclusive option to acquire.

Aspirin (generic): 200–300 mg daily. Adjuvant setting. Benefit confirmed only for PIK3CA exon 9/20 hotspot mutations. Prospective meta-analysis of 10 trials (7,043 patients) ongoing.

Standard preoperative rectal regimen: Capecitabine plus pelvic radiation — the ARISTOTLE trial (75 UK hospitals) confirmed no benefit from adding irinotecan.

Triton 1 (Neptune Medical): Robotic flexible endoscopy system. Dynamic Rigidization technology. 510(k) cleared. CARE 1 pivotal study completed. Commercial launch pending.

Evidence Uncertainty

STELLAR-303: Primary OS positive (HR 0.80), but dual primary endpoint (OS in non-liver mets subgroup) *not* met (HR 0.77, CI 0.59–1.01). Higher grade 3+ toxicity (59% vs 37%). FDA extension suggests close scrutiny of benefit-risk.

KST-6051: First-in-human — no human efficacy or safety data yet. Pan-KRAS inhibition is a novel mechanism; potential for on-target side effects in normal tissues is unknown (general medical guidance).

Aspirin meta-analysis: Post-hoc subgroup pooling across three trials. ASCOLT started earlier (different staging, chemotherapy timing). SAKK excluded rectal cancer. Interaction P=0.049 for non-exon 9/20 mutations signals heterogeneity. The approximately 11% exon 9/20 prevalence comes from the ASCOLT translational cohort (45/397).

Robotic surgery study: Retrospective, single-center (Sun Yat-sen), propensity-matched but residual confounding likely (OS benefit attenuated after adjustment). Longer operative time is a consistent robotic drawback.

Triton 1: n=50, single center (Poland), 14-day follow-up only. ADR 54.2% is promising but benchmark (35%) is for average-risk screening — not directly comparable. Long-term outcomes (missed cancer rates, complication profiles) unknown.

What To Watch

FDA decision on zanzalintinib + atezolizumab (March 3, 2027 PDUFA) — watch for ODAC meeting or approval letter.

FALCON trial dose-escalation results and expansion cohort launches for KST-6051 — timing of data presentation not yet announced.

Prospective aspirin trial (e.g., ADD-ASPIRIN, ASCOLT follow-up) testing exon 9/20-specific randomization.

CRE ARTEMIS and other early-onset consortia reporting on environmental/microbiome risk factors — may inform prevention for rising young-adult incidence.

Triton 1 multi-center pivotal trial and real-world ADR/missed lesion data post-commercialization.
