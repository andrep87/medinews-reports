FDA Approves First Immunotherapy Allowing Some Rectal Cancer Patients to Avoid Surgery, Radiation, and Chemotherapy

"On October 19, 2026, the FDA granted accelerated approval to dostarlimab (Jemperli) for adults with stage II or III locally advanced rectal cancer that is mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H). This biomarker-driven immunotherapy enables many patients to achieve a complete response without needing surgery, radiation, or chemotherapy, preserving bowel, bladder, and sexual function."

On October 19, 2026, the FDA granted accelerated approval to dostarlimab (Jemperli) for adults with previously untreated stage II or III locally advanced rectal cancer that is mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) [tumor features showing the cancer struggles to fix DNA errors, which makes it especially responsive to immunotherapy], confirmed by an FDA-authorized test.
Approval was based on the AZUR-1 trial (154 patients). Results differed by how response was measured: by independent central review, about 47% of evaluable patients had a sustained complete response at 12 months; by investigator assessment, about 68% did; the overall response rate was 79% (independent fact-check: the draft led with the 68% investigator figure; the FDA primary endpoint by central review was ~47%).
Overall, 110 of 154 patients (71%) received dostarlimab alone without additional treatment or disease progression; 26 (17%) had surgery and 13 (8%) received additional therapy or radiation.
An earlier Memorial Sloan Kettering study of 50 patients showed very high sustained complete-response rates; one local recurrence was later reported at 3+ years (independent fact-check: corrected from the draft's 'no recurrences'). This is the first immunotherapy approved specifically for dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer, which represents roughly 5-10% of such cancers (one source cites about 3% for locally advanced disease).
Standard treatment for locally advanced rectal cancer usually combines chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery, which can cause lasting bowel, bladder, and sexual problems. Immunotherapy alone may let some patients avoid all three.
Patients who achieve a complete response can enter a 'watch-and-wait' surveillance program [close monitoring with scans and scope exams instead of immediate surgery], potentially preserving organ function (general medical guidance).
The approval establishes biomarker-guided immunotherapy as a curative-intent strategy for a molecularly defined group, shifting away from one-size-fits-all treatment.
Longer-term follow-up from the earlier study suggests some patients may be cured, though accelerated approval means long-term certainty is still pending (general medical guidance).
Adults with newly diagnosed stage II or III (locally advanced) rectal adenocarcinoma that has not spread to distant organs.

The Hope

  • Hope: for the first time, a subset of rectal cancer patients may be cured with immunotherapy alone, avoiding surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy.
  • Reality: only about 5-10% of rectal cancers are dMMR/MSI-H; most patients still need standard combined treatment.

The Reality

  • Reality: in AZUR-1, roughly 17% still needed surgery and 8% needed additional therapy; watch-and-wait requires intensive, long-term surveillance (general medical guidance).
  • Reality: accelerated approval could be withdrawn if the confirmatory trial does not confirm long-term benefit, and insurance coverage for a new, costly therapy may vary (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Dostarlimab (Jemperli, GSK): a PD-1 inhibitor [a drug that releases a brake on the immune system so it can attack cancer] given 500 mg IV every 3 weeks for up to 9 cycles (about 6 months). Accelerated approval for dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer.

AZUR-2 trial (ongoing): testing perioperative dostarlimab (before and after surgery) versus standard approaches; this is the confirmatory trial required for full approval.

NCCN guidelines include neoadjuvant PD-1 blockade as an option for dMMR locally advanced rectal cancer, based on earlier data (general medical guidance).

Other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (pembrolizumab, nivolumab) are approved for dMMR/MSI-H metastatic colorectal cancer; their role specifically in locally advanced rectal cancer continues to be studied (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

AZUR-1: 154 patients, single-arm (no control group). The primary endpoint was sustained clinical complete response at 12 months by independent central review (about 47%); investigator-assessed response was higher (about 68%); overall response rate was 79%.

At a median follow-up of 26.3 months, none of the patients with a complete or near-complete response had experienced progression.

Key uncertainty: accelerated approval means long-term relapse risk is not fully known; continued approval requires the confirmatory AZUR-2 trial to show lasting benefit.

Safety: serious adverse events occurred in about 20%; common side effects included fatigue, rash, diarrhea, and itching; a small percentage stopped treatment due to side effects (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

AZUR-2 phase III results (expected around 2028) comparing perioperative dostarlimab with standard of care — important for full approval.

Full AZUR-1 data presentation at the ESMO Congress (October 2026), including longer follow-up and quality-of-life data.

International regulatory decisions in other countries reviewing the drug (general medical guidance).

Expansion of immunotherapy into earlier-stage dMMR colon cancers and other dMMR solid tumors based on this precedent (general medical guidance).
