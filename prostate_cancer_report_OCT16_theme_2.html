Biomarker-Driven First-Line Therapy Is Reshaping Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer

"New FDA approvals increasingly match first-line treatment to specific tumor biomarkers in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC): BRCA2 mutations for abiraterone plus niraparib (AKEEGA), PTEN loss for abiraterone plus capivasertib, and PSMA-PET-positive disease for ARSI plus Pluvicto. This shifts care from one-size-fits-all toward precision therapy, though overall survival data remain immature and biomarkers can overlap."

AMPLITUDE trial: adding niraparib (a PARP inhibitor [a drug that blocks a DNA-repair enzyme, which is especially lethal to cancers with faulty repair genes]) to abiraterone plus ADT improved radiographic progression-free survival in mHSPC with homologous-recombination-repair (HRR) mutations; the FDA approved AKEEGA (a niraparib-plus-abiraterone combination pill) for BRCA2-mutated patients.
CAPItello-281 trial: adding capivasertib (an AKT inhibitor [a drug that blocks a cell-growth signaling protein]) to abiraterone plus ADT improved radiographic progression-free survival in PTEN-deficient mHSPC; the combination was approved in 2026.
PSMAddition trial: adding Pluvicto (177Lu-PSMA-617) to an ARSI [androgen receptor signaling inhibitor, a hormone-blocking pill] plus ADT improved radiographic progression-free survival in PSMA-PET-positive mHSPC; approved in 2026.
Standard doublet (ADT plus ARSI) and triplet (ADT plus ARSI plus docetaxel chemotherapy) remain options, especially for high-volume disease without these biomarkers (general medical guidance).
Historically, most mHSPC patients received similar therapy (ADT with or without docetaxel and an ARSI). Now tumor genetics (BRCA2, PTEN) and PSMA-PET imaging can direct distinct targeted combinations.
For biomarker-selected patients, targeted combinations can avoid chemotherapy's acute side effects, though they carry their own risks (anemia with PARP inhibitors; diarrhea and high blood sugar with capivasertib) (general medical guidance).
Median overall survival in modern mHSPC trials now often exceeds 5 years, which opens discussions about preserving quality of life over long treatment courses (general medical guidance).
Newly diagnosed metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (either present at diagnosis or recurrent).
BRCA2 mutation (inherited or tumor-only) → may be eligible for ADT plus abiraterone plus niraparib (AKEEGA).
PTEN loss on tissue testing → may be eligible for ADT plus abiraterone plus capivasertib.
PSMA-PET-positive disease → may be eligible for ADT plus ARSI plus Pluvicto (requires a licensed radioligand facility).
In general, comprehensive biomarker testing (HRR genes, PTEN, and PSMA PET) at diagnosis helps match patients to these options (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask for germline (inherited) genetic testing and tumor genomic profiling at diagnosis, since results determine eligibility for AKEEGA or other PARP combinations (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: chemotherapy-free intensive options for biomarker-defined subgroups; an oral combination (AKEEGA) adds convenience; Pluvicto offers a non-chemotherapy option for PSMA-positive patients, including some who cannot take chemotherapy.
  • Reality: no regimen has proven an overall survival benefit yet in this setting; biomarkers can overlap, and there are no data on combining these targeted agents; capivasertib's intermittent schedule can challenge adherence; and Pluvicto access is limited to licensed centers (general medical guidance).

The Reality

  • The long-term toxicity of years of continuous targeted therapy is not yet known; treatment-interruption strategies are being studied (general medical guidance).

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

AKEEGA (niraparib plus abiraterone combination pill): taken once daily with prednisone and ADT; anemia is a main side effect, managed with dose adjustment. (Exact doses follow the product labeling) (general medical guidance).

Capivasertib plus abiraterone plus ADT: capivasertib is taken on an intermittent schedule (several days on, several days off); side effects include diarrhea, rash, and high blood sugar, requiring monitoring (general medical guidance).

Pluvicto (177Lu-PSMA-617) plus ARSI plus ADT: given by IV over several cycles; requires PSMA-PET positivity and a licensed facility; side effects include dry mouth, fatigue, and low blood counts (general medical guidance).

Talazoparib plus enzalutamide (studied in the ZZ-FIRST phase 2 trial in high-volume mHSPC): the broader talazoparib-plus-enzalutamide program is being evaluated for benefit beyond BRCA2; regulatory status for the mHSPC setting is still developing (general medical guidance).

Evidence Uncertainty

The three approvals were based on radiographic progression-free survival; overall survival data are still immature (hazard ratios favor treatment but are not yet statistically mature).

In AMPLITUDE, BRCA2 appeared to drive the benefit; some other HRR genes showed less clear benefit. Guidelines tend to emphasize BRCA2 (general medical guidance).

For capivasertib, benefit appears to correlate with the depth of PTEN loss; there is no head-to-head comparison against the docetaxel triplet (general medical guidance).

In PSMAddition, crossover (control-arm patients later receiving Pluvicto) can obscure overall survival; a notable feature was benefit regardless of disease volume (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

Regulatory decisions on talazoparib plus enzalutamide for broader HRR alterations (general medical guidance).

Trials refining when to add docetaxel based on early PSA response (general medical guidance).

Treatment-interruption trials (for example, pausing ADT/ARPI after a deep response) using testosterone recovery as a patient-centered endpoint (general medical guidance).

Real-world evidence on the best sequence of PARP inhibitors, Pluvicto, and chemotherapy, and cost-effectiveness of biomarker-guided pathways (general medical guidance).

Validation of genomic instability scores to detect HRD beyond BRCA (general medical guidance).
