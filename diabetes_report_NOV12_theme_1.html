Tandem Mobi Insulin Pump Recall — Critical Safety Alert for Over 80,000 Users

"The FDA has issued a Class 1 recall (the most serious type) for all Tandem Mobi insulin pumps in the U.S. due to false occlusion alarms that can stop insulin delivery. Over 230 users have needed medical care or hospitalization. If you use this pump, you must respond to every alarm and keep a backup insulin method ready."

The FDA classified this as a Class 1 recall, meaning use of the device may cause serious injury or death. (general medical guidance)
Increased friction in the pump's drive module reduces motor power, triggering false "occlusion" (blockage) alarms that pause insulin delivery. (general medical guidance)
Tandem has received 230 reports of users needing medical intervention or hospitalization as of late October 2026; no deaths have been reported.
The recall covers more than 80,900 Mobi pumps distributed in the U.S.; all models and serial numbers may be affected. (general medical guidance)
An insulin pump that stops delivering insulin without warning can cause dangerously high blood sugar (hyperglycemia) and diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a life-threatening emergency. (general medical guidance)
False alarms may become more frequent over time, making the pump unreliable for the basal (background) insulin many users depend on 24/7. (general medical guidance)
This is the second major safety issue with the Mobi system in two years; a software malfunction in 2025 also risked insulin delivery errors. (general medical guidance)
Tandem is contacting affected users directly to arrange replacement pumps, but the process takes time. (general medical guidance)
Every person currently using a Tandem Mobi insulin pump in the United States, regardless of how long they have had the device. (general medical guidance)
Caregivers and family members who help manage the pump for a child or dependent adult. (general medical guidance)
Healthcare providers (endocrinologists, diabetes educators, primary care doctors) who prescribe or support this device.
Anyone considering starting the Mobi pump should discuss the recall status with their care team before proceeding. (general medical guidance)
Do not stop using your pump yet. Continue wearing it, but treat every occlusion alarm as real: check the infusion site, troubleshoot per the manual, and restart insulin delivery immediately. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to keep a backup insulin delivery method (insulin pens or syringes with rapid-acting and long-acting insulin) at home, at work/school, and in your travel bag at all times. (general medical guidance)
Enable mobile app notifications on your smartphone so you never miss an alarm, especially during sleep. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to contact Tandem customer support (1-877-801-6901) if alarms persist after troubleshooting, or to confirm your device is on the replacement list and schedule a swap. (general medical guidance)

  • Reality: Insurance coverage for backup supplies (pens, syringes, extra sensors) during the transition may vary; verify with your plan now.

Research Progress

Tandem is replacing affected pumps with updated devices that have modified drive modules to reduce friction. (general medical guidance)

The company's first tubeless pump option, Mobi Tubeless, is in late-stage FDA review and may offer a different mechanical design. (general medical guidance)

Tandem is also collaborating with the University of Virginia on a fully closed-loop system (no meal announcements needed) with a pivotal trial planned before year-end.

New CEO Jeannette Bankes (formerly of GE HealthCare) begins November 16, 2026; she has prioritized the tubeless launch and pharmacy-channel coverage.
