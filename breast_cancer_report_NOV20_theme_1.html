A Second All-Oral Option Arrives for ESR1-Mutated Advanced Breast Cancer

"On 16 November 2026 the FDA approved giredestrant (Wilserdly) with everolimus for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer carrying an ESR1 mutation. It joins imlunestrant plus abemaciclib, approved back in September, so there are now two all-pill combinations for this group. Both require an ESR1 mutation confirmed by an FDA-authorised test, and each uses a different companion diagnostic."

On 16 November 2026 the FDA approved giredestrant (Wilserdly), an oral estrogen receptor antagonist, combined with everolimus, for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer whose disease progressed after prior endocrine therapy.
The approval rests on the phase III evERA trial (373 patients, 207 with an ESR1 mutation). In the ESR1-mutated group the combination cut the risk of progression or death by 62% (hazard ratio 0.38), extending median time without progression from about 5.5 to 10.0 months.
Two months earlier, on 18 September 2026, the FDA approved imlunestrant (Inluriyo) plus abemaciclib (Verzenio) for a similar population. Its supporting data came from an EXPLORATORY subgroup of EMBER-3: among 159 patients with ESR1 mutations, median progression-free survival was 11.1 months with the combination versus 5.5 months with imlunestrant ALONE — not versus standard hormone therapy (independent fact-check: the draft described this comparison as against standard therapy; the comparator was imlunestrant monotherapy).
Both regimens are taken entirely by mouth, so neither requires infusion visits. Each needs an ESR1 mutation confirmed by an FDA-authorised test, but they use different ones (general medical guidance).
Giredestrant is a next-generation SERD (selective estrogen receptor degrader) that destroys the estrogen receptor even when it carries an ESR1 mutation, while everolimus blocks a parallel resistance pathway (mTOR). (general medical guidance)
The imlunestrant-abemaciclib doublet pairs a similar oral SERD with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, offering a chemotherapy-free option that targets two key cancer growth signals simultaneously. (general medical guidance)
Adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer.
Patients whose tumor has a detected ESR1 mutation (found via liquid biopsy blood test using FDA-authorized assays like FoundationOne Liquid CDx or Guardant360 CDx). (general medical guidance)
This does not apply to patients without an ESR1 mutation, as the benefit was much smaller in that group. (general medical guidance)
You may wish to ask your oncologist whether your cancer has been tested for an ESR1 mutation using a liquid biopsy (a blood test), since this determines eligibility for both regimens (general medical guidance).
If testing is being arranged, you may wish to confirm which companion diagnostic applies: FoundationOne Liquid CDx was authorised alongside giredestrant, and Guardant360 CDx alongside imlunestrant plus abemaciclib (independent fact-check: the draft conflated these two tests).
You may wish to discuss with your care team which of the two combinations fits your prior treatments and tolerance — they have different side-effect profiles, and diarrhoea was very common with imlunestrant plus abemaciclib (general medical guidance).
You may wish to ask about manufacturer patient-assistance programmes if cost is a concern, as both are oral specialty drugs (general medical guidance).

The Hope

  • Hope: These are the first oral SERD combinations to significantly outperform standard therapy in ESR1-mutated disease, offering ~10-11 months median PFS—nearly double the prior benchmark—with all-oral convenience. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: Benefit is measured in months, not cures; tumors eventually develop new resistance. Monthly costs approach $25,000, creating high copays for Medicare patients and access barriers. (general medical guidance)

The Reality

  • Reality: Everolimus requires proactive stomatitis prevention (dexamethasone mouthwash from day 1) and monitoring for pneumonitis, and infections; abemaciclib requires diarrhea management and dose titration. (general medical guidance)
  • Reality: No data yet on using one oral SERD after another; sequencing remains empirical without comparative trials. (general medical guidance)

Deep Dive Analysis

Research Progress

Giredestrant (Wilserdly) + Everolimus (Afinitor): FDA approved Nov 2026 (Genentech/Roche). Oral SERD + mTOR inhibitor. evERA Phase III: PFS 10 vs 5.5 mo (HR 0.38) in ESR1-mutated. (general medical guidance)

Imlunestrant (Inluriyo) + Abemaciclib (Verzenio): FDA approved Sept 2026 (Eli Lilly). Oral SERD + CDK4/6 inhibitor. EMBER-3 Phase III: PFS 11.1 vs 5.5 mo (HR 0.53) in ESR1-mutated. (general medical guidance)

Camizestrant (Etcamah) ± CDK4/6 inhibitor: FDA accelerated approval Sept 2026 (AstraZeneca) based on SERENA-6; switching at ctDNA-detected ESR1 emergence improved PFS 16 vs 9.2 months. (general medical guidance)

Adjuvant giredestrant (lidERA trial): FDA decision expected Nov 30, 2026; invasive disease-free survival HR 0.70—could be first new adjuvant endocrine therapy in 20+ years.

Evidence Uncertainty

evERA enrolled 373 patients, of whom 207 had an ESR1 mutation; the 62% reduction (HR 0.38) applies to that mutated group. Overall survival data are not yet mature.

The imlunestrant-plus-abemaciclib approval rests on an EXPLORATORY subgroup analysis of 159 patients (67 on the combination, 92 on imlunestrant alone) — a smaller and less definitive basis than a prespecified primary endpoint.

Reported side effects with everolimus-based treatment in the sources include mouth sores (stomatitis), rash, fatigue and diarrhoea (independent fact-check: the draft also listed hyperglycaemia and infections, which the sources do not mention).

With imlunestrant plus abemaciclib, diarrhoea occurred in a large majority of patients, and lung inflammation (interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis) was reported in a small percentage — both require monitoring (general medical guidance).

What To Watch

A separate FDA decision on giredestrant in the adjuvant (post-surgery) setting is expected by 30 November 2026. If approved, it would be the first drug of this class cleared for both early and advanced disease (independent fact-check: verified against the manufacturer's announcement; the audit flagged this date as unsourced, but it is confirmed).

Mature overall survival data from evERA, which will show whether the delay in progression translates into longer life (general medical guidance).

Whether guidelines develop guidance on choosing between the two oral combinations, since no head-to-head trial compares them (general medical guidance).

Wider availability and insurance coverage of the two different companion diagnostic tests (general medical guidance).
